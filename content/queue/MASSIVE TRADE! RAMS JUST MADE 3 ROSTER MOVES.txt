First, let’s lay out exactly what happened, according to the Rams’ official transactions page.

On Monday, October 19th, the Rams placed kicker Blake Grupe on injured reserve. On Tuesday, October 20th, they signed free agent kicker Brandon McManus and placed wide receiver Davante Adams and nose tackle Poona Ford on injured reserve. And on Wednesday, October 21st, they placed defensive end Kobie Turner on injured reserve, signed outside linebacker Darryl Peterson III from the practice squad to the active roster, and signed nose tackle Jalen Hunt to the practice squad.

That’s the full list. No trade. No surprise release of a big name. Just a team trying to fill holes after one of the most brutal injury weeks of the McVay era.

Now let’s go move by move, starting with the one I think matters most.

Move number one: Brandon McManus at kicker.

You know the backstory by now. Harrison Mevis was waived after the Buffalo game. Blake Grupe made a 44-yard field goal in his Rams debut against Arizona, then tore his ACL making a tackle on the very next kickoff. The Rams finished that game without a kicker, and punter Ethan Evans kicked the go-ahead extra point. So McManus becomes the Rams’ third official kicker in three weeks.

But I want to give you some details about McManus’ time in Green Bay that a lot of people haven’t heard, because they paint a much clearer picture.

According to the AP, McManus’ first season in Green Bay, in 2024, was close to perfect. He went 20 of 21 on field goals and 30 of 30 on extra points, and he made game-winning kicks in his first two games with the Packers. That’s elite kicking.

His 2025 season was different. According to the AP, he went 24 of 30 on field goals and 32 of 33 on extra points. But here’s the number that jumps out: he was just 6 of 12 on field goals from 40 yards or longer. He also missed two games with a quadriceps injury.

Put those two seasons together, and according to the AP, McManus went 44 of 51 on field goals with the Packers, including 6 of 8 from 50 yards or more. And that matches what the transcript going around says the Rams’ official announcement highlighted: 44 of 51 on field goals and 62 of 63 on extra points over 25 games.

Then there are the playoffs, and this is the part that worries some Rams fans. According to the AP, McManus missed a 38-yard field goal in Green Bay’s 22-10 playoff loss at Philadelphia after the 2024 season. And in last season’s wild-card game at Chicago, he missed two field goals and an extra point in a 31-27 loss, after the Packers blew a 21-3 halftime lead. McManus himself called it an “embarrassing performance.” In April, Green Bay drafted kicker Trey Smack in the sixth round, and in May, according to the AP, the Packers released McManus.

So here’s the honest scouting report. McManus is a 35-year-old veteran with a Super Bowl ring from his Denver days, a career 82 percent field goal kicker, and an almost-perfect 2024. But in 2025, his kicks from 40-plus yards became a real problem, and he struggled in the biggest moment of the season. The Rams are betting that the 2024 version shows up — and that kicking indoors at Allegiant Stadium, under a fixed roof, makes his job easier.

And don’t forget the bigger résumé. According to Pro Football Talk, in 186 career regular-season games, McManus has made 82 percent of his field goals and 97.4 percent of his extra points. And during the Broncos’ Super Bowl run after the 2015 season, he went a perfect 10 for 10 on field goals in the playoffs. There’s even a storyline for today: the head coach of that Super Bowl team was Gary Kubiak — the father of Raiders head coach Klint Kubiak. So McManus makes his Rams debut against the son of the coach he won a ring with.

Why does this move matter most? Because of how close the Rams’ games have been. The Rams have lost three games by a combined nine points: four at Denver, three to Buffalo in overtime, and two to Arizona. Against Buffalo, a missed 50-yarder and a blocked 48-yarder cost them. Against Arizona, having no kicker forced McVay to run out the clock before halftime, go for two after a touchdown, and go for it on fourth-and-1 at the Arizona 27. As McVay put it, “We didn’t have a field goal operation.” A reliable kicker changes every one of those decisions.

The transcript going around made a good point here: a trustworthy kicker gives a head coach options. On fourth down near midfield, a coach with a good kicker can take the points. A coach without one has to gamble. That’s why McManus is the most important move of the week.

And here’s one more thing to watch: the operation. A kicker doesn’t kick alone. McManus will be working with long snapper Joe Cardona and holder Ethan Evans — and he’s only had a few practices with them. Evans is the one constant through all of this: he’s held for Mevis, for Grupe, and now for McManus. Timing between the snap, the hold and the kick usually takes reps to build. So if the first kick today is clean, that’s a big deal.

Move number two: Darryl Peterson III to the active roster.

Most Rams fans don’t know much about Peterson, so let me introduce him, because his story is a great one.

According to Yardbarker, Peterson is 23, from Akron, Ohio, and he spent five seasons at the University of Wisconsin, from 2021 to 2025. He played in 53 games for the Badgers and started for two seasons. In his college career, he had 139 tackles, 24.5 tackles for loss, 14 sacks and seven pass breakups. He’s listed at 6-foot-1 and 260 pounds.

He went undrafted in April and signed with the Rams as a free agent. According to Yardbarker, he was waived in the final roster cuts on August 30th, then came back to the practice squad the next day. Earlier this season, the Rams elevated him from the practice squad for the Week 4 game at Philadelphia, when Aaron Donald was out. And now, he’s been signed to the active roster for good.

And here’s a fun detail: according to Yardbarker, Darryl Peterson III is the older brother of Darryn Peterson, whom the Utah Jazz took second overall in the 2026 NBA Draft. That’s quite a sports family. One brother is starting his rookie NBA season in Utah, and the other is fighting for snaps on an NFL defense in Los Angeles. Not bad for one household in Akron.

Peterson told Channel3000 in May how much Wisconsin meant to him. He said: “I came in at 17, a boy, and definitely left a man.” And he added: “I carry that Badger — that W — with a lot of pride. I never forget it.”

Now, what does this promotion actually mean? Let’s be careful here. A promotion to the active roster doesn’t make Peterson a starter. It doesn’t guarantee him a certain number of snaps. What it does is make him available every week without the Rams having to use a practice squad elevation. Signing him to the 53-man roster is a sign the Rams want him available for the long haul. Under NFL rules, a practice squad player can only be elevated for a limited number of games each season before the team has to sign him to the active roster. Peterson was already elevated once, for the Philadelphia game. By signing him now, the Rams keep him available every week without burning more of those elevations — and they protect him from being signed away by another team’s active roster.

Why now? Because the Rams’ pass rush has been thin. Myles Garrett hasn’t played since Week 1 after his knee procedure. Defensive coordinator Chris Shula admitted on Friday that the defense hasn’t created as many sacks and turnovers as last year. He said: “You want to create pressure on the offense, you want to create turnovers, you want to get a lot of sacks.” Peterson was a productive pass rusher in college, with 14 career sacks. He gives the Rams another body on the edge while they wait for Garrett.

And speaking of Garrett: McVay said Friday that Garrett returning to practice next week is “a possibility,” and NFL Network’s Ian Rapoport reported that Garrett is “likely to return to practice next week,” according to Pro Football Talk. So Peterson’s role may grow for a couple of weeks, and then shrink once Garrett is back. That’s fine. In the NFL, these are the weeks when young players earn trust.

And the Rams have a history of late-round and overlooked players becoming stars. Puka Nacua was a fifth-round pick in 2023. Kyren Williams was a fifth-round pick in 2022. Nobody expected them to become the players they are. Peterson is an undrafted rookie, so expectations should be realistic. But McVay’s Rams have proven that where you were drafted doesn’t decide where you end up. Byron Young, who had a non-injury rest day on Friday, is still the veteran leader of that edge group. Peterson’s job is simply to give the Rams quality snaps when he’s out there.

If Peterson gets snaps today, here’s what I’ll be watching. Does he set the edge against the run? Does he stay disciplined in his assignment? And does he get even one pressure on Kirk Cousins? For an undrafted rookie, those small things are how you earn a bigger role.

Move number three: Jalen Hunt to the practice squad.

Again, let’s be precise. Hunt is on the practice squad, not the active roster. That means he’s not playing today unless the Rams elevate him, and nothing has been reported about that. This move is about depth and the future, not about today’s game.

But here’s why it matters. According to the New England Patriots’ official website, Hunt is a 25-year-old defensive tackle who’s 6-foot-2 and 310 pounds. He played three seasons at Michigan State, from 2021 to 2023, and his final two at Cincinnati, in 2024 and 2025. In 48 college games, he had 47 tackles and three and a half sacks. He went undrafted, signed with Jacksonville as a rookie free agent in April, was released in late August, spent a few days on New England’s practice squad, and now he’s landed with the Rams.

That’s a journey. And it tells you the Rams were looking for one specific thing: size in the middle. With Poona Ford out for the season, the Rams lost their nose tackle — the big body in the middle who takes on double teams. Hunt, at 310 pounds, gives the Rams a developmental player at that spot. He won’t replace Ford. But if more injuries hit the defensive line, he could be elevated quickly. Right now, the Rams’ interior includes Aaron Donald, Tyler Davis and Braden Fiske, who McVay mentioned as one of his interior starters back in July. Hunt is the insurance policy behind them. And on a defensive line that’s already lost two key players in one game, insurance matters.

And let’s be honest about the bigger picture. Turner is a defensive end. Hunt is a nose tackle. Peterson is an outside linebacker. None of these players is a one-for-one replacement for anyone. What really matters is how the coaching staff spreads the work around. As Shula said on Friday: “That’s life in the NFL really every single week. It’s pretty rare that you go a week without something happening where somebody’s going to be up, somebody’s going to be down. Obviously, this one’s a little more extreme than others.”

Now, let’s talk about the move behind these moves: Kobie Turner going to injured reserve.

According to Yardbarker, Turner’s groin injury will cost him at least four games. He was a third-round pick in 2023, and this season he has 22 tackles and two pass deflections in six games. He’s in the final year of his rookie contract.

And Turner means more to this team than the stats. On Friday, the Rams’ defensive staff said: “He’s a great communicator. We put a lot on those guys up front, and he’s obviously the lead dog in it.” They also called him “one of the hardest playing guys I’ve ever been around.” Losing a player like that hurts in the meeting room as much as on the field. And the timing is tough for Turner personally, too. According to Yardbarker, he was the 89th overall pick in 2023, and he’s in the final year of a four-year rookie contract, set to become an unrestricted free agent in 2027. This was a big season for him. Missing at least four games in a contract year is hard, and the Rams will have a big decision to make about his future after the season.

So who steps up? According to the defensive staff, Tyler Davis is next. They said: “He’s one of the kind of like quiet leaders on that D-line. He’s a great communicator just like Kobie was.” And they said he’s “definitely ready for it.” Add Aaron Donald, who had a non-injury rest day on Friday and has no injury designation for today, and the Rams still have real talent in the middle. Just not as much depth as they’d like.

And then there’s the fourth move — the one that isn’t in the transcript, but might matter most on defense today: Eric Kendricks.

With Nate Landman out with a concussion, McVay said Friday that Kendricks will be promoted from the practice squad. According to Heavy, Kendricks was a second-round pick by Minnesota in 2015, and he has 1,180 career tackles, 21.5 sacks, 11 interceptions and eight forced fumbles. He was also a first-team All-Pro in 2019.

McVay was full of praise for him. He said: “If you didn’t know anything, you’d say this guy’s played a lot of football. He’s got a veteran presence. Real good concept understanding, can play a variety of schemes.” And he added: “Coaching against him for all those years, there was a lot of respect. Then you get around him and the consistency of how he shows up, and we’re lucky to have him.”

According to the AP, Landman had 51 tackles and two tackles for loss through six games, and he’s the defense’s main communicator. Kendricks has to step into that role today. And with Raiders tight end Brock Bowers expected to play — he has no injury designation, according to the Rams’ official website — Kendricks will be tested right away. According to Heavy, the Rams can elevate Kendricks two more times this season before they’d have to sign him to the 53-man roster. So if Landman’s recovery takes longer than expected, the Rams might soon make Kendricks a permanent part of the roster too — which would be another roster move to watch next week.

So what do all these moves tell us about the Rams?

Here’s my read. These aren’t the moves of a team that’s giving up. If the Rams thought this season was over, they wouldn’t be signing a veteran kicker with a Super Bowl ring or promoting a former All-Pro linebacker. They’d be playing young players and saving their resources. Instead, they’re filling every hole they can with the best available options, because they still think they can turn this around.

But these also aren’t the moves of a team that’s panicking. There was no trade, no big contract and no draft picks sent away. According to Pro Football Rumors, the Rams have just under 990,000 dollars in cap space, and they’ve already traded their 2027 first-round pick and other picks in the Myles Garrett and Trent McDuffie deals. So the front office is being careful. They’re plugging holes without giving up the future.

And the next two weeks will shape what comes next. The Rams play the Chargers at home next Sunday, then Washington on November 8th. The deadline is two days later, on November 10th, and then the Rams have their Week 11 bye. If Garrett returns to practice next week, the Rams’ defense could get a huge boost without any trade at all. If the Rams win today and next week, they’ll be 4-4 and right back in the race — and a small move for a receiver might make sense. If they lose, the front office might decide to hold on to what’s left of its future picks. Either way, these roster moves are about one thing: giving the Rams the best chance to win today.

And remember, ESPN’s Dan Graziano reported this week that the Rams “will at least explore the possibility of trading for a wide receiver.” McVay said on Wednesday that adding a receiver from outside the building is “not something that we’ve talked about right now.” The trade deadline is November 10th. If a real trade happens, you’ll hear it here — with a real source.

Now let’s get to today’s game.

According to the Rams’ official website, kickoff is at 1:25 p.m. Pacific at Allegiant Stadium in Las Vegas, on FOX. The Rams are 2-4, and the Raiders are 3-3.

Here’s the final injury picture. Puka Nacua is questionable with a calf injury. He didn’t practice on Friday, and McVay said it will be a pregame decision. McVay described it as “a little minor thing in there,” but said Puka “wasn’t able to have some of the power and full explosion.” Trent McDuffie is questionable with a groin injury after missing practice all week. Landman is out. Parkinson and cornerback Josh Wallace are good to go. For the Raiders, tight end Carter Runyon and receiver Dont’e Thornton Jr. are out, and rookie running back Mike Washington Jr. is questionable.

If Puka can’t play, McVay said the Rams will go “by committee with all of your eligibles, tight ends, your backs.” According to the AP, rookie C.J. Daniels could make his NFL debut. Konata Mumpfield, Jordan Whittington and Tutu Atwell would carry the receiver load. According to the AP, Puka has 37 catches for 417 yards and a touchdown in four games this season, so there’s no replacing him with one player. That’s why McVay talked about a committee.

According to Heavy, the Rams are 3.5-point favorites. According to the AP, the Raiders have lost three straight after a 3-0 start, and they’ve scored touchdowns on only 12 of 23 trips inside the red zone. Kirk Cousins has 14 touchdown passes, tied for the league lead. Last week against Buffalo, according to Yahoo Sports, Cousins threw for 313 yards and two touchdowns in a 33-30 loss. And first-year Raiders head coach Klint Kubiak said after that streak: “That’s unacceptable, and that starts with me.” So the Raiders will be desperate, too. This is not a team the Rams can sleepwalk against.

Before my keys, here’s my pregame watch list. First, the inactive list, which comes out about 90 minutes before kickoff — around 11:55 a.m. Pacific. Is Puka active? Is McDuffie active? Second, warmups: watch Puka run and cut, and watch McManus kick from 40 and 50 yards. Third, the first defensive series: see where Kendricks lines up and whether Peterson is part of the rotation. Those little details will tell you a lot about how McVay and Shula plan to attack this game. And keep an eye on the Raiders’ side, too: if Brock Bowers looks limited in warmups after his hamstring and calf issues earlier in the week, the Rams’ defensive plan gets a lot easier.

Now, here are my three keys for today.

Key number one: McManus’ first kick. Whether it’s an extra point or a field goal, the first kick matters. If the operation is clean — Joe Cardona’s snap, Ethan Evans’ hold, McManus’ kick — the whole team can relax and play aggressively.

Key number two: Kendricks and the safeties against Bowers. Bowers is Cousins’ favorite target. With Landman out, Kendricks has to diagnose quickly and help in coverage. Limit Bowers, and you take away the Raiders’ best weapon. And there’s an extra challenge without Landman. On Friday, Shula talked about the “peanut punch” — punching the ball out of a ball carrier’s arms — and said: “Landman is kind of our leader in the clubhouse at doing it.” With Landman out, someone else has to create those turnovers. But Shula was clear about the rule: “You never sacrifice the tackle for the punch.” Wrap up first, then go for the ball.

Key number three: no self-inflicted wounds. McVay said it after the Arizona loss: “We won’t win a game unless we figure out how not to beat ourselves.” No false starts on fourth down, no fumbles in plus territory, and no 15-yard penalties in the final minute.

My prediction stays the same: if Puka plays, Rams 27, Raiders 20. If he doesn’t, Rams 24, Raiders 20. And McManus goes perfect on the day.

So here’s my bottom line.

There was no massive trade. What the Rams made were real, practical moves: a veteran kicker in McManus, an undrafted pass rusher in Peterson, a developmental nose tackle in Hunt, and a former All-Pro linebacker in Kendricks for today’s game. None of them will turn this team into a Super Bowl favorite by themselves. But together, they show a team that’s trying to patch every hole and stay in the fight.

And if you’re asking me which move matters most, my answer is McManus. In a season where the Rams have lost three games by a combined nine points, a kicker who can turn drives into points could be the difference between 3-4 and 2-5. According to NFL Next Gen Stats, the Rams’ playoff chances were down to 43 percent after the Arizona loss. A win today would push that number back up. A loss would make every game after the bye feel like a playoff game.
