First, some perspective — because context matters when you're deciding whether to panic. The Rams have been in this exact position before. Since winning Super Bowl 56, this team's September record is just 7 and 9. Over the last four seasons they're 37 and 31 overall, and that includes the miserable 5-12 season in 2022. Slow starts are, unfortunately, nothing new around here — this franchise has repeatedly started sluggish and then found itself as the calendar turned. But let's be honest about the other side of that coin: it can't keep happening. Not with this roster, not with the investments this front office just made, and not in a conference where every September loss comes back to haunt you in January seeding.

Now — what actually went wrong in week one, and what was salvageable? The offense never found anything close to the rhythm we expected from this group. We broke down McVay's own diagnosis in our last video: no continuity, drives that died without points, an operation that never got rolling. If those problems show up again tonight, starting 0-2 becomes a very real possibility. But there were genuine positives buried in that ugly tape. The offensive line did not allow a single sack against San Francisco — against that front, that's worth something, even if some of the individual pass-blocking grades came in lower than you'd like. Puka Nacua carried his usual massive workload and remains the engine of this passing game. And on defense, Kobie Turner gave you real moments. The pieces are there. The performance wasn't.

Extra recovery, extra installs, extra time for McVay and this staff to sit with the film and fix what broke. And unlike week one, there's no travel at all this time. And now the elephant in the room: life without Myles Garrett. He's on injured reserve, out at least four games, and the pass rush has to come from somewhere. So let's talk about the actual plan — because "somebody step up" is not a plan.

First, the outside option. Could the Rams bring in a veteran edge off the street? A name like Leonard Floyd is actually out there — still unsigned, still productive late into his career, and a guy who knows this building from his Rams years. But here's the cold water: the Rams are sitting on only around three million dollars in cap space. They could restructure and create more room if they wanted to, but if the medical staff genuinely believes Garrett misses only the four-game minimum, burning cap flexibility on a rental probably isn't the move. Reading between the lines, Los Angeles is going to solve this internally. Which puts three names under the spotlight.

Name one: Byron Young. And I'd argue nobody on this roster has more riding on the next month. Young is in a contract year. He's also been managing a knee issue of his own that dates all the way back to OTAs and training camp. Against San Francisco he recorded just two pressures — and with Garrett gone, that number simply has to climb. But flip it around and look at the opportunity: for the next four games, Byron Young gets to audition as the number one edge rusher on a contending roster. Produce now, with all the attention on him, and he makes himself serious money. This stretch is his career, condensed.

Name two — and this is the one I'm genuinely excited about: Josaiah Stewart. We've mentioned him in every video this week, but today let's put real numbers on it, because the numbers are why the coaching staff trusts him. As a rookie last season, playing mostly a rotational role in sub packages, Stewart finished with three sacks and 21 pressures on just 165 pass-rush snaps according to Pro Football Focus. Do the math on that: one pressure every 7.86 pass-rush snaps. That's not "nice for a rookie" efficiency — that's legitimate production per opportunity. And here's the stat that should really get your attention: PFF credited Stewart with a higher win rate than Jared Verse in true pass-rush sets. The guy the Rams traded away. In week one, Stewart played 28 snaps, notched a pressure, absorbed some of Garrett's early-down work, and graded out as one of the better tackling edge defenders in the league for the week. McVay has called him a complete player — someone who can defend the run and rush the passer — and tonight, the rotational label comes off. This is his shot.

Name three is really a pair: Desjuan Johnson, who has quietly developed on the edge for two years and produced when called upon, and Wesley Bailey, the undrafted rookie whose monster preseason earned him a roster spot. Let's stay realistic — nobody, and I mean nobody, replaces Myles Garrett with one body. The honest formula is a committee: Young's speed, Stewart's efficiency, Johnson's snaps, Bailey's upside, and Kobie Turner wrecking things from the inside. Pressure by consensus until the record-breaker returns.

But there's one more name. And tonight, he changes everything.

Aaron Donald is back — and this is why I called him an addition at the top of the video, because technically, that's exactly what this is. Tonight, the Rams add him to the active roster — a new weapon, hours before kickoff, without giving up a pick or a dollar of that thin cap space. Let's walk through how we got here, because the timeline tells you how the Rams handled this perfectly. Donald came out of retirement at the end of the preseason and has spent the weeks since grinding himself back into football shape — practice reps, building up to full 11-on-11 work. He didn't travel to Australia, and at the time, the Rams had the luxury of patience; Donald was on the commissioner's exempt list, which meant he wasn't even occupying a roster spot while he ramped up. Smart, unhurried, zero pressure. Then Garrett's knee changed the math overnight, and suddenly the luxury became a necessity — that was our whole last video. Through it all, McVay stayed consistent: the decision belongs to Donald and his body. Then came the dominoes. Ian Rapoport reported the hope was for Donald to play Monday night. Donald himself said he expects to play. And now McVay has confirmed it: Aaron Donald returns tonight, against the Giants, in prime time.

And if anyone out there thinks "huge weapon" is an exaggeration for a player coming off a two-year retirement, let me remind you who we're talking about. Three-time Defensive Player of the Year. A decade as the most unblockable interior force this league has ever seen. And the author of the single most famous defensive snap in Rams history — the final pressure that sealed Super Bowl 56 in this very building. That résumé doesn't rush the passer by itself, but it tells you what this man's standard is, and everyone who's watched him practice these past weeks says the standard hasn't moved.

Think about what that means schematically. Even at this stage of his career, after two years away, Aaron Donald is the one player an offensive coordinator cannot treat like just another lineman. You have to know where he is on every single snap. You slide protections toward him. You chip, you double, you pray. And every bit of that attention creates exactly what this Garrett-less front desperately needs: one-on-ones for everybody else. Kobie Turner benefits. Byron Young benefits. Josaiah Stewart benefits. The blitz packages get scarier because the interior threat is real again. No, we shouldn't expect vintage 2021 Donald in his first game back after two years — that would be unfair to any human being. But his gravity alone reshapes how the Giants have to call this game. The timing could not be more perfect.
