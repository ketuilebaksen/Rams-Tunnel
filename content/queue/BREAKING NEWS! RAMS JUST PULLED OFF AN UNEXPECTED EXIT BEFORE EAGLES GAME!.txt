And there's a problem nobody wants to say out loud: Garrett's knee has been barking since training camp. He barely strung together consecutive practices all summer. The swelling kept coming back. So the Rams make the call that ninety percent of teams make in that spot: they trust the toughness. Forty defensive snaps against San Francisco. Trent Williams on the other side. The result? Zero tackles. Zero sacks. Zero pressures. A superstar rendered invisible — and worse, a knee pushed past its limit. Within days: arthroscopic surgery. Injured reserve. He played because he's wired to play. And the organization let him — because stopping a warrior from fighting is the hardest decision in sports.

Think about what actually changed between those two decisions. The situation is arguably MORE desperate now — the Rams were 0-0 in Melbourne; they're 1-2 heading into Philadelphia with the season's narrative on the line. Every incentive screams "play him." The player wants to play. The fans want him to play. The game matters enormously. And the Rams... sat him. The only thing that changed between September and October is knowledge — the scar tissue of watching the Garrett gamble detonate. You played a compromised superstar once this season. It cost you forty wasted snaps, a surgery, a month of your defensive centerpiece, and arguably two losses. You do not get to make that mistake twice and call yourself a learning organization. The Donald decision is the receipt that the lesson took.

This is exactly what a two-year layoff plus elite effort looks like: the engine is magnificent, and the chassis needs maintenance windows. And one more layer of context from this very season: remember why the Baltimore Ravens walked away from a completed Maxx Crosby trade in March — a knee concern on a pass rusher. Franchises treat these bodies like nine-figure investments because they are. The Rams spent June learning they'd accepted risk the Ravens wouldn't touch.
