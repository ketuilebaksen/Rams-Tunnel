Part one: how this clip is built

If you’ve watched these breakdowns before, you’ll recognize the setup right away.

The clip opens with a “spoiler warning” and tells you Keenan Allen ends up with the Rams for a sixth-round pick, and that Adam Schefter says “this trade is going to happen.” It says this all came out “live on ESPN First Take” on Wednesday, November 4th.

Then it fills time with stories. There’s a karate belt test: the Rams are a nervous kid, the trade deadline is the wooden board, Schefter is the judge, and Allen is a “white belt.” There’s a story about trying to teach a niece how to whistle on a porch when the news broke. There’s a “clearance sale” bit about the Colts. And there’s a version of Stephen A. Smith “shouting” that Schefter confirmed it.

The stories are there to keep you watching. They make it feel like a real person reacting to real news. But none of them is evidence. Here’s the thing to check. If Schefter had actually said on First Take that a trade is “going to happen,” it would be on his X account within seconds, on ESPN’s front page within minutes, and in every Rams beat writer’s column by the afternoon. It’s in none of those places.

And here’s a detail I want you to notice. A few minutes in, the clip admits it, sort of. It says the status is “expected, not official,” that “nothing has been announced,” and that “nobody has reported that Indianapolis agreed to anything.” Later it says, “Nobody has reported that Indianapolis wants that exact change. Nobody has reported a counter offer.” So the clip’s own narrator tells you there’s no deal, then keeps saying Schefter confirmed it. Those two things can’t both be true.

Part two: what’s real in the clip

As usual, the fake clip is built on a pile of real facts. That’s what makes it convincing. So let’s give credit to the parts that are true.

The Colts are selling. This part is real. The Colts traded Quenton Nelson to Seattle and DeForest Buckner to San Francisco. And on October 19th, they fired general manager Chris Ballard after a 2-4 start and a loss to the Titans, who hadn’t won a game until then. So when the clip says the person who built the roster has left the building, that’s accurate.

Dan Graziano did list Keenan Allen. The clip spells it “Graciano,” but it’s Dan Graziano of ESPN. On November 4th, he and Jeremy Fowler published their Week 9 intel column. In it, Graziano lists Keenan Allen among the Colts players likely to be moved, along with Josh Downs, Grover Stewart, and others. But here’s what that column does not say: it doesn’t name a single team interested in Allen. It doesn’t give a price. And it doesn’t mention the Rams at all. The clip takes a real list and adds “the Rams” and “sixth-round pick” to it, then puts the result in Schefter’s mouth.

Allen is in the final year of his contract. True. He signed a one-year deal with the Colts in August.

The Rams spent their 2027 first and third on Garrett and McDuffie. This is true too, and it’s a good example of how these clips work. The Rams sent Jared Verse, their 2027 first-round pick, a 2028 second, and a 2029 third to Cleveland for Myles Garrett in June. In March, they sent their 2027 third, along with picks in this year’s draft, to Kansas City for Trent McDuffie. So the clip is right that the Rams’ remaining picks are mostly middle and late rounds.

Allen knows Los Angeles. Also true. He spent eleven seasons with the Chargers from 2013 through 2023, went to Chicago in 2024, then came back to the Chargers in 2025. He’s played a lot of home games in SoFi Stadium, the same building the Rams call home.

So the clip has five real facts. Then it adds three fake ones on top: the Schefter quote, the Rams as the destination, and the sixth-round price. Mix five real things with three fake ones and present all eight in the same confident voice, and most people won’t be able to tell which is which.

Part three: what’s wrong in the clip

Now let’s go through what’s false or misleading.

The Schefter quote. There’s no record of Adam Schefter saying anything about Keenan Allen and the Rams on First Take, or anywhere else. The most recent Keenan Allen item I can find from Schefter is about something very different, which I’ll get to in a minute.

The Stephen A. Smith quotes. Same thing. I can’t find any record of Stephen A. shouting that Schefter confirmed an Allen trade. That’s part of the script.

Where does “sixth-round pick” come from? Here’s my guess, and I’m being clear that it’s a guess. On October 22nd, Athlon Sports published a write-up of a Locked On Rams podcast episode. Host Gavin Carlson argued that Allen would be the Rams’ best trade path after the Adams injury, and suggested that a fifth- or sixth-round pick could be enough. That’s an opinion from a podcast host, not reporting. But it’s a real sentence on the internet with “Keenan Allen,” “Rams,” and “sixth round” in it. It’s very possible the clip took that opinion and pinned it on Schefter. Either way, no insider has reported that price.

“Nobody has reported a timeline for Adams.” This one is just wrong, and I want to correct it because it matters for the whole trade conversation. On October 19th, Sean McVay said Davante Adams’ ruptured hamstring would keep him out six to ten weeks. Later, according to Heavy, McVay said Adams’ procedure “went really well” and that he’s trending toward the shorter end of that window. So there is a timeline. It’s public. And it changes how much the Rams should pay for a temporary receiver.

Part four: the detail the clip never mentions

This is the most important part of today’s video, so please stay with me.

Keenan Allen is facing an NFL suspension that hasn’t been served yet.

Here are the facts, as reported. In August, hours after his Colts debut, Allen was arrested in downtown Indianapolis on a charge of operating a vehicle while intoxicated. Police said his blood alcohol level was 0.224, nearly three times the legal limit. On September 20th, Adam Schefter reported that Allen is facing a minimum three-game suspension from the NFL. That minimum comes from the league’s collective bargaining agreement, and it can be longer when the measured level is that high.

But there’s a catch. The NFL usually waits for the legal case to finish before it imposes discipline. Colts head coach Shane Steichen said the team expected Allen not to miss time while the process plays out. Back in September, the next step in the case was an attorney conference on October 26th. I couldn’t find any reporting on what happened at that conference or whether the case has been resolved, so I’m not going to guess.

I want to be fair to Allen. He’s entitled to his legal process, and I’m not here to pile on. Back in September, Chris Ballard said, “good people make mistakes,” and, “give him some grace.” But I’m making a football point, not a personal one.

Think about what this means for any team that trades for him. You get a 34-year-old receiver who could be suspended at least three games at any point once his case is resolved. That might be November. It might be December. It might be the playoffs. If you’re the Rams and you’re trading for a placeholder until Davante Adams gets back, there’s a real chance the suspension lands right when you need him most.

And notice that in nearly ten minutes of karate metaphors, the clip never mentions this. Interesting, right? A real Schefter report would. Schefter is the one who broke the suspension news in the first place.

Allen also missed a game earlier this season with a groin injury. The Colts ruled him out of their London game against Washington. So he’s been banged up, too.

Part five: Keenan Allen, the player

Now, set the suspension aside for a moment. Is Allen still a good football player? Here’s the honest picture.

He’s 34, the oldest active wide receiver in the NFL this season. He signed a one-year deal with Indianapolis on August 17th, worth up to $8.32 million. Only $3.32 million of that is base value, with $2.81 million fully guaranteed. The other roughly $5 million is incentives, plus $30,000 per-game active roster bonuses.

Through seven games this season, he has 36 catches for 325 yards and one touchdown. That’s about 9 yards per catch.

Last year with the Chargers, he led the team with 122 targets and put up close to 800 yards. Pro Football Rumors says 789. Other outlets list 777, which happens to be Ladd McConkey’s total, so one of those numbers may be mixed up. Either way, it was a solid year for a veteran slot receiver. Entering this season, he had 1,055 career receptions, 13th most in NFL history. He’ll be part of the Hall of Fame conversation one day.

The clip calls him a “route technician,” and that part I agree with. Allen has been one of the best route runners of his generation. He knows how to get open at the sticks on third down, and quarterbacks trust him. Even the San Francisco Chronicle’s Eric Branch, pitching Allen to the 49ers this week, described him as “known for his elite route-running ability.”

Here’s my problem with the fit, though. And this is not a report, just my analysis. The Rams’ biggest offensive issue right now isn’t third-and-four. It’s explosive plays. According to Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak, the Rams’ explosive pass rate through eight weeks is 4.1 percent, down from 17.5 percent at this point last year. Since Adams got hurt, they’ve had exactly one completion longer than 20 yards. Stafford’s average depth of target over the last four weeks ranks 25th of 27.

Keenan Allen at 9 yards a catch helps you move the chains. It doesn’t help you hit the big play. He’d add another short, reliable option to an offense that already has plenty of those: Puka Nacua catching screens, three tight ends, and a strong run game. What the Rams are missing is the guy who makes a safety back up.

So in the clip’s karate story, Allen is the “white belt that was never the problem.” Funny enough, I’d flip it. The Rams’ problem isn’t the belt or the technique. It’s that nobody’s breaking the board deep.

Part six: cost, cap, and competition

Let’s run the numbers anyway, because if the Rams wanted Allen, he would be cheap. Remember, this is not a report. I’m walking through a hypothetical so you know what to look for.

Draft picks. The Rams own their 2027 sixth-round pick, along with a second, a fourth, and a fifth. A sixth-rounder for a 34-year-old rental with a pending suspension isn’t a crazy price, as far as I can tell. It’s in the range a podcast host suggested. It’s just not something anyone has reported.

Cap space. The Rams have about $206,000 in cap space, according to Athlon and Over The Cap. Allen’s base value for the full year is $3.32 million. In a midseason trade, the new team takes on the rest of that, plus his per-game bonuses. I couldn’t find an exact remaining figure, but by my own rough math, it’s somewhere around $1.5 to $2 million. That’s my estimate, not a reported number. But either way, it’s more than $206,000. So even this “cheap” trade would need the Rams to create a little room, probably with a restructure. A Kyren Williams restructure has been estimated to free up around $3.5 million, so it’s doable. It’s just not automatic.

Who else is interested? The 49ers’ name has come up. Eric Branch of the San Francisco Chronicle proposed Allen as a trade target for San Francisco this week, and Kyle Shanahan said “receiver is still something we’re looking into.” Again, that’s a proposal, not a report of talks. But if a contender shows up with the same late pick, the Colts might go with whichever team is willing to add something extra.

When could it happen? Jordan Schultz reported this week that the Colts plan to wait until after Week 9 to make more trades, hoping offers go up closer to the deadline. So if Allen moves anywhere, Monday or Tuesday is much more likely than this weekend.

Part seven: Allen vs. Downs vs. Thomas, my comparison

Let’s put the three receiver names that keep coming up next to each other. This is my analysis, not reporting.

Keenan Allen. Thirty-four years old, expiring contract, pending suspension. Cheap in picks, probably a sixth. Great route runner, around 9 yards a catch this year. Helps on third down. Doesn’t fix the explosive-play problem. Biggest risk: the suspension.

Josh Downs. Twenty-five, final year of his rookie deal, requested a trade. ESPN’s Ben Solak proposed a Rams deal of Downs plus a 2027 fifth for a 2028 third. Schefter reported the Colts want a third, though a fourth might get it done. The Steelers and Chiefs are the main suitors, per ESPN. Quick, separates well against man coverage, also returns punts. Biggest concern: blocking. Biggest risk: getting into a bidding war.

Brian Thomas Jr. Young, explosive, the one with real deep speed. He’s struggled badly this season and was benched in Jacksonville. ESPN’s Bill Barnwell called him the “exciting” option for the Rams this week, and Grisak wrote, without naming a source, that the Rams have called about both Downs and Thomas. Biggest concern: cost. He’d probably take the most draft capital of the three.

If you’re asking me which one fixes the actual problem, it’s Thomas, because he’s the one who can stretch the field. If you want the safest bet for the price, it’s Downs. And Allen is the cheapest, but he also comes with the most uncertainty, because of the suspension, his age, and an injury already this season.

Part eight: the real triple dose of good news

Okay. The title promised a triple dose of great news. The clip didn’t deliver it, so I will. Here are three real pieces of good news for the Rams this week, all confirmed.

Good news number one: Matthew Stafford’s elbow looks fine.

On Sunday, he left the locker room with ice on his right arm, and on Monday McVay said only that Stafford had “had some soreness there.” People worried. But Stafford practiced fully on Wednesday and Thursday, and here’s what he said:

“It feels good.”

“Full practice, as I have been all year, and hope to continue to be that way.”

“I haven’t missed a rep at practice. I haven’t missed a rep in a game, and I feel good.”

And: “Listen, I’m 38 years old, trying to keep up with these young bucks.”

There’s an unconfirmed radio report, from Doug McCain of 97.1 The Fan, passed along by Grisak, that the elbow was originally hurt trying to tackle Talanoa Hufanga on a pick-six against Denver, and that surgery was considered. I want to be clear: the team hasn’t confirmed that. What’s confirmed is two full practices and a quarterback saying he feels good.

Good news number two: Myles Garrett is back on the practice field.

Garrett returned to practice on Wednesday for the first time since Week 1, and he was limited again on Thursday. He had knee arthroscopy in mid-September after playing 40 snaps in the opener and has missed seven games. Friday’s practice decides his status for Sunday. One report says that if he plays, he could be on a pitch count, which makes sense.

And if he does go, Pro Football Talk noted that Sunday could be the first time Garrett and Aaron Donald play together. That’s the real “massive addition” this month, and it didn’t cost a pick.

Good news number three: Davante Adams is ahead of schedule, and Washington is short-handed.

I’ll count this as one dose with two parts. First, per Heavy, McVay said Adams’ surgery “went really well” and that his recovery is trending toward the shorter end of six to ten weeks. Six weeks from October 19th lands right around the end of November, which would be right before the late-season stretch.

Second, the Rams’ opponent this week is banged up. Washington put defensive tackles Daron Payne and Javon Kinlaw on injured reserve, along with running back Jeremy McNichols. Each will miss at least four games. Losing two interior defensive linemen in the same week is a big deal against a Rams offense that ran for 129 yards in the second half against the Chargers.

So there it is. A real triple dose: a healthy-sounding quarterback, a superstar pass rusher back on the field, and a top receiver ahead of schedule, all heading into a game against a weakened opponent.

Part nine: the bad news we can’t skip

I’d be lying if I said this week was all good. Here’s the other side.

Jaylen Watson is out for the season. The starting cornerback re-aggravated the shoulder he dislocated in Week 3, and he needs surgery. McVay said, “The likelihood of it coming out and doing further damage to it is a very high probability,” and called it “a big blow,” adding, “He’s been such a stud for us.”

Josh Wallace will take Watson’s spot in the starting lineup, and Emmanuel Forbes moves into the number three cornerback role. That’s a fun storyline. Forbes was Washington’s first-round pick in 2023, the Commanders moved on from him in 2024, and the Rams claimed him off waivers. On Sunday, he gets to play against his old team.

On top of that, Trent McDuffie missed practice Wednesday and Thursday with a back injury, and safety Kam Curl missed both days with a foot injury. Tight end Colby Parkinson also missed with a rib injury. Long snapper Joe Cardona is still out with an ankle injury, and it’s unclear whether he plays Sunday.

That’s a lot of secondary depth missing heading into a game against Jayden Daniels. If you’re going to argue the Rams need something at the deadline, my opinion is that cornerback has moved way up the list this week, maybe even ahead of a veteran slot receiver.

Part ten: Washington preview

Rams at Commanders, Sunday at 1 p.m. Eastern on FOX. The Rams are 4-4, tied with Seattle for second in the NFC West behind San Francisco at 6-1. Washington is 2-4-1, coming off a 27-10 win over the Eagles. The Rams are about 5.5-point favorites, per DraftKings.

Here’s what I’m watching.

The run game against a thin interior. With Payne and Kinlaw on IR, Washington’s middle is vulnerable. Kyren Williams and Blake Corum should get plenty of work. Corum had 73 yards and a 23-yard touchdown against the Chargers. If the Rams lean on the run early, they can avoid another slow start like last week, when they went five straight drives without a first down to open the game.

One deep shot. Washington needs help at cornerback, according to CBS. If Stafford is healthy, this is the week to take a couple of shots downfield. If the average depth of target is still around five yards against this secondary, the receiver trade talk is going to get much louder.

The Rams’ secondary against Daniels. No Watson. McDuffie and Curl uncertain. Josh Wallace starting and Forbes against his old team. Daniels can extend plays, so the pass rush has to finish. If Garrett is active, even on a limited snap count, he changes how Washington has to protect.

Prediction. I’ll stay where I was: Rams 24, Commanders 19. The run game controls the second half, Puka has his usual 9 or 10 catches, and the defense forces one key turnover. If McDuffie and Curl are both out, I’ll trim that margin.

Part eleven: how to beat the next fake clip

With four days to the deadline, we’re going to see a lot more of these. So here’s the checklist one more time.

One: does the clip contradict itself? Today’s clip said Schefter confirmed it, then said nothing’s been announced and nobody’s reported that the Colts agreed to anything. If the narrator is hedging, the “report” isn’t real.

Two: check the small facts. Today’s clip said there’s no timeline for Davante Adams. There is: six to ten weeks, trending toward the short end. When a clip gets something that public wrong, don’t trust the rest.

Three: does the real reporting mention the team? ESPN’s actual column listing Allen named no suitors and didn’t mention the Rams. If the real list doesn’t include your team, the fake clip added it.

Four: what’s missing? A real report about Keenan Allen would mention the pending suspension, because that’s the first question every GM would ask. The clip never mentioned it. When a “report” leaves out the most important fact, it isn’t a report.
