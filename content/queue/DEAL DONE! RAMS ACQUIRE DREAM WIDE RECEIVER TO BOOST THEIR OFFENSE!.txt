Part two: who is Brennan Presley?

So why do the Rams keep bringing Presley back? Let’s look at who he is.

He’s 24 years old and in his second NFL season. He went undrafted in 2025. He’s small for a receiver, listed at 5-foot-8 and about 174 pounds.

But his college career was impressive. At Oklahoma State, he finished as the school’s all-time leader in receptions. And he was a two-time All-Big 12 honoree. That’s a lot of production at a big program.

Here’s the NFL side, according to Heavy. Over two seasons, Presley has played just nine NFL snaps. Seven of those were on special teams. In the Arizona game, he returned one kickoff for 20 yards.

So right now, his value to the Rams is mostly on special teams. He’s not a regular part of the offense. At least not yet.

McVay likes him, though. Before the Arizona game, he said Presley is “just one of those guys that makes the plays when his number’s called.” He called him “a great teammate, really smart,” and said he “can contribute on special teams, and he’s got some return ability.”

Part three: the elevation rule

Here’s an interesting detail from Heavy’s report.

Presley has already been elevated from the practice squad once this season. And he has two standard elevations remaining.

What does that mean? In the NFL, a team can bring a practice squad player up to the active roster for a game day without signing him to the 53-man roster. That’s called an elevation. After the game, he goes back to the practice squad automatically. No waivers needed.

But each player can only be elevated a limited number of times. Presley has two left.

So that gives the Rams flexibility. If they need an extra returner or receiver for a game, they can elevate Presley without using a permanent roster spot. And with the bye week, there’s no game-day decision to make right now.

That’s why this move makes sense. The Rams keep Presley in the building, they keep his elevations available, and they keep their 53-man roster spots open for other needs.

According to Heavy, six different Rams players have returned at least one kickoff this season:

Alex Bachmann: 13 kickoff returns.

So who’s left? Smith and Rivers are on the 53-man roster. Bachmann and Presley are on the practice squad.

And Rivers has been the main kickoff returner in recent weeks. He had a 34-yard return in Arizona.

There’s one more important detail. Kickoff returns and punt returns are different jobs. Punt returns take different timing, decision-making, and ball security. And according to Heavy, only Bachmann and Smith have returned punts for the Rams this season.

So even if Presley is good on kickoffs, that doesn’t automatically make him the punt returner.

Part five: the bigger story, only four IR designations left

Now let’s get to the story that actually matters.

According to SB Nation’s Turf Show Times, the Rams have only four injured reserve return designations remaining this regular season.

Here’s how it works. Every NFL team gets eight return designations per season. Each one lets a team bring a player back from injured reserve. Once you use all eight, any player still on IR can’t come back during the regular season, even if he’s healthy.

The Rams have already used four. Here’s who got them:

An offensive tackle, designated on October 15 and activated two days later, to help with offensive line depth.

Terrance Ferguson, the young tight end, designated on November 4 after an ankle injury. But then a hamstring problem slowed his recovery. As of the November 18 report, he hadn’t been activated yet. The Rams still have to make a decision before his 21-day window runs out.

Myles Garrett, activated on November 7. Garrett has played in three games through Week 10: San Francisco, Washington, and Arizona.

Ronnie Rivers, also activated on November 7. He’s given the Rams depth behind Kyren Williams and Blake Corum, and he’s been a key part of special teams.

So that’s four used. Four left.

The Garrett and Rivers designations paid off

Before we go further, let’s look at two of the designations the Rams already used, because they’ve worked out great.

Myles Garrett. I think a lot of fans forgot that Garrett spent time on injured reserve earlier this season. The Rams used a designation to bring him back, and he was activated on November 7. Since then, he’s played in three games: San Francisco, Washington, and Arizona.

And look what he did against Washington: three sacks, three tackles for loss, and a strip-sack before halftime. That’s what you spend a designation on. He’s still building back up from his knee issue, and he said himself he felt “a little bit slow” in that game. But even a slow Myles Garrett changes everything for a defense.

Ronnie Rivers. He was activated the same day, November 7. And in the Arizona game, he had 15 carries for 81 yards and closed out the game. PFF gave him an 84.7 grade, the highest of any Rams running back that day. He also had a 34-yard kickoff return. McVay said after the game: “Thought Ronnie ran really well at the end there.”

So two of the four designations the Rams used have already paid off in a big way. That’s good roster management.

Part six: why “designated” doesn’t mean “back”

Here’s something a lot of fans get confused about.

When a player gets a return designation, it doesn’t mean he’s back on the active roster. It means his practice window opens. He can start practicing with the team. Then the team has 21 days to decide whether to activate him.

That’s where Ferguson is right now. He’s been designated, so he used up one of the eight. But he hasn’t been activated. And if the Rams don’t activate him within his window, he stays on IR for the rest of the season.

So the Rams are in a tough spot with Ferguson. They already spent the designation. Now they need him healthy enough to actually come back.

The Ferguson deadline is right around the corner

Let’s spend another minute on Ferguson, because his decision is coming fast.

According to the report, Ferguson was designated to return on November 4. When a player is designated, the team has 21 days to activate him. Do the math, and that window closes right around November 25. That’s the day of the Packers game.

So the Rams have to decide very soon. Either Ferguson is healthy enough to be activated, or he stays on IR for the rest of the season.

McVay said earlier this month that Ferguson’s hamstring would take “probably a couple weeks at least.” The bye week gave him extra time to heal. But a hamstring can be tricky.

Here’s why it matters so much. The designation is already spent. If Ferguson can’t be activated, the Rams used one of their eight on a player who never came back. That would make the remaining four even more precious.

And on the field, the tight ends are a huge part of this offense right now. McVay said they’re “still so banged up.” Getting Ferguson back would give Stafford another weapon and let Higbee, Parkinson, and Davis Allen get some rest.

Part seven: the big three still on IR

Now, here’s the problem. The Rams have several important players still on injured reserve who might need one of those four remaining designations.

Davante Adams. According to the report, Adams suffered a ruptured hamstring in Week 6 against Arizona and had surgery to clean out the tendon area. His reported recovery timeline was six to 10 weeks.

A possible return against the Packers in Week 12 has been talked about. But that would only be about five and a half weeks into his recovery, at the very early end of that timeline. And McVay’s own words were that “after Green Bay is a realistic timeline.” So I’d expect Adams after the Packers game. There’s no confirmed medical clearance or activation yet.

And here’s the risk. Rushing a hamstring back can lead to a bigger problem. With so much football left, the Rams can’t afford to lose Adams twice.

Puka Nacua. Puka injured both his MCL and PCL near the end of the Washington game in Week 9, and he’s also dealing with turf toe. According to the report, Week 15 against Dallas is the earliest he can return. That’s eligibility, not a guarantee. His recovery will decide when he’s actually ready.

Kobie Turner. Turner suffered a grade 2 groin strain in Week 6, with an estimated recovery of six to eight weeks. He’s already missed the required four games. The writer at Turf Show Times expects Turner to miss the Packers game, but thinks the Chiefs game in Week 13 could be realistic. That’s a prediction, not an announcement from the Rams.

And Turner matters a lot right now, because Poona Ford tore his triceps in Week 6. Ford’s recovery is estimated at five to six months, which effectively ends his season. So getting Turner back would really help the middle of the defensive line.

Part eight: the math problem

Here’s where it gets tight.

The Turf Show Times writer predicts that Adams, Puka, and Turner will get three of the four remaining designations.

If that happens, the Rams will have just one regular-season designation left.

And there are more players who could want it.

Justin Dedich, an offensive lineman. He’s been on IR since late August with a hand injury he suffered in July. With all the problems on the offensive line, a healthy lineman could be really valuable.

Dean Connors, a running back. He broke his hand in Week 8 against the Chargers.

Jordan Whittington, a receiver and returner. He was placed on IR on November 10 with a lingering back issue.

Dylan McMahon, the center. He has a partial patellar tear from the Arizona game.

So that’s four more players competing for one spot. Some of them won’t get to come back this season.

The writer sees Dedich as a possible choice for that last designation. But he also suggests the Rams could save it until their needs become clearer. And I think that makes a lot of sense. If you use your last designation too early, and then a star gets hurt in December, you’d have no way to bring him back.

Part nine: the players who don’t need a designation

Now here’s the good news. Some injured players don’t need a designation at all.

Coleman Shelton is expected to miss four to six weeks with a hamstring injury. But he wasn’t on injured reserve at the time of the report.

Steve Avila hurt his hamstring in Week 9 and was expected to miss multiple games. He also wasn’t on IR.

Joe Cardona, the long snapper, is also injured. But the report suggested he’s unlikely to go on IR.

Here’s why that matters. If a player is hurt but stays on the active roster, he doesn’t need a return designation when he’s healthy. He just starts playing again.

So by keeping Shelton and Avila off IR, the Rams are saving their designations for players who really need them. The downside is that those players take up active roster spots while they heal. But with only four designations left, that’s a smart trade-off.

Part ten: the players who won’t be back

And then there are the players whose seasons are over.

Jaylen Watson, the cornerback, had shoulder surgery. According to the report, he’s not expected to return even if the Rams make the playoffs.

Blake Grupe, the kicker, tore his ACL in Week 6 and is out for the season. That’s why Brandon McManus has been kicking.

Poona Ford, the defensive tackle, tore his triceps and is expected to miss the rest of the year.

So those three aren’t part of the designation math. They’re done for 2026.

The injury calendar since Week 6

Let me put all of this on a timeline, because when you see it all together, you realize how much this team has been through.

Week 6 against Arizona: Davante Adams ruptured his hamstring. Kobie Turner suffered a grade 2 groin strain. Poona Ford tore his triceps. And kicker Blake Grupe tore his ACL. Four major injuries in one game. And the Rams lost that game 18-16.

Week 8 against the Chargers: Running back Dean Connors broke his hand. And Trent McDuffie hurt his back, which kept him out of the Washington game.

Week 9 against Washington: Puka Nacua suffered his MCL and PCL sprains plus turf toe. Steve Avila hurt his hamstring.

Week 10 against Arizona: Coleman Shelton hurt his hamstring. Dylan McMahon came in and suffered a partial patellar tear a few plays later.

Along the way: Jordan Whittington went on IR with a back issue. Jaylen Watson needed shoulder surgery. And long snapper Joe Cardona got hurt too.

Now here’s the part that blows my mind. Since that brutal Week 6 game, the Rams have won four straight. Raiders. Chargers 24-9. Washington 20-13. Arizona 34-6.

McVay said after the last game: “We’re never victims. We’re not going to go that route.” When you look at this calendar, you understand how much he means it.

Part eleven: the playoff bonus

Here’s one more detail that matters.

According to the report, if the Rams make the playoffs, they get two additional IR return designations for the postseason.

That’s a big deal. It means a player who’s still on IR at the end of the regular season could come back for the playoffs.

But the Rams haven’t earned those yet. So right now, they have to make careful choices with the four they have.

And this is another reason the Packers game matters so much. ESPN’s Football Power Index gives the Rams a 47.1% chance to make the playoffs. Win on Wednesday, and that number goes up. And so does the chance of getting those two extra designations.

Part twelve: the real dilemma

So here’s the dilemma McVay and Les Snead face.

Option one: prioritize the receivers. Use designations on Adams and Puka. They’re the two best weapons in the passing game. The offense has been winning without them, but it’ll be much more dangerous with them.

Option two: reinforce the defensive line. Use one on Kobie Turner. With Poona Ford out for the season, Turner is huge for the interior defense.

Option three: protect the offensive line. Save one for Dedich or McMahon. The line just lost two centers in one game.

Option four: save one for an emergency. Keep the last designation in your pocket until December, in case a star gets hurt.

This is not a report, it’s my take: I’d go with Adams, Turner, and Puka, in that order, and I’d save the last one as long as possible. Adams and Turner are closest to returning. Puka is the most important long-term. And keeping one in reserve protects you against the unknown.

Part thirteen: why this connects to Presley

So how does the Presley move connect to all of this?

It’s about flexibility. The Rams are trying to manage a lot of injuries while keeping as many options open as possible.

Putting Presley on the practice squad does a few things. It frees up a spot on the 53-man roster for someone else. It keeps Presley in the building. And it keeps his two elevations available for game days.

That’s the same mindset behind the IR decisions. Don’t use resources before you have to. Keep options open. Plan for the injuries you know about, and leave room for the ones you don’t.

McVay said after the Arizona game that the bye is a time to “creatively come up with new ideas based on who’s available and who’s not.” That’s true for the roster too, not just the playbook.

Let’s look ahead. This is not a report. It’s my best guess based on the timelines we have.

So if everything goes well, the Rams could look a lot healthier by the middle of December. And that’s when the playoff race gets real.

But “if everything goes well” is a big if. Injuries rarely follow the schedule.

The kicker situation

One more injury that doesn’t get talked about enough: the kicker.

Blake Grupe tore his ACL in Week 6 and is out for the season. Brandon McManus has been kicking since then.

In Arizona, McManus made field goals from 24 and 43 yards. So the field goals have been fine.

But remember what McVay said about the kickoffs after the game: “We want to be able to keep those kicks where we’re actually keeping them in play. That wasn’t a positive for us today.” Special teams got a B-minus from Blaine Grisak because two kickoffs missed their landing zone or the field of play.

So with the kicker situation, the Rams don’t need an IR designation. Grupe is done for the year. They just need the kickoffs cleaned up. In a close game against the Packers, field position could matter a lot.
