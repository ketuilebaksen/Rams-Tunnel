What those odds actually mean

Let me quickly explain what those numbers mean, because a lot of fans see "plus-1200" and aren't sure.

Plus-500 means that if you bet $100, you'd win $500. Plus-1200 means a $100 bet would win $1,200. The bigger the number, the less likely the oddsmakers think it is.

If you convert those odds into percentages, roughly:

Plus-500 is about a 17% chance.

Plus-700 is about 12.5%.

Plus-1200 is about 8%.

So at the bye, the oddsmakers were giving the Rams roughly an 8% chance to win the Super Bowl. That's still seventh-best in the league. But it's a long way from being the favorite.

To be clear, this is just to explain the numbers, not betting advice. And odds change every week. One big win against the Packers or the Chiefs could move those numbers a lot.

The Rams have done this before

Here's something that makes Stephen A's pick a little less crazy.

The Rams have already won a Super Bowl at SoFi Stadium. Super Bowl 56, in February 2022. They beat the Bengals 23-20, in their own building.

And think about what happened that season. In November 2021, the Rams lost Robert Woods, one of their best receivers, to a torn ACL. A lot of people thought that would hurt the offense too much.

Instead, the Rams adjusted. They added Odell Beckham Jr., Cooper Kupp had one of the best seasons a receiver has ever had, and the defense, led by Aaron Donald and Von Miller, took over in the playoffs. Donald made the game-sealing pressure in the Super Bowl.

So the Rams have been here. They lost a top receiver in November and still won it all at SoFi.

Now, this year is different. There's no trade deadline move coming. The deadline passed on November 10. And Puka is a bigger loss than Woods was. But the blueprint is similar: lean on the quarterback, find a way on offense, and let the pass rush win in January.

Stafford is the key on offense

The defense might be the reason Stephen A believes. But if the Rams are going to make a run, Matthew Stafford has to carry the offense.

And he's done it before. In that 2021 season, he threw for over 4,800 yards and led the Rams to the Super Bowl after Robert Woods went down. In the playoffs, he made big throws in big moments, including the final drive to beat the Bengals.

This year, he's 38 and in his 18th NFL season. But he's still playing at a high level. In Arizona, his expected points added per pass ranked second in the league that week, behind only Brock Purdy, according to Blaine Grisak.

McVay said before that game that Stafford's "steadiness is the key thing." He said Stafford is "truly an extension of the coaching staff" and that "there's just a confidence that he breathes" that rubs off on the whole huddle.

That's what the Rams need now. Without Puka, Stafford has to make young receivers better every single week. If he can do that, the Super Bowl talk stays alive.

The road ahead, step by step

Let's map out what the Rams need to do. This is not a report. It's just how I see it.

Step one: beat the Packers. It's a home game, a head-to-head tiebreaker, and the first game without Puka for good. A win puts the Rams at 7-4.

Step two: survive the Chiefs and 49ers. If Adams comes back for the Chiefs, that helps a lot. Going 1-1 in those two would be okay. Going 2-0 would be huge.

Step three: take care of Dallas. That was the game Puka was supposed to return for. Now it's just another game the Rams need to win.

Step four: split with Seattle, at least. Stephen A said one win against the Seahawks might be realistic. If the Rams get one, they're probably in good shape. If they get two, they could win the division.

Step five: get healthy for January. Shelton back, Ferguson back if possible, and a defense at full strength.

If the Rams can do all that, they'll be in the playoffs. And once you're in, anything can happen. Especially with a pass rush like this one.
