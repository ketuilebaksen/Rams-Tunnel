Part one: McVay’s opening statement

McVay started by giving credit to the defense.

“Really well done by our group today,” he said. “I thought our defense played really well.”

He named the two players who intercepted Carson Beck: Kamren Kinchens and Wesley Bailey. And he said “a lot of guys got action in terms of getting in the backfield, creating negatives.” That means sacks and tackles for loss. The Rams had five sacks in this game.

Then he moved to the offense, and he said something that matters: “Once we kind of settled in offensively.” That’s an honest admission. The Rams offense didn’t start well. They were down 6-3 for a long time. But once they settled in, they scored four touchdowns in about ten minutes of game time.

And then McVay explained what happened on the offensive line, which was chaos.

Part two: the offensive line chaos

This is the part of the press conference I want every Rams fan to hear.

McVay said: “Really credit to our offensive line. The depth was really challenged today.”

Here’s what happened. Steve Avila was already out with a hamstring injury. Then, during the game, Coleman Shelton got hurt. Dylan McMahon came in for him, and McVay said he “did a nice job.” Then McMahon got hurt too.

So the Rams were down to their third center. And McVay gave a huge shout-out to Beaux Limmer: “Can’t say enough about Beaux Limmer. He starts the game at guard, slides into center.” Then Bill Murray came in at guard.

And here’s the line that tells you how thin they were: “We needed all eight of our offensive linemen.”

All eight. Every lineman who was active played. McVay also mentioned that David Quessenberry came in and played some snaps as an extra tight end.

Stafford said the same thing in his press conference: “Obviously down a couple guys at the center spot. We’re down to the third center. Guards are moving and shaking. It was a lot of moving parts there for a couple drives, and we kind of got settled in there and went.”

And then Stafford gave them huge credit: “Really proud of those guys and just their composure, their effort, and their ability to not get a lot of reps in practice and go in there and execute in the game. That’s awesome. It’s great work by those guys.”

Think about that. Bill Murray and the backup linemen don’t get many practice reps with the starters. And in the middle of a divisional road game, they had to come in and protect Stafford. And the Rams still scored two touchdowns after those injuries and closed the game out with the run game.

That’s not luck. That’s preparation.

Part three: the two-back look, and why McVay used it

A reporter asked about the new personnel grouping with two running backs on the field.

McVay explained it simply: “It was really just a way to be able to get two of our best players on the field at the same time, with Blake and Kyren.”

In football terms, that’s called “20 personnel.” Two running backs, zero tight ends. And McVay said it also helped get “those different receivers involved.”

But here’s the important part. He said the tight ends “are still so banged up.” Even though they all played, and even though “13 was going to be a big part of the game plan,” the Rams wanted to “supplement some of their snaps.” 13 personnel means one running back and three tight ends.

So the two-back look was partly about protecting the tight ends. Higbee, Parkinson, Davis Allen, and Max Klare all played, but they’re not 100%. Using two running backs gave them a break.

And there was one more reason: “Take advantage of something different that we hadn’t put on tape yet.”

That’s classic McVay. He’s always looking for something the other team hasn’t seen. If Arizona’s defensive coaches watched every Rams game this season, they’d never have seen Kyren and Corum on the field together like this. So they had nothing to prepare with.

McVay also gave credit to Arizona’s defensive coordinator, Nick Rallis: “I got a lot of respect for Nick Rallis and the problems that they present and the different things they do defensively.”

Stafford agreed. He said McVay did an “unbelievable job of figuring out how to attack this defense, which is really multiple and difficult to kind of solve.” And he said players were “doing things that maybe they don’t have a ton of reps at or really accustomed to in the two-back stuff.”

So the Rams didn’t just win. They won with something brand new.

Part four: the Xavier Smith screen was not McVay’s call

Now the moment that I’d actually call surprising.

A reporter asked McVay how the coaching staff comes together creatively. And McVay gave the credit away.

“I got to give a tip of the cap to Nate Scheelhaase on the screen that we called Xavier Smith,” McVay said. “That was his play call suggestion.”

Nate Scheelhaase is one of the Rams’ offensive coaches. And he suggested the play that broke the game open. A quick screen to Xavier Smith, right before halftime, when most teams would have settled for a field goal.

McVay said: “Really cool for X-Man. Did a great job breaking the initial tackle and to be able to punch that in at the end of the half to capitalize.” And he pointed out the Rams also scored “coming out of the half.” That’s the 14-point swing that decided the game.

Then McVay said something about why he loves coaching: “You feel really fortunate to be surrounded by great guys that have an open mind about, hey, let’s figure out how to accentuate all these different guys’ skill sets.”

And this line: “That’s why you get into coaching, is to help people shine, to see them get moments like today.”

Think about that. Xavier Smith was on the 49ers practice squad a couple of weeks ago. Now he has the first touchdown of his career. And McVay, the head coach, is giving the credit to an assistant and talking about how happy he is for the player.

McVay finished: “I think the best is yet to come for them.”

Part five: “I don’t pretend to have all the answers”

Then a reporter asked McVay why he’s so open to ideas from other coaches.

And McVay’s answer was humble. He started with a joke: “I don’t know that everybody would agree with you on that.”

Then he got serious. He said he’s been “really fortunate” over the last ten years to have “a lot of great coaches that can come through here, a lot of great players.”

And then: “I think you just stay constantly curious. There’s always things to learn. There’s always times to grow.”

Remember, McVay said on Friday that CJ Daniels is “very curious,” and that’s what he wants from young players. Now he’s saying the same thing about himself. Stay curious.

And then this: “Don’t pretend to have all the answers, but I’ve been surrounded by a really great group of people.” And: “Sometimes I get in my own way.”

That’s a head coach who has won a Super Bowl, saying he sometimes gets in his own way. That kind of honesty is rare. And it’s probably part of why his players and coaches trust him.

Part six: Trent McDuffie is back

Here’s the injury update a lot of you were waiting for.

McVay said: “It was great to get Trent McDuffie back out there today.”

So McDuffie played. After missing the Washington game with his back injury, and being questionable all week, he was on the field in Arizona.

That’s huge. McDuffie is the leader of this secondary. Remember what McVay said on Friday? Some players call McDuffie “the blueprint.” McVay talked about “his competitive stamina” and how he’s always asking, “How can you uplift and elevate?”

With McDuffie back, the Rams secondary held a rookie quarterback to 108 passing yards and two interceptions.

And McVay said the defense is “really starting to hit their stride.” With McDuffie healthy and Garrett getting stronger, that’s a scary thought for the rest of the NFC.

Part seven: Aaron Donald is “one of one”

This was the most emotional part of the press conference.

A reporter brought up Aaron Donald’s sack. The reporter mentioned a number, about 1,050 days, which appears to be how long it had been since Donald’s last sack. And Donald got his first sack of the season in this game, forcing a punt.

McVay’s answer was pure respect.

“I’m never surprised,” he said. “He never ceases to amaze me.”

Then: “I think the term one of one is thrown around way too loosely this day and age, but it’s not with him. He’s one of one.”

He kept going: “He can do things, and he’s done things, and his impact, whether he has to say it or just show it, he’s a total G.”

And then this: “I love everything he’s about, and he is one of one, and he’s had a tremendous impact on this team, and it certainly is not exclusive to just getting a sack today.”

McVay finished with the best line of the whole press conference: “He’s a guy that everybody looks at, and they say, watching the way that he moves, if you just mimic and emulate that, we’ll be in good shape.”

Think about how much that means. A sack is great. But McVay is saying Donald’s real value is how he leads. Young players watch how he works, how he prepares, how he moves. And they copy it.

That’s what a legend does for a locker room.

Part eight: credit for the whole defense

McVay also gave quick shout-outs to other defenders.

He mentioned “having Myles” on the field. Myles Garrett was questionable all week with his knee, and he played.

He mentioned Tyler Davis, who also got his first sack of the season.

And he mentioned Wesley Bailey “making a pick.” That was the first interception of Bailey’s career.

And then McVay spread the credit to all three groups of coaches: “The defensive coaches, the offensive coaches, and our special teams coaches.” He said, “I think we’ve really improved in a lot of areas there,” talking about special teams.

Part nine: the one thing McVay didn’t like

Here’s something that most people will miss.

McVay wasn’t happy with everything. He talked about the kicking game: “We want to be able to keep those kicks where we’re actually keeping them in play. That wasn’t a positive for us today.”

That sounds like a kickoff problem. If a kickoff goes out of bounds, the other team gets the ball at a good spot. And McVay noticed.

It’s a small thing in a 34-6 win. But that’s McVay. Even after a blowout, he’s looking for what needs to get better. Don’t be surprised if that’s something the special teams unit works on during the bye.

Part ten: Blake Corum is catching his stride

A reporter asked about the run game, and specifically about Blake Corum’s breakout.

McVay said: “He’s been awesome.” He talked about “the commitment to it” and said, “There’s an identity” in the run game now.

Then he described what he’s seeing from Corum: “It’s been cool to see Blake really be able to kind of catch his stride. You can really feel the twitch, the explosiveness. He’s seeing things really well, staying grounded in close quarters.”

That’s a great scouting report. “Twitch” and “explosiveness” mean quickness. “Seeing things really well” means vision. And “staying grounded in close quarters” means he keeps his balance when he gets hit near the line.

Corum had 17 carries for 100 yards and a touchdown in this game.

McVay also gave credit to Ronnie Rivers: “Thought Ronnie ran really well at the end there.” Rivers had 15 carries for 81 yards and helped close out the game.

And McVay made sure to include Kyren: “Blake and Kyren have always been outstanding.”

Then he explained why the run game matters so much: “If you are going to try to sustain possession, what is the strength of your unit? And us being able to run it has been one of the things that has allowed us to maintain possession, be able to convert, get into some of the shorter third-down windows.”

In other words, running the ball gives the Rams third-and-short instead of third-and-long. And with Puka and Adams out, that’s the difference between moving the chains and punting.

And one more detail. McVay said closing out the game with the run “was really cool.” Then he added: “Would have liked to have been able to do that in Washington.” That’s an honest admission. Against the Commanders, the Rams won 20-13, but it got close at the end. This time, they finished the job on the ground.

Part eleven: the response since the low point

A reporter asked about the first Arizona game, 28 days earlier, and called it “a low point.” The Rams lost 18-16, and Davante Adams got hurt.

McVay said what impressed him most was “just the response.”

Then he explained: “In those tough moments, it sucks and it doesn’t feel good, but it does force the most growth if you have the right people that can respond and go back to work.”

He said the team didn’t let that loss “do anything other than motivate you.” The mindset was: “All right, this is a growth opportunity. This wasn’t what we want to be about.”

And he said: “It starts with us as coaches.” That’s McVay taking responsibility first.

He also said: “I’ve been really proud of the resilience, the mental toughness that’s been developed over the last month.”

And then: “We’ve leaned into our process. I think we’ve leaned into the core values.”

Part twelve: “We’re never victims”

A reporter said the depth being challenged was “putting it lightly,” and asked about the next-man-up mentality.

McVay said it’s about taking it one day at a time, having “agility,” and having “confidence in other guys to be able to step up.”

He said the last month has “really tested our team,” but it’s also shown “there’s a lot of guys that are capable.” He talked about “confidence earned through evidence,” which I love. You don’t just believe in a backup player because you want to. You believe in him because you’ve seen him do it.

And then the best line: “You could have reasons to make excuses, but we’re never victims. We’re not going to go that route.”

Think about everything this team has dealt with. Adams, Puka, Ferguson, Whittington, Kobie Turner, Poona Ford, Jaylen Watson. Now Avila, Shelton, and McMahon on the offensive line. That’s a lot of reasons to make excuses. And McVay is saying: no excuses. We’re never victims.

He finished: “I’m proud of this group, and I’m really excited to be able to build as we move into the second half of the season.” And: “They deserve a ton of credit. Love this group.”

If “we’re never victims” should be the motto for this team, drop a “never victims” in the comments.

Part thirteen: why McVay won’t say “relief”

A reporter asked if there was a sense of relief after four straight wins, given all the unique circumstances.

McVay pushed back on the word: “I don’t know that I would use the word relief as much as I’m proud of where the group’s at.”

He said it’ll be good to “take a deep breath and take a step back.” He said the bye “always represents an opportunity to get recharged, refreshed,” but also to think about “what are the new iterations that you want to try to be able to build on,” and to “creatively come up with new ideas based on who’s available and who’s not.”

He also called it “an accelerated bye,” because the Rams play the Packers on the Wednesday before Thanksgiving. So the break is shorter than usual.

Then he said something interesting about the last few years: “Each of the last handful of years, really last year was kind of an exception, we’ve been used to dealing with adversity early on in the season, and I think it’s calloused us the right way.”

“Calloused us the right way.” That means the tough starts have made this team tougher.

And finally: “All I’m really worried about is the next game.” Then: “It’ll be good to be able to just take a step back, and then attack it full speed ahead when we get back at it.”

So no relief. Just pride, and then back to work.

Part fourteen: Stafford on spreading the ball

Let’s go to Stafford’s press conference.

A reporter pointed out that nine different players caught a pass, and the Rams needed all eight offensive linemen.

Stafford said: “Just trying to spread the ball around. Obviously wasn’t perfect, but proud of those guys. Each guy kind of got their opportunity and made the most of it, which I was happy for.”

He also praised the tight ends and backs: “I thought our tight ends played fantastic. Both our backs played great. We leaned on them heavily, both in the run and the pass game.”

And then Stafford was honest about the game: “It wasn’t, like I said, perfect, right? Wasn’t 28-nothing before you can look.” He said: “It was kind of battling, and then we finished the half really strong, offense and defense, and then came out in the second half and put another touchdown on the board.”

And this is the key: “We’ve done that at a pretty consistent clip, and it’s really helped us.”

Finishing the first half strong and starting the second half strong. That’s been a pattern during this winning streak. And against Arizona, it turned a 6-3 game into a 24-6 game.

Stafford finished 18 of 26 for 249 yards and three touchdowns, to Higbee, Smith, and Davis Allen.

Reading between the lines

McVay never tells you his plan directly. But this press conference gave us some strong hints. Here’s what I picked up. This is my analysis, not McVay’s words.

The two-back look is here to stay. McVay said it was “something different that we hadn’t put on tape yet.” Now it’s on tape. But it worked so well that I’d expect to see it again, maybe with new wrinkles. Every defense the Rams face now has to prepare for Kyren and Corum on the field together.

The run game is the identity. McVay used the word “identity” himself. That’s not something he’d say about a one-week plan. With Puka out until mid-December, the Rams are going to keep running the ball, and running it a lot.

The assistants have a voice. When McVay credits Nate Scheelhaase for a play call, it tells you the offensive staff is working together on ideas. That’s going to matter during the bye, when McVay said they’ll “creatively come up with new ideas based on who’s available.”

The kicking game will get attention. McVay rarely complains after a 34-6 win. When he does, it means something. Expect the special teams unit to work on kickoffs during the bye.

Donald’s role is bigger than stats. McVay went out of his way to say Donald’s impact “is not exclusive to just getting a sack.” That tells you how much the coaches value him as a leader for the young defenders.

What comes next

The Rams are 6-4 with four straight wins. They get a short bye, then host the Packers on the Wednesday before Thanksgiving.

After that, the Chiefs come to SoFi Stadium, and then the Rams travel to San Francisco on December 13.

The big questions during the bye: How serious are the injuries to Coleman Shelton and Dylan McMahon? When does Steve Avila return? Is Terrance Ferguson ready before his 21-day window runs out? And how much healthier can the tight ends get?

McVay said it himself: “Hopefully, we’ll get a little bit healthier, and we’ll see where we’re at.”

Three things to watch during the bye

Before we wrap up, here are the three things I’ll be watching most closely over the next ten days.

One: offensive line updates. This is number one by far. Avila, Shelton, and McMahon are all hurt. The Rams finished the game with their third center. If McVay gives good news on any of those three, it changes everything for the Packers game.

Two: Terrance Ferguson’s status. His 21-day window is running, and the Rams want him back before it closes. With the tight ends still “so banged up,” as McVay said, getting Ferguson back would give Stafford another weapon.

Three: new ideas. McVay said the bye is a time to think about “new iterations.” After seeing the two-back look against Arizona, I can’t wait to see what he brings out against Green Bay.
