Now — the nightmares. Let’s start with the one that should keep Buffalo’s defensive coordinator up at night.

Nightmare number one: Puka Nacua and Davante Adams against a wounded Buffalo secondary.

Let’s look at the numbers from Sharp Football Analysis’s game worksheet. The Bills’ defense allows 21 targets per game to wide receivers — 28th in the NFL. They allow a touchdown rate to receivers that ranks 27th. Against outside receivers specifically, they allow nine catches per game, 26th in the league, and a touchdown rate that ranks 29th. That’s a defense that bleeds production to receivers — and now they’re facing two of the best in football.

Puka came back from his groin injury in Philadelphia and caught nine of twelve targets for 125 yards while running a route on more than 90 percent of the Rams’ dropbacks. That’s a full workload — no limitations. And remember what Puka did the last time Buffalo came to SoFi, in December of 2024: twelve catches for 162 yards, both season highs at the time. He’s already proven he can torch this defense. Then there’s Davante Adams, who had seven catches for 137 yards in Denver. Here’s an interesting wrinkle from Sharp’s data: Davante’s target rate is about 23.5 percent when Puka is on the field and about 33.5 percent when Puka is off it. So in Philly, with Puka back, Davante’s volume dropped. But that’s exactly what makes this duo a nightmare: you can’t double both of them. If Buffalo rolls coverage to Puka, Davante gets one-on-ones on the outside — against a secondary that’s already been giving up a high touchdown rate to outside receivers. And with nickel Dee Alford questionable, the inside matchups get even tougher for Buffalo.

And the quarterback throwing to them is heating up in one important way. Stafford has thrown for more than 300 yards in three straight games. According to Sharp’s numbers, Buffalo allows the second-most passing fantasy points per game in the league and ranks 29th in the rate of passing touchdowns allowed. In plain English: quarterbacks have been moving the ball and finding the end zone against this defense all season. If Stafford takes care of the football, the yards — and the touchdowns — should be there.

NFL.com’s Dan Parr summed it up in his pick: he expects Stafford to bounce back against a Bills defense that struggles to cover Puka and Davante, and he said “the Rams look like the more well-rounded squad at the moment.”

Nightmare number two: Kyren Williams in space.

The Rams’ official preview made a strong statement this week: Kyren’s last two games — in Denver and in Philadelphia — might be the best back-to-back performances of his career. In Philly, he had 26 touches for 147 yards and two touchdowns, including ten catches and the seven-yard game-winner where he broke three tackles. Over the past two weeks, according to Sharp’s data, Kyren has a 20.2 percent target share — that’s a running back getting targeted like a starting receiver — and he’s played over 76 percent of the snaps. He’s averaging 5.4 yards per carry with a 53.7 percent success rate.

And here’s the matchup that makes Buffalo’s coaches nervous: the Bills allow 13.8 receiving fantasy points per game to running backs — that’s 30th in the league. In other words, Buffalo has had real trouble covering backs out of the backfield. Put that next to Kyren’s ten catches in Philly, and you can see why McVay is going to keep finding ways to get him the ball in space. Screens. Checkdowns. Option routes against linebackers. When a pass rush comes hard — and Buffalo has one of the best pass rushers in the league, which we’ll get to — the quick pass to the running back is the antidote. Kyren is the release valve and the knockout punch at the same time.

And don’t forget Blake Corum. The Rams have split the backfield all season, and it’s paid off — Kyren has been fresh enough in the fourth quarter to break three tackles at the goal line in Philadelphia. Corum gives the Rams a second downhill runner who can wear down a defense that’s already missing Ed Oliver in the middle. If the Rams can run the ball effectively early, it opens up play-action for Stafford and keeps Josh Allen sitting on the sideline. And every minute Allen spends on the bench is a minute he can’t hurt you.

Nightmare number three: Trent McDuffie and Quentin Lake.

This is the one nobody’s talking about nationally, and it might be the most impressive stat of the Rams’ season. According to the Rams’ official preview, McDuffie and Lake lead the entire NFL in pass breakups. Not the Rams. The entire league. And McDuffie’s coverage numbers are absurd: he’s played 62 snaps of man coverage in his first four games — the most by a Rams defensive back since at least 2018 — and he’s allowed just two receptions for 14 yards in those snaps. That’s 0.2 yards per coverage snap, the second-fewest of any defensive back in the league. Across all coverage, PFF has McDuffie allowing just five catches on 18 targets this season, with an 89.1 grade. Lake’s grade is even higher, at 90.0.

Sean McVay said this about McDuffie: “The consistency at which he operates with is inspiring to me.” When a head coach uses the word “inspiring” about a cornerback, that tells you everything. Remember, the Rams gave up a first-round pick and more to get McDuffie from Kansas City in March. Four games in, he’s playing like a player worth every bit of it.

And this is why the Rams can be so aggressive with their blitzes. When your corners can lock up receivers one-on-one, your defensive coordinator can send extra rushers without fear. Lake’s questionable tag matters for that reason — he’s the communicator in the back end. But even if he can’t go, Josh Wallace, who earned the defense’s highest grade in Philadelphia while filling in for Jaylen Watson, can play safety, nickel or corner. And Watson is back tonight.

Nightmare number four: the pass rush — and Aaron Donald’s return.

Since the Week 1 loss in Melbourne, Chris Shula has turned this defense into one of the most aggressive in football. According to Rams On SI’s film review, the Rams blitzed on just 13.9 percent of snaps in Week 1, then jumped to 38.6 percent over the next three games — sixth-highest in the NFL — with man coverage on over 36 percent of snaps, fourth-highest. In Philadelphia, the Eagles went zero-for-twelve on third down for the first time in franchise history.

The stars of that rush? The Rams’ official preview says Kobie Turner is playing at an All-Pro level. Byron Young is coming off a game with five pressures, three quarterback hits, two and a half sacks and a forced fumble — the strip-sack that sealed the win in Philly. And now, Aaron Donald returns to the middle of that front. Here’s the key: Buffalo will be without Ed Oliver, one of its best interior defenders, on the other side. So tonight, one team’s interior defensive line gets stronger while the other team’s gets weaker. That’s a matchup advantage the Rams have to exploit on both sides of the ball.

And there’s a turnover angle here too. According to the Rams’ preview, Buffalo has seven giveaways over its last two games — the most in the league in that span — and Josh Allen is responsible for four of them. After a turnover-free start to the season, the Bills have suddenly gotten sloppy with the football. A defense that disguises pressure as well as the Rams have been doing over the last three weeks, against a quarterback who’s been pressing? That’s a recipe for takeaways.

Remember how Buffalo’s last game ended. The Bills lost at home to the Patriots as six-and-a-half-point favorites, and the play that sealed it was a Josh Allen interception — on a throw his own head coach, Joe Brady, said he trusted him to throw away. That’s a quarterback trying to do too much. And Brady himself is in his first season as a head coach, promoted from offensive coordinator in January. Until last week, everything had gone his way. Tonight is his first real test of adversity: a cross-country trip, a primetime crowd, a banged-up roster, and a coach on the other sideline in Sean McVay who has seen every kind of season imaginable in his ten years running the Rams. First-year head coaches learn the most about themselves in weeks like this.

Nightmare number five: the Rams’ sheer volume.

The Rams run 69 offensive plays per game, third-most in the NFL. They’ve topped 400 total yards in each of their last three games, with 1,668 yards on the season — second-most in the league. The Rams’ official preview says they’re still the seventh-most efficient offense in football by the measures it cites. And on defense, they allow just 4.2 yards per play, second-best in the league, with only five pass plays of 20 yards or more allowed all season — tied for the fewest. Put those together and you get a team that runs a ton of plays on offense, gives up very little on defense, and wears opponents down over four quarters. That’s exactly how the Rams beat Philadelphia: they outgained the Eagles by more than 240 yards, and eventually the Eagles’ defense broke.

Now — I promised you honesty, so let’s flip it around. What are the nightmares Buffalo can create for the Rams? Because there are real ones.

Buffalo’s nightmare number one: Josh Allen and the most explosive offense in the league. The Bills are averaging nearly 32 points per game, first in the NFL. The Rams’ preview calls them the most explosive offense in football, running and passing. And here’s a stat that jumped off the page: running back James Cook leads the NFL with 15 explosive carries — tied for the most through four weeks since at least 2016. That’s a direct challenge to a Rams run defense that has been top ten in the league. The Bills also use multiple tight ends on more than half of their plays, which can make life hard for a defense that wants to blitz from the second level.

Buffalo’s nightmare number two: Greg Rousseau. According to the Rams’ preview, Rousseau leads the entire NFL with six sacks — including three strip-sacks — plus 16 quarterback hits and eight tackles for loss. He’s an early Defensive Player of the Year candidate. And ESPN’s Seth Walder predicted that Bills defensive tackle Deone Walker would sack Stafford tonight, pointing out that Walker leads all defensive tackles with 15 pressures. Even with Ed Oliver out, this pass rush can hurt you — and strip-sacks are exactly the kind of turnover the Rams can’t afford.

Buffalo’s nightmare number three: the Rams’ own mistakes. And this is the scariest one, because it’s the one the Rams control. According to the Rams’ preview, Los Angeles has had multiple giveaways in all four games and hasn’t won the turnover battle yet this season. Stafford has six turnovers in the first month. The Rams still don’t have a non-offensive touchdown. Their offense ranks 29th in average starting field position, and their defense ranks 31st — meaning the defense keeps getting stuck defending short fields because of turnovers and special teams. And the penalties have been historic in the worst way: the Rams have had 21 accepted penalties for 191 yards over their last two games — the most in back-to-back games in Sean McVay’s ten seasons as head coach.

Davante Adams addressed it head-on this week. He said the team has been hurting itself with penalties, especially in what he called the “strike zone” or the “fringe area” — right around the red zone. “We have to make sure we’re not beating ourselves,” he said. And Stafford was honest about his own turnovers, saying there were bright moments but also “stuff that I definitely wish I could take back.”

And I love that those words came from Davante. He’s a veteran who has been around long enough to know what wins in January and what loses. The Rams’ offensive staff also explained the balance they’re trying to strike: you don’t want a lineman who’s afraid to block hard on the next snap after getting a holding call, because some “competitive penalties” will always come from playing hard. The goal is cutting out the avoidable ones. And Stafford’s response to his own mistakes this week was just as strong: “Crawling in a hole — not an option.” McVay backed him completely, pointing to the 98-yard and 78-yard touchdown drives in the fourth quarter in Philadelphia and saying, “There’s nobody that I’d rather have leading us.” That’s a locker room that’s owning its problems instead of hiding from them.

Let me explain why the field position number is so important, because it’s easy to skip past. When your offense ranks 29th in average starting field position, it means you’re constantly starting drives deep in your own territory — partly because your defense keeps getting stuck on short fields after turnovers and special-teams breakdowns, and partly because your own return game hasn’t been creating anything. And when your defense ranks 31st in the starting field position it allows, it means opponents keep starting drives in great spots. Think about Denver, where two third-quarter touchdown drives each covered 52 yards or less. Think about Philadelphia, where an out-of-bounds kickoff and a big punt return set the Eagles up. The Rams’ defense has been elite despite constantly defending short fields. Imagine what it looks like if the offense and special teams finally stop putting it in those spots.

And here’s a fun one for the superstitious Rams fans out there: according to the Rams’ preview, referee Scott Novak is working tonight’s game — and the Rams are 0-4 in games he’s officiated, most recently a loss in Carolina on Thanksgiving weekend. I don’t put much stock in stuff like that. But with how penalty-heavy the Rams have been lately, every flag tonight is going to feel magnified.

Now let’s remember what happened the last time these two teams met, because it might be a preview of tonight. December of 2024, at SoFi: Rams 44, Bills 42. The two teams combined for 86 points and 902 total yards. According to the Rams’ preview, both teams scored more than 40 points without committing a turnover — the only game like that in NFL history. Josh Allen threw three touchdown passes and ran for three more touchdowns, becoming only the second player ever to do that in a game, joining the legendary Otto Graham in the 1954 NFL Championship. And the Rams won anyway, thanks to Puka’s twelve catches for 162 yards and a blocked punt returned for a touchdown. That’s the kind of game Allen can produce. And that’s why tonight’s total is up around 54 and a half points.

But this Rams defense is a very different animal than the 2024 version. This group has allowed just 990 total yards in four games — second-fewest in the league — and no opponent has reached 260 yards since the 49ers put up 379 in Week 1. The 2024 Rams defense couldn’t stop Allen. The 2026 version has been stopping everybody.

So how do the Rams turn all these nightmares for Buffalo into a win? Let me give you my five keys.

Key one: win the turnover battle for the first time all season. The Rams haven’t done it yet in 2026. Buffalo has seven giveaways in two games. If the Rams’ defense forces even one turnover and the offense plays clean, the math swings heavily in the Rams’ favor. Stafford has to protect the ball against Rousseau — two hands on the football in the pocket, and no forcing throws into coverage.

Key two: feed Puka early and use Davante as the counterpunch. Test Benford, test the nickel, and force Buffalo to commit safety help. When the safety cheats toward Puka, Davante’s one-on-ones on the outside are where the touchdowns come from.

Key three: Kyren as the pressure release. Against a pass rush led by Rousseau and Walker, quick throws to Kyren in space neutralize the rush and attack Buffalo’s biggest coverage weakness. And near the goal line, give him the ball. “By any means.”

Key four: disguise against Allen. Josh Allen is less efficient against the blitz, according to the film review — but he’s excellent against man coverage. So Shula has to mix it up: zone pressures, four-man rushes with Donald and Turner, and the blitz as a surprise on key downs. And keep James Cook from breaking explosive runs, because a Bills team that can run explosively makes Allen almost impossible to stop.

Key five: no penalties in the fringe area. Twenty-one accepted penalties in two games cannot happen again. Not on a night when every flag will be magnified. Not against an offense that can score 40. Stay aggressive, but stay disciplined.
