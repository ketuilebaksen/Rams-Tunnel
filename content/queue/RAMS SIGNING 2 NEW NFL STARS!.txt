And Hill’s agent, Drew Rosenhaus, said this week that as soon as Hill is medically cleared, he’s “letting teams know they can sign him.” Put those things together, and people started connecting dots.

Now here’s what’s not real, at least not yet. Nobody has reported that the Rams have reached out to Hill, brought him in for a visit, or made him an offer. And Hill hasn’t been medically cleared after the serious knee injury he suffered last season.

So today, we’ll go through the whole Tyreek Hill situation: where he is in his recovery, why the Rams’ restructures got people talking, why free agents can still be signed after the trade deadline, and whether this actually makes sense for Los Angeles. Then we’ll look at the real receiver situation heading into Arizona.

Part one: where Tyreek Hill stands right now

Let’s start with the facts about Hill himself.

The injury. In Week 4 of last season, playing for the Dolphins, Hill dislocated his left knee and tore multiple ligaments, including his ACL. It was a devastating injury. He hasn’t played a game since.

The release. According to Pro Football Network, the Dolphins released him in February. So he’s been a free agent since then, rehabbing and waiting to get healthy.

The surgeries. PFN reported that Hill has had multiple surgeries and is still rehabbing.

Medical clearance. This is the key part. Hill has not been medically cleared yet. His agent, Drew Rosenhaus, said on November 10th that he expects clearance “within the next few weeks.” And Rosenhaus said: “As soon as Tyreek Hill is healthy and cleared medically, I’m letting teams know they can sign him.”

His age. Hill turns 33 next spring.

So here’s the picture. A five-time All-Pro, one of the fastest and most dangerous receivers of his generation, coming back from a knee injury that kept him off the field for more than a year. He’s not cleared yet, but his agent says that’s coming soon.

Part two: why the Rams’ restructures started the talk

This is where the speculation came from.

On November 11th, Heavy reported that the Rams restructured the contracts of running back Kyren Williams and safety Kam Curl. Those moves created about $3 million in cap space. Both contracts now run through 2030 because of added void years. Before, both were under contract through 2028.

Here’s why that matters. For weeks, the Rams basically had no cap room. According to Over The Cap, the Rams were actually about $600,000 over the cap before these moves. So doing two restructures right after the deadline, at a time when the Rams can’t make trades anymore, got people asking: what’s the room for?

And that’s when the Tyreek Hill connection started. Bleacher Report analysts had already pitched the Rams as a fit for Hill. Former Pro Bowl receiver T.J. Houshmandzadeh said on the “Pregame” show: “If I’m a team that needs a receiver, the Rams, I’m willing to sign him today and just hope he gets healthy.”

But here’s the important caution. Rams Wire said the restructures don’t necessarily mean a signing is coming. Teams restructure contracts for lots of reasons. With this many injuries, the Rams might just need room to promote practice squad players, sign depth, or handle the roster moves McVay has been talking about all week. And remember, Heavy’s report says there are no reported visits or formal interest from the Rams in Hill.

So the restructures are real. The connection to Hill is speculation.

Part three: the deadline is over, but free agency isn’t

This is something a lot of fans don’t realize, and it’s the reason the Hill story is even possible.

The trade deadline only stops trades. It doesn’t stop free agent signings. After November 10th, the Rams can’t trade for anybody until the offseason. But they can still sign any free agent who’s available, as long as they have the cap room and the roster spot.

Tyreek Hill is a free agent. So if he’s medically cleared, and if the Rams want him, and if he wants the Rams, they could sign him without giving up a single draft pick. No trade needed.

That’s different from all the names we talked about before the deadline. Josh Downs, Keenan Allen, Brian Thomas Jr., Dyami Brown, Jerry Jeudy: all of them were under contract with other teams, so the Rams would have had to trade picks to get them.

And quick reminder on how those names turned out. The Chiefs traded for Josh Downs on deadline day, sending a 2027 fourth and fifth to the Colts. The 49ers got Keenan Allen for a 2027 sixth. As far as I’ve seen, Brian Thomas Jr. and Dyami Brown weren’t traded. And Jeudy stayed in Cleveland.

So the Rams missed out on the trade market. The free agent market, with Hill in it, is still open.

Part four: why the Rams would even think about it

Let’s be honest about why this idea makes sense on paper.

The receiver room is empty. Puka Nacua has an MCL and PCL sprain and turf toe, and McVay said Wednesday that even though the Rams won’t put him on IR, “he still is going to be out for an extended period of time.” Davante Adams is on IR after hamstring surgery, with McVay saying “after Green Bay is a realistic timeline.” Jordan Whittington is on IR with a back injury. And tight end Terrance Ferguson has a hamstring injury that’s “a couple weeks at least.”

What’s left is thin. Konata Mumpfield, Tutu Atwell, CJ Daniels, and Xavier Smith at receiver. Per Rams Wire’s Cameron DaSilva, Mumpfield has 162 receiving yards this season and Atwell has 99. Against Washington, outside of Puka, the receivers combined for four catches.

The offense needs speed. According to Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak, the Rams’ explosive pass play rate through eight weeks was 4.1 percent, compared with 17.5 percent at this point last year. Since Adams got hurt, the Rams had exactly one completion over 20 yards before Washington. They need someone who makes a safety back up.

And at his best, nobody made safeties back up like Tyreek Hill. That’s why his name is so tempting.

Part five: why it might not work

Now the other side, because there are real reasons to be careful.

He hasn’t been cleared. That’s the biggest issue. Until doctors clear Hill, no team can count on him. And even after he’s cleared, he’d need time to get into football shape. He hasn’t played a game in more than a year.

The knee injury was severe. A dislocated knee with multiple torn ligaments is one of the hardest injuries to come back from. Hill’s speed was his superpower. Nobody knows yet whether he’ll have that same explosiveness after this injury. That’s not a criticism of Hill. It’s just the reality of an injury like this.

Age. He turns 33 next spring. Receivers who rely on speed usually decline in their thirties, even without a major injury.

Timing. Even if Hill is cleared “within the next few weeks,” like Rosenhaus said, he’d probably need practice time before he could play. By then, Davante Adams might be back. McVay said “after Green Bay” for Adams. Puka could follow in December. So Hill might arrive right as the Rams’ top two receivers come back.

Roster spots. McVay has been talking all week about how tight the roster is. He said Wednesday, “This is as much as I can remember, spending time with Les in the early parts of the week, of just the roster mechanics.” Adding Hill would mean cutting someone, at a time when the Rams are already trying to save spots for Ferguson and keep Puka on the active roster.

Nobody has reported Rams interest. I’ll keep saying this, because it matters. As of now, there’s no report of the Rams contacting Hill or bringing him in.

So this is not a sure thing. It’s a possibility built on a free agent who isn’t cleared yet and a restructure that might have other purposes.

Part six: my honest take

Here’s what I think. This is opinion, not reporting.

If Hill gets medically cleared, I think the Rams should at least bring him in for a workout. There’s almost no risk in looking. He wouldn’t cost any draft picks. With the restructure room, the Rams might be able to fit a modest, incentive-heavy contract. And if he still has even 80 percent of his old speed, he’d be the most dangerous deep threat on the roster.

McVay said something this week that fits this perfectly. When asked about bringing back injured players, he said that as long as there’s no risk of further injury, a player who’s “not feeling great” can still help, and “we’ll always explore those things.” He was talking about Adams, but the same logic applies to a veteran like Hill.

But I wouldn’t commit a lot of money or a roster spot until I saw him move. And I definitely wouldn’t expect him to save the season. If he signs, think of him as a bonus for the December and January stretch, not a replacement for Puka.

The best-case scenario looks like this. Adams comes back after Green Bay. Hill is cleared, signs, and gets a few weeks of practice. Puka returns in December. And by the playoffs, Stafford has Puka, Adams, and a healthy Tyreek Hill. That would be a scary offense.

The worst case: Hill isn’t cleared in time, or his speed isn’t what it was, and the Rams spent a roster spot and cap room on a player who can’t help.

Who Tyreek Hill was at his best

For younger fans, or anyone who needs a reminder, let’s talk about what Tyreek Hill was before the injury.

He started his career with the Kansas City Chiefs and became one of Patrick Mahomes’ favorite weapons. He won a Super Bowl with Kansas City. Then the Chiefs traded him to Miami in 2022, and he somehow got even better. In 2023, he led the NFL with 1,799 receiving yards. He’s a five-time All-Pro.

What made Hill special wasn’t just speed. Plenty of players are fast. It was how he used it. Defenses had to change their whole coverage plan because of him. Safeties stayed deep. Cornerbacks gave him a cushion. That opened up everything underneath for other receivers and the run game.

That’s exactly what the Rams’ offense is missing right now. With Puka and Adams out, defenses can sit on short routes and crowd the line against Kyren Williams and Blake Corum. A healthy Tyreek Hill would force them to back off.

The question isn’t whether that version of Hill would help. Of course it would. The question is whether that version of Hill still exists after the knee injury. And nobody knows that yet, maybe not even Hill.

The Odell Beckham Jr. comparison

If this all sounds familiar, there’s a reason. The Rams have done something like this before.

Back in November 2021, the Rams signed Odell Beckham Jr. midseason, after he was released by Cleveland. He wasn’t the same player he’d been at his peak with the Giants, but he fit right in. He gave Matthew Stafford another reliable target next to Cooper Kupp, and he played a big role in the Rams’ Super Bowl run that season.

So there’s real precedent. McVay and Les Snead have shown they’re willing to add a big-name veteran receiver in the middle of the season when the fit is right. And it worked.

But there are important differences. Beckham was healthy enough to play right away when he signed. Hill hasn’t been medically cleared yet. And Beckham joined a healthy offense with Kupp at his best, while Hill would be joining an offense missing its top two receivers for the next few weeks.

Still, the Beckham signing tells you this front office doesn’t rule out moves like this. If Hill is cleared, I’d expect the Rams to at least think about it.

What a Hill contract might look like

Let’s talk about what a deal could look like, if it ever happens. This is purely speculation, not a report.

With about $3 million created by the Kyren Williams and Kam Curl restructures, and after being about $600,000 over the cap per Over The Cap, the Rams don’t have a lot of room. So any Hill deal would probably be small up front, with incentives tied to games played or production. That protects the Rams if his knee isn’t ready, and rewards Hill if he plays well.

A midseason contract is also cheaper than it sounds, because a player signed partway through the season only gets paid for the weeks he’s on the roster.

And Hill would have choices. If he’s cleared, other contenders with more cap space and healthier rosters might be interested too. Heavy wrote that Hill has “one obvious suitor” and that it isn’t the Chiefs. So the Rams wouldn’t be the only team calling, if they called at all.

The bottom line: even if the Rams want Hill, they’d have to compete for him with very limited money. That’s not easy.

Two: do the Rams make another roster move? If they release a player or open a roster spot without an obvious reason, that’s worth watching.

Part eight: Puka, Adams, and the timeline

Let’s put the receiver timeline together, because it’s the key to the Hill decision.

Puka Nacua. On Monday, McVay said Puka has an MCL and PCL sprain and turf toe, and that “we’re thinking it’s going to be at least a month.” On Wednesday, McVay said the Rams won’t put him on IR “just because of some semantics with the roster,” but that “he still is going to be out for an extended period of time.” So the best estimate is still December.

Davante Adams. McVay said Wednesday, “I would think after Green Bay is a realistic timeline for him. But he looks great. I talked to him the other day, and he’s feeling good.” So Adams could be back for the first game in December.

Terrance Ferguson. “Probably a couple weeks at least,” McVay said. His 21-day window runs out around the Green Bay game. If he’s not activated by then, he’d go on IR for the rest of the season.

Tyreek Hill, if he signed. Medical clearance “within the next few weeks,” per his agent. Then practice time. Realistically, he might not be ready until December either.

So here’s the honest picture. Everything points to December. The next two games, Arizona this Sunday and Green Bay after the bye, are when the Rams’ receiver room is at its thinnest. Hill wouldn’t help with those two games. He’d be a December and January piece, alongside Adams and Puka.

That’s why I say Hill is a bonus, not a fix.

Part nine: what the Rams need right now

So if Hill can’t help this week, what does the Rams’ offense look like in Arizona?

McVay talked about it Wednesday. He said the game plan is built “in incremental steps,” and that it’ll be “a great opportunity for some new guys to get chances that they haven’t gotten yet.”

He also said he’d “explore everything,” including two-back sets, and he quoted Bill Parcells: “There’s a way to win all of them.”

Here’s what I expect.

More run game. Kyren Williams and Blake Corum combined for 192 rushing yards against the Raiders, 129 against the Chargers, and 124 against Washington. McVay called having both backs “really valuable.”

More tight ends. Tyler Higbee, Davis Allen, who McVay called “tough as hell,” and Colby Parkinson if he’s healthy.

Tutu Atwell as the deep shot. He had the Rams’ biggest pass play since Adams got hurt, a 40-yard catch against the Chargers.

Xavier Smith on returns. Rams On SI’s Grisak expects him to take over kickoff returns, and maybe punts too, so Kyren doesn’t have to.

And the defense leading the way. The Rams held the Chargers to 9 points and Washington to 13. Myles Garrett had three sacks in his first game back. McVay said he’s “optimistic” Trent McDuffie can return from his back injury this week.

Part ten: a word on the restructures

Let’s go back to those restructures one more time, because they matter regardless of Hill.

The Rams restructured Kyren Williams and Kam Curl, creating about $3 million in cap space, per Heavy. Both contracts now run through 2030 with void years.

What does that mean in plain English? The Rams moved some of this year’s salary into future years, so they have more room now. The cost is that those void years add to future cap charges. It’s a common move for teams trying to win now. The Rams have done it before.

And here’s why it matters even if Hill never signs. Before the restructures, the Rams were about $600,000 over the cap, according to Over The Cap. They literally couldn’t promote a practice squad player or sign a replacement without making room. Now they can. With Puka out, Whittington on IR, Ferguson’s clock ticking, and Avila hurt, that flexibility might be the most important thing about this move.

So yes, the restructures could be for Hill. But they could just as easily be for keeping the roster functional. My guess is a little of both: give the Rams options, including the option to sign a player like Hill if he’s cleared and the price is right. That’s my analysis, not a report.

Rams strengths: The pass rush, with Garrett and Aaron Donald together. The fan debate, both sides

I’ve seen a lot of Rams fans arguing about this already, so let me lay out both sides as fairly as I can.

Sign him. T.J. Houshmandzadeh put it best: “If I’m a team that needs a receiver, the Rams, I’m willing to sign him today and just hope he gets healthy.” The Rams are trying to win now. Stafford is 38. Garrett and Donald are on the same defensive line. The Rams already traded picks for Garrett and McDuffie. Why not take a low-cost shot on one of the most explosive receivers of this era? If it works, the playoff offense could be scary. If it doesn’t, you’ve lost a little cap space.

Wait. Hill isn’t cleared. The knee injury was severe. He’s about to turn 33. The Rams have no roster spots to spare, and McVay has been fighting all week just to keep the roster functional. Adams is coming back after Green Bay, and Puka in December. Spending a roster spot and cap room on a player who might not be ready until after those two return doesn’t solve the problem the Rams actually have right now.

My middle ground. Watch closely. If Hill is cleared, bring him in for a workout during or right after the bye. If he looks explosive, make a small, incentive-heavy offer. If he doesn’t, walk away. There’s no downside to looking.
