The Presley timeline, one more time

Let’s get the facts straight first.

November 12: The Rams placed Puka Nacua on injured reserve and promoted Brennan Presley from the practice squad to the active roster. They also signed receiver Tyler Scott to the practice squad.

November 15: Presley played against Arizona. His confirmed contribution was one kickoff return for 20 yards.

November 17: The Rams waived Presley, then signed him back to the practice squad the same day. To make room, they terminated the practice squad contract of nose tackle Yoland Hunt. They also placed center Dylan McMahon on injured reserve.

McVay explained the original promotion before the Arizona game. He said: “We needed the roster spot.” And he praised Presley: “He’s just one of those guys that makes the plays when his number’s called.” He also called him “a great teammate, really smart,” and said he “can contribute on special teams, and he’s got some return ability.”

Now, why the waiver and re-signing? The team hasn’t explained it, and I’m not going to pretend I know the private reasons. But here’s the most common reason teams do this. Once a player is on the 53-man roster, you usually can’t just move him back to the practice squad. You have to waive him first. If nobody claims him, you can sign him back.

And according to Heavy, Presley still has two standard elevations left this season. That means the Rams can bring him up for a game day without using a permanent roster spot. After the game, he’d go back to the practice squad automatically.

So this move keeps Presley in the building, keeps his elevations available, and frees up a spot on the 53-man roster. That’s not a dramatic story. It’s smart roster management.

Why the timing made sense

There’s one more reason this move happened when it did: the bye week.

The Rams don’t play again until Wednesday, November 25. So right now, they don’t need Presley on the active roster. There’s no game to play.

That made it the perfect time to clean up the roster. Put McMahon on IR. Move Presley back to the practice squad. Open up space. Then, when the Packers game gets closer, the Rams can decide who they actually need on the 53-man roster and who they want to elevate.

If they had waited until game week, they’d have had less time to make those decisions, and less flexibility if something went wrong.

So the timing wasn’t random. It was planned around the bye.

Good news number one: the Rams build with overlooked players

Here’s why I think the Presley story is actually good news.

Presley went undrafted in 2025. He’s 5-foot-8 and about 174 pounds. He’s played just nine NFL snaps over two seasons, according to Heavy. By the usual standards, he’s a long shot.

But he’s also Oklahoma State’s all-time leader in receptions. And the Rams keep finding ways to keep him around. That tells you they see something.

And here’s the thing. The Rams have a long history of turning overlooked players into real contributors. Look at this roster right now.

Puka Nacua was a fifth-round pick in 2023. A fifth-round pick. And before his injury this season, he had 70 catches, 715 yards, and four touchdowns in just seven games.

Kyren Williams was a fifth-round pick in 2022. He became the Rams’ lead running back.

Ronnie Rivers went undrafted out of Fresno State. In Arizona, he had 15 carries for 81 yards and the highest PFF grade of any Rams running back at 84.7.

Wesley Bailey went undrafted too. According to Blaine Grisak of Rams On SI, he’s the first undrafted rookie since at least 2000 to have an interception, multiple sacks, and multiple forced fumbles in one season. He graded 93.0 in Arizona.

Xavier Smith was on the 49ers practice squad a couple of weeks ago. Then he scored the biggest touchdown of the Arizona game on a screen pass right before halftime. PFF graded him 92.6.

CJ Daniels was a sixth-round pick, and he was inactive for the first six games of the season. Then he caught his first NFL pass in Arizona.

So when people say Presley is “just a practice squad guy,” I get it. But this team is full of players who were once “just” something. That’s why I think keeping Presley around is good news. The Rams have earned the benefit of the doubt when it comes to spotting talent other teams missed.

The Rams’ draft-day steals

And it’s not just undrafted players. The Rams have built a lot of this team with picks outside the first round.

Cooper Kupp was a third-round pick in 2017. He became a Super Bowl MVP and won the receiving triple crown.

Kobie Turner was a third-round pick in 2023. He became one of the most productive interior pass rushers in the league before his groin injury this season.

Byron Young was also a third-round pick in 2023. He graded 73.4 in Arizona and has been a steady part of the pass rush.

Think about that. The Rams famously traded away a lot of first-round picks over the years, including their 2027 first this year. So they’ve had to get really good at finding players in the later rounds and after the draft.

That’s the bigger reason I don’t laugh off a move like keeping Presley. This front office has made a living off players other teams passed on.

Why Presley’s role is special teams first

Let’s be realistic about Presley, though.

He didn’t have a breakout receiving game in Arizona. He didn’t catch a touchdown. His confirmed contribution was one 20-yard kickoff return.

That’s useful, but it’s small. So right now, his path to playing time is special teams.

And there’s competition there. According to Heavy, six Rams have returned at least one kickoff this season. Alex Bachmann has 13 returns. Jordan Whittington has 12. Xavier Smith has seven. Ronnie Rivers has three. Dean Connors and Presley have one each.

But Whittington is on IR with a back issue, and Connors is out with a broken hand. So the healthy options are Rivers and Smith on the active roster, and Bachmann and Presley on the practice squad.

Rivers has been the main kickoff returner lately. He had a 34-yard return in Arizona. And only Bachmann and Smith have returned punts this season, according to Heavy.

So Presley’s best shot is probably as a kickoff option if the Rams need one on a given week. That’s why the two remaining elevations matter so much. They give the Rams a cheap, flexible way to use him if needed.

That’s not a starring role. But for an undrafted second-year player, it’s a real opportunity. And that’s how a lot of NFL careers start.

Good news number two: the injury picture is about to turn

Here’s my second piece of good news, and it’s a big one.

For the last month, the Rams have been losing players almost every week. But over the next few weeks, the trend should start to reverse. Players are going to start coming back.

Let’s go through it.

Myles Garrett and Ronnie Rivers are already back. They were both activated from injured reserve on November 7, according to SB Nation’s Turf Show Times. Garrett had three sacks against Washington. Rivers was great in Arizona.

Trent McDuffie and Kam Curl are back too. They both played in Arizona. McVay said after the game, “It was great to get Trent McDuffie back out there.” PFF graded McDuffie at 85.3.

Davante Adams could be next. He ruptured his hamstring in Week 6 and had surgery, with a reported recovery timeline of six to 10 weeks. McVay said, “After Green Bay is a realistic timeline.” So he could be back for the Chiefs game.

Kobie Turner might be close too. He suffered a grade 2 groin strain in Week 6, with a six-to-eight-week recovery estimate. The Turf Show Times writer predicted he could return for the Chiefs game in Week 13. That’s a prediction, not an announcement.

Terrance Ferguson has been designated to return. His window closes around the Packers game. If he’s healthy, he could be activated soon.

Coleman Shelton is out four to six weeks with a hamstring strain, but he’s not on IR. So he can come back as soon as he’s ready, without using a designation.

Puka Nacua has the longest road, but his earliest possible return is December 20 against Dallas, according to the report. That’s eligibility, not a guarantee.

So think about where this could go. The Rams have been winning without Puka, Adams, Turner, and others. If those players start coming back in December, this team could get a lot better at the exact time the playoff race heats up.

That’s real good news.

The IR designation math, quickly

There’s one catch, and I’ll be honest about it.

According to Turf Show Times, the Rams have only four injured reserve return designations left this regular season. They started with eight and have used four: an offensive tackle in October, Terrance Ferguson, Myles Garrett, and Ronnie Rivers.

The writer predicts Adams, Puka, and Turner will get three of the remaining four. That would leave one for players like Justin Dedich, Dylan McMahon, Dean Connors, and Jordan Whittington.

So not everyone on IR will get to come back. That’s the bad part.

But here’s the good part. If the Rams make the playoffs, they get two additional designations for the postseason. And the Rams have been smart about this. They kept Shelton and Avila off IR, which saves designations for the players who really need them.

So yes, the math is tight. But the players who matter most are likely to get their spots.

Good news number three: the numbers are on the Rams’ side

Here’s my third piece of good news.

The Rams are 6-4 with four straight wins. Raiders, Chargers 24-9, Washington 20-13, and Arizona 34-6.

The run game is historic. Over the last four games, the Rams have outrushed opponents 653 yards to 175. According to ESPN Research, that 478-yard advantage is the franchise’s biggest four-game rushing differential since 1984, when Eric Dickerson ran for 2,105 yards.

McVay reinvented the offense. According to ESPN’s Sarah Barshop and NFL Next Gen Stats, the Rams used 11 personnel on 53% of their plays coming into Arizona, then used it only three times in that game. Instead, they ran 17 plays out of 20 personnel, with two running backs, for 197 yards and a touchdown.

The defense is rising. Arizona managed only 123 total yards. The Rams had five sacks and two interceptions.

The playoff odds are good. ESPN’s Football Power Index gives the Rams a 47.1% chance to make the playoffs. The Packers are at 33.3%.

McVay is great after byes. Over the previous three seasons, his teams went 23-7 after their bye weeks. In 2023, they started 3-6 and went 7-1 after the bye. In 2024, they started 1-4 and went 9-3 after the bye.

Put all that together, and the Rams are in a strong spot.

Now, the caution side

Okay, I promised you honesty. So here’s the other side.

The offensive line is thin. Coleman Shelton is out four to six weeks. Dylan McMahon is on IR with a partial patellar tear. Steve Avila is dealing with a hamstring injury. The Rams finished the Arizona game with their third center, Beaux Limmer, who moved over from left guard.

The schedule is brutal. ESPN Analytics says the Rams have the toughest second-half schedule in the NFL. Packers, Chiefs, then 49ers.

The receivers are still young. Smith, Mumpfield, Atwell, and Daniels all made plays in Arizona. But they’ll face defenses that have film on them now.

The kickoffs need work. Special teams got a B-minus from Blaine Grisak after two kickoffs missed their target. McVay said that “wasn’t a positive for us today.”

The IR math is tight. Only four designations left, with more players than spots.

So the good news is real. But this team still has real problems to solve.

What Presley’s story says about the culture

Let me come back to Presley for one more minute, because I think his story connects to something bigger.

Think about what he’s been through. Undrafted. Practice squad as a rookie. Practice squad again this season. Promoted. Played a few snaps. Waived. Signed back to the practice squad. All in about a week.

That’s hard. A lot of players would get frustrated.

But the Rams keep bringing him back. And that tells you something about how this team works. McVay talked after the Arizona game about “confidence earned through evidence.” He said, “There’s a lot of guys that are capable.” And he said, “We’re never victims.”

That’s the culture. Nobody is promised anything. But if you keep working and show you can help, the door stays open.

Xavier Smith walked through that door in Arizona. Wesley Bailey walked through it. Ronnie Rivers walked through it. Maybe Presley is next. Maybe not. But he still has a chance, and that’s more than a lot of undrafted players get.

Three realistic paths for Presley

Let’s look at what could happen next for Presley. This is not a report. These are just the realistic options.

Path one: keep developing on the practice squad. He keeps practicing, keeps learning the offense, and stays ready. Slow and steady, but that’s how a lot of careers are built.

Path two: another game-day elevation. If the Rams need an extra receiver or return option, they can elevate him. He has two standard elevations left.

Path three: another active roster chance. If he keeps showing value, or if more injuries hit, the Rams could sign him back to the 53-man roster at some point.

Which is most likely? I’d say path one for now, with path two possible depending on injuries. But nothing is guaranteed.

The Hunt side of the move

I also want to say something about Yoland Hunt, the nose tackle whose practice squad contract was terminated to make room.

That doesn’t mean Hunt did anything wrong. Practice squad spots are limited. When one player comes in, another one usually has to go. It’s just the math of roster management.

Could it mean the Rams are prioritizing receiver and special teams depth right now? Maybe. That would make sense with Puka, Adams, and Whittington all on IR. But one transaction doesn’t prove a big strategy shift. So I won’t read too much into it.

Respect to Hunt. Hopefully he lands somewhere soon.

What McVay’s offense demands from depth receivers

Here’s one more reason the Rams like having guys like Presley around.

McVay’s offense is hard to learn. It’s built on timing, communication, precise routes, and trust between the quarterback and receivers. A receiver has to know where to line up, read the coverage, and run the right route at full speed, sometimes with only a split second to adjust.

You can’t just sign a random free agent off the street and expect him to know all that in a week.

That’s why practice squad players are so valuable. Presley has been in this system since 2025. He knows the playbook. He’s practiced against the Rams defense. If the Rams need him on short notice, he’s ready.

That’s the hidden value of depth. It doesn’t show up in the box score. But when injuries pile up, it can be the difference between a team that keeps winning and a team that falls apart.

What the bye means for the young players

One more thing about the bye week, because it matters for players like Presley.

McVay said the bye is a time to “creatively come up with new ideas based on who’s available and who’s not.” And the Rams have 10 days between games, including a five-day break before players return to the building, according to ESPN’s Sarah Barshop.

For veterans, that time is about rest. For young players on the practice squad, it’s a chance to show the coaches something when practice starts again. Every rep matters when you’re fighting for a game-day elevation.

So when the Rams get back to work, keep an eye on who gets elevated for the Packers game. That will tell you which young players impressed the coaches during the break.
