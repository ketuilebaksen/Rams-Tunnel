Rams House, what is going on? It’s James, welcome back to Rams Tunnel.

The trade deadline has come and gone, and the headline making the rounds today says, “Massive trade confirmed! Rams sign a wide receiver from the 49ers!” Let me fix that right away, because it mixes up two very different things.

There was no trade. The Rams didn’t make a single deal at the deadline. What they did do is sign wide receiver Xavier Smith off the San Francisco 49ers’ practice squad, and place Jordan Whittington on injured reserve with a back injury. So yes, technically, the Rams signed a receiver “from the 49ers.” But it wasn’t a trade, nothing was sent to San Francisco, and it definitely wasn’t massive.

And here’s the part that stings for a lot of Rams fans. The 49ers did make a real receiver trade on Tuesday. They acquired Keenan Allen from the Colts for a 2027 sixth-round pick. Remember the fake “Schefter on First Take” clip from last week that said Keenan Allen was going to the Rams for a sixth? The price was right. The team was wrong. He went to San Francisco instead.

So today, we’ll break down what the Rams actually did, why they brought Xavier Smith back, what losing Whittington really means, and whether standing pat at the deadline was a mistake, all heading into Sunday’s game at Arizona.

Part one: what the Rams actually did

Here are the confirmed moves.

Jordan Whittington to injured reserve. On Monday, Sean McVay said Whittington “continues to battle with some just soreness in his back,” and that they’d “see what that means for him moving forward.” Now we know. He’s on IR.

Xavier Smith signed off the 49ers’ practice squad. Smith was with the Rams earlier this season before they released him. According to Kyle Posey of SB Nation, he joined San Francisco’s practice squad on October 7th, after Dante Pettis went on injured reserve. Now the Rams have brought him back.

Quick explanation of how this works, because a lot of fans don’t know it. Practice squad players aren’t locked in. Any other team can sign them to its active roster at any time. The original team doesn’t get anything in return, and it can’t match the offer. So this wasn’t a trade. The Rams simply offered Smith a spot on their 53-man roster, and he took it.

That’s it. That’s the whole “massive trade.” One receiver to IR, one receiver signed back.

Part two: the deadline came and went

Let’s be honest about what happened at the deadline, because a lot of you were hoping for more.

On Monday, McVay said about a trade, “I would never rule it out.” With Puka Nacua likely headed to IR with MCL and PCL sprains and turf toe, and McVay saying he’d be out “at least a month,” a lot of people expected the Rams to add a receiver.

They didn’t. And here’s what happened with the names we covered all week.

Keenan Allen went to the 49ers for a 2027 sixth-round pick. That’s real, reported first by NFL Network and ESPN. Allen has 40 catches for 351 yards and a touchdown this season, and 1,095 catches for 12,402 yards in his career. He’s 34. And remember, he’s facing a pending minimum three-game NFL suspension from his August arrest, which the NFL is expected to impose after his legal case is resolved. The 49ers took on that risk.

Josh Downs stayed in Indianapolis, as far as I can find. CBS’s deadline coverage said the Colts were open to moving him “at the right price,” and that Pittsburgh reportedly wasn’t going to make a big move. I haven’t seen any report of Downs being traded.

Brian Thomas Jr. also stayed put, as far as I can find. CBS reported the Chiefs showed interest, but Jacksonville was cautious about sending him to an AFC contender.

Dyami Brown, the cheap Washington receiver ESPN’s Aaron Schatz proposed for a late-round pick swap, wasn’t traded either, as far as I’ve seen.

So the Rams didn’t add a receiver by trade. And their biggest division rival did.

Part three: was standing pat a mistake?

This is the big question, so let me give you both sides.

The case that it was a mistake. The Rams are now without Puka Nacua, Davante Adams, and Jordan Whittington at receiver. Against Washington, outside of Puka, the receivers combined for four catches. Per Rams Wire’s Cameron DaSilva, Konata Mumpfield has 162 receiving yards this season and Tutu Atwell has 99. Those are the guys who now have to carry the passing game. And the 49ers got Keenan Allen for just a sixth-round pick. The Rams own a 2027 sixth. They could have matched that.

The case that it wasn’t. The Rams have almost no cap space. The most recent number I’ve seen, from Athlon Sports citing Over The Cap, is just a few thousand dollars. Allen’s contract would have required a restructure or salary going back. And Allen’s pending suspension could hit at any time, which makes him a risky rental. Downs and Thomas would have cost more in picks, and the Rams don’t have a 2027 first or third. On top of that, Puka is expected back in about a month, and Adams after that. A trade would’ve bought a few weeks of help.

My take, and this is opinion, not reporting. I think the Rams could have found a way to add someone cheap. Allen for a sixth was a reasonable price for third-down help, even with the suspension risk. But I also understand why they didn’t. This front office has gone all in with the Garrett and McDuffie trades, and it’s betting on its injured players returning. McVay said it a week ago: “Hopefully, some of those trades will be guys getting back from injury.” That’s the plan, for better or worse.

Part four: why losing Whittington matters more than his stats

On paper, Jordan Whittington doesn’t look like a big loss. He has two catches for 12 yards this season. But Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak made a strong case that he matters a lot more than the box score shows.

Blocking. Grisak called Whittington arguably the best blocking wide receiver on the team. In McVay’s offense, that matters a lot. Receivers aren’t just route runners. They’re blockers on almost every run play. And the run game has carried the Rams for three straight weeks, with 192 rushing yards against the Raiders, 129 against the Chargers, and 124 against Washington. Losing your best blocking receiver hurts that.

Kickoff returns. Whittington was averaging 26.3 yards per kickoff return, per Grisak. That’s real field position. When your offense is short-handed, starting drives at the 30 instead of the 20 makes a big difference.

His season might be over. This is the tough part. Grisak suggested Whittington’s season is likely done, because the Rams may not have enough IR return designations left to bring him back. I want to be clear: that’s Grisak’s assessment of the roster math, not an official announcement. But it lines up with what McVay said Monday, when he admitted, “We’re getting to be where that number is getting so high that we’re going to run out of those eight to be able to return.”

Think about how many players need those slots. Garrett already used one. Ronnie Rivers used one. Adams, Kobie Turner, and Puka will all need one. If the Rams are down to their last few, a receiver with two catches is probably near the bottom of the list. That’s harsh, but it’s the reality of this season.

So the Rams aren’t just replacing two catches. They’re replacing their best blocking receiver and their kickoff returner, all at once.

Part five: why Xavier Smith, and why now?

So why bring back a player the Rams already released?

Familiarity. Smith knows the Rams’ system. He knows the terminology, the special teams calls, and the coaches. With the Arizona game on Sunday, the Rams don’t have time to teach a new player everything. A guy who’s already been in the building can contribute right away.

Returns. Before the Rams released him, Smith was their primary returner. Grisak expects him to take over Whittington’s kickoff return job, and possibly punt returns too. That’s Grisak’s projection, not a confirmed role from the coaching staff, but it makes a lot of sense.

And here’s an important detail. After the Rams released Alex Bachman, running back Kyren Williams handled punt returns against Washington. That’s not ideal. Kyren is your starting running back, one of the most important players in your offense right now. You don’t want him taking extra hits on punt returns. Bringing Smith back should solve that.

Depth. With Puka, Adams, and Whittington all out, the Rams simply needed another healthy receiver on the roster.

Now, the honest part. Grisak also argued that Smith’s size limits what he offers as a receiver. That’s the writer’s evaluation, not McVay’s. But it tells you how to think about this signing. Smith is a depth and special teams move. He’s not a replacement for Puka. He’s not even a replacement for Whittington’s blocking.

And yes, I know a lot of Rams fans have complicated feelings about Xavier Smith, going back to last season’s NFC Championship Game loss in Seattle and that muffed punt. That’s part of his story here, and it’s fair to remember it. But one play doesn’t define a career. If he takes back the return job and helps the Rams flip field position in a big game this season, that would be a pretty good redemption story.

Part six: who carries the passing game now?

Here’s the real question for Sunday. With Puka, Adams, and Whittington all out, who catches the ball?

Grisak identified three receivers who should take on bigger roles: Konata Mumpfield, Tutu Atwell, and CJ Daniels.

Konata Mumpfield. He has 162 receiving yards this season, per Rams Wire, and caught two passes for 20 yards against Washington. He’s going to see a lot more targets.

Tutu Atwell. He has 99 receiving yards this season. But he also had the Rams’ biggest pass play since Adams got hurt: a 40-yard catch against the Chargers that set up a touchdown. Atwell is the one real deep threat left on the roster.

CJ Daniels. He wasn’t targeted against Washington. Now he’ll likely get real snaps.

Xavier Smith. Mostly returns, with some offensive snaps if needed.

And then the tight ends, who might be the real answer. Tyler Higbee. Davis Allen, who McVay said is “tough as hell” after he played through a back injury against Washington. Colby Parkinson, if his ribs are healthy. The Rams already use more two- and three-tight-end sets than almost anyone in the league. Without their top three receivers, expect even more.

McVay put it this way on Monday: “There are other guys that are very capable of doing some good stuff for us, and we’ll look to see those guys step up. It’ll be a great opportunity for them.”

That’s the bet now. The Rams didn’t trade for help, so the help has to come from inside the building.

Part seven: the 49ers side of the story

Since Smith came from San Francisco, let’s look at what was going on over there, because it’s connected.

The 49ers placed one of their defensive tackles on injured reserve, which opened a roster spot. According to Posey, Kyle Shanahan said that player was scheduled for surgery this week and that he’d be surprised if he returned this season, even if the 49ers made a deep playoff run. That roster spot went to Keenan Allen.

Posey also noted that there was no roster move involving Brandon Aiyuk on the transaction wire, and suggested that might not change until Aiyuk’s roster exemption expires on November 23rd. That’s 49ers business, not Rams business, but it tells you San Francisco’s receiver room is going through changes too.

So here’s the picture in the NFC West. The 49ers, at the top of the division, added DeForest Buckner earlier and now Keenan Allen. Seattle added Quenton Nelson. And the Rams added Myles Garrett back from IR and Xavier Smith back from the 49ers’ practice squad.

As The Athletic’s Nate Atkins put it a couple of weeks ago: “The NFC West arms race is on.” The Rams just chose to arm themselves from inside.

What Keenan Allen in San Francisco means for the Rams

Let’s spend a minute on the Allen trade, because it affects the Rams directly.

First, it makes the 49ers better on third down. Allen isn’t the player he was at his peak. NFL.com’s write-up said he “isn’t as steady as he once was,” but “can be counted on for third downs.” That’s exactly the kind of player who hurts you in a close division game. And the Rams play the 49ers in San Francisco on December 13th.

Second, there’s the suspension. Back in September, Adam Schefter reported that Allen faces a minimum three-game NFL suspension stemming from his August arrest, and that the league is expected to wait for his legal case to finish before imposing it. Nobody knows exactly when that will happen. If it lands in December, the 49ers could lose him right around the time they play the Rams. If it’s delayed until after the season, they get him for the whole stretch run. That’s the gamble San Francisco took.

Third, it’s a reminder of what the Rams could have done. A sixth-round pick for a veteran receiver who knows how to get open on third down is a low price. The Rams own a 2027 sixth. The difference is cap space. The 49ers had room to take on Allen’s salary. The Rams had almost none.

So in a way, the Allen trade is the perfect summary of the Rams’ deadline. The player was available. The price was cheap. The Rams just didn’t have the cap room, or maybe the appetite, to make it work.

And it makes that fake clip from last week even stranger to look back on. It said “Keenan Allen to the Rams for a sixth-round pick.” Then the real trade happened: Keenan Allen to the 49ers for a sixth-round pick. Some of those fake clips get the price right and the team wrong. That’s exactly why you always wait for the real report.

Part eight: the Garrett factor

Here’s the thing that makes me less worried about the deadline than I might otherwise be.

The Rams’ best addition this month didn’t come from a trade. It came from the injury report. Myles Garrett had three sacks, three tackles for loss, and a strip-sack right before halftime in his first game back against Washington. McVay said Garrett and Aaron Donald’s first game together was “unscouted,” and that Donald “had a couple awesome things that allowed him to be able to get some one-on-ones.”

And Garrett’s snap count is going to go up. McVay said Monday that his role may change “as you continue to increase his snap count as he’s feeling better and getting back to being able to play that workload that he’s been accustomed to throughout his whole career.”

So if the Rams’ offense is short-handed for the next few weeks, the defense might have to carry them. And with Garrett and Donald together, it can. Washington scored 13 points. The Chargers didn’t score a touchdown. This defense is good enough to win low-scoring games.

That’s the real deadline strategy. Not a receiver. A defense that keeps games close until the offense gets healthy.

What the rest of the NFC did

To understand where the Rams stand, it helps to look at what the other contenders did over the last week and a half.

Chicago Bears. The biggest swing. They sent two first-round picks to Tennessee for defensive tackle Jeffery Simmons. The Bears are 6-2 and clearly going for it.

Detroit Lions. They added safety Andre Cisco from the Jets for a 2028 sixth-round pick, per CBS’s deadline coverage.

San Francisco 49ers. DeForest Buckner from the Colts a couple of weeks ago, and now Keenan Allen.

Seattle Seahawks. Quenton Nelson from the Colts, adding an All-Pro guard to their offensive line.

Philadelphia Eagles. They actually sold, sending defensive tackle Jordan Davis to Jacksonville for a 2027 first-round pick and a 2029 second-round pick.

So most of the NFC contenders added somebody. The Rams didn’t. That doesn’t automatically make it wrong. The Rams made their big moves in the offseason: Garrett in June, McDuffie in March. They paid the price up front. But it does mean the Rams are counting on their own injured players to be their deadline additions, while their rivals added from outside.

My deadline report card for the Rams

Let me grade the Rams’ deadline, honestly. This is my opinion.

Cap discipline: A. The Rams didn’t blow up their future to chase a short-term fix. With almost no cap space and no 2027 first or third, that took discipline.

Urgency: C. With Puka out for at least a month, Adams’ timeline uncertain, and Whittington on IR, I think the Rams needed one more receiver than they got. Keenan Allen for a sixth, or Dyami Brown for a late-round swap, would have helped for the next month without hurting the future.

Special teams fix: B-plus. Bringing back Smith fixes a real problem. Kyren Williams shouldn’t be returning punts, and losing Whittington’s 26.3-yard kickoff average needed a replacement.

Big picture: B. The Rams’ plan is clear: ride the defense, run the ball, and get healthy. With Garrett back, that’s a reasonable plan. But it leaves very little margin if another receiver gets hurt.

Overall: B-minus. Not a bad deadline. Not a great one. A “trust the plan” deadline. And now we find out whether the plan works.

The road ahead

Here’s the schedule that will decide whether the deadline approach was right.

Sunday: at Arizona. The team that beat the Rams 18-16 in October. A win means four straight and 6-4.

Then the bye. A week to heal. McDuffie, Avila, and Davis Allen all get extra time. Kobie Turner gets closer to coming off IR.

Then Green Bay. That’s the first game after the bye, and a big test against a playoff-caliber team.

Then December. That’s when Adams and Puka could start coming back. McVay said Puka’s out “at least a month,” which points to sometime in December. Adams’ original timeline was six to ten weeks from October 19th, trending toward the shorter end, though McVay wouldn’t give a date on Monday and said he’s “definitely concerned.” If both are back for the final stretch, the Rams could have their full offense for the playoff race.

So the next three or four games are the bridge. If the Rams can go 2-1 or 3-1 in that stretch with this short-handed offense, standing pat will look smart. If they go 1-3, everyone will be asking why they didn’t add a receiver.

Three questions for Sunday

Let me leave you with three questions I’ll be watching in Arizona.

One: who leads the Rams in targets? For the last two games, it’s been Puka by a mile, with 12 targets against the Chargers and 19 against Washington. Without him, does it spread out across the tight ends, or does one receiver step up?

Two: does Stafford take more shots downfield? With Atwell as the only real deep threat, will McVay call more vertical plays, or keep it short and safe?

Three: how much does Garrett play? McVay said his snap count will go up. If Garrett plays 70 or 80 percent of the snaps and gets home two or three more times, this defense might be the best in the NFC.

Part nine: Arizona preview

The Rams play at Arizona on Sunday, then finally get their bye.

Arizona beat the Rams 18-16 back on October 18th. That’s the game where Adams got hurt. Now the Rams will face the Cardinals without Adams, without Puka, without Whittington, and probably without guard Steve Avila, who has a hamstring strain.

Here’s what the Rams need to do.

Run the ball. Kyren Williams and Blake Corum have to carry this offense. Three straight games with at least 124 rushing yards. Keep that going.

Protect Kyren. With Smith back to handle returns, Kyren can focus on being the lead back.

Feature the tight ends. Higbee, Allen, and Parkinson should be Stafford’s top targets.

Find one big play. Atwell is the one receiver who can stretch the field. One deep shot can change a game like this.

Let the defense win. Garrett, Donald, Byron Young, and hopefully a healthy Trent McDuffie. McVay said Monday that McDuffie is “making good progress” with his back and could be back this week.

Get to 6-4. That’s the goal. A win in Arizona would mean four straight wins, revenge for the October loss, and a 6-4 record going into the bye. That’s a playoff team.

My prediction: Rams 20, Cardinals 16. A grind-it-out game. The run game controls the clock, a tight end scores, and Garrett gets at least one more sack. I’ll update this if the injury report changes.

Part ten: how to read headlines after the deadline

Now that the deadline is over, the fake trade clips should slow down, since nobody can be traded until after the season. But the misleading headlines won’t stop. Here’s what to watch for.

“Trade” versus “signing.” Today’s headline called a practice squad signing a “massive trade.” After the deadline, teams can still sign free agents and practice squad players, but they can’t trade. If a headline says “trade” between now and the end of the season, it’s wrong.

“From the 49ers.” That’s technically true about Smith, but it makes it sound like a big deal between rivals. It was a practice squad signing.

“Confirmed.” Today’s transactions really are confirmed: Smith signed, Whittington to IR. But “confirmed” doesn’t make something massive.

And for the record, we covered fourteen fake “Schefter on First Take” trade clips before the deadline. None of those trades happened. The real deadline story for the Rams turned out to be: no trade, and a returner coming back from the practice squad.

Wrapping it up

Let’s bring it home.

The Rams didn’t make a trade. The headline is wrong. They signed Xavier Smith off the 49ers’ practice squad and placed Jordan Whittington on injured reserve with a back injury. Whittington’s season may be over because of how few IR return slots the Rams have left, though that’s an assessment, not an official ruling.

Meanwhile, the 49ers traded a 2027 sixth-round pick for Keenan Allen, the same price a fake clip promised the Rams last week. Josh Downs and Brian Thomas Jr. stayed put, as far as I can find.

So the Rams head to Arizona without Puka, Adams, or Whittington, relying on Mumpfield, Atwell, Daniels, Smith, and the tight ends in the passing game, with Kyren and Corum carrying the run game and Garrett leading the defense.

Was standing pat the right call? We’ll find out over the next month.
