And neither one happened.

Part one: what Les Snead actually did

Let’s lay it out plainly.

No trades. The Rams made zero trades at the deadline.

Xavier Smith signed off the 49ers’ practice squad. Smith was with the Rams earlier this season before they released him. McVay called him “X-Man” on Wednesday and said, “It’s good to be able to get X-Man back, familiarity with our system.”

Jordan Whittington placed on injured reserve with a back injury. McVay said, “It’s a bummer we had to put Jordan Whittington on injured reserve. He’s been pushing through with his back.”

Puka Nacua kept off injured reserve. This one surprised people. On Monday, McVay said there was “a strong chance” Puka would go on IR after his MCL and PCL sprain and turf toe. On Wednesday, McVay said, “We’re not going to put him on IR just because of some semantics with the roster and things like that. But he still is going to be out for an extended period of time.”

That’s the entire list. One returner back from the practice squad, one receiver on IR, and one roster decision about Puka. Not exactly “two new NFL stars.”

Part two: what happened to the trade targets

Let’s go through the names from the original video and what actually happened.

Dyami Brown, Washington. ESPN’s Aaron Schatz proposed a trade where the Rams would get Brown and a 2027 seventh-round pick, and Washington would get a 2027 sixth. Basically, the Rams would move down one round late in the draft to add a fast receiver who blocks well. Brown is 27, a former third-round pick, and had 14 catches for 229 yards and a touchdown during Washington’s playoff run two years ago. But he hurt his calf in practice before the Rams game and missed it. As far as I’ve seen, he wasn’t traded.

Brian Thomas Jr., Jacksonville. The big swing. A 1,282-yard rookie season in 2024, 4.33 speed, and a rookie contract that runs through 2027. But a rough 2026: just 10 catches for 117 yards through eight games, per the original video, and he was benched earlier this season. CBS reported during deadline week that the Chiefs had shown interest, but Jacksonville was cautious about sending him to an AFC contender. As far as I can find, he stayed in Jacksonville.

Josh Downs, Indianapolis. CBS reported the Colts were open to moving him “at the right price,” and that Pittsburgh reportedly wasn’t going to make a big move. As far as I can find, he stayed put too.

Keenan Allen, Indianapolis. He was traded, but to the 49ers, for a 2027 sixth-round pick. That’s real, reported first by NFL Network and ESPN.

Jerry Jeudy, Cleveland. Tom Pelissero reported before the deadline that the Browns expected to keep him unless they got a “truly crazy offer.” He stayed in Cleveland, as far as I’ve seen.

Calvin Ridley. His name came up in Rams Wire’s list. I haven’t seen any reporting that connected him to an actual Rams deal.

So the only receiver on that whole list who moved went to the Rams’ biggest division rival.

Part three: why Snead passed, in McVay’s words

On Wednesday, McVay explained the thinking behind the quiet deadline, and it was one of the most honest answers I’ve heard from him.

When asked whether the Rams looked at other moves, he said: “We look at all that kind of stuff.” Then: “It kind of takes two, and got to be able to see what you’re willing to give, what’s out there, what does it look like in terms of envisioning things.”

When asked whether the playbook’s complexity kept them from adding someone, McVay said it was “not as much that as much as just getting guys back, and then, okay, who are those players that you would acquire, and what does that really look like?”

And then he said something that tells you exactly how busy Snead has been: “This is as much as I can remember, spending time with Les in the early parts of the week, of just the roster mechanics.”

He explained why: “We’ve just had a lot of different things across the board at a number of spots.” And he finished with the key line: “The main thing is you’re hopeful that you’re getting some of these guys back, and sooner than later, rather than saying, okay, these guys are out for the year and we’re going to try to fill that void for the year.”

So the logic is clear. The Rams think their key injured players, Puka, Davante Adams, Terrance Ferguson, Kobie Turner, are coming back this season. If you believe that, you don’t spend scarce assets on a short-term fix.

Part four: the money and the picks

Here’s the part a lot of fans forget when they see “Rams signing two stars” headlines. Les Snead didn’t have much to work with.

Cap space. The most recent number I’ve seen, from Athlon Sports citing Over The Cap, is just a few thousand dollars. Not millions. Thousands. Any trade would have needed a restructure, like Kyren Williams’ deal, which was estimated to free about $3.5 million, or a player with salary going the other way.

Picks. The Rams don’t have their 2027 first-round pick. Cleveland owns it from the Garrett trade. They don’t have their 2027 third. Kansas City owns it from the McDuffie trade. They don’t have their 2028 second, which also went to Cleveland. And they traded their 2027 seventh to Baltimore in the Tre’Davious White deal. What’s left: a 2027 second, fourth, fifth, and sixth, and a 2028 third.

So when people ask “Why didn’t Snead do something?”, the honest answer is that he’d already done the big things. He did them in March and June, not November.

Part five: Les Snead’s history

The headline says Snead “has done it again.” So let’s talk about what Snead has actually done in the past, because his reputation is real.

Snead has made four deadline-day trades over the years. He’s never been afraid of a big swing. This is the general manager who traded for Jalen Ramsey, Matthew Stafford, and Von Miller, then won a Super Bowl. And this year, he made two huge offseason moves: Trent McDuffie in March, and Myles Garrett in June, sending Jared Verse and three picks, including a 2027 first, to Cleveland.

So Snead isn’t shy. When he sees a deal he likes, he makes it. That’s why this quiet deadline stands out. It wasn’t that he couldn’t pull the trigger. It’s that he decided the deals out there weren’t worth it.

My honest take, and this is opinion: I think Snead looked at the price for receivers, looked at his empty cap, looked at McVay’s timeline for Adams, and decided to wait. Whether that was right will depend on how the next three games go.

Part six: the 49ers got Keenan Allen, and why it matters

Let’s spend a minute on the one receiver trade that did happen, because it affects the Rams directly.

The 49ers sent a 2027 sixth-round pick to the Colts for Keenan Allen. He’s 34. He has 40 catches for 351 yards and a touchdown this season, and 1,095 catches for 12,402 yards in his career. NFL.com wrote that he “isn’t as steady as he once was,” but “can be counted on for third downs.”

The Rams own a 2027 sixth-round pick. So in theory, they could have matched that price. The difference was cap space. The 49ers had room for Allen’s salary. The Rams had almost none.

There’s also a risk the 49ers took on. Back in September, Adam Schefter reported that Allen faces a minimum three-game NFL suspension stemming from his August arrest, and that the league was expected to wait for his legal case to finish before imposing it. If that suspension lands during the stretch run, San Francisco loses him at the worst time.

And the Rams play the 49ers in San Francisco on December 13th. So whether Allen is available that day could matter in a big division game.

Part seven: the receiver room right now

So what does Snead’s decision leave the Rams with?

Out: Puka Nacua, with an MCL and PCL sprain and turf toe, out “for an extended period of time.” Davante Adams, on IR after hamstring surgery. Jordan Whittington, on IR with a back injury. Terrance Ferguson, the tight end who’s “really an extension of that passing game,” in McVay’s words, with a hamstring injury that’s “a couple weeks at least.”

Available: Konata Mumpfield, Tutu Atwell, CJ Daniels, and Xavier Smith at receiver. Tyler Higbee, Davis Allen, and Colby Parkinson at tight end.

Against Washington, outside of Puka, the receivers combined for four catches. Per Rams Wire’s Cameron DaSilva, Mumpfield has 162 receiving yards this season, Atwell has 99, and Whittington, now on IR, had 12.

That’s the group Matthew Stafford has to work with in Arizona on Sunday. And that’s why I think Snead’s quiet deadline is a real gamble, not a safe play.

Part eight: the good news, Adams is coming

Here’s what makes Snead’s bet more reasonable.

On Wednesday, McVay gave the first real timeline for Davante Adams since the surgery: “I would think after Green Bay is a realistic timeline for him. But he looks great. I talked to him the other day, and he’s feeling good.”

He also said, “What we are optimistic is Davante’s return is sooner than later, which is a big deal to us.”

So here’s the schedule. Arizona this Sunday. Then the bye. Then Green Bay. Then, if McVay’s timeline holds, Adams is back. That’s the moment the Rams’ offense starts to look normal again.

And McVay talked about how he thinks about bringing back a veteran who might not be 100 percent. The rule is no risk of further injury. But if that box is checked, he said, they’ll “explore those things,” because Adams has “great awareness, great understanding of how he physically feels.”

That’s Snead’s real deadline acquisition: a healthy Davante Adams, probably in early December.

Part nine: Puka and the roster puzzle

Let’s go back to the Puka decision, because it’s connected to everything Snead did.

McVay said keeping Puka off IR was about “semantics with the roster.” Here’s my read, and this is analysis, not a quote. On Monday, McVay said the Rams were running low on IR return designations: “We’re getting to be where that number is getting so high that we’re going to run out of those eight to be able to return.”

Garrett used one. Ronnie Rivers used one. Adams and Kobie Turner will need them. Ferguson’s situation is tied to one too. McVay said Ferguson’s 21-day clock runs out around the Green Bay game, and if he’s not activated by then, “he would go on IR for the rest of the season.”

So the Rams kept Puka on the active roster to save a return slot, even though it means carrying a player who can’t play for a while. That’s the kind of decision Snead and McVay were spending all week on.

This is what a GM’s job looks like in a season like this. Not big splashy trades. Hours of roster math, deciding who gets a spot and who has to wait.

Part ten: the McDuffie update and the defense

One more piece of good news. McVay said Wednesday about Trent McDuffie: “I think we’re optimistic that this week he will be able to go.” He called McDuffie “so damn tough,” and finished with, “Hopefully this is the week.”

That matters, because the defense has to carry this team right now. The Rams held the Chargers to 9 points and Washington to 13. Myles Garrett had three sacks, three tackles for loss, and a strip-sack in his first game back. And McVay said Aaron Donald “had a couple awesome things that allowed him to be able to get some one-on-ones.”

He also said Donald is having fun out there: “You can tell when he’s having fun and when he’s not.” And, “He’s playing great.”

So if the offense is short-handed for a few weeks, the defense, led by two future Hall of Famers, is good enough to keep the Rams in every game. That’s another reason Snead could afford to wait.

Part eleven: McVay’s game plan without Puka

McVay was asked whether he’d consider a two-back offense, or even a T formation, with the receiver room this thin. He said, “I think you explore everything.”

He talked about the coaches he respects most: “the people that make the most out of whatever circumstances they are. They’re putting players in positions to be successful, to allow their skill set to shine.”

And he quoted Bill Parcells: “There’s a way to win all of them.” He said, “What that looks like doesn’t have to be the same week in and week out.”

So expect the Rams to look different in Arizona. More of Kyren Williams and Blake Corum, who combined for 192 rushing yards against the Raiders, 129 against the Chargers, and 124 against Washington. More tight ends. And, as McVay said, “a great opportunity for some new guys to get chances that they haven’t gotten yet.”

He even joked about bringing back Robert Woods. “I’ve definitely been tempted,” he said. “I think that’d be a violation of the CBA.” That’s a joke, not a real move. But it tells you how thin things are.

Part twelve: my grade on Snead’s deadline

Here’s my honest grade, and this is opinion.

Discipline: A. Snead didn’t trade away the future for a few weeks of help, even under pressure.

Roster management: A-minus. Keeping Puka off IR, saving a spot for Ferguson, and bringing back a returner who knows the system all show careful planning.

Urgency: C. With Puka, Adams, Whittington, and Ferguson all out, one cheap receiver would have helped. Keenan Allen went to the 49ers for a sixth. A Dyami Brown-type move would have cost almost nothing in picks.

Overall: B. A smart, careful deadline that might leave the offense short for the next two games. If the Rams go 2-0 against Arizona and Green Bay, this grade goes up. If they go 0-2, people will ask why Snead didn’t add anyone.

So “Les Snead has done it again”? Not this time. This time, Les Snead did nothing, on purpose. And we’re about to find out if that was the right call.

Part thirteen: Arizona preview

The Rams play at Arizona on Sunday, then get their bye.

Arizona beat the Rams 18-16 on October 18th, the game where Adams got hurt. Now the Rams go back without Adams, Puka, Whittington, or Ferguson, and maybe without guard Steve Avila, who has a hamstring strain.

Keys for the Rams:

Run the ball with Kyren and Corum.

Use the tight ends as the main passing targets.

Take a shot or two to Tutu Atwell, the one real deep threat left.

Let Xavier Smith help the return game, so Kyren doesn’t have to return punts.

And let Garrett and Donald take over up front.

My prediction: Rams 20, Cardinals 16. A grinding, defensive game, and the Rams go into the bye at 6-4 with four straight wins.
