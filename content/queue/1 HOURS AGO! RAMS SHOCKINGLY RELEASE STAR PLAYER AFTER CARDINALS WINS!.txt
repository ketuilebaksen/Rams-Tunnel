Part one: the big picture

Quick reminder of where we are.

The Rams beat Arizona 34-6. That’s four straight wins, and they’re now 6-4. They did it without Puka Nacua, without Davante Adams, without Terrance Ferguson, and without Steve Avila. And during the game, they lost both Coleman Shelton and Dylan McMahon on the offensive line.

Blaine Grisak of Rams On SI put out position grades for the game. ESPN’s Sarah Barshop weighed in too. And Pro Football Focus had individual grades. Let’s go through them group by group.

Part two: Matthew Stafford, grade B

Grisak gave Stafford a B. His PFF grade was 81.6.

Stafford went 18 of 26 for 249 yards, three touchdowns, and one interception. And he completed passes to nine different players.

Here’s the stat that jumped out at me. Stafford averaged 0.63 expected points added per pass. That ranked second in the league for the week, behind only Brock Purdy.

What does “expected points added” mean? It’s a way of measuring how much each play helps your team score. A big number means almost every throw was moving the Rams closer to points. So by this measure, Stafford was one of the most efficient quarterbacks in the NFL that week.

So why only a B? Because of the turnover. Grisak said turnovers still need work. That interception to Cody Simon early in the game was a mistake. But when your run game dominates the way the Rams’ did, efficiency is enough.

Part three: running backs, grade A

This was the star of the show. ESPN’s Sarah Barshop gave the running backs an A.

Blake Corum, Kyren Williams, and Ronnie Rivers combined for 44 carries and over 200 rushing yards. Corum had 100 yards and a touchdown by himself.

And now, the stat of the week.

The Rams have outrushed their opponents by 478 yards over their last four games. That’s their biggest four-game rushing differential since 1984.

Why does 1984 matter? That was the year Eric Dickerson ran for 2,105 yards, which is still the NFL single-season rushing record. So this Rams run game is putting up numbers we haven’t seen from this franchise since one of the greatest seasons any running back has ever had.

Now, let’s be clear. This is a historical comparison. It’s not a prediction that anyone in this backfield is going to match what Dickerson did. But it tells you how dominant the Rams’ run game has been during this four-game streak.

Now here’s a surprise from the PFF grades.

Ronnie Rivers: 84.7. The highest grade of the three backs.

Blake Corum: 73.7.

Kyren Williams: 46.5.

I know, that might surprise you. Corum had the 100 yards. But PFF grades measure more than yards. They look at things like breaking tackles, gaining yards after contact, blocking, and making the most of each carry. Rivers had 15 carries for 81 yards and closed out the game, and PFF liked how he ran.

And for Kyren, one game with a low grade isn’t a big deal. He’s been the lead back for years. The good news is that the Rams have three backs who can all contribute. McVay said it after the game: “Blake and Kyren have always been outstanding,” and “Ronnie ran really well at the end there.”

Why grades and stats don’t always match

Before we go further, let’s talk about grades in general, because some of these numbers are going to surprise you.

Box score stats tell you what happened. Yards, catches, touchdowns. Grades try to tell you how well a player did his job on every snap, even the plays that don’t show up in the box score.

So a running back can have 100 yards but a lower grade if some of those yards came from great blocking, and he missed a hole or two. And a running back with fewer yards can grade higher if he broke tackles and made something out of nothing.

The same thing goes for pass rushers. A defensive lineman can get a sack but still have a low grade if he lost a lot of his other matchups. And a lineman with no sacks can grade high if he was getting pressure all day.

So grades aren’t perfect. They’re one person’s or one company’s opinion on each play. But they help you see things the box score misses. That’s why I like looking at both.

Keep that in mind as we go through the rest of the team.

Part twelve: the Brennan Presley waiver

Now let’s talk about the move behind the headline.

According to Athlon Sports’ Chris Phelps, the Rams waived Brennan Presley on Monday, November 16. That’s just a day after his NFL debut in Arizona.

So who is Presley? He was a two-time All-Big 12 honoree at Oklahoma State. He went undrafted in 2025 and signed with the Rams. He ended up on the practice squad after final cuts in his rookie year, and the same thing happened this season.

Then, with Puka and Adams out, the Rams promoted him for the Arizona game. McVay explained the move before the game, saying Presley “makes the plays when his number’s called,” and that he could help as a returner. Presley played only a few snaps in his debut.

And then on Monday, he was waived.

Here’s what fans need to understand. Being waived doesn’t mean the Rams don’t like him. It’s often about roster spots. Teams move players between the active roster and the practice squad all the time, depending on who’s healthy and who they need that week.

Blaine Grisak said he strongly expects Presley to come back to the practice squad. But let’s be clear: that’s his expectation, not an official signing.

Here’s how it works. When a player is waived, every other team gets a chance to claim him. If nobody claims him, he “clears waivers,” and then the Rams can sign him back to the practice squad. But another team could claim him first. Or the Rams could go in a different direction.

So don’t confuse a likely move with a done deal. Until the Rams actually sign him back, it’s not official.

And we also don’t know exactly why the Rams waived him. The reports don’t say. So I’m not going to guess.

How the practice squad shuffle works

Let me explain the practice squad a little more, because a lot of fans get confused by moves like this.

Every NFL team has 53 players on its active roster. Then it has a practice squad, which is a group of extra players who practice with the team but usually don’t play in games.

Teams can promote a practice squad player to play in a game. That’s called a game-day elevation. Or they can sign him to the active roster, which is what happened with Presley before the Arizona game.

But once a player is on the 53-man roster, moving him back to the practice squad isn’t automatic. In most cases, the team has to waive him first. Then, if no other team claims him, he can go back to the practice squad.

That’s why you see “waived” so often. It sounds dramatic. But a lot of the time, it’s just the first step in moving a player back to the practice squad.

And remember why Presley got the spot in the first place. McVay said the Rams needed a roster spot, which is why Puka went on IR. Now, with McMahon going to IR and other injuries piling up, the Rams have to keep moving pieces around to fill the needs they have this week.

Part thirteen: Dylan McMahon goes to IR

The other roster move is the one I’d actually worry about.

Per Grisak, the Rams placed center Dylan McMahon on injured reserve. McMahon hurt his knee in Arizona after coming in for Coleman Shelton.

That means McMahon will miss at least the next four games. And it leaves the Rams thin at center. Shelton has a hamstring injury, and we don’t have an official timeline for him yet. Avila is also out at guard.

So Beaux Limmer, who slid from guard to center during the game, could be important going forward. He graded 75.6 in Arizona, which is a solid number for a guy changing positions in the middle of a game.

Grisak also reported that the Rams released a defensive lineman from the practice squad. That’s a smaller move.

And here’s a connection worth noting. When McMahon goes to IR, the Rams need a roster spot for another lineman. That might be part of why roster spots are moving around, including Presley’s. But the reports don’t say that directly, so take it as my guess, not a fact.

This is not a report, it’s my concern: the Rams might need to add another center or guard before the Packers game. Keep an eye on that this week.

Part fourteen: the Puka timeline

Let’s clear up where Puka Nacua stands, because there’s some confusion.

Puka suffered his knee injury against Washington in Week 9. He has an MCL and PCL sprain plus turf toe, according to McVay. He was placed on injured reserve on November 12.

In seven games this season, despite missing the first two, Puka had 70 catches, 715 yards, and four touchdowns. All three lead the Rams. That’s huge production to lose.

Here’s how the IR rule works. A player on injured reserve has to miss at least four games. The Arizona game was the first. And here’s the key detail: the bye week doesn’t count as a game.

So Puka has to miss Arizona, Green Bay, Kansas City, and San Francisco at a minimum. That means the earliest he could come back is the game after San Francisco.

And remember, “earliest” doesn’t mean “will.” His recovery will decide when he’s ready. There’s no confirmed return date.

As for Davante Adams, McVay said “after Green Bay is a realistic timeline.” So Adams could be back for the Chiefs or the 49ers.
