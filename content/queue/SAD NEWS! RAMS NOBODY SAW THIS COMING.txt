Part one: Terrance Ferguson stays on IR

Let’s start with the first piece of “sad news.”

Terrance Ferguson, the second-year tight end, has been ruled out for today’s game. The Rams had opened his window to return to practice, which starts a 21-day clock. But he’s still going to stay on injured reserve for now.

Here’s why that’s not as bad as it sounds. When a player’s practice window opens, the team has 21 days to activate him. If they don’t, he’s done for the year. The Rams opened Ferguson’s window, then he dealt with a hamstring problem. McVay said earlier this week his recovery is “probably a couple weeks at least.”

So the plan is simple. Ferguson won’t play in Arizona. Then comes the bye week, which gives him extra time to heal without missing a game. And the Rams hope to have him back before his 21-day window runs out, which is around the Green Bay game.

In other words, the bye week is coming at the perfect time for Ferguson. He misses one game, and then he gets two weeks of recovery.

Is it disappointing? Sure. Ferguson is a talented young pass catcher, and the Rams could use every pass catcher they can get right now. But it’s not a disaster. And it’s definitely not something nobody saw coming.

Part two: Steve Avila is out

The second piece of bad news is more serious for today.

Steve Avila is out with a hamstring injury. Avila is a starting guard, and he’s been part of an offensive line that’s done a great job protecting Matthew Stafford this season.

The good news is that, for now, the Rams are not planning to put Avila on injured reserve. That tells you they expect him back fairly soon. If they thought he’d be out for a long time, they’d use the IR spot.

But for today, the Rams have to protect Stafford without one of their starting guards. And that matters a lot against Arizona, because the Cardinals blitz a lot. In October, Arizona’s pressure was a big part of why they beat the Rams 18-16.

McVay said on Friday that “protecting Matthew will always be at a premium and at the forefront of our minds.” Today, that’s going to be tested.

Part three: the Ladd McConkey call is real, but it’s over

Now let’s talk about the most interesting part of this story.

According to ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler, the Rams reached out to the Chargers before the trade deadline about Ladd McConkey. Several outlets picked it up, including Bleacher Report, ClutchPoints, and Yardbarker.

Let’s go through what’s real and what isn’t.

What’s real: the Rams called. They asked about McConkey. That’s a real report from a real ESPN insider.

What’s not real: there’s no sign the talks ever became serious negotiations. There’s no report of a deal being close. And the deadline passed on November 10 without the Rams making any trade at all.

So why does this matter? Because it tells us how Les Snead was thinking. When Puka Nacua went down with his knee injury, and Davante Adams was already on IR, Snead didn’t just sit still. He made calls. He went after a young, high-level receiver, not just a cheap veteran.

That’s very Les Snead. This is a general manager who traded for Jalen Ramsey, Matthew Stafford, and Von Miller. He always asks about the big names. Sometimes it works. Sometimes the other team says no.

And here, the Chargers almost certainly said no. Think about it. McConkey is one of the best young receivers in the NFL, on a rookie contract, and he’s Justin Herbert’s favorite target. The Chargers are a contender. There was never much chance they’d trade him to the team that shares their stadium, in the middle of the season.

So this is a real report, but it’s not a trade. It’s a phone call that went nowhere. And with the deadline gone, it’s in the past.

This is not a report: if you’re asking whether the Rams might try again in the offseason, I’d say it’s very unlikely. The Chargers would want a huge return, and the Rams have already traded away their 2027 first-round pick. That’s my opinion, not reporting.

Why the Rams stood still at the deadline

So if Snead was making calls, why didn’t the Rams make any trade at all?

The biggest reason is money. Before the deadline, Over The Cap had the Rams about $600,000 over the salary cap. The restructures of Kyren Williams and Kam Curl created about $3 million in space. That’s enough to keep the team running, but it’s not enough to take on a big contract without sending salary back.

The second reason is draft picks. The Rams already traded their 2027 first-round pick and their 2027 third-round pick. They still have a 2027 second, plus some later picks. But a receiver like McConkey would cost a lot more than that.

And the third reason is what McVay said himself earlier this week about trades: “It kind of takes two.” You can make all the calls you want. If the other team doesn’t want to deal, there’s no deal.

So the Rams made calls, listened, and decided to trust the players they already have. That’s the honest story.

Part four: who replaces Puka and Adams?

So the Rams didn’t trade for McConkey. They didn’t trade for anyone. That means the answer has to come from inside the building.

With Puka Nacua and Davante Adams both on IR, here’s who Stafford will be throwing to today.

Tutu Atwell. The veteran. Atwell has speed, and he knows this offense well. He’s probably the most experienced receiver available today.

Konata Mumpfield. A young receiver who’s earned McVay’s trust with smart route running and reliable hands.

CJ Daniels. The rookie sixth-round pick. Let’s be realistic. Nobody should expect Daniels to suddenly become the center of the passing game. Atwell and Mumpfield are probably ahead of him in targets. But McVay praised him on Friday, saying he “plays big at the catch point” and has “a mature presence.” This is the kind of game where a young player can start proving himself.

Brennan Presley and Xavier Smith. Presley just got promoted to the active roster, and Smith came back after the Rams signed him off the 49ers practice squad. Both could play some offense, but their biggest role might be on kick returns.

And then there are the tight ends. McVay said all four are expected to play: Tyler Higbee, Colby Parkinson, Davis Allen, and rookie Max Klare.

Here’s the thing. Nobody has to replace Puka by themselves. That’s impossible. Puka had 70 catches for 715 yards in seven games. No one on this roster is doing that.

What the Rams need is for everyone to do a little more. Atwell gets a deep shot. Mumpfield moves the chains on third down. Daniels makes a tough catch in traffic. Higbee catches five or six. A running back catches a screen for 20 yards. That’s how you replace a star when you can’t actually replace him.

Part five: Myles Garrett is expected to play

Now for some good news.

Myles Garrett is listed as questionable. Whenever a player like Garrett shows up on the injury report, fans get nervous. But here’s what McVay said.

Garrett did not suffer a new injury. He didn’t have a setback in practice. The Rams are just being careful with the knee issue he’s been managing. McVay said Garrett has been “feeling good” and “had a really good week.”

And when a reporter asked directly, “Do you expect Myles to play?” McVay said: “I do.”

So questionable doesn’t mean unlikely. It just means the Rams haven’t 100% committed yet.

Now, there’s one more factor. The bye week is next. If Garrett’s knee wasn’t responding well, the Rams could rest him today and give him almost three weeks off. But based on everything McVay said, that’s not the plan. I expect Garrett to play, with about the same workload he had against Washington.

And what a workload that was. Against the Commanders, Garrett had three sacks, three tackles for loss, and a strip-sack before halftime. And he said afterward that he felt “a little bit slow.”

Imagine what he does when he feels fast.

Part six: McDuffie and Curl

This is the part of the injury report that really worries me.

Trent McDuffie is questionable with a back injury that started in the Chargers game. He missed the Washington game. This week, he did some individual work, but his practice was limited. McVay said he was “chomping at the bit” to play, and that his back “felt a little sore” but he “was able to hit some really good numbers.”

The challenge for McDuffie isn’t running in a straight line. It’s the quick cuts and direction changes a cornerback makes on every play. That’s what stresses a back. So he’s a true game-time decision.

Kam Curl is questionable with a toe injury. There’s been less good news about Curl’s progress than about Garrett’s. McVay did say, “I feel good about Cam and Trent.” But there’s still some uncertainty.

So think about what that could mean. If both McDuffie and Curl can’t play, the Rams are missing two important starters in the secondary at the same time. And they’ve already lost Jaylen Watson for the season.

That means guys like Josh Wallace and Emmanuel Forbes would have to carry a big load again, like they did against Washington. Forbes made a great tackle for a loss in that game, and McVay mentioned it on Friday.

But here’s the key. If the secondary is banged up, the pass rush matters even more. A rookie quarterback can’t pick apart a secondary if he’s on his back.

Part seven: the plan against Carson Beck

Arizona is starting rookie quarterback Carson Beck. This is his first NFL start.

For the Rams defense, the goal is simple. Don’t let him get comfortable.

Don’t let him settle in the pocket. Don’t give him easy throws early to build confidence. Make him make quick decisions under pressure, all game long.

We saw against Washington what this front can do. Garrett was everywhere. And the Rams didn’t need to blitz on every play to get pressure. That’s huge. When you can get pressure with four rushers, you can drop seven into coverage, and that helps protect a banged-up secondary.

Here’s the matchup to watch. A rookie quarterback in his first start, behind an offensive line that has to deal with Myles Garrett. If the Rams get a sack or two early, Beck could start rushing his throws. And rushed throws turn into turnovers.

This is not a report, it’s my prediction: I think the Rams force at least two turnovers today.

Part eight: lean on the run game

Now let’s talk about the offense.

Without Puka and Adams, the passing game is going to look different. Stafford has spent his whole career making receivers around him better. But asking him to carry the entire offense with this receiver group would be asking too much.

That’s where Kyren Williams and Blake Corum come in.

The Rams have run the ball really well during this three-game winning streak. They had 192 rushing yards against the Raiders, 129 against the Chargers, and 124 against Washington. And Corum has been playing really well lately. There’s a strong case for giving him a big role today alongside Williams.

Ronnie Rivers could also get some carries if McVay wants to keep his backs fresh.

The idea isn’t to stop passing. It’s to run well enough that Stafford isn’t stuck in third-and-long all day. Because third-and-long against a blitzing defense, without your two best receivers and without your starting guard, is a really tough spot.

And a good run game opens up play-action. That’s when receivers like Atwell, Mumpfield, and Daniels can get open without having to beat a cornerback one-on-one. The linebackers bite on the fake, the safety steps up, and suddenly there’s space behind them.

But the Rams also have to be careful not to get too predictable. Arizona knows the Rams are missing their receivers. They’ll be expecting the run. So McVay has to mix it up and stay creative.

That’s what McVay does best. He adjusts to the players he has, not the players he wishes he had.

Part nine: the Puka IR decision and the math behind it

Now let’s talk about the decision that could matter well beyond today.

McVay explained on Friday why the Rams put Puka on injured reserve. He said, “We needed the roster spot.” And he said Puka was never going to return before then anyway. The move didn’t change his timeline. Puka was always expected to be out until around Week 15 or 16.

So the IR move was about opening a roster spot to promote Brennan Presley. McVay likes what Presley can do as a returner, and special teams has been a problem area for the Rams.

But here’s where it gets complicated.

NFL teams only get eight return designations per season. That’s the number of times you can bring a player back from injured reserve. And the Rams are running low.

Think about who’s on IR right now and could come back: Puka Nacua, Davante Adams, Kobie Turner, Jordan Whittington, and Terrance Ferguson. That’s five players who could all need a return designation. Add in any the Rams have already used this year, and they could be very close to using all eight.

So here’s the risk. If another important player gets hurt in December, and the Rams have no designations left, that player can’t come back this season, even if he heals in time for the playoffs.

That’s the real cost of putting Puka on IR. It wasn’t about Puka’s knee. It was about using up one of a very limited number of chances to bring players back.

Now, here’s the other side. If Presley gives the Rams a reliable returner, that could mean better field position every game. And when your offense is missing Puka and Adams, a shorter field helps a lot. Starting at your own 35 instead of your own 20 makes everything easier for Stafford.

So it’s a calculated risk. The Rams are solving a problem now and hoping they don’t pay for it later. Only time will tell if it was the right call.

This is not a report, it’s my take: I think it was the right call. You can’t play today’s games worrying about injuries that haven’t happened yet. And the return game needed help right now.

Part ten: special teams, finally getting help

Let’s stay on special teams for a minute, because it’s been an issue.

With Jordan Whittington on IR, the Rams lost their main kick returner. Whittington was averaging 26.3 yards per kickoff return, per Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak. That’s a big loss.

Against Washington, the Rams were so short on options that Kyren Williams was returning punts. That’s not ideal. You don’t want your starting running back taking extra hits on special teams.

Now the Rams have three options: Brennan Presley, Xavier Smith, and Ronnie Rivers. McVay said you could see “a combination of either one of those guys back with kick returns, as well as Ronnie Rivers. And that’s a positive for us.”

And McVay said about Presley: “He’s just one of those guys that makes the plays when his number’s called.”

Today, his number’s going to be called.

Part eleven: why this game matters so much

Let’s talk about the stakes.

The Rams are 5-4. They’ve won three straight games: the Raiders, the Chargers 24-9, and Washington 20-13. A win today gets them to 6-4 heading into the bye.

And look at what comes after the bye. Home against Green Bay. Home against Kansas City. Then at San Francisco on December 13. That’s a brutal stretch, and Puka will miss all three of those games.

So today is the game the Rams have to win. If they go into the bye at 6-4 with four straight wins, they have momentum and two weeks to get healthier. Adams is expected back “after Green Bay,” per McVay. Ferguson should be back around then too. Avila gets extra time.

But if they lose, they’re 5-5, and they have to face the Packers, Chiefs, and 49ers without their best receiver.

And there’s a revenge angle. Arizona beat the Rams 18-16 in October. That’s also the game where Davante Adams got hurt. The Rams haven’t forgotten that.

Plus, the NFC playoff race is tight. There isn’t much room for losses you could have avoided. And a loss to a team starting a rookie quarterback in his first game would be one of those.

Part twelve: what this game tells us about the offense

Here’s why I’m watching today’s game closely, beyond the win or loss.

This is the first full game without both Puka and Adams. Today we find out what this offense really looks like without its two best receivers.

If Atwell, Mumpfield, or Daniels make some big plays today, McVay will have a lot more confidence going into the Green Bay game. He’ll know he has options.

But if the passing game struggles, the Rams will have to lean even harder on the defense and the run game. That puts a lot of pressure on Kyren Williams and Blake Corum to carry the offense through December.

So today isn’t just about getting to 6-4. It’s a test. And the answers will shape how the Rams play the next month.

Five keys to winning today

Here are my five keys for the Rams in Arizona.

One: Protect Stafford. Without Avila, the line has to hold up against Arizona’s blitz. Quick throws, screens, and tight ends staying in to block.

Two: Run the ball. Kyren Williams and Blake Corum have to keep the offense on schedule. Avoid third-and-long.

Three: Get to Beck early. Make the rookie uncomfortable from the first drive. A sack or a hurried throw early can change his whole day.

Four: Win the field position battle. This is where Presley, Smith, and Rivers come in. One good return could set up an easy score.

Five: Somebody steps up at receiver. Atwell, Mumpfield, Daniels, or a tight end. Somebody has to make a big play when Stafford needs one.

If the Rams do four of these five, I think they win comfortably.
