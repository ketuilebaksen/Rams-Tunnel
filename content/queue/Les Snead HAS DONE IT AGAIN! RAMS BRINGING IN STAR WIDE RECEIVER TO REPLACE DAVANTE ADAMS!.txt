First, let’s make sure we’re all working from the same facts about Davante Adams, because a lot of videos are getting this part wrong.

Adams suffered a ruptured hamstring late in the Rams’ 18-16 loss to Arizona. Not 18-6 — 18-16. McVay said on Monday that Adams is expected to miss six to 10 weeks, and the Rams placed him on injured reserve on Tuesday, according to the team’s transactions page. According to the Rams’ official website, McVay also said the injury requires a “cleanup” procedure.

I’ve also seen a claim going around that Adams is aiming to return right after the Week 11 bye. I couldn’t find that reported anywhere I trust, so I’m not going to present it as news. But let’s do the math ourselves. Adams got hurt on October 18th. Six weeks from that date lands on November 29th. And that’s right around the Rams’ first game after their bye — the Packers. So if Adams heals on the fast end of McVay’s timeline, he could be back around that game. If it’s closer to 10 weeks, you’re looking at late December — and the Rams play in Seattle on Christmas Day.

Either way, the Rams will be without one of Matthew Stafford’s most important weapons for at least four more games before the bye: Las Vegas this Sunday, then the Chargers, then Washington on November 8th, and one more after that. And before he got hurt, Adams had 30 catches for 480 yards and four touchdowns in six games. That’s a lot of production to replace.

And here’s the number that really explains why everybody’s talking about a trade. According to Newsweek, apart from Puka Nacua and Adams, the Rams’ wide receivers have 13 combined catches this season. Thirteen. In six games. That’s how much this offense has relied on two players.

Now let’s talk about the report, because it’s real and it matters.

ESPN’s Dan Graziano wrote this week: “My understanding is that they will at least explore the possibility of trading for a wide receiver in the wake of the Davante Adams injury.” According to Rams On SI, Graziano also said the Rams “had the highest of hopes for 2026” and still believe they can save this season.

That’s the key point. The Rams are 2-4. According to Newsweek, they’re four games out of first place in the NFC West. A lot of teams in that spot would start thinking about next year. Graziano’s reporting says the Rams aren’t there yet. With Stafford still playing at a high level and McVay still on the sideline, the organization believes it can climb back.

And you can see why they believe it. The Rams have lost three games by a combined nine points: four at Denver, three to Buffalo in overtime, and two to Arizona. And for three straight weeks, Stafford has led a touchdown drive in the final two minutes: the go-ahead score in Philadelphia with 1:10 left, the game-tying touchdown against Buffalo with five seconds left, and the go-ahead touchdown against Arizona with 47 seconds left. This isn’t a team getting blown out every week. It’s a team losing close games, often because of its own mistakes. Stafford put it simply after the Arizona loss: “We are what our record is.” But the locker room’s message hasn’t changed. As safety Quentin Lake put it, there’s “no sense of panic,” but there is a “sense of urgency.” That’s exactly the kind of team that looks for one more piece instead of giving up.

But Graziano also pointed out the problem, and it’s a big one: draft picks. According to Rams Wire, via Yahoo Sports, the Rams have already traded their 2027 first-round pick, their 2028 second-round pick and their 2029 third-round pick in the Myles Garrett trade, and their 2027 third-round pick in the Trent McDuffie trade. So Les Snead doesn’t have the premium picks that usually get a deal like this done.

That’s why Graziano floated a different kind of idea. According to Rams Wire, he asked whether another team would be interested in backup quarterback Stetson Bennett, if the Rams decide rookie Ty Simpson is ready to be Stafford’s backup. And here’s an interesting detail: Graziano noted that Browns head coach Todd Monken coached Bennett in college at Georgia. We’ll come back to that in a minute, because it connects to one of the receivers on the list.

Now, the other side of the story: McVay.

According to the Rams’ official website, before Wednesday’s practice McVay was asked about adding a receiver from outside the building. He said: “That’s not something that we’ve talked about right now.” And he added: “We’ve got some depth on our roster. Maybe we talked about the practice squad, but not anything external.”

So does that mean Graziano’s report is wrong? Not necessarily. Here’s how I’d read it.

First, look at the words “right now.” It’s Wednesday of a game week. McVay’s job this week is to beat the Raiders. Head coaches almost never talk publicly about trade targets, especially three days before a game, and especially when the players they’d be replacing are sitting in the meeting room. Saying “we trust our guys” is exactly what a coach should say.

Second, the trade deadline isn’t until November 10th. That’s almost three weeks away. “Explore the possibility” — Graziano’s exact words — is not the same as “make a trade.” Exploring means making calls, checking prices and seeing who’s available. That job belongs to Les Snead and the front office, not the head coach on a Wednesday.

And third, the next two or three games will decide a lot. If the Rams beat the Raiders and the Chargers, they’re 4-4 and back in the race, and a small move makes a lot of sense. If they lose both, they’re 2-6, and giving up more future picks gets very hard to justify.

So the honest summary is this: the Rams are expected to look at the market. McVay isn’t talking about it publicly. And nothing has happened yet.

Let’s talk about who’s already in the building, because McVay specifically said he trusts the depth on his roster.

According to the Rams’ official website, behind Puka and Adams, the 53-man roster has Konata Mumpfield, Jordan Whittington, Tutu Atwell, Alex Bachman and C.J. Daniels Jr. Brennan Presley is on the practice squad — and that might be the “practice squad” option McVay was talking about.

And don’t sleep on Mumpfield. When Puka missed Weeks 2 and 3, Mumpfield and Whittington stepped up. In the Week 3 loss at Denver, Mumpfield had four catches for 93 yards and a go-ahead touchdown. Stafford said this week that Mumpfield “soaks it all up,” and called him “a really talented kid.” That’s high praise from a quarterback who doesn’t hand it out easily.

The Rams also have a deep group of tight ends. Colby Parkinson, who caught the go-ahead touchdown against Arizona, was a full participant in practice on Wednesday despite a shoulder issue. Tyler Higbee and Davis Allen give McVay more options. In this offense, the tight ends can absorb a lot of targets.

But let’s be honest: none of these players has done what Adams has done. That’s why the outside options keep coming up.

Michael Haney of Ramblin’ Fan wrote an article this week naming three receivers the Rams should target. These are his opinions, not reported talks, but they’re the three names everyone is discussing. Let’s go through all three — and then I’ll add a fourth that I think is getting overlooked.

Option number one: Tyreek Hill.

This is the name that gets everyone excited, and for good reason. At his best, Hill was one of the most dangerous players in the NFL. According to Heavy, he’s an eight-time Pro Bowler with Kansas City and Miami, and he had back-to-back seasons of more than 1,700 receiving yards in Miami. His best year was 2023: 119 catches, 1,799 yards and 13 touchdowns.

And here’s why Hill is different from every other name on this list: he’s a free agent. According to Heavy, Miami released him in February, and he hasn’t signed anywhere since. So the Rams wouldn’t have to give up a single draft pick. For a team that’s already traded away so much of its future, that’s a big deal. And think about the fit on paper: Puka winning underneath and over the middle, with a healthy Hill stretching the field over the top. Defenses couldn’t roll their coverage toward Puka without giving Hill room to run. That’s the dream scenario — if the speed is still there.

Bleacher Report’s Alex Kay named the Rams as the best fit for Hill, according to Athlon Sports. And his agent, Drew Rosenhaus, told ESPN that Hill is “continuing his rehab with the goal of being on a team in the immediate future.”

So why hasn’t anyone signed him? Because there are real questions.

The first question is his health. According to Athlon, Hill tore multiple ligaments in his knee early in the 2025 season. Heavy reports it was a dislocation with multiple torn ligaments. That’s a serious injury, and he hasn’t played in more than a year. Rosenhaus said: “I don’t know if he’s 100 percent today, but he’s close.” Close isn’t the same as ready.

The second question is age. Hill is 32. For a receiver whose game has always been built on elite speed, that matters. Before the injury in 2025, he had 21 catches for 265 yards in four games — solid, but his 2024 season was already down to 959 yards after those two huge years.

The third question is time. Even if Hill signed today, he’d need to pass a physical, get into football shape, and learn McVay’s offense. That could take weeks. By the time he’s ready, Adams might be close to coming back.

And there’s one more thing I have to mention to be complete. According to Heavy, a Miami jury recently found Hill liable in a civil case involving his estranged wife, and the NFL is reviewing the matter. No suspension has been announced. But any team signing Hill has to consider that the league’s review isn’t finished.

Rosenhaus also said back on October 14th that “nothing is imminent.” So right now, there’s no sign the Rams have even brought Hill in for a visit.

Haney called signing Hill “a pull of the slot machine,” and I think that’s fair. My verdict: if Hill is healthy, a workout costs nothing, and I’d at least take a look. But I wouldn’t count on him to save the season.

Option number two: Brian Thomas Jr.

We’ve covered him a lot on this channel, so I’ll keep it short. He’s the most talented option on the list. As a rookie in 2024, he had 1,282 yards and 10 touchdowns. Haney calls him the best direct replacement for Adams as an outside “X” receiver.

But the price is the problem. Haney used the Marvin Harrison Jr. trade between Arizona and Houston as a benchmark and estimated Thomas would cost about a second-round pick. And according to Tom Pelissero, as reported by Bleacher Report, the Jaguars have “shown no interest” in trading Thomas, even though calls keep coming in.

So you’d be paying a second-round price for a receiver whose team might not even sell. For a Rams team without its 2027 first or its 2028 second, I just don’t see it. And remember, Adams isn’t gone forever. When he comes back, how much would a second-round receiver even play? That’s a lot to give up for a player who might be the third option by December.

Option number three: Jerry Jeudy.

Haney called Jeudy the “middle ground” option, and I think that’s exactly right. He’s not as exciting as Hill or Thomas, but he’s the most realistic.

Quick refresher: Jeudy was the 15th overall pick in 2020 and made the Pro Bowl in 2024 with 1,229 receiving yards for Cleveland. This season, he has just 12 catches, mostly because the Browns drafted two rookie receivers. According to Pro Football Rumors, his 2026 base salary is only 1.5 million dollars, which the Rams could actually afford. And the reported asking price is in the Day 3 range. Haney estimated a Day 3 pick. NFL insider Jordan Schultz said Cleveland wants something in the “early third to fourth range,” according to Bleacher Report. An executive told The Exhibit’s Josina Anderson a fourth or fifth could be enough, according to Pro Football Rumors.

Now here’s where the Stetson Bennett idea gets interesting. Remember, Graziano pointed out that Browns head coach Todd Monken coached Bennett at Georgia. Monken was the offensive coordinator when Bennett won two national championships there. Graziano didn’t connect the dots any further than that. But you can see why people are talking.

So let me be very clear: what I’m about to say is my own idea. This is not a report. Could a deal built around Stetson Bennett and Jerry Jeudy make sense for both teams? Cleveland gets a quarterback its head coach already knows and trusts. The Rams get a receiver without giving up another valuable draft pick. On paper, it’s interesting.

But I’d be careful. Stafford is 38, and quarterback depth matters a lot. Ty Simpson is a rookie who’s been the inactive third quarterback. If you trade Bennett, Simpson becomes the backup immediately. And if Stafford misses even one game, the season could depend on a rookie making his first start. That’s a big risk to take for a receiver who’d be the second option for six weeks.

And Bennett does more for this team than people realize. Remember the Arizona game? When the Rams had no kicker, it was Bennett who held for punter Ethan Evans on the go-ahead extra point with 47 seconds left. He’s been with the Rams since they drafted him in the fourth round in 2023, he knows McVay’s offense inside and out, and he gives the coaching staff a quarterback they trust in an emergency. Trading him wouldn’t just move a backup. It would remove a safety net in a season where the Rams have already lost Adams, Garrett, Ford, Turner and two kickers to injuries or roster moves. Right now, I’d keep Bennett — unless the Browns throw in something more than Jeudy.

There’s also the question of whether Cleveland even wants to sell. The Browns are 4-2 and in first place in the AFC North, and Jeudy made the biggest catch of their win over Baltimore. Teams in first place don’t usually move veterans in October.

And now, option number four — the name I think is getting overlooked: Josh Downs of the Indianapolis Colts.

According to Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak, Downs had more than 750 yards in each of his first two seasons and 800 yards with five touchdowns in 2024. He’s in the final year of his rookie deal, and his role has been limited this season. Grisak suggested the Colts could move him for a fourth- or fifth-round pick.

Here’s why he’s interesting for a McVay offense. Downs is 5-foot-9 and about 170 pounds, but according to Grisak, in 2025 he had a 75.3 percent success rate against man coverage and an 82.4 percent success rate against press. He was fourth in the NFL in first downs per route on third and fourth down. And Reception Perception’s Matt Harmon wrote that his route profile “shows a player who can win at all three levels.”

Third-down production is exactly what the Rams have been missing. Remember the false start that turned fourth-and-3 into fourth-and-8 against Arizona? This offense needs reliable separators on money downs. Downs fits that description.

The concern is size. Downs plays mostly in the slot, and the Rams’ biggest need without Adams is an outside receiver. So he’s not a perfect fit. But for a Day 3 pick, he could be a valuable piece.

And if you’re wondering whether Les Snead would really make a move in the middle of a tough season, just look at his history. In 2019, he traded two first-round picks and a fourth for Jalen Ramsey in the middle of the season. In 2021, he sent a second- and a third-round pick to Denver for Von Miller right at the deadline — and Miller helped the Rams win the Super Bowl a few months later. And this year alone, he’s already traded for Myles Garrett and Trent McDuffie. Snead has never been afraid to use draft picks. The difference this time is that he has fewer of them left to use. That’s why the realistic options are all in the Day 3 range — or free agents like Hill who cost nothing but money.

So how would I rank these four options? Let me give it to you straight.

Number one: Jerry Jeudy. He’s the best mix of talent, low price and low salary. I’d make the call at a fourth- or fifth-round pick. I’d think much harder before giving up Bennett.

Number two: Josh Downs. A smart, cheap option who wins on third down. Not a perfect fit as an outside receiver, but good value.

Number three: Tyreek Hill. No draft picks required, which is a huge plus. But between the knee, his age, the time it would take to get him ready, and the league’s open review, he’s the biggest gamble on the list. I’d bring him in for a workout, but I wouldn’t promise him a starting role.

Number four: Brian Thomas Jr. The most talented player, but the price is too high, and Jacksonville doesn’t seem to want to sell.

And honestly, there’s a fifth option: stand pat. If Mumpfield, Whittington and the tight ends play well against the Raiders and the Chargers, maybe the Rams don’t need to spend anything. McVay clearly believes in his young receivers. The next two games are an audition.

Now let’s get to Sunday, because none of this matters if the Rams lose in Las Vegas.

According to Yahoo Sports, the Raiders started 3-0 and have now lost three straight, including a 33-30 loss to Buffalo last week, where Kirk Cousins threw for 313 yards and two touchdowns. They’re 3-3 under first-year head coach Klint Kubiak.

Here’s what the Wednesday injury reports look like, according to the Raiders’ official website. For Las Vegas, star tight end Brock Bowers was limited with hamstring and calf issues, and left tackle Kolton Miller was limited with a shoulder injury. Defensive tackle Tonka Hemingway didn’t practice because of a concussion. And receiver Dont’e Thornton Jr. returned to practice from injured reserve. Bowers’ status is the big one to watch. If he’s limited on Sunday, Cousins loses his favorite target.

For the Rams, Trent McDuffie did not practice on Wednesday because of a groin injury, and linebacker Nate Landman did not practice because he’s in the concussion protocol. Parkinson and cornerback Josh Wallace were full participants. And according to Graziano, via Newsweek, Myles Garrett is expected to miss another two or three weeks. That’s a lot of defense missing — Garrett, Kobie Turner, Poona Ford, possibly McDuffie and Landman.

And of course, Sunday is Brandon McManus’ first game as a Ram. After two weeks of kicking chaos, the Rams need a quiet, steady performance from him — inside a dome with a fixed roof, which should help.

Here are my three keys for Sunday.

Key number one: get the young receivers involved early. If Mumpfield, Whittington or Atwell catches a couple of passes on the first two drives, it opens up everything for Puka. It’s also the best way to prove McVay right about the depth he trusts.

And it matters for Puka’s health, too. Puka was targeted 24 times against Buffalo, and he’s already dealt with a hip and groin injury this season. If the Rams throw to him 15 or 20 times every week with Adams out, they risk wearing down their best player before the stretch run. Spreading the ball around isn’t just good strategy. It’s how the Rams keep Puka healthy for December.

Key number two: slow down Brock Bowers if he plays. With the Rams’ linebackers banged up, Bowers could be a nightmare matchup. The safeties have to help.

Key number three: no self-inflicted wounds. McVay said it himself after the Arizona loss: “We won’t win a game unless we figure out how not to beat ourselves.” No false starts on fourth down. No fumbles in plus territory. No late penalties that hand the other team field position.

My prediction stays the same: Rams 27, Raiders 20. And I’ll add one more detail — I think Mumpfield has his best game since that Denver game. If that happens, the trade conversation gets a lot quieter.

So here’s my bottom line.

No, the Rams are not “bringing in” a star wide receiver — at least, not yet. What’s real is that ESPN’s Dan Graziano reported the Rams will explore the trade market for a receiver, and McVay said on Wednesday that adding someone from the outside isn’t something they’ve talked about right now. Both things can be true. The coach is focused on the Raiders. The front office has until November 10th to make calls.

If the Rams do make a move, I don’t think it’ll be a superstar. It’ll be a smart, affordable one — someone like Jerry Jeudy or Josh Downs at a Day 3 price, or a low-risk look at Tyreek Hill if he’s truly healthy. And if the young receivers step up in the next two games, the best move might be no move at all.
