The two of them sat down, talked about life in LA, Garrett’s trip to Japan, a Family Feud appearance, and then played a game called “QB Carousel,” where they had to guess quarterbacks from the list of teams they played for.

So the only “decision” Stafford made in this video was what name to write on his board. And spoiler: he didn’t always get it right.

But here’s why this video is still worth your time. It shows you the relationship between the Rams’ quarterback and the Rams’ best pass rusher. It tells you a lot about how Garrett thinks about quarterbacks. And it gives us some great stories about Joe Burrow, Aaron Rodgers, Jameis Winston, Nick Foles, and Josh McCown.

Part one: what this video actually is

Let’s set the scene first, because a lot of people will see that headline and think something big happened.

“QB One-on-One” is a show where Stafford hosts a guest and they talk football and life. It’s casual. It’s funny. Nobody is announcing anything.

Stafford opened the show by introducing Garrett, and the introduction tells you how much respect Stafford has for him. He called Garrett a “two-time Defensive Player of the Year, five-time first-team All-Pro, seven-time Pro Bowler, a damn good photographer, and one of our newest Rams.” Then he added: “Unbelievable player.”

That photography line is real, by the way. Garrett is known around the league for his photography, and Stafford made sure to give him credit for it.

And the timing of this show is worth noting. Based on the conversation, it looks like it was recorded earlier in Garrett’s time with the Rams, when he was still settling in. So don’t take anything here as news from this week. It’s a fun look at two teammates getting to know each other.

Part two: Garrett on life in LA

Stafford’s first question was simple: how has LA been treating you?

Garrett said: “Pretty good. Treating me kindly.”

Stafford asked if he’d spent much time in LA before the trade. Garrett said, “Maybe a week or two at a time.” So he wasn’t totally new to the city, but living here full time is different. He mentioned the Valley being a little newer for him.

This matters more than you’d think. When a star player gets traded, the adjustment isn’t only about the playbook. It’s a new city, a new home, new routines, new people. Garrett spent his whole career in Cleveland before this. Coming to Los Angeles is a big change.

And from everything we’ve seen on the field, he’s handling it. Three sacks, three tackles for loss, and a strip-sack against Washington last week. That’s not a guy who’s struggling to settle in.

Part three: a month and a half in Japan

This was one of the best parts of the show.

Stafford asked if Garrett did any traveling in the offseason. Garrett said: “Went to Japan.” Then: “Went to Korea.”

Stafford got excited: “I really want to go to Japan. Would you recommend?” And Garrett said: “Absolutely. It’s a dream.”

Then the big detail. Garrett said he goes on long trips, and he was in Japan for “almost a month and a half.” Stafford’s reaction was basically: “Oh my god.”

And of course, Stafford asked the football question. Where did you work out?

Garrett said he trained there. And when Stafford asked if he was “the biggest giant in the gym,” Garrett said: “Biggest giant in the gym. Every gym.”

I believe it. Myles Garrett is six-foot-four and around 270 pounds. In a gym in Tokyo, he must have looked like a superhero who walked off a movie set.

But here’s the part that tells you who Garrett really is. He said he scouts gyms before he travels. In his words: “It’s kind of like scout gyms before I go, and it’s like I kind of build my trip and I’m timing everything off of that.”

Think about that. He’s on vacation in Japan for six weeks, and he plans the whole trip around where he can train. That’s the kind of discipline that gets you two Defensive Player of the Year awards.

Part four: Garrett on Family Feud

Here’s a fun fact I did not know.

Stafford asked Garrett if he’d ever been on a game show. Garrett said: “Family Feud, back in the day.”

It was a rookies versus veterans episode. Stafford asked who else was on his team, and Garrett said he thought Leonard Fournette was with him, but he couldn’t remember the rest.

Stafford said his wife was “really jealous,” because she really wants to go on that show.

And think about how that would go. Garrett on a buzzer is probably as fast as Garrett off the edge. Rookies versus vets is a classic, too, because the vets always think they know everything, and the rookies always hit the buzzer too early.

So now I have a new dream. A Rams Family Feud episode. Stafford, Garrett, McVay, Puka, and Kyren against the 49ers. Somebody make it happen.

Part five: the QB Carousel game

Now we get to the main event.

The game was called “QB Carousel.” Here’s how it worked. They’d see a list of teams, both college and NFL, that a quarterback played for. Then they’d each write down who they thought it was, and reveal on three.

Stafford set it up with a great line: “It’s only right. I’m a quarterback. Myles sacks quarterbacks all the time. Luckily, I’m not in his graveyard of quarterbacks and hopefully never will be. That’s why I have the red jersey on at practice.”

That’s a great joke, and it’s also true. In practice, quarterbacks wear red jerseys so defenders know not to hit them. And with Garrett on your team, that red jersey is the most important piece of equipment Stafford owns.

Stafford also admitted something before the game started: “This is where I get exposed. I’m like a terrible speller.”

So let’s go through each quarterback.

Part six: Joe Burrow and the record

The first clue was easy. Ohio State, LSU, Bengals.

Stafford called it “an easy win, layup to start.” They both wrote down Joe Burrow.

And then Garrett said two words that tell a big story: “Number 23.”

Here’s what he meant. Garrett said Burrow was his first sack of the year and his last sack of the year. And that last one was number 23. Back in 2025, Garrett finished the season with 23 sacks, which set the NFL’s single-season record. And the sack that got him there came against Burrow.

Garrett joked: “So he really helped me out. Way to go, Joe.” Stafford added: “We thank you for your service, Joe.”

But the best part was what happened after. Garrett said that after the sack, Burrow wasn’t really in a talkative mood on the field. But later, he reached out to Garrett. And Garrett described the message as something like: it’s an honor to play against you, but why does it always have to be me?

That’s a great moment. Burrow is competitive, but he’s also respectful. He knew Garrett was breaking a record, and he still reached out. Even if he wasn’t happy about being the guy on the wrong end of it.

Stafford also had his own Burrow stories. They did joint media around the Super Bowl, the one the Rams won against the Bengals, and he talked about Zoom calls with him. Stafford said: “He’s a great dude. Obviously a heck of a football player.” And he said he’s run into Burrow in LA during the offseason a few times, adding that he has “a ton of respect for his game and who he is as a player.”

Remember, Stafford beat Burrow in that Super Bowl. And he’s still nothing but respectful about him. That’s Stafford.

Part seven: Aaron Rodgers

The next one was another easy one. They both got it: Aaron Rodgers.

Stafford asked Garrett how many times he’d sacked Rodgers. Garrett’s answer: “None.”

He explained that every time they played each other, Rodgers seemed to be at the top of his game. And Garrett said: “It’s been a pain going against him, but it’s awesome to see him back there and the presence he has with the offense. Just how he navigates, how he plays the game. It’s a joy to see.”

That’s a big compliment from a guy who’s sacked basically every quarterback in the league.

Then Stafford gave his own view, and this one is personal. Stafford played against Rodgers twice a year for 12 years in Detroit, when they were both in the NFC North.

Stafford said: “He is a unique talent. One of one kind of guy.” He added: “A ton of memories playing against Aaron. Unfortunately, not many of them ended up the way I wanted them to end up.”

He talked about those Green Bay teams being great, while the Lions were “trying to find our way in Detroit.” But he finished with respect: “Always been really cool to me. And the way he plays the game, he’s unique. Kind of unlike anybody else in our game, I think.”

That’s a classy answer. Stafford lost a lot of games to Rodgers. And instead of being bitter about it, he praised him.

Part eight: Jameis Winston

The third quarterback was Jameis Winston.

One of them said he was a Florida State fan growing up and had watched a lot of Jameis, and that he’d “played against him a bunch of times.”

Then they got to the fun part: Jameis on the mic.

If you know Jameis, you know he gives some of the most passionate speeches in football. Locker room speeches, pregame speeches, postgame speeches. He’s famous for it.

And Garrett actually played with Jameis in Cleveland. He said Jameis “did something similar when he was with us,” and Garrett joked that he told him, basically, we’re not going to get clipped doing this too, so you need to “tone it down” and “switch up the vibe.”

But then Garrett gave Jameis real respect. He said: “He really means it. He’s intentional. He’s authentic to himself.” And: “He’s not trying to be goofy or funny. He just speaks from the heart, and I appreciate it. We all did.”

Stafford added: “Gunslinger, for sure.” Then they mentioned the season where Jameis had 30 touchdowns and 30 interceptions. That was 2019 in Tampa Bay. Stafford called it “30 for 30. Legendary stuff right there.”

I love this part, because it shows how players really see each other. From the outside, fans laugh at Jameis’s speeches. But the guys in the locker room with him? They respect that he means every word.

Part nine: Nick Foles

This is where the game got hard.

The clue had a long list of teams, and Stafford noticed one detail right away. He said: “Wait, this guy transferred before the portal was a thing.”

Then Garrett pointed out the player “did two stints with the Eagles.” Stafford was going through Eagles quarterbacks in his head and couldn’t picture one.

And this is where Stafford said one of the funniest lines of the show. He admitted that when they said “retired,” his mind went way back. He said: “I was like, did Randall Cunningham go to all these? I was just going way back.” Then: “It’s a terrible idea by me.”

The answer was Nick Foles.

Garrett said he only knew two of Foles’ teams, the Eagles and the Jaguars. And Stafford gave Foles his credit: “That’s quite the journey right there. Super Bowl winner.”

They both mentioned the Philly Special, the famous trick play in the Super Bowl where Foles caught a touchdown pass. Stafford called him “a legend in that town, I would imagine.”

And yes, Foles’ journey was a long one. He was drafted by the Eagles, then went to the Rams, by the way, then the Chiefs, then back to the Eagles, where he won the Super Bowl. Then the Jaguars, the Bears, and the Colts. That’s a lot of moving for a Super Bowl MVP.

Fun fact for Rams House: Foles played for the Rams back in 2015, when they were still in St. Louis. So this one should have been a little easier for a Rams quarterback.

Part ten: Josh McCown

The last quarterback in this part of the show was the hardest one.

The clue started with SMU and Sam Houston. Then a long list of NFL teams. Plus the Hartford Colonials of the old UFL.

Stafford asked a great question: were they talking about the Houston Texans or the old Houston Oilers? It was the Texans.

The first guess was Josh Johnson. And honestly, that’s a smart guess. Josh Johnson has played for so many teams it’s become a running joke in the NFL. But the answer was no. As one of them said, Johnson had played for “too many” teams. Even more than this list.

Then the next clue: the player is retired, but “still in the league as a coach.”

And then came the funniest moment. Stafford realized: “Oh, I have history with this person.” He asked if they were on the Lions at the same time, and the answer was no.

The answer was Josh McCown.

So what’s the history? McCown played for the Lions in 2006, a few years before Stafford was drafted there in 2009. So they were never teammates in Detroit. But McCown was part of that franchise’s history.

And McCown also played in Cleveland in 2015 and 2016, a little before Garrett arrived in 2017. So both Stafford and Garrett have teams in common with McCown, even though they never played with him.

McCown played for a lot of NFL teams over almost two decades. He’s one of the great journeymen in NFL history. And now he’s coaching in the league, which makes sense. Guys who’ve been in that many quarterback rooms usually have a lot to teach.

And the clip ends with Stafford basically being told to apologize to the camera for forgetting him. That’s a good look for a 38-year-old quarterback who can’t remember everyone he’s crossed paths with.

The graveyard of quarterbacks, and today’s guest

Let’s go back to Stafford’s joke for a minute, because it fits today perfectly.

Stafford said he’s glad he’s not in Garrett’s “graveyard of quarterbacks.” Think about who’s in that graveyard. Burrow, the first sack and the record sack. Plenty of others over nine seasons in Cleveland. And now, with the Rams, every quarterback in the NFC West gets a turn.

Today, it’s Carson Beck’s turn. Beck played his college football at Georgia and then Miami, and today is his first NFL start. He’s never seen an NFL pass rush in a real game as the starter. And the first one he sees includes the guy who holds the single-season sack record.

That’s a tough way to start a career.

Now, rookies can surprise you. Some quarterbacks play loose in their first start because nobody expects anything. Arizona will probably try to help Beck with quick throws and a lot of running plays, to keep Garrett from teeing off.

But here’s the thing about Garrett. He told Stafford he scouts gyms before he even takes a vacation. Imagine how hard he’s been scouting Carson Beck this week.

This is not a report, it’s my prediction: Garrett gets to Beck at least once, and the pressure forces at least one bad throw.

Part eleven: what this tells us about Stafford and Garrett

Okay, this was a fun video. But let me tell you why I think it matters for the Rams.

First, the chemistry is real. You can’t fake the way these two joke with each other. Stafford teasing about being in Garrett’s “graveyard of quarterbacks.” Garrett calling himself “the biggest giant in the gym.” Stafford admitting he’s a terrible speller. These two are comfortable with each other.

And that matters on a team. When your franchise quarterback and your best defender are close, it sets the tone for the whole locker room. Remember what McVay said on Friday about the shadow boxing league: “When you’re hearing laughs, I think that’s a good sign of a good culture.” This video is more proof of that.

Second, Garrett studies quarterbacks. Listen to how he talked about Rodgers. He didn’t just say “great player.” He talked about how Rodgers “navigates” and the “presence he has with the offense.” That’s a pass rusher who watches how quarterbacks move, how they feel pressure, and how they get rid of the ball.

That’s bad news for Carson Beck today. Garrett has spent his whole career studying the best quarterbacks in the world. Now he gets a rookie in his first NFL start.

Third, Garrett respects his opponents. Burrow, Rodgers, Jameis. He talked about every one of them with real respect. That’s not a guy who’s going to take Beck lightly. He’s going to prepare for him like he’s facing Rodgers.

Fourth, Stafford is a student of the game too. He remembered details about every quarterback. He has deep respect for the guys he’s played against. And he showed it again here.

Part twelve: Garrett’s record and what he’s doing now

Let’s talk about Garrett’s sack record for a second, because it’s a big part of why he’s a Ram.

In 2025, Garrett finished with 23 sacks. That broke the single-season record. And the final one came against Joe Burrow, the same guy who he says was also his first sack that year.

So when the Rams traded for Garrett, they got the best pass rusher in football, coming off the best sack season ever.

And here’s what’s impressive. Garrett has dealt with a knee issue this season, and he’s said himself he’s not all the way back. After the Washington game, he said: “I felt like I was a little bit slow, and it picked up in the second half.” And: “You can only get in football shape by playing football and being on the field.”

And still, he had three sacks in that game.

McVay said on Friday that Garrett is questionable for today’s game, but when asked directly if he expects Garrett to play, McVay said: “I do.” He said Garrett “had a really good week” and the Rams are “being smart with him.”

So today, Garrett should be on the field in Arizona. And if he’s getting closer to 100%, the Cardinals are in trouble.

Part thirteen: Stafford’s week

While Garrett is getting healthier, Stafford is dealing with a tough situation.

Puka Nacua is on IR with his knee injury. Davante Adams is on IR. Terrance Ferguson is out. Steve Avila, a starting guard, is out with a hamstring injury.

That means Stafford’s receivers today are Tutu Atwell, Konata Mumpfield, CJ Daniels, Brennan Presley, and Xavier Smith, plus his four tight ends.

McVay said on Friday that Stafford’s “steadiness is the key thing” in a week like this. He said Stafford is “truly an extension of the coaching staff,” and that “there’s just a confidence that he breathes because of how calm he is, and that rubs off on everybody else in the huddle.”

And you can see that calm in this video too. Stafford is relaxed, funny, and comfortable. That’s who he is, on a podcast and in a two-minute drill.
