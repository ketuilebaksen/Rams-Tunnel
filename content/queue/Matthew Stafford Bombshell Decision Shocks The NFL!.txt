Let’s start with the real news: Stafford’s arm.

According to RotoWire, after Sunday’s 24-9 win over the Chargers, Stafford left the locker room with ice on his right arm. According to Heavy, LA Daily News reporter Adam Grosbard said Stafford was seen with a bag of ice wrapped around his right elbow. That’s his throwing arm.

On Monday, McVay was asked about it. According to Pro Football Talk, McVay said: “He’s had some soreness there.” But he didn’t want to go into detail. He said: “You’ll have to talk to him about that.” He added: “I think he’d be better equipped once you guys talk to him to answer that.” And he said: “I can’t attest to stuff that I’m not necessarily aware of.”

So here’s what we know: Stafford has some soreness in his throwing arm. Here’s what we don’t know: what the specific injury is, how serious it is, or whether it will affect him this week. Pro Football Talk said the arm issue isn’t clearly identified, and Heavy said it hasn’t seen a diagnosis, a severity level or a timeline.

According to RotoWire, the Rams’ first injury report of the week comes out on Wednesday. That will tell us whether the arm affects Stafford’s practice time. And Stafford usually speaks to reporters on Wednesday, so we may hear directly from him then.

Now, I want to be careful here. Ice after a game is very common for a 38-year-old quarterback, and a little soreness doesn’t mean something is wrong. According to Pro Football Talk, Stafford has a long history of playing through injuries, and the site said ice on the arm won’t keep him from playing. Stafford is also the 2025 MVP, and he’s been one of the toughest quarterbacks in the league for years.

But it’s also fair to keep an eye on it. According to Heavy, Stafford had a back injury before last season, and a neck injury cut his 2022 season short. He’s 38. And according to Pro Football Talk, he only threw 25 passes against the Chargers. That’s partly because the Rams leaned on the run game — but it’s worth watching whether that continues.

And there’s some history here that longtime Rams fans will remember. From my own memory, Stafford dealt with elbow trouble in his throwing arm during the 2022 offseason and needed treatment on it before that season — the same season that later ended early because of the neck injury Heavy mentioned. I’m sharing that from my own recollection, not from this week’s reporting, and nobody has said this week’s soreness is related. But it’s one reason fans get nervous when they see ice on that elbow.

On the other hand, look at what Stafford has done since. He won the 2025 MVP. And this season, before Week 8, he had 1,989 passing yards, 14 touchdowns and seven interceptions, according to NFL.com. Just a little over a week ago, in the win over the Raiders, he became the Rams’ all-time leader in touchdown passes, according to the LA Times, and the first player in NFL history to lead two different franchises in career touchdown passes. This is not a quarterback who sits out because of a little soreness.

So what happens if the arm limits him a bit this week? The Rams actually have a plan for that already. Over the last two games, they’ve built their offense around the run. Against the Raiders, according to the AP, they ran 38 times and threw just 29 passes. Against the Chargers, according to Pro Football Talk, they ran for 129 yards in the second half alone. And according to Athlon Sports, they used three tight ends heavily, with Tyler Higbee, Davis Allen and Max Klare all getting big snap counts. An offense built on Kyren Williams, Blake Corum and tight ends asks less of the quarterback’s arm. That’s my own read, but it’s a reason not to panic.

Now, let’s connect this to something else we’ve seen. According to the Rams’ official website, before the Chargers game, Stafford had averaged under five air yards per attempt in his previous two games — something he hadn’t done in his prior 23 outings. And against the Chargers, according to Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak, Stafford’s QBR was 35.7 and Pro Football Focus gave him a 63.8 grade.

Is the arm the reason for the short passing? I want to be very clear: nobody has said that. There’s no report connecting the soreness to the shorter throws. The more obvious explanation is that the Rams are missing Davante Adams and Terrance Ferguson and are leaning on the running game and three tight ends. I’m only pointing it out because it’s the kind of thing Rams fans will be asking about. Until Stafford or McVay says more, it’s just a question — not a conclusion.

And just so everyone knows the backup situation: according to Pro Football Rumors, the Rams named Stetson Bennett their No. 2 quarterback after the preseason, with rookie Ty Simpson in a developmental role. I’m not saying Bennett is going to play. I’m just giving you the facts so nobody panics if they see his name this week.

Now, here’s what Stafford actually said after the game, because it ties into the trade deadline.

According to Heavy, Stafford talked about building chemistry with his pass-catchers. He said: “Obviously, I have a ton of reps with Puka.” He said the offense is “just trying to spread the ball around” and “keep everybody involved.” And he said: “It’s something that’ll continuously build, but you know, excited to move forward.”

That sounds simple, but it matters. According to Grisak, Puka Nacua caught 11 of his 12 targets against the Chargers, and the rest of the Rams’ receivers combined for two catches on six targets. So when Stafford says he has “a ton of reps with Puka,” that’s the reality: Puka is the one receiver Stafford fully trusts right now.

And that’s why some Rams writers are calling for a trade. According to Heavy, The Athletic’s Nate Atkins wrote on November 1st: “It’s why they have to consider a trade for a wide receiver Stafford can trust.” Grisak said on X that the Rams need another weapon before the deadline. And according to Heavy, USA Today’s Jacob Camenker argued the Rams should go after Chargers receiver Quentin Johnston, who averaged 53 catches, 723 yards and eight touchdowns across 2024 and 2025.

But McVay has been consistent. According to Heavy, McVay has said the Rams’ “deadline acquisitions” are essentially Davante Adams and other injured players coming back. And this week, according to the Rams’ official website, McVay announced that Myles Garrett will return to practice, and it’s “more likely than not” that Terrance Ferguson’s practice window opens too.

So you have two sides. Writers like Atkins and Grisak want a receiver. McVay is pointing to the players coming back. The deadline is Tuesday, November 10th, at 4 p.m. Eastern. We’ll find out soon who wins that argument.

And here’s what CBS Sports thinks the Rams’ biggest need is. In an October 30th article listing one position of need for every team, CBS’s Josh Edwards picked wide receiver for the Rams. He wrote that “Davante Adams is going to miss an extended period of time, and Puka Nacua has been dealing with his own injuries.” He listed Josh Downs, Jerry Jeudy, Brian Thomas Jr., Calvin Ridley, Jauan Jennings and Quentin Johnston as possible options. Notice who’s not on that list: any cornerback, and definitely not Amik Robertson.

And while we’re on Johnston, here’s some context from ESPN. According to ESPN’s trade rankings, Johnston was ranked 20th with a 25 percent chance of being traded, and ESPN listed the 49ers, Raiders and Steelers as fits. ESPN noted he has only eight catches this season and 11 career drops, though he went over 700 yards in both 2024 and 2025. And according to Breer’s reporting last week, the Chargers had been turning down calls on players like Johnston. So he’s a long shot, too.

As for George Pickens, according to Heavy, CBS Sports’ John Breech pointed out that the Rams would need roughly 18 million dollars in cap space to fit Pickens’ prorated salary. The Rams have about 206,000. That one isn’t happening.

Now let’s talk about the fake clip, because this one has a twist I haven’t seen before.

Here’s what the clip claims. It says that on Monday, November 2nd, Adam Schefter went on First Take and said: “Amik Robertson and the Los Angeles Rams.” It says Schefter claimed ClutchPoints listed Robertson as an option for teams needing help, that Washington is a seller, that the price is a sixth-round pick, and — of course — “this trade is going to happen.”

Fake: “I can confirm the Rams are making this call.” I couldn’t find it on ESPN, on Schefter’s X account or anywhere in NFL coverage.

Fake: “A sixth-round pick” and “this trade is going to happen.” No trusted reporter has said that.

Wrong: the clip calls Robertson a “rotation quarterback” — twice. He’s a cornerback. That’s the second straight clip that called a cornerback a quarterback. When the same mistake shows up in different clips, it’s a sign they’re coming from the same kind of template.

And here’s the twist I mentioned. The Rams play the Commanders this Sunday. So the clip is claiming the Rams are about to trade with the exact team they’re playing in six days. That’s not against the rules, but it would be very unusual. And notice that the clip even admits Washington is only a “seller candidate,” not a declared seller.

Now, what’s real?

Real: Amik Robertson is a trade candidate. According to Athlon Sports, via Yahoo, Robertson is 28 years old and has played well this season. Pro Football Focus has him with a 69.8 overall grade, 19th out of 102 graded cornerbacks, and he leads all cornerbacks with eight pass breakups. According to Athlon, he’s allowed 22 catches and a 92.9 passer rating in coverage. Athlon described him as “a capable man-coverage corner who can play inside and outside.”

Real: his contract makes him movable. According to Athlon, his deal has no guaranteed money in 2027, and trading him would leave Washington with about 3 million dollars in dead money. Athlon suggested a mid-round pick could be a possible return.

Real: the teams linked to him. According to Athlon, The Athletic’s Daniel Popper listed the Bills, Lions, Bengals, Jaguars and Steelers as fits for Robertson. Detroit is his former team. The Rams were not on that list.

And real: Washington’s situation is complicated. According to an October 27th report on Yahoo Sports, the Commanders were looking like sellers on veterans like Daron Payne, Amik Robertson and Stefon Diggs. But the same report said Washington was also linked as a possible buyer for Jaguars receiver Brian Thomas Jr. because of an injury to Terry McLaurin. And according to RotoWire, the Commanders beat the Eagles 27-10 last Sunday to improve to 2-4-1. A win like that can make a team think twice about selling.

And here’s the detail that really breaks the clip’s story. In that same October 30th CBS Sports article, Josh Edwards listed the Commanders’ biggest need as — cornerback. He wrote: “Cornerback has been a problem all season for the Commanders.” So think about that. The clip says Washington is going to sell one of its cornerbacks. But according to CBS, Washington’s biggest need is more cornerbacks. A team that’s short at cornerback usually doesn’t trade away a cornerback who leads the league in pass breakups.

And CBS also listed Robertson as a possible option for a bunch of other teams that need cornerback help — the Ravens, Steelers, Titans, Bears, Packers, Lions, Dolphins and Panthers. The Rams weren’t on that list either, because CBS had the Rams’ need as wide receiver. So between ESPN, The Athletic and CBS, I couldn’t find a single real outlet connecting Robertson to the Rams.

And one more Washington note. According to Yahoo Sports, defensive tackle Daron Payne was leading Washington’s possible sell list, and the reported suitors included the Bears, 49ers, Lions and Jaguars. If the 49ers add Payne after already trading for DeForest Buckner, that’s another move by the team leading the Rams’ division. That’s a more important Washington trade story for Rams fans than any fake Robertson clip.

So the honest summary is: Robertson is a real trade candidate, he’s playing well, and he’s cheap. But nobody has connected him to the Rams, the Rams aren’t on the list of fits, and the clip asks you to believe the Rams will trade with their next opponent because Schefter “confirmed” it. He didn’t.

By my count, this is the ninth fake “Schefter on First Take” clip involving the Rams this month — after Jerry Jeudy, two Vita Vea clips, George Pickens, Khalil Mack, Josh Downs, A’Shawn Robinson and Donte Jackson. Every one of them ends with “this trade is going to happen.” None of them has happened.

Here’s a quick way to spot these. First, check whether the headline matches the clip — this one promised a Stafford decision and delivered a cornerback rumor. Second, check the position — two clips in a row called a cornerback a quarterback. Third, check whether the team is even on the real list of fits. And fourth, search for the quote. If Schefter said it, it would be everywhere.

If you’ve learned to spot these clips by now, drop “fake clip nine” in the comments.

And why do these clips keep coming? My honest opinion: because they work. A headline with “bombshell,” “blockbuster” or “shocking” gets clicks. Attaching Adam Schefter’s name makes it sound official. And by the time anyone checks, the video already has its views. That’s my own view, not a report. But it’s why I keep doing these breakdowns. The more fans know how to spot them, the less power they have. And honestly, the real Rams story right now — a 38-year-old MVP quarterback managing arm soreness, a Defensive Player of the Year about to return to practice, and a team tied for second place one week before the deadline — is more interesting than anything these fake clips invent.

Now, let’s be fair to the football idea, because there’s a small piece of logic in it.

The Rams might actually need cornerback help. According to the Rams’ official website, McVay said Jaylen Watson “aggravated his shoulder again” against the Chargers. According to ESPN, it’s his previously injured right shoulder, and McVay said the team will “take his status a day at a time.” Trent McDuffie, who took a knee to the back, “came in feeling better than maybe what we expected,” according to the Rams’ website.

So if Watson misses time, the Rams’ depth at corner gets tested. And Watson’s shoulder has a history. According to the Rams’ official website, he dislocated it in the first half of the Week 3 game against Denver, missed Week 4 and came back in Week 5 playing with a harness — and still intercepted Josh Allen on Monday Night Football. That tells you how tough he is. But it also tells you this shoulder has now been hurt twice in about six weeks. According to Grisak, Emmanuel Forbes played well against the Chargers, with a 75.9 PFF grade. But depth is depth, and a veteran who can play inside and outside — like Robertson — is the kind of player a team with a cornerback injury might look at.

But here’s the problem: money. According to Athlon Sports, citing Over The Cap, the Rams have 206,406 dollars in cap space. I couldn’t find Robertson’s exact salary, but any veteran would cost more than that. According to Grisak, restructuring Kyren Williams’ contract could free about 3.5 million dollars. And the Rams have other possible needs, too — especially at receiver.

So a cornerback trade isn’t crazy. A trade for Robertson, with Washington, six days before playing Washington, because of a fake Schefter quote — that’s a different story.

According to ESPN, he said: “I want to be careful to even set any sort of expectations.” And he said the Rams will take it “a day at a time.” So I’d treat any Garrett snaps this Sunday as a bonus, not an expectation.

According to Grisak, Connors had the Rams’ best special teams grade against the Chargers, so that loss will be felt on the coverage units.

Key number three: the cornerbacks. If Watson can’t go, Diggs is a veteran who knows how to find the weakest cornerback on the field. Forbes and McDuffie will have to hold up.

And here’s one more thing to think about. Washington is coming off its best win of the season, and the Commanders are at home. But the Rams’ defense has been outstanding. According to the Rams’ official website, the Rams haven’t allowed a touchdown in their last two home games, and according to ESPN, the Chargers didn’t score a touchdown in their last nine quarters, including Sunday. The road is a different test, but this defense is playing with a lot of confidence. And the offense will be facing a Washington defense that, according to CBS, has had problems at cornerback all season. If Stafford’s arm is fine, that’s a matchup the Rams should be able to attack.
