How the franchise tag would work for Puka

Since the clip compares Olave's cost to "Puka's franchise tag," let me explain what that means.

When a player's contract runs out, he becomes a free agent in March and can sign anywhere. But a team can use the franchise tag to keep him for one more year. The tag comes with a high one-year salary, based on what the top players at his position make.

There are two main types. With the exclusive tag, no other team can talk to him. With the non-exclusive tag, other teams can make an offer, but his team can match it. And if they don't match, they get two first-round picks back.

Teams often use the tag to buy time to work out a long-term deal. It's a business tool, not a sign that a team wants to move on.

The clip never gave a number for Puka's tag. So when it says Olave would be "cheaper," there's nothing to compare. That's one more reason the clip doesn't hold up.

And remember what McVay said. The Rams want to figure out "mechanics" to keep their best players. The tag is one of those mechanics. A long-term deal is another.

Why fake clips hurt real fans

I want to take a moment to explain why I keep calling these out.

Fake clips aren't harmless. They get real fans excited about trades that were never happening. Then, when nothing happens, fans feel let down, or they start thinking the front office "failed" to make a deal that was never real.
