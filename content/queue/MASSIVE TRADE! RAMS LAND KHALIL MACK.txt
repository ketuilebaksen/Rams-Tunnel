Part one: the clip, and why it’s fake

If you’ve been watching this channel for the last few weeks, you know this pattern. By my count, this is the tenth fake “Schefter on First Take” clip we’ve covered since the middle of October. Jerry Jeudy. Vita Vea, twice. George Pickens. Josh Downs. A’Shawn Robinson. Donte Jackson. Amik Robertson. And now Khalil Mack, for the second time. The first Mack version showed up right before the Chargers game, and we broke it down then. This is the sequel.

Every one of these clips follows the same script. It opens with a confident line along the lines of “I can confirm, this trade is going to happen.” Then you get a run of real-sounding facts, a contract number, a stat, a quote pulled from somewhere else, all held together by a voice that sounds close enough to the real thing. And there’s always a hook at the end to keep you watching the next video.

This one goes heavy on metaphor. There’s a “greenhouse,” there’s a “sheepdog,” all the language you’d use to sell a veteran mentor joining a young defense. And the clip borrows a real quote from Mack about being “where your feet are” and twists it into something it never meant. More on that in a minute.

Here’s the thing to remember. Adam Schefter does not break trades on First Take with poetry about greenhouses. When a real trade happens, it shows up on his X account first, in one or two short sentences: team, player, compensation, “per sources.” Then ESPN’s main site picks it up within minutes, and NFL Network and every beat writer follow. If the only place you can find a “trade” is a YouTube clip with a still image and a voice track, it didn’t happen.

I checked ESPN, NFL.com, Pro Football Talk, the Rams’ official site, and the beat writers who cover this team every day. Not one of them has the Rams trading for Khalil Mack. Not one has the Rams even reported as interested this week.

Part two: what Khalil Mack actually said

Now let’s talk about the real quote, because it’s the part of the clip most likely to fool people.

After the Chargers lost to the Rams 24-9 on Sunday, Mack talked to Eric Williams of Fox Sports about the trade rumors swirling around him. Here’s what he said, as reported by Yahoo Sports and Sports Illustrated’s Chargers site:

“You’ve got to be where your feet are, man, ultimately.”

And then: “That can be the most difficult thing when you’re dealing with a lot of outside noise and stuff. Just trying to be where your feet are at all times. That’s kind of been my thing in years past.”

Read that again. “Be where your feet are” is a well-worn sports phrase. It means stay present, focus on today, don’t let the noise get in your head. Mack is telling you he’s trying to ignore the trade talk and do his job for the team he’s on. That’s the opposite of a player angling for a move.

The fake clip drops “where your feet are” into a trade story as if it were a hint about where Mack is headed next. That’s not what he said, and it’s not what he meant. If you want the real quote, it’s in the Yahoo article and the SI Chargers piece, both linked in the description.

There’s one more quote that goes back a few weeks. On October 15th, Mack said, “We’re still ring chasing here.” Some people read that as a hint he wanted out. But “here” means the Chargers. At the time, he was saying he still believed in his own team.

Part three: what Jim Harbaugh said on Monday

This is the biggest piece of evidence against the clip, and it’s from the one person whose opinion on this matters most.

On Monday, November 2nd, Jim Harbaugh was asked directly whether the Chargers might trade Mack before the November 10th deadline. Per Pro Football Talk and the Chicago Sun-Times, here’s what he said:

“I want Khalil Mack here. Khalil Mack wants to be here. He’s one of the pillars of our team.”

He also said, “I’m focused on the Texans. I know Joe, Chad, our organization are always looking to improve the team.” Joe is general manager Joe Hortiz, and Chad is assistant GM Chad Alexander.

Now, be fair about this. A head coach saying “I want him here” a week before the deadline is not a contract. Coaches say things like that all the time, and sometimes the player is gone a week later. Harbaugh even left a little room by mentioning that the front office is “always looking to improve the team.” So I’m not telling you a Mack trade to somebody is impossible.

What I am telling you is this. Every credible signal from the Chargers over the past week points the same direction.

On October 30th, Albert Breer reported that the Chargers had “shut down trade inquiries” and were “unwilling to wave any sort of white flag.”

This week, Ian Rapoport said on ESPN programming, per ClutchPoints, that Hortiz “has shown no indication that he wants to sell at all.” He added that the Chargers “like how Khalil Mack has helped the young edges and helped the locker room,” and that “they do not want to give up on the season.”

Then there’s Harbaugh himself: “I want Khalil Mack here.”

That’s three separate sources in one week, all saying the Chargers aren’t selling, and certainly not Mack. And the fake clip wants you to believe they not only sold him but sold him to the team that just beat them, for a late-round pick? No chance.

Part four: checking the clip’s numbers

Let’s go through the specific numbers in the clip one by one, because this is where these videos sneak past people. They mix real facts with fake ones so the whole thing sounds believable.

“Mack played 35 snaps against the Rams.” I couldn’t confirm this one. None of the game recaps or articles I found listed Mack’s exact snap count for Sunday. It might be right, it might be close, it might be invented. I’m flagging it as unverified rather than calling it false. What I can tell you is what the Rams did to the Chargers’ offense, because that’s real: the Chargers didn’t score a touchdown, Justin Herbert was pressured on 17 of 37 dropbacks, and the Rams’ defense picked him off twice.

“Mack is owed about $8 million.” This is half right, which is the most dangerous kind of wrong. Mack signed a one-year, $18 million deal with the Chargers, fully guaranteed. His base salary for this season is $8 million, so the $8 million figure is a real number. But in a midseason trade, the new team only takes on what’s left of the base salary. According to ESPN’s Bill Barnwell, that’s around $5.3 million remaining as of late October, and it shrinks a little more with every game. So the real cost to a new team would be roughly five million dollars, not eight. Hold onto that, because it matters a lot when we get to the Rams’ cap situation.

“Mack has 14.5 sacks over the last three seasons.” Here’s a twist: this one adds up. Mack had 6 sacks in 2024, 5.5 in 2025, and 3 so far this season. Six plus five and a half plus three is fourteen and a half. So the clip got that one right. This is how these videos work. They put one or two true numbers in so that when you check something and it holds up, you trust the rest. Don’t let one correct stat sell you a fake trade.

For a fuller picture of Mack’s 2026 season: through seven games, he has 3 sacks, 21 tackles, 5 tackles for loss, and 6 quarterback hits, per Yardbarker’s numbers. He’s 35 years old. He’s still a good player and a smart one, but he isn’t the 17-sack monster from 2023 anymore, the year he set the Chargers’ single-season record.

“The Rams are sending a fifth-round pick.” No insider, beat writer, or national outlet has reported any compensation for a Mack trade, because no Mack trade has happened. The fifth-rounder is invented. One more thing: the Rams do own a 2027 fifth-round pick, so the clip picked a pick that exists. Same trick as the sacks number. Real details make fake stories easier to swallow.

“This is the second of three Rams stories, before we get to the pass rusher from Arizona.” That line is a hook to keep you watching the next clip. I found no reporting of any trade between the Rams and an Arizona pass rusher. Division trades are rare in the NFL to begin with, and the Cardinals and Rams play each other in Week 10. If someone sends you an “Arizona pass rusher to the Rams” clip next, check it the same way we’re checking this one.

Part five: where Mack would actually go, if he goes anywhere

Let’s set the fake clip aside and look at the real Mack market. I want to be honest with you here, and that means telling you the Rams aren’t in it.

ESPN’s trade candidate list. ESPN ranked Mack seventh among likely trade candidates and gave him about a 35 percent chance of being moved. They listed four logical fits: the Bears, the Browns, the Bills, and the Cowboys. The Rams aren’t on the list.

Last Word on Sports. On Tuesday they ran a column arguing the Chargers are making a mistake by not trading Mack. The contenders they named were the Bears, the 49ers, and the Lions. Again, not the Rams.

49ers. Matt Maiocco of NBC Sports Bay Area, who covers the 49ers every day, said this weekend: “I certainly would not rule out the possibility that they could get Khalil Mack.” That’s not a report of talks. It’s a beat writer saying he wouldn’t be surprised. But it’s worth knowing that the team sitting on top of the NFC West at 6-1 is the one being mentioned, not the Rams.

Bears. This is where Mack played from 2018 to 2021, and where the trade buzz has been loudest. The Bears are 6-2 after beating Seattle 34-17 on Monday night, and they’ve already gone all in. Last week they traded their 2027 and 2028 first-round picks to Tennessee for Jeffery Simmons, one of the best defensive tackles in football. But on Tuesday, Sports Illustrated’s Bears site said Harbaugh’s comments hurt the Bears’ chances of getting Mack.

So if Mack does get traded, and right now the Chargers say he won’t be, he’s much more likely to end up in Chicago, Santa Clara, or Detroit than in Inglewood.

Here’s the part that makes this a little funny. The Rams did try to get Khalil Mack once. Back in 2018, when the Raiders were shopping him, ESPN and Adam Schefter reported that the Rams offered a first-round pick and a third-round pick. The Raiders took the Bears’ bigger package instead, and Mack went to Chicago. So “Rams want Khalil Mack” is a story with real history behind it. It’s just eight years old.

Part six: could the Rams even do this?

Now I want to try a thought experiment. This is not a report. Nobody is saying the Rams are trying to do this. I just want to show you why the fake clip falls apart even if you ignore everything Harbaugh said.

Money. The Rams have about $206,000 in cap space, according to Athlon Sports and Over The Cap. That’s not $2 million. It’s two hundred and six thousand dollars, roughly enough to sign a practice squad player. To take on Mack’s remaining salary of around $5 million, the Rams would need to clear about $5 million in room before the deadline.

There are ways to make room. Restructuring Kyren Williams’ deal could free up something like $3.5 million. The Chargers could eat part of the salary, too, if they wanted to. But that means a restructure plus salary retained by a team that doesn’t want to sell, plus maybe another move on top. That’s a lot of moving parts for a 35-year-old rental, and none of it has been reported.

Picks. The Rams don’t have their 2027 first- or third-round picks, their 2028 second, or their 2029 third. For 2027 they still have a second, a fourth, a fifth, and a sixth. Could a fifth-round pick buy Khalil Mack? Maybe, if the Chargers were desperate to sell. Rentals of older players often go for mid-to-late round picks. But the Chargers aren’t desperate. They’ve said so publicly. If the Bears or 49ers got in a bidding war, a fifth wouldn’t come close.

What McVay has said. This might be the biggest piece. When Sean McVay was asked about new trade talks, his answer was “No.” Then he added, “Hopefully, some of those trades will be guys getting back from injury.” That’s coach-speak, sure, but it tells you how the Rams see it. They think their big “acquisitions” are already on the roster, and the biggest one is about to come back.

What the Rams actually need. If the Rams make a move before Tuesday, every credible analyst I’ve read says it’ll be for a wide receiver, not a pass rusher. CBS Sports’ Josh Edwards listed receiver as the Rams’ top deadline need on October 30th. Look at Sunday: Puka Nacua caught 11 of 12 targets for 80 yards and a touchdown, and the rest of the wide receivers combined for 2 catches on 6 targets, both by Tutu Atwell. That’s the hole. The names that keep coming up in real reporting and real analysis are Josh Downs, who has requested a trade from the Colts per Jeremy Fowler, Brian Thomas Jr., and Dontayvion Wicks. Again, none of those are reports of Rams talks. They’re names analysts think fit.

Les Snead’s history. If something does happen, history says to watch Tuesday afternoon. Snead has made four deadline-day trades over the years. Some beat writers circled this past Monday as a possible trade day, and it came and went without a move. So stay tuned, but trust the timeline: Tuesday, November 10th, 4 p.m. Eastern.

Part seven: the Rams’ real “Mack” is already in the building

Here’s the real pass-rush story, and it’s a better one than the fake clip.

Myles Garrett is back on the practice field this week.

On Monday, McVay said Garrett would practice this week, which opens his 21-day window to come off injured reserve. Garrett had arthroscopic knee surgery in mid-September after playing the opener at less than 100 percent, and he hasn’t recorded a counting stat as a Ram yet.

McVay was careful about it. Here’s the quote: “I want to be careful to even set any sort of expectations.” He said they’ll take it “a day at a time,” and called it “real positive that we’re starting his clock.”

Ian Rapoport added some useful detail on the Pat McAfee Show. He called Garrett’s situation “an interesting one” and said the Rams expected him back last week, but Garrett wasn’t comfortable on the knee yet, so they decided to wait. Rapoport thinks this week is in play depending on how Garrett responds to practice, and said once it turns, “it should all happen very quick.”

Let’s think about what that means. Without Garrett, the Rams’ pass rush pressured Justin Herbert on 17 of 37 dropbacks on Sunday. That’s almost half his dropbacks. The defense had two interceptions, a strip-sack, and a fourth-down stop late in the game. Trent McDuffie and the secondary graded out at an A-plus in several postgame report cards.

Now add one of the best pass rushers of his generation to that group. That’s the defensive upgrade the Rams are counting on, and it doesn’t cost a draft pick or a dime of cap space they don’t have.

I’m not going to promise you Garrett plays Sunday in Washington. McVay won’t promise it, so I won’t either. But “Myles Garrett is practicing” is real news. “Rams land Khalil Mack” is not.

Tight end Terrance Ferguson’s window may open soon too. McVay called that “more likely than not.” Colby Parkinson is “making really good progress.” Those are the trades McVay was talking about.

Part eight: the Stafford elbow situation

Now for the story I’m watching most closely this week, and I want to be careful with it.

After Sunday’s win, Matthew Stafford left the locker room with ice wrapped around his right arm, his throwing arm, according to reporter Adam Grosbard. On Monday, McVay was asked about it. Here’s what he said: “He’s had some soreness there.” And then: “You’ll have to talk to him about that.” When pressed, he added: “I can’t attest to stuff that I’m not necessarily aware of.”

That’s not a lot of information, and McVay clearly didn’t want to give more. Turf Show Times ran a headline saying the Rams added Stafford to the injury report with elbow soreness. I couldn’t open the article itself, so I can’t tell you his exact practice designation. Stafford usually talks to the media on Wednesdays, and the expectation is that he’ll address it himself today. I’ll update you in the community tab as soon as he does.

Ian Rapoport’s read, on Tuesday: “I haven’t heard anything to have me believe that Stafford won’t be ready.” That’s the most important quote here. As of now, nobody credible is saying Stafford will miss Sunday.

But here’s why I’m paying attention anyway. Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak has been tracking Stafford’s passing numbers, and they tell a story. Over the last four weeks, Stafford’s average depth of target has been 5.5 yards, which ranks 25th of 27 qualifying quarterbacks. His intended air yards per attempt dropped from about 9.5 earlier in the season to 5.23. Against the Chargers on Sunday, his completed air yards per completion were 2.2. Against the Raiders the week before, 2.1.

Put simply, the Rams have stopped throwing the ball down the field. Sunday’s line was 17 of 25 for 157 yards, a touchdown, and an interception.

There are a few ways to read that. Maybe it’s game plan. The Rams have leaned into the screen game and the run game, and Sunday’s second half, with 129 rushing yards after just 10 in the first half, is a good example. Maybe it’s the lack of a second receiver who can win deep, which ties right back to the wide receiver trade talk. Or maybe the elbow has been bothering him longer than anybody’s saying. That last one is speculation. It is not a report. I’m raising it because the timing lines up with the drop in air yards, and it’s fair to ask.

There’s also history. Stafford dealt with elbow soreness in 2022, and McVay later said, “People forget how big of a deal that was.” So when the Rams go quiet about Stafford’s elbow, it’s worth paying attention.

One last note, and it’s a fun one. Kelly Stafford went on Instagram Tuesday to complain about the schedule, specifically how long the Rams have had to wait for their bye in Week 11. It’s not injury news, but it tells you the family feels how long this stretch has been.

Part ten: Washington preview

Let’s get to football. The Rams are 4-4, tied with Seattle for second in the NFC West. San Francisco leads the division at 6-1 coming off their bye, and Arizona is 3-5 after beating Dallas 33-30 in overtime. Seattle fell to 4-4 after losing 34-17 to the Bears on Monday night, so the Rams are now even with the defending champs.

Next up is Washington on the road. Sunday, November 8th, 1 p.m. Eastern, on FOX.

Washington is 2-4-1, but don’t get too comfortable. They’re coming off their best game of the season, a 27-10 win over the Eagles. Jayden Daniels threw a touchdown to Stefon Diggs, and they looked like a different team.

Here’s how I see the matchup.

Rams offense vs. Washington’s defense. CBS listed cornerback as Washington’s top deadline need, so their secondary has holes. This is a game where the Rams should take shots. If Stafford’s elbow is fine, I want to see the average depth of target climb back up. If it stays at 5 yards a throw against a weak secondary, that tells us something. Watch it.

Rams run game. The second half on Sunday was the blueprint. Blake Corum ran 11 times for 73 yards, including a 23-yard touchdown. Kyren Williams added 56 yards and a score. If the Rams can run like that, they control the clock and keep Daniels on the sideline.

Rams defense vs. Jayden Daniels. This is the real test. Herbert is a pocket passer, and the Rams lived in his face. Daniels can escape, and a pressure on him can turn into a 20-yard scramble if the rush lanes break down. The pass rushers need to stay disciplined. Watch whether Garrett is active. Even 20 snaps from him would change how Washington protects.

The slow start. On Sunday, the Rams went five straight drives without a first down to open the game, the first time that’s happened in the McVay era. Their first 15 plays gained 24 yards. You can get away with that against an 0-7 team that hasn’t scored a touchdown in nine quarters. You won’t get away with it against Daniels coming off a big win.

Prediction. I like the Rams, but closer than people think. Let’s say Rams 24, Commanders 20. Kyren and Corum go over 120 yards combined, Puka gets his 8 to 10 catches, and the defense makes one late stop. If Stafford’s elbow turns out to be more than soreness, all bets are off, and I’ll update the pick.

Part eleven: how to spot the next fake before it fools you

We’ve got six days until the deadline, and the fake clips are going to get worse before they get better. So here’s my four-point checklist one more time. Screenshot it, share it in your group chat, whatever helps.

One: compare the headline to the content. Today’s headline says “Rams land Khalil Mack.” Watch the whole clip and see if anyone with a name and a job title ever says the trade is done. Usually the headline promises a trade and the clip delivers a lot of “could,” “might,” and “sources say,” with no source.

Two: check the small facts. We’ve seen clips call a cornerback a quarterback, mix up teams, and get contract numbers wrong. Today’s clip had a real quote taken out of context and a salary number that was technically real but wrong for a trade. If the small stuff is sloppy, the big stuff isn’t real.

Three: check whether the team is on any real list. ESPN, CBS, and The Athletic all publish deadline previews with logical fits. If the Rams aren’t on any credible list for a player, and for Mack they aren’t, be very skeptical of a clip saying it’s done.

Four: search for the quote. If a clip says Schefter said something on First Take, search for it. Real Schefter quotes get written up on ESPN, Pro Football Talk, and a dozen other sites within an hour. If you only find it in YouTube clips, it isn’t real.

And here’s a bonus rule: if a “breaking” trade comes with a teaser for the next video, like “the pass rusher from Arizona,” that’s a content strategy, not reporting.
