Now, the problem the video describes is real, so let’s look at it carefully.

Davante Adams ruptured his hamstring against Arizona in Week 6, and since then, the passing game has run through one player. Against the Raiders, according to the LA Times, Puka caught eight passes for 68 yards and a touchdown. Against the Chargers, according to Pro Football Talk, he caught 11 passes for 80 yards and a touchdown. That’s 19 catches for 148 yards and two touchdowns over two games — the same numbers the original video gave, and they check out when you add them up.

But look at what happened to everyone else. According to the AP, in the Raiders game, Konata Mumpfield’s 15-yard catch was the only reception by a Rams wide receiver other than Puka. And according to Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak, against the Chargers, the rest of the receivers combined for two catches on six targets, and Tutu Atwell was the only one who caught anything.

Atwell did make the biggest play of the game — a 40-yard catch on the two-minute drive before halftime that set up Kyren Williams’ touchdown. According to The Sporting Tribune, that came after the Rams’ first five drives produced no first downs. But according to Sbisa, Atwell has only 11 catches in 15 games going back to last season. So he’s a big-play threat, not a consistent target.

And here’s the part that really shows the problem. According to Grisak, over the last four weeks, Stafford’s average depth of target has been just 5.5 yards, which ranks 25th out of 27 quarterbacks. His intended air yards over the last four weeks have averaged 5.23, down from 9.5 in the four weeks before that. And his completed air yards were just 2.2 against the Chargers and 2.1 against the Raiders. In simple terms: the Rams are throwing short, catching short and relying on yards after the catch.

Let me quickly explain those terms, because they’re useful. Average depth of target is how far down the field, on average, the quarterback’s passes travel — whether they’re caught or not. Intended air yards is basically the same idea. And completed air yards is how far the caught passes traveled in the air before the catch. So when completed air yards are around two, it means the average completion is being caught about two yards past the line of scrimmage, and almost everything after that comes from the receiver running with the ball. That’s my own explanation of the stats, but it tells you the Rams’ passing game has turned into an extension of the running game.

Now, to be fair, short passing isn’t automatically bad. Against the Raiders, according to the Rams’ official website, Stafford completed 79 percent of his passes — his best mark as a Ram — and the Rams won by 14. McVay has said the key is being “efficient on early downs.” And efficient short passes help the run game and keep the defense off the field. The problem is what happens against a team that takes away the short stuff. Without a downfield threat, defenses can crowd the line and dare the Rams to throw deep. That’s the real concern.

So when the original video says the Rams “have increasingly relied on short passes and yards after the catch,” it’s right. The numbers back it up.

Now let’s talk about the reason this got more serious this week: Stafford’s elbow.

According to Grisak, after Sunday’s win, Stafford was seen leaving the locker room with a bag of ice wrapped around his right arm, based on a post from reporter Adam Grosbard. On Monday, according to Pro Football Talk, McVay said: “He’s had some soreness there.” He deferred the details to Stafford, saying: “I can’t attest to stuff that I’m not necessarily aware of.”

According to a Turf Show Times headline, the Rams added Stafford to the injury report with elbow soreness. I wasn’t able to access the full article, so I can’t tell you his exact practice status. I’ll update you when the full report is clear.

Now, here’s why some people are concerned. According to Grisak, Stafford had elbow soreness in 2022 that limited him, and the Rams downplayed it at the time. Later, McVay said: “People forget how big of a deal that was.” So Rams fans have seen this movie before.

And according to Grisak, Stafford’s efficiency has slipped this season. He ranks 15th in EPA per pass, has a 92.6 passer rating and already has eight interceptions, which matches his total from all of last season.

But I want to be fair. Grisak himself wrote that the decline might have nothing to do with the elbow, and that the severity of the soreness is unknown. And according to Heavy, NFL Network’s Ian Rapoport said on November 3rd: “I haven’t heard anything to have me believe that Stafford won’t be ready.” So the most likely outcome is that Stafford plays on Sunday. The question is whether the elbow affects how far he throws the ball.

And that connects directly to the trade debate. If Stafford’s elbow is limiting his deep throws, adding a deep-threat receiver might not help much. If the short passing is about the receivers, then adding a receiver could make a real difference. Nobody outside the building knows which one it is yet. That’s my own read — but it’s the most important question of the week.

Now let’s go through the three receivers, one by one, with the real numbers.

Target number one: Josh Downs of the Indianapolis Colts.

The Colts are falling apart. According to Sbisa, Indianapolis is 2-6 after a 42-7 loss to the Jaguars in Week 8. According to CBS Sports, Downs had four catches for 23 yards against Jacksonville, including a 6-yard touchdown — his first touchdown of the season and the Colts’ only score. For the season, according to CBS Sports, he has 30 catches on 51 targets for 330 yards and one touchdown. And according to Sbisa, five of those catches went for 20 yards or more.

And according to ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler, Downs has requested a trade.

So he’s available, and he fits the need for a reliable target. But here’s the problem: the price. According to Sbisa, the Colts reportedly want a third-round pick. I couldn’t find another report confirming that asking price, so treat it with caution. And it’s very different from what other writers have estimated. Grisak proposed a 2027 fourth-round pick, and The Athletic’s Nate Atkins suggested a fifth- or sixth-round pick, according to Heavy. And the Rams don’t even have a 2027 third-round pick to give, according to Pro Football Rumors.

There’s also a fit question. According to Heavy, Atkins wrote that “Downs is a slot-only player, which makes him a harder fit with the three-tight end sets the Rams love.” And against the Chargers, according to Athlon Sports, the Rams used three tight ends heavily — Tyler Higbee played 45 snaps, Davis Allen 40 and Max Klare 25.

And there’s competition. ESPN listed the Chiefs and Steelers as fits for Downs, and NFL insider Jordan Schultz reported that the Steelers are interested, according to Heavy.

So Downs is the most available of the three, but not necessarily the best fit.

To be fair to Downs, there’s more to his résumé than this season. According to Heavy, he topped 700 receiving yards in each of his first two seasons and has 2,447 career receiving yards and 11 touchdowns. According to Atkins, he can also return punts, which the Rams could use. And Grisak compared him to Jamison Crowder, the slot receiver McVay coached in Washington. According to Grisak, McVay once said about Crowder: “It gives us the ability to kind of move him around and use him in a variety of ways.” So there’s a role for a player like Downs in McVay’s system — it just might be a smaller role than fans expect.

When Downs was asked about his trade request, according to ESPN, he said: “I’m open to whatever. Whatever comes my way, come my way and we go from there.” According to Heavy, Schultz reported that “the expectation is a trade will eventually happen.” So Downs will probably be somewhere else after Tuesday. The question is where.

Target number two: Brian Thomas Jr. of the Jacksonville Jaguars.

This is the high-upside option. As a rookie in 2024, Thomas had 87 catches for 1,282 yards and 10 touchdowns. But this season has been a struggle. According to CBS Sports, through the first six games, he had just nine catches for 105 yards and one touchdown, and he was benched for the second half of the Jaguars’ Week 6 loss to Houston.

The original video mentioned that the article it was based on included a strange stat for Thomas, and it was right to be careful. The Ramblin’ Fan article listed Thomas with “10 catches for 1,107 yards,” which is clearly a typo. So I’m sticking with the CBS numbers.

According to Sbisa, the Jaguars likely want a second-round pick, based on what the Cardinals got in the Marvin Harrison Jr. trade. Grisak has also estimated a second-rounder. That’s the Rams’ best pick left in 2027. And according to ESPN’s trade rankings, Thomas was ranked second overall, with a 45 percent chance of being traded, and ESPN listed the Chiefs, Steelers and 49ers as fits.

And here’s something to think about. The Jaguars just beat the Colts 42-7. When a team is winning, it’s less likely to sell a talented young receiver for less than full value. Jaguars coach Liam Coen has also defended Thomas publicly. According to CBS Sports, after the benching, Coen said: “Week to week. Guys have to step up and make plays.” And according to NBC Sports, Coen later said Thomas was “gonna be able to help us make plays.” So Jacksonville may not be in a hurry to move him. That’s my own read, but it’s another reason I’d be surprised if Thomas ends up a Ram.

Thomas is cheap this year. According to Grisak, he’d cost the Rams only about 600,000 dollars for the rest of the season. But Grisak also noted earlier that Thomas ranked 85th out of 86 receivers in adjusted catch percentage. So he’s a big gamble on talent — the kind of gamble a team with plenty of picks can afford, and the Rams can’t.

And there’s a connection worth mentioning. According to The Athletic, Jaguars general manager James Gladstone worked for the Rams from 2016 to 2024 and has relationships with both Les Snead and Sean McVay.

If you’d take the gamble on Thomas, drop “BTJ” in the comments.

Target number three: Dontayvion Wicks of the Philadelphia Eagles.

This is the name the original video called “the most practical option.” According to Heavy, Wicks has 19 catches for 284 yards and a touchdown in six games this season. The Eagles traded for him from Green Bay, sending the Packers a 2026 fifth-round pick and a 2027 sixth-round pick, according to Heavy. So the Eagles didn’t pay much for him.

But here’s the catch the original video glossed over. According to Heavy, Wicks signed a one-year extension worth 12.5 million dollars that keeps him under contract through 2027, with a significant guaranteed portion. That’s a real commitment for a team with almost no cap space.

Now, there’s a conflict in the reporting I need to flag. Back on October 20th, Grisak wrote that Wicks would only cost the Rams about 700,000 dollars in 2026 and have a 4-million-dollar cap hit in 2027. That’s very different from the 12.5-million-dollar figure Heavy reported. It’s possible the extension is structured so most of the money hits later, but I couldn’t confirm which number is right. So I’ll just say this: Wicks might be cheap this year, but he likely comes with a bigger bill next year.

And here’s my question about Wicks: would Philadelphia even sell? The Eagles lost to Washington 27-10 last week, according to RotoWire, and according to NBC Sports Dallas-Fort Worth, Jalen Hurts is out for the season. But the Eagles also just traded for Wicks this year. Selling him a few months later would be unusual. That’s my own read, not a report.

And these three aren’t the only names that have been mentioned. According to CBS Sports’ Josh Edwards, the Rams’ biggest need at the deadline is wide receiver, and he listed Josh Downs, Jerry Jeudy, Brian Thomas Jr., Calvin Ridley, Jauan Jennings and Quentin Johnston as possible options. ESPN listed the Rams as a fit for Jennings, with a 50 percent chance he’s traded. And according to Heavy, USA Today’s Jacob Camenker suggested the Rams go after Chargers receiver Quentin Johnston. So Wicks is actually one of the less common names. The more established rumors around the Rams are Downs and Jennings.

And Jennings deserves a quick look, because he’s the one receiver ESPN’s own writers tied to the Rams. According to Athlon Sports, Jennings had 55 catches for 643 yards and nine touchdowns with the 49ers in 2025, but just four catches for 43 yards this season with Minnesota. He’s a physical outside receiver who blocks — which fits a run-first, tight-end-heavy offense better than a slot receiver does. But according to Athlon, he has an 8-million-dollar base salary, and the Vikings were unbeaten. So he’s a better fit and a harder deal.

And there’s one more reason the receiver question matters: Puka himself. According to CBS Sports’ Josh Edwards, Puka “has been dealing with his own injuries” this season. He played through a calf issue against the Raiders. If the Rams’ entire passing game runs through one player and that player gets banged up, there’s no safety net. That’s the strongest argument for adding someone — not to replace anyone, but as insurance.

Now let’s talk about the problem that ties all three together: money and picks.

According to Athlon Sports, citing Over The Cap, the Rams have 206,406 dollars in cap space. Any of these players would require the Rams to clear space first. According to Grisak, the most realistic way is restructuring Kyren Williams’ contract to free up about 3.5 million dollars.

And the picks are thin. According to Sbisa, the Rams have only four picks in next year’s draft. According to Grisak, those are a second, a fourth, a fifth and a sixth. According to Pro Football Rumors, the Rams already traded their 2027 first and third, their 2028 second and their 2029 third in the Myles Garrett and Trent McDuffie deals.

So if the asking prices are what Sbisa reported — a third for Downs, a second for Thomas — the Rams either can’t pay it or would have to give up their best remaining pick. That’s why the realistic answer might be a smaller move, or no move at all.

Now let’s talk about the other side of the argument, because it got stronger this week.

According to the Rams’ official website, McVay said on Monday that Myles Garrett will return to practice this week. And according to Heavy, Rapoport said: “I was told, when he gets on the field for practice, that it should all happen very quick.” He added: “I think this week is in play, but we need to see how he actually responds.” So Garrett could even play against Washington on Sunday.

And there’s an interesting backstory there. According to Heavy, Rapoport called Garrett’s situation “an interesting one.” He said he had expected Garrett to return the week before, but Garrett didn’t feel comfortable on the knee yet. According to ESPN, Garrett has been on injured reserve since arthroscopic knee surgery in mid-September. He played in the season opener but said afterward he wasn’t 100 percent. So the Rams are being careful, and McVay said this week: “I want to be careful to even set any sort of expectations.” But if Garrett is back soon, that’s the Rams’ biggest “addition” of the season — on the defensive side.

And on offense, McVay said it’s “more likely than not” that tight end Terrance Ferguson’s practice window opens this week. According to Sbisa, Davante Adams could be back around Week 12 or 13 at the earliest. That lines up with what McVay said last week about Adams being on the “shorter end” of his six-to-10-week timeline. So help is coming — just not immediately.

That’s why McVay keeps saying what he said last week. According to the New York Post, he said: “Hopefully, some of those trades will be guys getting back from injury that haven’t played for us.”

If you trust McVay’s plan, drop “trust the plan.” If you want a receiver now, drop “make the trade.”

Here’s my honest take, and remember — this is my opinion, not a report.

If the Rams make a receiver move, I think Downs is the most realistic, because he’s asked out and he’s on an expiring deal. But if the Colts really want a third-round pick, the Rams can’t pay that, because they don’t have a 2027 third. Thomas has the most talent, but a second-round pick for a receiver with nine catches through six games is a lot. And Wicks might be cheap this season, but his contract runs through 2027.

And honestly, the Stafford elbow situation makes me more cautious. If the elbow is limiting how far he throws, the solution isn’t just adding a receiver. It’s protecting Stafford and leaning on the run game while he heals — which is exactly what the Rams have been doing the last two weeks. According to Pro Football Talk, the Rams ran for 129 yards in the second half against the Chargers. That formula works.

So my prediction is this: if the Rams do anything before Tuesday’s deadline, it’ll be small. And it’s very possible they do nothing at all and bet on Garrett, Ferguson and Adams.

And here’s a timing note. According to Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak, Les Snead’s history suggested Monday was the most realistic day for a Rams trade this week. Monday came and went, and nothing happened. According to Grisak, Snead has made four trades on deadline day itself during his time as general manager. So if something happens, the most likely day is next Tuesday, November 10th, before the 4 p.m. Eastern deadline. That’s two days after the Washington game, which gives Snead one more look at the offense — and at Stafford’s elbow — before deciding.

Now let’s look ahead to Sunday.

According to ESPN’s game page, the Rams play at Washington on Sunday at 1 p.m. Eastern on FOX. According to RotoWire, the Commanders beat the Eagles 27-10 last week to improve to 2-4-1. And the Rams are 4-4, tied with Seattle for second place in the NFC West after the Seahawks lost to the Bears 34-17 on Monday night, according to The Everett Herald.

Here are my three keys.

Key number one: watch Stafford’s throws. If he’s pushing the ball down the field, the elbow is probably fine. If everything is short again, that tells you something.

And Washington might be the right defense to test it against. According to CBS Sports’ Josh Edwards, “Cornerback has been a problem all season for the Commanders.” If the Rams can’t find some downfield shots against a secondary with real cornerback issues, that would be a big red flag. If they can, it’ll calm a lot of nerves about both the elbow and the receivers.

Key number two: keep running the ball. Kyren Williams and Blake Corum have carried this offense for two weeks. According to Pro Football Talk, Corum ran for 73 yards and a touchdown against the Chargers, and Kyren scored too.

Key number three: get a second receiver involved. Whether it’s Atwell, Mumpfield or a tight end, someone besides Puka has to make plays. That’s the best way to make the trade debate go away.

My prediction: Rams 24, Commanders 20. I expect another game built on the run and the defense, with Washington keeping it close because Jayden Daniels can make plays with his legs. But if Garrett plays, or if Stafford hits even one or two deep shots, the Rams could pull away late.

So here’s my bottom line.

No, the Rams are not signing two wide receivers. The video with that headline admits there’s no confirmed trade or negotiation. What’s real is that a FanSided writer, John Sbisa, named Josh Downs, Brian Thomas Jr. and Dontayvion Wicks as possible targets — and that the Rams’ passing game really has become very short and very Puka-heavy since Davante Adams got hurt.

What’s also real is the new concern this week: Stafford’s elbow. McVay confirmed the soreness, Grisak pointed out the 2022 history, and Rapoport said he expects Stafford to be ready. We’ll find out more as the week goes on.

With 206,000 dollars in cap space, only four picks next year and Garrett about to return, I think the Rams will be careful at the deadline. If they add a receiver, it’ll be a small move. If they don’t, they’ll be betting on getting healthy — and on a defense that has carried them through two straight wins.
