“Not even close to good enough. It cost us the game tonight.” Those were Sean McVay’s words after one of the most painful regular-season losses of his career. Matthew Stafford threw for 383 yards and three touchdowns without a single interception. Puka Nacua caught 17 passes — the most ever by any player in a Monday Night Football game. The Rams scored 34 points, erased a seven-point deficit with five seconds left in regulation, and then got the ball in overtime with a chance to win it. And it all came down to one 48-yard field goal. The kick was blocked. Buffalo got the ball back. Tyler Bass made his. Bills 37, Rams 34. And now the Rams are 2-3, three games behind an undefeated 49ers team in the NFC West. Let’s start at the very beginning, because the tone of this game was set on the very first play. Ray Davis took the opening kickoff 70 yards to the Rams’ 26-yard line. Ethan Evans forced him out of bounds and saved a touchdown, but the damage was done. A few plays later, Tyler Bass kicked a 40-yard field goal. Buffalo 3, Rams 0, before the Rams’ offense had even touched the ball.

Then the Rams drove on their first possession — and Harrison Mevis missed a 50-yard field goal. Two Rams penalties on that opening drive didn’t help. The Bills went back down the field and Bass kicked another field goal, this one from 23 yards, with ten seconds left in the first quarter. Bills 6, Rams 0. If you’d been watching our channel all week, you’d recognize the pattern immediately: special teams leaks and self-inflicted mistakes, putting the defense in a hole before it ever got going.

But the Rams answered. In the second quarter, Stafford found Davante Adams for a four-yard touchdown to give Los Angeles a 7-6 lead. Then Buffalo struck back with a big play: a 40-yard screen pass to Ty Johnson that set up a 17-yard touchdown run by James Cook with 5:09 left in the half. Bills 13, Rams 7.

And then the defense made a play I want you to remember. Jaylen Watson — the cornerback who dislocated his shoulder in Denver, playing tonight in a harness — picked off a Josh Allen deep ball near the end of the first half. Watson coming back from that injury and taking the ball away from an MVP? That’s toughness. And the first-half defensive numbers were outstanding. Allen had just 60 passing yards and 21 rushing yards at halftime. The game plan was working.

And let’s give the defense its credit for the overall pass defense, too. Josh Allen finished just 12 of 19 for 210 yards, one touchdown and one interception. Buffalo completed only twelve passes the entire night. Chris Shula’s coverage — McDuffie, Watson, Lake, Forbes — largely took away Buffalo’s passing game. The problem wasn’t the coverage. The problem was what happened when the coverage held and Allen took off running, and what happened when Buffalo decided to hand the ball to James Cook over and over again. The Bills didn’t beat this defense through the air. They beat it on the ground.

Then came the second half — and it turned into one of the wildest games of the NFL season. According to CBS Sports, the two teams combined for 34 points in the fourth quarter alone, the most in any fourth quarter this season.

The Rams took the lead early in the third quarter. Tyler Higbee drew a defensive pass interference flag that set up Blake Corum’s one-yard touchdown run. Rams 14, Bills 13. Buffalo answered with a seven-yard touchdown pass from Allen to Keon Coleman — and Tyler Bass actually missed the extra point wide right after a false start pushed it back. Bills 19, Rams 14. Then Stafford hit Puka Nacua for a two-yard touchdown, and the Rams went for two but were sacked on the conversion attempt. Rams 20, Bills 19.

Some fans will question that two-point attempt, so let’s talk about it. Down 19-14, a touchdown made it 20-19. Going for two there gives you a three-point lead, which means Buffalo would need a touchdown — not just a field goal — to take the lead back. It’s a reasonable, aggressive decision, and plenty of analytics models support it. The issue wasn’t the call. It was the execution: Stafford was sacked on the conversion attempt. And in a game that ended up being decided by three points, that one extra point would have changed every decision that followed.

And then came a moment that will haunt Rams fans. Nate Landman punched the ball away from James Cook, and the Rams initially recovered — but the officials reversed it and gave the ball back to Buffalo. Allen eventually punched it in himself on a one-yard quarterback sneak with 11:28 left in the fourth quarter, then hit Dalton Kincaid for the two-point conversion. Bills 27, Rams 20. And to make it even more painful, Aaron Donald had his first sack of the season in this game — but it was wiped out by an illegal contact penalty on Trent McDuffie.

But this Rams offense refused to quit. Stafford hit Davante Adams for a five-yard touchdown — Davante’s second of the night — and the game was tied at 27. Then Buffalo went right back down the field, and James Cook scored from five yards out with 1:39 left. Bills 34, Rams 27.

And then — the drive. A minute and change on the clock. Sixty-two yards to go. Stafford was surgical. He moved the Rams down the field in just over a minute, hit Kyren Williams over the middle to set it up, and Kyren punched it in from one yard out with just five seconds left. 34-34. SoFi Stadium exploded. Overtime.

Now — overtime. The Rams won the toss and deferred, so Buffalo got the ball first. And immediately, Allen hit Khalil Shakir for 57 yards. It looked like the Bills were about to end it. But then Nate Landman did it again. He punched the ball out of James Cook’s hands — his second forced fumble of the night — and Aaron Donald fell on it at the Rams’ 20-yard line. A turnover in overtime, recovered by number 99. The stadium was shaking.

Stafford went to work. A 21-yard completion to tight end Davis Allen. Another 21 yards to Puka Nacua. Suddenly, the Rams were at the Buffalo 35-yard line. One more first down and Mevis would have a chip shot. But on third down, the Rams didn’t get the first down. And McVay chose to kick a 48-yard field goal rather than try to get closer.

The kick was blocked by Greg Rousseau — the same pass rusher who came into this game leading the NFL in sacks. Buffalo took over. Allen scrambled to the Rams’ 27-yard line, and Tyler Bass made a 45-yard field goal to win it. Bills 37, Rams 34. Josh Allen’s first career overtime win — he had been 0-7 in overtime games, including the playoffs, according to CBS Sports.

And after the game, McVay didn’t hide from his own role in it. He said: “I wish I would have had a better play call and gone for it, got it a lot tighter.” That’s the head coach admitting that the decision-making on that final drive wasn’t good enough either. And I respect that honesty. But it doesn’t change the result.

Think about the field position there. From the Buffalo 35, a 48-yard attempt is right on the edge of Mevis’s comfort zone — remember, every one of his misses this season has come from beyond 40 yards. One more first down, even five or six more yards, turns it into a kick in the low 40s or high 30s, and dramatically changes the odds. With Stafford completing 40 passes on the night and Puka catching everything thrown his way, there was a strong case for staying aggressive and trying to finish the drive. McVay knew it the moment the kick was blocked, and he said so publicly. The lesson for the rest of the season is simple: with this offense, trust the offense.

Now let’s talk about the kicking problem, because this is the story of the season — and frankly, the story of the last two seasons.

Harrison Mevis missed both of his field goal attempts on Monday night: the 50-yarder on the opening drive and the blocked 48-yarder in overtime. According to Yahoo Sports, he’s now five of nine on field goals this season, with three misses in his last four attempts. All of his misses have come from beyond 40 yards. To be fair to him, he’s a perfect 14-for-14 on extra points, and the blocked kick wasn’t only on the kicker — a block is a protection issue too. After the game, Mevis said: “I felt like I hit it good, that’s all I can tell you... They had a good rush game.”

But McVay’s message was crystal clear: “The kicking game, especially with our PAT field goal, not even close to good enough. It cost us the game tonight... I’m going to evaluate everything.”

And here’s why that phrase — “evaluate everything” — matters so much. We’ve seen this movie before. Last season, special teams mistakes cost the Rams the NFC West title and the number one seed, which is why their NFC Championship game was played in Seattle instead of at SoFi. A muffed punt in that game helped end their season. McVay fired special teams coordinator Chase Blackburn after a Thursday night overtime loss in Seattle. The Rams replaced kicker Joshua Karty with Harrison Mevis. This offseason, they hired Bubba Ventrone as the new special teams coordinator, signed long snapper Joe Cardona and linebacker Grant Stuard, and put more starters on the blocking, return and coverage units. And yet here we are, five games into 2026, and the third phase of the game just cost the Rams another winnable primetime game.

So what happens now? Let me be clear: this is not a report. Nobody has said the Rams are cutting Mevis or bringing in another kicker. But when a head coach says he’s going to evaluate everything after his kicker misses two field goals in a game that ends on a field goal, you have to take it seriously. And here’s one thing worth knowing: even with a cap situation as tight as the Rams’ — roughly a million dollars in space, according to Turf Show Times — kickers are some of the most affordable players in football. If the Rams decide they need a change, money won’t be the reason they can’t make one.

Now let’s flip to the positives, because there were real ones, and they matter for the rest of the season.

Puka Nacua was historic. Seventeen catches on 24 targets for 138 yards and a touchdown. According to CBS Sports, his 17 receptions are the most ever in a single Monday Night Football game. He fell just one catch short of the Rams’ franchise record of 18, set by Tom Fears back in 1950. And he became only the fifth Rams player ever with at least 15 catches, 100 receiving yards and a touchdown in a game — the first since Danny Amendola. Remember all the talk last week about Puka’s groin and that outside doctor’s “pre-hernia” opinion? Two weeks back from injury, and he just had one of the greatest receiving nights in franchise history. Every Rams fan should feel good about that.

But let’s also be realistic about the workload. Twenty-four targets is an enormous number for a receiver who was dealing with a groin injury just two weeks ago. Puka has been the engine of this passing game, and the Rams clearly trusted his health completely. Over the next few weeks, it’ll be worth watching how the team manages his snaps and targets, especially with a long season ahead. The best version of this offense spreads the ball between Puka, Davante, Kyren and the tight ends — and that’s how Davante ended up with two touchdowns in this game, because Buffalo had to account for Puka on every snap.

And Matthew Stafford — what a response. Forty of 54 for 383 yards, three touchdowns and, most importantly, zero interceptions. That ends the streak of an interception in every game this season. Last week he said “crawling in a hole — not an option,” and he backed it up with his cleanest game of the year. After the game, he said: “I love the fight that we had as a team.” And on the overtime: he credited the defense for giving the offense another chance with Landman’s forced fumble. Stafford also said it’s “tough to find a perfect game,” and that the offense is going to “try to go out there and score every time we touch the ball.” Those 98-yard and 78-yard drives in Philly weren’t a fluke. The final drive in regulation against Buffalo — 62 yards in just over a minute to tie the game — was another example of a quarterback at his best when it matters most.

Davante Adams had two touchdowns. Blake Corum had a rushing touchdown and a 34-yard catch. Kyren Williams scored the game-tying touchdown with five seconds left. And Nate Landman — who missed practice early in the week with a shoulder injury — forced two fumbles, including the one in overtime. Jaylen Watson came back from a dislocated shoulder and intercepted Josh Allen. Aaron Donald recovered a fumble in overtime. And Josaiah Stewart came up with a third-down sack that held Buffalo to a field goal.

Donald’s night deserves its own moment. In his first game back from the back injury, he had a sack wiped out by a penalty in the secondary, and then he was the one who fell on the football in overtime to give the Rams a chance to win. Those are the kinds of plays that don’t always show up on the stat sheet, but they show you what he still means to this team. And Watson playing through a shoulder harness and intercepting Allen? That tells you everything about the toughness in that secondary. The defense gave the Rams every opportunity it could in overtime. The offense moved the ball into range. The final step — the kick — is where it fell apart.

Now, the honest part — the things that need to be fixed beyond the kicking game.

The run defense got gashed. Buffalo rushed for 234 yards. James Cook had 25 carries for 111 yards and two touchdowns. And Josh Allen ran ten times for 121 yards — and according to CBS Sports, 107 of those yards came on scrambles. In my preview, I said the key to stopping Allen was to keep him in the pocket and tackle him when he runs. The Rams did that for a half — remember, Allen had just 21 rushing yards at halftime. But in the second half and overtime, he escaped again and again. Including the scramble to the Rams’ 27-yard line that set up the game-winning field goal.

The pass rush wasn’t consistent enough either. The Rams officially recorded just one sack — Stewart’s — after Donald’s was wiped out by a penalty. Against a quarterback who’s less efficient against pressure, one sack isn’t enough. When Myles Garrett returns — expected around Week 9, according to reporting from Ian Rapoport — that should help. But the Rams can’t wait for Garrett to fix everything.

And the penalties are still a problem. The two teams combined for 24 penalties. The Rams had two on the opening drive alone, and a false start right after Watson’s interception. Last week, we talked about how the Rams had 21 accepted penalties in their previous two games — the most in back-to-back games in McVay’s ten seasons. It didn’t get cleaned up the way it needed to.

And here’s a painful bit of irony. The last time these two teams met, in December of 2024, the Rams won 44-42 — and one of the plays that decided it was a blocked punt returned for a touchdown by the Rams. Special teams won that game for Los Angeles. This time, a blocked kick decided it the other way. And it was Greg Rousseau — the pass rusher who came into the night leading the NFL in sacks — who got a hand on it. Buffalo’s best defensive player made the biggest play of the game, and he did it on special teams.

Give Buffalo credit, too. The Bills came across the country, banged up, without Ed Oliver and Joshua Palmer, coming off a home loss to the Patriots. First-year head coach Joe Brady faced his first real adversity of the season, and his team responded with a road win in primetime. They’re 4-1 now. That’s a dangerous team, and the Rams may very well see them again in February if both teams keep climbing. And remember, the Super Bowl is at SoFi this season.

Now let me grade my own predictions, because I always do. I picked Rams 31, Bills 27. I got the result wrong. I said Stafford would play a game without an interception — that one I got right. I said Puka would go over 100 yards — right, with 138. I said the defense would make a big play on Allen in the second half — Landman’s overtime forced fumble, so I’ll take partial credit. But the key I called the most important — clean special teams — is exactly what lost the game. I said in our preview that in a game where the spread is a field goal, special teams could literally decide the outcome. Unfortunately, that’s exactly what happened.

Now, the standings — and they’re not pretty. The Rams are 2-3. The Bills are 4-1. In the NFC West, the 49ers are 5-0 after beating Seattle on Sunday, and the Seahawks are 3-2. That puts the Rams three games behind San Francisco, who already beat them in Melbourne, and a game behind the defending Super Bowl champions. According to Pro Football Talk, the Rams are now 1-3 in primetime games this season.

But here’s what I need every Rams fan to hear: the season is not over. Not even close. There are twelve games left. The Rams still play the 49ers on December 13th and the Seahawks twice — on Christmas Day and in the regular-season finale. Myles Garrett is on the way back. And the next stretch of the schedule is very manageable: the Arizona Cardinals at SoFi this Sunday, then the Raiders, the Chargers and Washington. And look at the losses. Melbourne was lopsided, but the other two came by four points in Denver and by three points in overtime tonight — and in both of those games, special teams mistakes played a major role. The Rams are close. They’re just not finishing.

And McVay knows it. After the game, he said: “This stings, and it’s going to hurt. But we can’t let this team beat us twice.” In other words: feel it tonight, then move on, because if you let this loss linger, it’ll cost you the next game too. That’s the challenge for this week. The Cardinals come to SoFi on Sunday, and the Rams cannot afford a hangover.

And the Cardinals game is bigger than it looks. Arizona came into this weekend at 1-3, winless since its Week 1 victory. The Rams host them on Sunday at 1:05 p.m. Pacific at SoFi, and then they’ll see the Cardinals again on the road in Week 10, right before the bye. Two division games against the team at the bottom of the NFC West in a span of five weeks. If the Rams are going to climb back into the division race, these are the games they have to win — and win convincingly. You don’t make up three games on the 49ers by beating them once in December. You make it up by stacking wins in October and November.

McVay also said something else that I think tells you about the culture in that locker room: “Really proud of our group... and I’m sick that we came up short.” Both of those things can be true at once. This team fought back from seven points down with a minute left. It forced turnovers in big moments. It didn’t quit. That’s the kind of resilience that matters over a seventeen-game season. But pride doesn’t count in the standings. Wins do.

And the timing of this loss makes the next few weeks even more important. The trade deadline is November 10th. The Rams have limited cap space and no 2027 first-round pick, so a blockbuster move was always unlikely — and nothing about tonight changes that. But a kicker, a special-teams specialist or a veteran run defender? Those are the kinds of smaller, cheaper moves a contender can still make. And remember, the Rams already added former All-Pro linebacker Eric Kendricks to the practice squad last week. With the run defense giving up 234 yards, don’t be surprised if the conversation about linebacker depth picks up again.

So what needs to happen this week? Three things. One: decide the kicking situation. Whether that means sticking with Mevis and fixing the protection, or bringing in competition, McVay said he’s evaluating everything — and he has to come out of this week with an answer the whole team trusts. Two: fix the run defense. Giving up 234 rushing yards is not who this defense has been all season, and the division opponents ahead — especially San Francisco — will run right at that weakness. Three: be more aggressive in the big moments. McVay admitted he wished he had gone for it to get closer before the overtime kick. That’s a lesson. When you have a quarterback playing at this level and a receiver who just caught 17 passes, trust them to finish the drive.

Here’s my bottom line, Rams House. This loss hurts more than most because the Rams did so many things right. Stafford was clean. Puka made history. The offense scored 34 points. The defense forced turnovers in big moments. And they still lost — because the kicking game failed again, the run defense cracked late, and one decision in overtime didn’t go their way. The good news is that every one of those problems is fixable. The bad news is that they’ve been fixable for a long time, and they’re still not fixed. At 2-3, the margin for error is gone. The Rams don’t need to be perfect over the next five weeks. They just need to stop losing games they should win.
