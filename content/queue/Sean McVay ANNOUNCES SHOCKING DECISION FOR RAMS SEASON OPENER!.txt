Forty-seven seconds. Sean McVay has now had a full day to look at the film of those 47 seconds, and the decisions he’s making this week are all about making sure it never happens again. But before we go any further, I want to clear something up, because I’ve seen headlines calling this a “shocking decision for the Rams’ season opener.” The Rams’ season opener already happened — on September 10th in Melbourne, Australia, a 27-7 loss to the San Francisco 49ers. There’s no new opener and no shocking announcement about it. What is real is this: after the 18-16 loss to Arizona, McVay is facing the hardest week of his season. He has to sign the Rams’ third kicker of the year. He has to decide whether Trent McDuffie plays. He has to replace Davante Adams for six to 10 weeks. And he has to fix a defense that held Arizona without a touchdown for 59 minutes — and then gave up a game-winning field goal drive in the final 47 seconds. So today, we’re going inside those last 47 seconds, play by play. We’ll give Colby Parkinson the credit he deserves, ask the honest questions about the final defensive series, and look at every decision McVay has to make before Sunday in Las Vegas. And think about how much has changed since that opener. In Melbourne, Kyren Williams scored the Rams’ only touchdown. Myles Garrett played in that game — and then had knee surgery the following Monday and hasn’t played since. Davante Adams was healthy. Harrison Mevis was the kicker. Six weeks later, Garrett is still on injured reserve, Adams is out for six to 10 weeks, the Rams are on their third kicker, and they’re 2-4. The opener feels like a lifetime ago. And that’s why the decisions this week matter far more than anything about that first game.

Let’s start with the drive that should have been the story of the season so far.

The Rams’ offense had been stuck all afternoon. According to Pro Football Talk, they had fewer than 100 yards at halftime and three three-and-outs. According to the AP, the NFL’s top-ranked offense coming into the game was held to 286 total yards. Arizona controlled the ball for 34 minutes and 52 seconds. The Rams turned the ball over twice in Cardinals territory in the fourth quarter — a Tyler Higbee fumble forced by linebacker Cody Simon with 7:47 left, and a turnover on downs after a false start turned fourth-and-3 into fourth-and-8. And they were trailing 15-9 with time running out.

Then Matthew Stafford found something. According to the Rams’ official website, he led an 86-yard touchdown drive in just over two minutes. The play that changed everything was a 42-yard strike to Puka Nacua that got the Rams to the Cardinals’ 35 with 1:50 left. Puka added a 16-yard catch to get to the 13. Kyren Williams picked up seven. And then Stafford found Colby Parkinson near the goal line.

The officials first ruled Parkinson down at the 1. The play went to review. And the replay showed he’d broken the plane. Touchdown. The Rams’ punter, Ethan Evans, who had never kicked in an NFL game, came out with backup quarterback Stetson Bennett holding, and drilled the extra point. Rams 16, Cardinals 15, with 47 seconds left.

And think about that extra point for a second. According to Yahoo Sports, Evans had never attempted a field goal in an NFL game, and in his senior season at Wingate University, he made 10 of 18 field goal attempts with a long of 41. He walked onto the field at SoFi Stadium with the game on the line and calmly made the kick. After the game, McVay said it was “pretty cool to be able to see Ethan punch in the extra point and Stetson to be able to hold.” That’s a moment Evans and Bennett will remember for the rest of their lives.

And here’s a stat I find remarkable: this is now three straight weeks that Stafford has led a touchdown drive in the final two minutes. In Philadelphia, the Rams took the lead with 1:10 left on Kyren Williams’ second touchdown. Against Buffalo, Stafford led a 62-yard drive and Kyren tied the game with five seconds left. And against Arizona, it was the 86-yard drive and the touchdown to Parkinson with 47 seconds to go. When the game is on the line, this quarterback delivers. The problem is everything that happens before and after those drives.

Now let’s give Colby Parkinson his flowers, because the loss shouldn’t swallow what he did.

Parkinson spent part of this season fighting through injuries. He missed the Eagles game with knee and shoulder injuries, and he was limited in practice last week with a shoulder issue before practicing fully on Friday. And when the Rams needed a touchdown with the season on the line, Stafford trusted him.

And this isn’t the first time. Rams fans, remember the wild-card round last January? On January 10th, 2026, against the Carolina Panthers, Stafford threw a 19-yard touchdown pass to Colby Parkinson with 38 seconds left to win the game 34-31. After that game, Parkinson described the throw like this: “Like perfect. It couldn’t have been placed any better.” And Stafford explained his mindset: “You can make it stressful or you can make it calm and collected.”

So twice now, with less than a minute left and the game on the line, Stafford has gone to Parkinson in the end zone. That’s not a coincidence. That’s trust. And with Davante Adams out for six to 10 weeks and tight end Terrance Ferguson on injured reserve, Parkinson’s role in this offense is about to get even bigger. When the Rams line up in their heavy tight end sets with Parkinson, Tyler Higbee and Davis Allen, Parkinson is the big target Stafford looks for when space gets tight.

So yes — the result hurt. But Parkinson did his job. Remember that.

And Puka deserves a mention, too. He caught six passes for 80 yards against Arizona — and 59 of those yards came on the final drive. After the game, he didn’t make excuses: “I think there’s just a lack of execution, obviously on our offensive side of the ball.” But he also made sure people knew where the locker room stands: “We’re still a connected group. There’s no pointing fingers, and there’s no arguing.” After a loss like this, that matters.

Now let’s go to the other side. The final 47 seconds.

After the extra point, the Rams kicked off for a touchback. Arizona needed a field goal, and they had a kicker in Chad Ryland who had already made five of them. Here’s how it went, according to the Rams’ official website. On the first play, Jacoby Brissett hit Jalen Brooks for 21 yards. On that play, cornerback Josh Wallace was flagged for unnecessary roughness, adding 15 more yards. Tyler Allgeier ran for five. And Ryland lined up from 43 yards and won it as time expired.

That’s it. One completion, one penalty, one run and a kick. Thirty-six of Arizona’s yards on that drive came from a single snap and the flag that came with it.

After the game, Mike LaFleur said: “Deep down, I thought we were going to win the game when we had 51 seconds.” And McVay summed up the Rams’ side in one sentence: “on the very first play of the two-minute,” the Rams gave up a big play, “and then they tack on the 15.”

Now, I want to be careful here, because a lot of channels will just scream blame. I don’t know the exact assignments on that final series, and anyone who says they know without studying the all-22 film is guessing. But there are fair questions to ask.

Question one: the coverage. When the opponent needs only a field goal and has under a minute, the priority is usually to take away the sideline and the intermediate throws that set up a kick. The 21-yard completion to Brooks did exactly what Arizona needed. Was the coverage built to stop that, or did the Cardinals simply execute a well-designed play? That’s for the film.

Question two: the penalty. A 15-yard unnecessary roughness call in that situation is the definition of a self-inflicted wound. Whether it was a split-second judgment or a lack of discipline, McVay has been preaching all season that the Rams “won’t win a game unless we figure out how not to beat ourselves.” That penalty was the biggest example yet. And I want to be fair to Josh Wallace. He’s been one of the toughest players on this defense. He was limited in practice last week with a shoulder issue before practicing fully on Friday, and with McDuffie out, he and Emmanuel Forbes Jr. had to take on bigger roles, according to Yahoo Sports. One play doesn’t define a player. But it’s a lesson every defender on this roster has to take with them to Las Vegas.

And question three: the pass rush. With Myles Garrett still on injured reserve, did the Rams have enough pressure on that final series to force Brissett into a bad throw? Against Arizona, the Rams’ defense created pressure at times — Aaron Donald had a tackle for loss and pressure on a third down early in the game — but the final drive was over almost before it started.

There’s also one more part of the sequence that fans have been talking about: the replay review. Had Parkinson been ruled down at the 1 and the call stood, the Rams would have had another snap to score from the 1-yard line, and the clock situation would have been different. Of course, the Rams still needed a touchdown either way — they were trailing by six. But it’s a reminder of how small details shape the end of a close game. That wasn’t a coaching decision. It was just football.

And it’s worth remembering how much the missing kicker shaped this game long before the final drive. With Grupe out after the first quarter, McVay ran out the clock at halftime instead of trying for a field goal. After Blake Corum’s touchdown in the second quarter, the Rams had to go for two, and Stafford was sacked and fumbled. In the third quarter, the Rams went for it on fourth-and-1 at the Cardinals’ 27 — a spot where they’d normally have a reasonable field goal try — and were stopped. In a game decided by two points, those were real points left on the field. None of that happens if Grupe stays healthy.

Now, here’s the important context. This defense was excellent for most of the afternoon. According to the Rams’ official website, Arizona reached the red zone five times — and the Rams held them to a field goal every single time. Zero touchdowns allowed. Quentin Lake intercepted Brissett on the Cardinals’ first possession. And they did it without Trent McDuffie, who was inactive with a groin injury. That’s why the ending hurts so much. It wasn’t a defense that got blown out. It was a defense that held and held — and then didn’t.

Now let’s zoom out, because there’s a bigger pattern here.

The Rams’ four losses this season: 27-7 to San Francisco in Melbourne, 30-26 to Denver, 37-34 in overtime to Buffalo, and 18-16 to Arizona. That’s three losses by a combined nine points — four, three and two. And in each of those three close losses, special teams mistakes were part of the story. In Denver, according to theScore, special teams mistakes helped cost the Rams the game. Against Buffalo, it was a missed field goal and a blocked 48-yarder in overtime. Against Arizona, it was a long kickoff return, the kicker injured on coverage, and no field goal operation for the rest of the game.

And the late-game defense has been a theme in two of those games. Against Buffalo in overtime, Khalil Shakir caught a 57-yard pass that set up Tyler Bass’ game-winning field goal. Against Arizona, it was the 21-yard completion and the 15-yard penalty. According to the AP, the Rams have allowed 30 or more points in seven of their last 14 games, and they’re 2-5 in those games.

But I also want to be fair, because this defense has closed games before. In Philadelphia in Week 4, the Rams’ defense made a goal-line stand at the 2-yard line, and the Eagles went 0-for-12 on third down — the first time in franchise history Philadelphia had done that. So this group knows how to finish. It just hasn’t done it consistently.

And let’s give Arizona its credit, too. Chad Ryland was perfect: six field goals from 34, 30, 22, 38, 27 and 43 yards. After the game, he said: “That’s being a good situationally aware football player and football team.” It was Mike LaFleur’s first win over his former boss, and according to the Cardinals’ website, it was Arizona’s first NFC West win since the 2024 season. LaFleur said he still wants to review why his offense didn’t score a touchdown on five red zone trips. But on Sunday, it didn’t matter.

That’s the difference between a good team and a great team. Good teams play well for 59 minutes. Great teams own the final 60 seconds.

Now let’s get to the decisions McVay has to make this week — because these are the real decisions, not the fake headlines.

Decision number one: the kicker. Blake Grupe, who made a 44-yarder in his Rams debut, tore a knee ligament making a tackle on the ensuing kickoff. According to the AP, he’s out for the season. That means the Rams have to sign their third kicker of the season before Sunday’s game in Las Vegas. According to NFL Network’s Mike Garafolo, the Rams worked out two veterans: Brandon McManus, who’s 34 and went 10 for 10 with Green Bay last season, and Matt Prater, who’s 42 and went 18 of 20 with Buffalo last season. Whichever one they choose, he’ll have only a few days to build timing with long snapper Joe Cardona and holder Ethan Evans.

Decision number two: Trent McDuffie. According to the AP, McVay said McDuffie is “feeling much better” after his groin injury, but the Rams won’t decide on his status for the Raiders game until later in the week. McDuffie has allowed the lowest passer rating in coverage of any cornerback in the NFL this season, according to Yahoo Sports. Getting him back would immediately make the last-minute defense stronger.

Decision number three: the defensive line. The injuries kept coming on Monday. According to the AP, defensive tackle Poona Ford is out for the season with a ruptured triceps. Ford is a nine-year veteran in his second season with the Rams and had been playing just under 40 percent of the defensive snaps. And Kobie Turner is going on injured reserve with a groin injury — McVay says he’ll miss “a handful of games.” That leaves Aaron Donald and Braden Fiske as the remaining starters, with Tyler Davis, Larrell Murchison and Ty Hamilton taking on bigger roles, according to the Los Angeles Times. McVay will have to decide how to rotate that group to keep Donald fresh for the fourth quarter — because the fourth quarter is exactly where this defense needs him most.

Decision number four: replacing Davante Adams. McVay confirmed Adams will miss six to 10 weeks with a significant hamstring injury. According to the AP, Adams led the NFL in touchdown catches last season, and this year he has 30 catches for 480 yards and four touchdowns. McVay said: “It’s significant when you don’t have a player like Davante, but we have to be able to adjust.” And then: “His contributions can’t be measured. You don’t replace somebody like him.”

And here’s the challenge. According to the AP, Konata Mumpfield has eight catches for 127 yards — the third-most by a Rams receiver this season. And last season, Stafford threw to his depth receivers less often than any other quarterback on a playoff team. So McVay has a decision to make: does he trust Mumpfield, Tutu Atwell, Jordan Whittington and rookie CJ Daniels with bigger roles? Does he lean even harder on the tight ends — Parkinson, Higbee and Davis Allen? Or does he look outside the building? According to the Los Angeles Times, the Rams may add a receiver. But as of now, no move has been made.

And decision number five, the one that matters most: the late-game plan. Right after the game, McVay didn’t hold back: “There’s nothing positive that I can say about today.” He called it “a good, humbling day,” and added: “This is the challenging part of sports.” He also said: “I have to do a much better job.” He said, “I don’t have those answers right now, otherwise it would have gotten fixed.” And when asked about the team’s identity, he said: “We’re figuring out. I can’t answer that right now.”

So what should McVay change? Let me give you my list of fixable things — no panic, no roster overhaul.

Fix number one: situational coverage. When an opponent needs a field goal with under a minute left, the priority is to keep everything in front, deny the sideline and force them to use the middle of the field, where the clock keeps running. Review exactly how that 21-yard completion developed and make sure the call matches the situation next time. This is also a big week for defensive coordinator Chris Shula. His defense has been one of the most aggressive in football this season — his blitz rate went from under 14 percent in Week 1 to more than 50 percent against the Eagles. That aggression has created a lot of big stops. But in a two-minute situation where the opponent only needs a field goal, Shula has to decide: send pressure and risk a big play, or drop more defenders into coverage and make the offense earn every yard. That decision could define the next close game.

Fix number two: discipline. A 15-yard penalty on the final drive has to be coached out. It’s not about effort — the Rams’ players are clearly playing hard. It’s about controlling that effort in the moments when one flag can decide a game.

Fix number three: the special teams contingency. The Rams were left without a field goal operation for nearly the whole game after Grupe got hurt. That forced McVay to run out the clock before halftime, go for two in the second quarter, and go for it on fourth-and-1 at the Cardinals’ 27 in the third. McVay said it directly: “We’re playing for a touchdown. We didn’t have a field goal operation.” Every team needs a plan B for that. The Rams need one now.

Fix number four: protect what works. The red zone defense was outstanding. Holding a team to zero touchdowns on five trips is a real accomplishment. Don’t overhaul what’s working. Just tighten the final minute.

And fix number five: start faster on offense. Stafford’s final drive showed what this offense can do. But it shouldn’t take until the last two minutes to find it. Stafford said it plainly: “Football is a game of execution.” And he added: “We are what our record is.”

None of that requires blowing up the roster. It requires cleaner execution in the moments where the margin is smallest.

And the Rams’ players seem to understand that. Safety Quentin Lake said there’s “no sense of panic” in the building, but “there has to be a sense of urgency.” McVay called the stretch ahead “a good test.” And he added: “We’ve got to be able to get up and keep swingin’.” That’s the right message. The question is whether the execution matches it on Sunday.

Now let’s look ahead to Sunday, because the Rams don’t have time to dwell on this.

The Rams travel to Las Vegas to face the Raiders. According to Fox Sports, Las Vegas is 3-3 under head coach Klint Kubiak, with Kirk Cousins at quarterback. Cousins has thrown for 1,582 yards, 14 touchdowns and five interceptions. The Raiders score 27 points per game and allow 27 points per game, and they’re giving up more than 237 passing yards per game. The Rams are 5.5-point favorites, according to Fox Sports. The Raiders haven’t run the ball well, averaging just 79.2 rushing yards per game, which is good news for a Rams defensive line that just lost Poona Ford and Kobie Turner. And the Rams’ pass defense has been one of the best in the league, allowing just 167 passing yards per game, according to Fox Sports. On paper, this is a game the Rams should win. And it’s a little bittersweet, too: Davante Adams spent parts of three seasons with the Raiders, and he’ll miss his return to Las Vegas.

After Las Vegas, the Rams host the Chargers and then travel to Washington on November 8th — two days before the trade deadline. Those three games will decide whether this front office is adding pieces or bracing for a long season.

And here’s the scary part, given what just happened: Cousins is a veteran quarterback who knows how to run a two-minute drill. If this game is close late, the Rams’ defense will face exactly the kind of test it failed on Sunday. That’s why the late-game fixes matter so much this week.

Here are my three keys for Sunday.

Key number one: finish the half and finish the game. No free yards in the final two minutes of either half. Keep everything in front.

Key number two: get Parkinson and the tight ends involved early. Without Adams, the tight ends are going to be a huge part of this passing game. Stafford trusts Parkinson in the biggest moments. Use him before the biggest moments, too.

Key number three: a clean day for the new kicker. Whoever it is, the Rams need a simple, clean operation. Snap, hold, kick. No drama.

Here’s my prediction, on the record. Rams 27, Raiders 20. Puka goes over 100 yards. Parkinson catches a touchdown. The new kicker makes every extra point and at least one field goal. And the defense gets a late stop to seal it — the exact moment it didn’t get against Arizona. I’ve missed two straight predictions, so hold me to this one.

So here’s my bottom line. There was no shocking decision about the Rams’ season opener — that game happened back in September. What’s real is a 2-4 team that keeps losing close games because of mistakes in the biggest moments. Colby Parkinson gave the Rams the moment they needed: a six-yard, go-ahead touchdown with 47 seconds left. But football doesn’t end when the ball crosses the goal line. It ends when the clock hits zero. And this week, McVay’s job is to make sure the Rams finally own those last 47 seconds.
