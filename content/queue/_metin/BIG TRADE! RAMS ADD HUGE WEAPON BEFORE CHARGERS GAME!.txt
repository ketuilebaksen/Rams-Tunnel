Let’s start with the real part, because the clip is built on top of it.

Real: On October 28th, ESPN’s Matt Bowen and Jeremy Fowler ranked the top 25 trade candidates before the deadline. According to ESPN, A’Shawn Robinson was ranked 21st, with a 30 percent chance of being traded. And the teams ESPN listed as fits for him were the Rams, the Ravens and the Giants — in that order. So when the clip says ESPN listed the Rams first, that’s true.

But here’s what that means and what it doesn’t. A 30 percent chance means ESPN thinks it’s more likely Robinson stays in Tampa Bay than gets traded at all. And “team fit” means the writers think he’d make sense for the Rams — not that the Rams have called. It’s an opinion about fit, not a report of talks.

And it’s worth knowing how ESPN’s list treated the Rams overall, because it tells you something. On that same list, according to ESPN, the Rams were named as a fit for exactly two players: Robinson, at 30 percent, and Vikings receiver Jauan Jennings, at 50 percent. ESPN did not list the Rams as a fit for Josh Downs — those fits were the Chiefs and Steelers — or for Khalil Mack, whose fits were the Bears, Browns, Bills and Cowboys. So if you’re trying to figure out which rumors have the most support from people who actually cover the league, Robinson and Jennings are the two names where ESPN’s own writers put the Rams on the list. That doesn’t make either deal likely. But it does make them more grounded than most of what you’ll see in fake clips.

Now let’s talk about A’Shawn Robinson, because he’s a real player with a real story.

According to Yahoo Sports, Robinson is 31 years old, 6-foot-3 and 320 pounds, and he’s on his fifth NFL team. From my own knowledge, he came into the league with the Detroit Lions in 2016 out of Alabama. Then he came to the Rams. According to SB Nation, via Yahoo Sports, Robinson won a Super Bowl with the Rams in 2021. Rams fans will remember him as one of the big bodies in the middle of that defensive line, playing next to Aaron Donald.

That 2021 season ended with the Rams beating the Bengals in Super Bowl LVI at SoFi Stadium — the same building where the Rams play the Chargers today. That’s my own memory of that game, and I think it’s a nice detail. If Robinson came back, he’d be returning to the field where he won a ring.

After the Rams, according to Yahoo Sports, he spent 2023 with the New York Giants, where he had 62 tackles and played in all 17 games. Then, according to SB Nation, he spent the last two seasons with the Carolina Panthers under former Rams assistant Ejiro Evero. And this offseason, according to Heavy, he signed a one-year, 10-million-dollar deal with the Buccaneers.

And he’s playing well. According to SB Nation, Robinson has 20 tackles, a sack and two interceptions this season, and 16 career sacks. Two interceptions in one season is really unusual for a defensive tackle. According to Heavy, earlier this month, Pro Football Focus gave him an 89.5 overall grade — the highest among defensive linemen with at least 90 snaps at the time — and an 88.8 run-defense grade.

So the clip gets some of his numbers right. The 16 career sacks and the two interceptions this season check out. What doesn’t check out is the “confirmation” and the “this trade is going to happen.”

Now, why would Robinson make sense for the Rams? There are real reasons.

Reason number one: the Rams have a hole in the middle of the defensive line. According to Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak, Poona Ford is out for the season and Kobie Turner is on injured reserve, though he’s expected back at some point. That’s two important interior linemen missing. Asking backups to play a lot of snaps against run-heavy teams for weeks is a real risk.

Reason number two: Aaron Donald needs help. According to the Rams’ official website, Donald is being double-teamed at the highest rate of his career — 76 percent of the time — and he still doesn’t have a sack in five appearances. A big body like Robinson next to him would make it harder for offenses to slide two blockers toward Donald on every play. And that’s exactly the job the clip described — “holding the interior” so others can get free. That part of the clip is actually a good explanation of what a player like Robinson does.

Reason number three: he knows the building. Robinson already played for the Rams. He knows the culture, the defensive language and the man lining up next to him. In the middle of the season, there’s no time for a long learning curve. A player you trade for in November has to contribute right away.

And let me explain why the interior of the defensive line matters so much, because it’s a position that rarely gets attention. A defensive tackle lines up right across from the center and guards — the biggest players on the field. On a running play, his first job is to not get pushed back. If he holds his ground, the running back has nowhere to go. If he gets moved off the ball, the whole defense is in trouble, because the linebackers behind him get blocked too. That’s my own explanation, but it’s why a player with an 88.8 run-defense grade from Pro Football Focus is so valuable even if he doesn’t put up big sack numbers.

And on passing plays, a big, strong interior lineman collapses the pocket from the middle. A quarterback can step up to avoid an edge rusher — but if the middle of the pocket is caving in, there’s nowhere to step. That’s what makes pass rushers on the outside more effective. So when the clip said Robinson “holds the interior,” that’s the one part that was well explained. It’s just attached to a fake “confirmation.”

There’s another reason this matters for today’s game specifically. According to NFL.com, Chargers running back Omarion Hampton leads the team with 289 rushing yards. With Ford out and Turner on injured reserve, the Rams’ interior will be tested by a team that wants to take pressure off Justin Herbert by running the ball. That’s exactly the kind of matchup where a player like Robinson would help.

Reason number four: the cost. According to Heavy, The Athletic’s Daniel Popper projected that Robinson would carry a cap hit of roughly 2 million dollars for an acquiring team this season. That’s a projection, not an official number. But if it’s close, Robinson is a lot more affordable than someone like Khalil Mack, who is owed about 5.3 million for the rest of the season, according to ESPN’s Bill Barnwell.

Now let’s be honest about why it might not happen, because there are several real obstacles.

But first, let me answer the skeptic, because there’s a fair argument against Robinson. Someone might say: “A defensive tackle with 16 career sacks in 11 seasons isn’t a difference-maker.” And if you’re judging him as a pass rusher, that’s true. Robinson has never been a big sack guy. But that’s not what he’d be for. He’d be a run defender and a space-eater — the guy who takes on blockers so Donald, Byron Young and eventually Garrett can make the plays that show up in the box score. According to FOX Sports, Byron Young leads the Rams with four sacks this season. If Robinson can take a blocker away from Young and Donald, those numbers could go up. His value would show up in other players’ stats.

And there’s a depth argument, too. If Turner comes back later in the season, the Rams wouldn’t just have a starter back — they’d have a real rotation. Robinson, Turner and Donald sharing snaps in December and January would keep everybody fresher for the playoff push. Defensive linemen wear down over a long season. Having three quality interior players instead of one or two makes a big difference late in the year. That’s my own reasoning, but it’s why a move like this can matter more than it looks on paper.

Problem number one: the Rams’ cap space. According to Athlon Sports, citing Over The Cap, the Rams have 206,406 dollars in cap space. Even a 2-million-dollar cap hit is about 10 times what the Rams have. So any deal would require a restructure — like the Kyren Williams restructure that Grisak estimated could free about 3.5 million dollars — or sending salary back to Tampa Bay.

Problem number two: competition. ESPN listed the Ravens and Giants as fits, too. According to Heavy, The Athletic’s Daniel Popper listed the Packers as a possible landing spot. And according to Heavy, Pro Football Focus listed the 49ers, Bears, Patriots and Raiders as trade fits. That’s a lot of teams who could use a run-stuffing veteran. If a team with more picks wants him, the Rams could easily get outbid.

Problem number three: the Buccaneers might not sell. According to A to Z Sports, the Bucs are 2-5 and play the Falcons this week in what it called a must-win game. If Tampa Bay wins and gets back into the race, they might decide to keep their veterans. If they lose and fall to 2-6, selling makes a lot more sense.

And what would the Rams even give up? According to Grisak, the Rams’ 2027 picks are a second, a fourth, a fifth and a sixth, and in 2028 they’re only missing their second-round pick. Grisak’s proposal for Robinson was a 2028 late-round pick. That’s realistic for a 31-year-old on a one-year deal. But if the Ravens or Giants offer a fifth instead of a sixth, the Rams would have to decide whether a slightly better pick is worth it. With so few picks left, every round matters.

Problem number four: McVay’s approach. This week, according to the LA Times, McVay was asked whether there had been any new trade discussions, and his first answer was “No.” According to the New York Post, he said: “Hopefully, some of those trades will be guys getting back from injury that haven’t played for us.” Kobie Turner is one of those guys. If the Rams believe Turner will be back soon, they might decide they don’t need Robinson.

And problem number five: the “freeing Myles Garrett” part of the clip. Garrett isn’t on the field yet. According to the Rams’ official website, the team didn’t open his 21-day practice window this week, and McVay said: “We’re just on a week-to-week basis right now.” So right now, Robinson wouldn’t be freeing Garrett for anything. He’d be helping a defense that’s still waiting on its best pass rusher.

Now, let me compare Robinson to the other names being talked about, because the Rams probably can’t afford all of them.

According to Grisak’s October 29th article, he proposed three trades: Josh Downs for a 2027 fourth, A’Shawn Robinson for a 2028 late-round pick and Khalil Mack for a 2028 late-round pick swap. Of those three, Robinson might be the most practical on defense. Downs, who requested a trade according to ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler, fills the most urgent need on offense. And Mack is the most exciting, but the most expensive and the least connected to the Rams.

And remember Grisak’s other article about Les Snead’s trade history. According to Grisak, Snead has made four trades on deadline day itself, two trades eight days before the deadline and one the day before. He suggested Monday is the most realistic day for a Rams move next week. So if the Rams are going to do something, the next real window is likely Monday — after today’s game.

So here’s what I’ll be watching this week. On Monday, look for any Rams roster moves, especially a contract restructure. A restructure is usually the first sign that a team is clearing space for a trade. On Wednesday, look at McVay’s press conference for any change in his tone about the deadline, and at whether the Rams open Myles Garrett’s practice window. If Garrett’s window opens, the Rams might feel even less need to add anyone on defense. And then on Sunday, the Rams play Washington, two days before the deadline at 4 p.m. Eastern on Tuesday, November 10th. That game could decide whether the Rams are buyers at all.

Let me give you my honest take, and remember — this is not a report.

I actually like the idea of a Robinson reunion more than most of the names being thrown around. He’s affordable compared to the other options, he’s playing well, he fills a real need and he already knows the Rams. If the Rams are going to make one quiet, sensible move on defense, this is the kind of move I’d want.

But I’d put the chances that it actually happens well below 50 percent. ESPN itself only gives him a 30 percent chance of being traded at all, there are several other teams that fit, and McVay is leaning on players returning from injury. If it happens, it’ll likely be close to the deadline, for a late-round pick — not because Adam Schefter “confirmed” anything on First Take.

Now let’s look at today’s game, because that’s the only thing that’s actually happening this afternoon.

According to Yahoo Sports, the Rams host the Chargers at 4:05 p.m. Eastern on FOX, with Adam Amin, Drew Brees and Kristina Pink on the call. The Rams are 3-4. The Chargers are 0-6. According to the AP, the Rams are favored by six and a half.

Here are some numbers that matter, according to NFL.com’s game center. Matthew Stafford has 1,989 passing yards, 14 touchdowns and seven interceptions this season. Kyren Williams has 492 rushing yards and five touchdowns. Puka Nacua has 485 receiving yards. On the Chargers’ side, Herbert has 1,141 passing yards, five touchdowns and eight interceptions. The Rams’ offense is first in the NFL in yards per game, with 399.1. The Chargers are last in the league in scoring, at 15 points per game. But both teams have a turnover differential of minus-5, tied for 29th in the league. And the Rams have just 10 sacks, which ranks 27th.

So the Rams can move the ball. The question is whether they can turn yards into points and stop giving the ball away. According to the Rams’ official website, the Chargers have the most giveaways in the NFL with 15, and Justin Herbert has thrown an interception in every game. This is a great chance for the Rams to finally win the turnover battle.

There’s one more number that tells you something about this Rams offense. According to the Rams’ official website, Stafford completed 79 percent of his passes against the Raiders, his highest as a Ram — but he’s averaged under five air yards per attempt in his last two games. Offensive coordinator Nate Scheelhaase said: “There are some games, like the last one, where your biggest gain is 17 yards.” So the Rams have been efficient but not explosive. Against a Chargers secondary missing Derwin James and Deane Leonard, this would be a great week to change that.

The injury report, according to the Rams’ official website: Joe Cardona is doubtful, so Jake McQuaide would be elevated to snap, according to McVay. Coleman Shelton, Colby Parkinson, Trent McDuffie and Jordan Whittington are all questionable. If Shelton can’t play, McVay said Dylan McMahon “would be the guy that would step up” at center.

For the Chargers, according to their website, Derwin James, Kayode Awosika, Deane Leonard and Brenen Thompson are out. And Khalil Mack is off the injury report and will play.

Here are my three keys.

Key number one: protect Stafford from Mack. If McMahon starts at center, the protection calls have to be clean. Run the ball, use quick throws and chip Mack with tight ends and backs.

Key number two: win the turnover battle. McVay said this week that turnover margin is one of the stats that matters most. The Chargers lead the league in giveaways. Get two takeaways and protect the ball, and the Rams should win comfortably.

Key number three: control the line of scrimmage on defense — which is exactly the reason Robinson keeps coming up. Without Ford and Turner, the Rams’ interior has to hold up against the Chargers’ run game. If it does, maybe the Rams feel less urgency to trade. If it doesn’t, Robinson’s name will get even louder by Monday.

My prediction: Rams 27, Chargers 17. The Rams have the best offense by yards in the league, according to NFL.com, and they’re facing the lowest-scoring team in football. If the Rams just avoid giving the ball away, their efficiency should be enough. But a winless team coming off a bye has had two weeks to prepare for this game, and Mack will want to make a statement in front of every contender watching before the deadline. So I expect a closer first half than people think, and then the Rams pulling away in the second half.

And here’s one thing to watch in the box score that ties back to today’s video: the Chargers’ rushing numbers. If the Rams hold the Chargers under 100 rushing yards with Ford and Turner out, the urgency for a defensive tackle goes down. If Hampton runs wild, expect a lot more Robinson talk on Monday.

So here’s my bottom line.

No, the Rams did not make a “big trade” before the Chargers game, and they didn’t add a “huge weapon.” The clip claiming Adam Schefter confirmed the Rams are “making this call” on A’Shawn Robinson is another fake — the seventh one this month by my count. What’s real is that ESPN listed the Rams first among the fits for Robinson, with a 30 percent chance he gets traded anywhere, and that Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak proposed a late-round pick for him.

Robinson makes real sense for this Rams team. He’s a former Super Bowl champion with the Rams, he’s playing at a high level, he fills a real hole and he’d be relatively affordable. But with about 206,000 dollars in cap space, several other teams that fit and McVay leaning on players returning from injury, I’d still call it possible — not likely.
