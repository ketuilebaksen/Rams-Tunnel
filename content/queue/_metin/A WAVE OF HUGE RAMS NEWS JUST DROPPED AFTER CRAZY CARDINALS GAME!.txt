Part one: the game didn’t start as a blowout

Let’s remember how this game actually felt.

The Rams didn’t flatten Arizona from the first snap. The Cardinals got a field goal on their opening drive. Then another one. At one point, Arizona led 6-3.

And for a while, it felt like one of those tight, ugly division games. You know the kind. Every drive matters. One mistake can flip everything. Rams fans were gripping the couch.

Stafford even threw an interception during that stretch. The offense was still trying to figure out how to play without Puka Nacua and Davante Adams.

Then everything changed.

Part two: 31 unanswered points

From that 6-3 deficit, the Rams scored 31 straight points. Thirty-one.

Let’s walk through it.

Stafford hit Tyler Higbee for a 5-yard touchdown with about a minute left in the first half. Rams 10-6.

Then Kamren Kinchens intercepted Carson Beck and returned it 33 yards. Four plays later, Xavier Smith turned a quick screen into a 22-yard touchdown, breaking a tackle on the way. Rams 17-6 at halftime.

Then the Rams opened the second half with a Blake Corum 1-yard touchdown run. 24-6.

Then Stafford found Davis Allen for 19 yards. 31-6.

And Brandon McManus added a 43-yard field goal in the fourth quarter. 34-6.

So what does that run tell us? It tells us this team didn’t panic. Being down 6-3 on the road in a division game didn’t rattle them. There were no forced throws, no meltdown. They settled in, found a rhythm, and started turning chances into touchdowns.

And that’s important, because earlier this season, Rams fans had questions about finishing drives and closing games. In Arizona, the finishing showed up.

Stafford said it himself after the game: “We finished the half really strong, offense and defense, and then came out in the second half and put another touchdown on the board.” And he said that’s something they’ve “done at a pretty consistent clip.”

If you were on your feet for that 31-point run, smash the like button.

Revenge, 28 days later

Let’s not forget the backstory here.

Just 28 days before this game, Arizona beat the Rams 18-16. It was a painful loss, and it’s also the game where Davante Adams got hurt. A reporter after Sunday’s game called that first Arizona game “a low point” for the Rams.

McVay’s answer was all about the response. He said: “In those tough moments, it sucks and it doesn’t feel good, but it does force the most growth if you have the right people that can respond and go back to work.”

And he said the team treated it as “a growth opportunity.” In his words: “This wasn’t what we want to be about.”

Since that loss, the Rams have won four straight. And they got their revenge in the same building, by 28 points.

It also says something about Arizona right now. According to ESPN, that was the Cardinals’ 11th straight home loss. They’re 3-7. So the Rams went from losing to this team to completely overpowering them in less than a month.

Part three: Stafford managed the game like a veteran

Matthew Stafford finished 18 of 26 for 249 yards, three touchdowns, and one interception.

On paper, that’s a solid day. But in context, it’s even more impressive. He didn’t have Puka. He didn’t have Adams. He was throwing to Tutu Atwell, Konata Mumpfield, CJ Daniels, Xavier Smith, and his tight ends.

And he spread the ball to nine different players.

That’s what a veteran quarterback does. Read the coverage. Trust the protection, even when the protection keeps changing. Find the open man. Don’t force it.

And let’s be honest, it wasn’t a perfect game. Stafford said so himself: “Obviously wasn’t perfect.” There was the interception. The offense was slow early.

But here’s what matters. He didn’t try to be a hero on every play. He took what Arizona gave him, and he made young players feel like they belonged. That’s something people overlook. When a quarterback trusts a young receiver, that receiver plays with more confidence.

McVay said before the game that Stafford’s “steadiness is the key thing.” That’s exactly what we saw.

Part four: Blake Corum’s breakout

Now let’s talk about Blake Corum.

17 carries, 100 yards, and a touchdown. Plus three catches for 19 yards.

Corum ran with purpose. He hit holes quickly, and he finished runs with physicality. And McVay noticed. After the game, he said: “It’s been cool to see Blake really be able to kind of catch his stride. You can really feel the twitch, the explosiveness. He’s seeing things really well, staying grounded in close quarters.”

Here’s why that matters so much. A good run game takes pressure off Stafford. When the defense has to respect the run, play-action gets cleaner. Passing windows open up. That’s how Mumpfield got catches of 27 and 44 yards, and Atwell got a 31-yarder on fourth down.

Now, I know some of you are already typing in the comments. So let me say it clearly. This is not a “Corum replaces Kyren” video. Kyren Williams is still a huge part of this offense. McVay said it himself: “Blake and Kyren have always been outstanding.”

In fact, McVay’s new two-back look was built to get both of them on the field together. He said it was “a way to be able to get two of our best players on the field at the same time.”

And then there’s Ronnie Rivers, who had 15 carries for 81 yards and closed out the game. McVay said he “ran really well at the end there.”

So the Rams have three backs who can create problems. Different styles, different rhythms. That’s a backfield that’s hard to prepare for.

Here’s my question for you: after a 100-yard game, should McVay give Corum an even bigger role? Tell me in the comments.

Part five: depth players took their moments

Injuries don’t just create problems. Sometimes they create opportunities. But opportunities only matter if somebody grabs them.

Xavier Smith grabbed his. First career NFL touchdown. A couple of weeks ago, he was on the 49ers practice squad. Now he has a touchdown that basically decided a division game. McVay called him “X-Man” and said, “Did a great job breaking the initial tackle.” And McVay gave the credit for the play call to assistant coach Nate Scheelhaase, saying it was “his play call suggestion.”

Davis Allen grabbed his. A 19-yard touchdown catch. When you’re missing receivers, a reliable tight end is worth a lot. He gives Stafford a target he trusts. And remember, Allen played through a back injury against Washington. McVay called him “tough as hell.”

Tyler Higbee scored too. The veteran tight end Stafford knows best.

CJ Daniels caught his first NFL pass.

That’s the story I love. The Rams didn’t sit around waiting for Puka and Adams to come rescue them. Other players stepped up.

But let’s be real. One great game doesn’t mean long-term consistency. Smith scoring once is exciting. Allen scoring once is encouraging. The real test is whether they can keep doing it when defenses start paying more attention to them. That’s where careers are built.

Part six: the defense was dominant

Now the other side of the ball, because the defense deserves a standing ovation.

Five sacks. Aaron Donald got his first sack of the season. Tyler Davis got his first of the season too.

Two interceptions. Kamren Kinchens had one and returned it 33 yards. Wesley Bailey had the first interception of his career.

A forced fumble. Shaun Dolac punched the ball out, setting up a McManus field goal.

123 total yards. That’s all Arizona managed. And Carson Beck, in his first NFL start, went 11 of 18 for just 108 yards with two interceptions.

Read that again. 123 total yards. That’s not an offense. That’s a team spending the whole day looking for a first down.

And here’s why it matters. When you pressure the quarterback, everything else gets easier. Coverage holds up longer. The offense never gets into a rhythm. And once a team loses its rhythm, the game can get away from them fast.

Trent McDuffie was back too. McVay said, “It was great to get Trent McDuffie back out there today.” With McDuffie back and Myles Garrett playing, McVay said the defense is “really starting to hit their stride.”

Now, let’s be fair. Arizona was missing a lot. They had a rookie quarterback, a third-string center, and a backup left tackle. ESPN’s crew made that point after the game. So this wasn’t against a full-strength offense.

But the Rams still had to go out and do it. And they did.

Could this be a sign the defense is getting more dangerous as the season goes on? I’d say yes. The pieces are coming together.

Special teams: one thing to clean up

Before we get to the warning sign on the offensive line, here’s a smaller issue McVay pointed out himself.

He talked about the kicking game: “We want to be able to keep those kicks where we’re actually keeping them in play. That wasn’t a positive for us today.”

That sounds like the kickoffs weren’t landing where the Rams wanted them. If a kickoff goes out of bounds, the other team gets the ball at a good spot. In a 34-6 win, it didn’t matter. In a close game against the Packers, it could.

The good news: the return game looked better. Ronnie Rivers had a 34-yard kick return. And McVay has been happy with the options he has now: Rivers, Brennan Presley, and Xavier Smith.

So special teams is improving. But McVay noticed the kickoff issue, and I’d expect them to work on it during the bye.

Part seven: the warning sign

Okay, now the mood changes.

The Rams should celebrate 34-6. But they can’t ignore what’s happening on the offensive line.

Steve Avila was already out with a hamstring injury before the game.

Then, during the game, Coleman Shelton hurt his hamstring. Dylan McMahon came in for him. Then McMahon hurt his knee.

So Beaux Limmer, who started the game at left guard, had to slide over to center. Bill Murray came in at guard. McVay said, “We needed all eight of our offensive linemen.” And Stafford said, “We’re down to the third center.”

Think about that. Two centers went down in one game.

Why does that matter so much? Because the center is basically the quarterback of the offensive line. He snaps the ball on every play. He helps make the protection calls. He works with Stafford before the snap to make sure everyone knows who’s blocking who. When that position gets shaky, the whole line can feel it.

So here are the questions every Rams fan should be asking:

Will Stafford face more pressure in the Packers game?

Can the line keep its chemistry with so many moving parts?

Can Limmer handle center if Shelton and McMahon miss time?

Will the Rams need to sign another lineman?

And now let me be careful. As of right now, I haven’t seen official timelines for Shelton or McMahon. So I’m not going to tell you they’ll miss specific games. That hasn’t been confirmed. We’ll find out when McVay gives his updates.

This is not a report, it’s my concern: the offensive line is now the biggest question mark on this team. You can replace a few catches. You can find another running back. But when the line starts losing important pieces, the whole offense is under pressure.

Why Stafford trusts this line anyway

One more thing on the offensive line, because it’s not all bad news.

After the game, Stafford praised the backups who came in: “Really proud of those guys and just their composure, their effort, and their ability to not get a lot of reps in practice and go in there and execute in the game.”

That’s important. Backup linemen rarely get practice reps with the starters. And still, after Shelton and McMahon went down, the Rams scored two more touchdowns and closed out the game on the ground.

So the line is banged up. But it showed in Arizona that it can handle chaos.

Part eight: where things stand with Puka and Adams

Let’s get the receiver timelines right.

Puka Nacua went on injured reserve on November 12. McVay explained that the move didn’t change his timeline. It was about opening a roster spot for Brennan Presley. Puka has an MCL and PCL sprain plus turf toe, and he’s expected to miss at least four games, including the Packers, Chiefs, and 49ers.

Davante Adams is on IR with a hamstring injury. Some videos are saying November 25 is his “possible return target.” But here’s what McVay actually said: “After Green Bay is a realistic timeline.” The Green Bay game is on November 25. So based on McVay’s words, Adams is more likely to return after that game, for the Chiefs or the 49ers.

Could that change? Sure. Injury timelines can move. But right now, there’s no official report that Adams will play against Green Bay. Until the Rams say otherwise, it’s a hope, not a plan.

So for the Packers game, the Rams will probably still be without both of their top two receivers.

The “never victims” mindset

Here’s a line from McVay’s postgame press conference that I think explains this whole winning streak.

A reporter asked about the next-man-up mentality. McVay talked about having “confidence in other guys to be able to step up” and “confidence earned through evidence.”

Then he said: “You could have reasons to make excuses, but we’re never victims. We’re not going to go that route.”

Think about every reason this team has to make excuses. Adams, Puka, Ferguson, Whittington, Kobie Turner, Poona Ford, Jaylen Watson. Now Avila, Shelton, and McMahon. That’s a long list.

And instead of excuses, they’ve won four straight.

McVay also said the last month has “really tested our team,” but it’s shown “there’s a lot of guys that are capable.” That’s exactly what we saw from Smith, Allen, Bailey, Rivers, and Limmer.

That mindset is going to matter a lot against the Packers, because the injury list isn’t getting shorter anytime soon.

Part nine: McVay’s coaching test

Let’s give McVay credit first.

He found a way to keep the offense productive without Puka and Adams. He brought out the two-back look, which he said was “something different that we hadn’t put on tape yet.” He used his tight ends. He let an assistant suggest the play that broke the game open. And he got contributions from nine different pass catchers.

That’s good coaching.

But a coach’s job doesn’t stop with one great game plan. He has to manage injuries, depth, and a long season.

So here are the tough questions for McVay:

Should Corum get an even bigger workload?

Should protecting Stafford come before everything else?

Does the offensive line need more depth?

Should the Rams lean on even more tight-end formations?

And how do they keep this offense working until Puka and Adams come back?

Let me be clear. I’m not saying McVay is panicking. I’m not saying there’s drama behind closed doors. Nobody has reported that, and I’m not going to make it up.

What I’m saying is that this is a real coaching test. And McVay seems to know it. After the game, he said the bye is a chance to “creatively come up with new ideas based on who’s available and who’s not.”

He also said: “We’re never victims.” That’s the right mindset.

My take: Corum should absolutely get a meaningful role going forward. Not because Kyren matters less, but because balance protects the quarterback and keeps the defense guessing. When your offensive line is banged up, a strong run game is your best friend.

Part ten: the Packers are a different test

Next up: the Green Bay Packers, at home, on the Wednesday before Thanksgiving.

This is a short week after an “accelerated bye,” as McVay called it. And Green Bay is a very different challenge than Arizona.

What worked against the Cardinals won’t automatically work against the Packers. Arizona was starting a rookie quarterback with a backup offensive line. Green Bay will be a much tougher opponent.

So here’s what the Rams need:

Offensive line stability. This is the biggest one. Stafford needs protection.

The run game. Corum, Kyren, and Rivers will probably need to carry the offense again.

The tight ends. McVay said they’re “still so banged up.” Getting them healthier during the bye is huge. And Terrance Ferguson might be back soon too.

The defense. It has to keep playing aggressive, disciplined football.

And let me be clear about one more thing. I’m not going to invent any Packers injury news, and I’m not going to promise that any Rams player will definitely return. What I’ll tell you is that this short bye matters a lot. How the Rams handle the center position and how McVay adjusts the offense could decide this game.

Part eleven: are the Rams becoming dangerous?

Let’s zoom out and ask the biggest question.

Here’s what we saw in Arizona:

They can score without depending on one receiver.

Stafford can spread the ball around.

Corum can deliver big rushing games.

Young players like Smith and Daniels can make plays.

And the defense can completely shut down a division opponent.

That’s a pretty good list.

But here’s the other side. One great game is not the same as real depth. Any team can look deep for one day. The real test is whether you can stay competitive for weeks while injuries keep piling up.

That’s what separates a nice story from a real contender.

And here’s the encouraging part. This wasn’t one game. It’s four straight wins. The Rams have beaten the Raiders, the Chargers 24-9, Washington 20-13, and now Arizona 34-6. And they’ve done a lot of it with injuries all over the roster.

So my honest assessment: if the key players come back healthy and the offensive line holds together, this Rams team has what it takes to be a serious threat in the NFC. Stafford is playing at a high level. The defense looks dangerous. And the run game has options.

But that’s a big “if.” And the next three games will tell us a lot.

Part twelve: the next three games

Here’s the stretch that will define this season.

Wednesday, November 25: Packers at home. Short week. No Puka. Probably no Adams. A banged-up offensive line.

Then the Chiefs at home. Adams could be back by then, based on McVay’s timeline.

Then at San Francisco on December 13. A huge division game. Puka is still expected to be out.

If the Rams go 2-1 in that stretch, they’re in great shape. If they go 3-0, they’re one of the best stories in football. If they go 1-2, they’re still in the race, but it gets tight.

This is not a report, it’s my prediction: I think the Rams go 2-1 in those three games. The run game and the defense carry them, and Adams comes back to give the offense a lift in December.
