Part one: McVay's opening injury update

McVay started the press conference with three injury updates.

Dylan McMahon: "Ended up getting a partial tear of his patella tendon. He's going to be put on IR. Bummer for him."

Coleman Shelton: "Got his hamstring pretty good. We'll reevaluate him in a couple weeks."

Puka Nacua: "Decided to get surgery on his MCL on Tuesday. So that'll be a couple months for him."

That's it. Three quick updates, and then he took questions. But the third one changed the whole season.

Part two: Puka is "pretty much" out for the season

The first reporter asked the obvious question: is Puka out for the rest of the season?

McVay answered: "Yeah, pretty much. Yeah, I mean, a couple months. So, you can pretty much say yes."

Let's do the math. A couple of months from mid-November takes you to mid-January. The regular season ends in early January. So Puka is not expected back in the regular season.

And then McVay explained that this wasn't a total surprise to him.

He said: "I kind of thought, when the initial injury occurred, that this was more likely the direction that this would go. So this isn't anything necessarily new on our end."

He explained that Puka "had to get some different information, talk to people," and that "there's a lot of thought and conversations that go into whether you're going to do that or not and what's the best plan of action for return to performance for him, not exclusive to this year, but for his career moving forward."

And he added: "He was very thorough and deliberate about that."

Then McVay said something that tells you how serious the injury was: "I knew that that was a serious injury that he sustained, whether he got it fixed or not. With the shot that he took to his knee, he had the toe injury. And so, in my mind, this was kind of always a direction that we had to plan upon anyways."

So McVay was already preparing for life without Puka. That's why the Rams changed the offense so much in Arizona.

Part three: what about the playoffs?

Another reporter asked the question every Rams fan wants answered: could Puka come back for the playoffs?

McVay said: "That would be getting way far ahead. Haven't thought about that." Then: "I don't think you would ever say no, but that's so far down the line."

And then this: "A couple months is kind of really where it's at, which, in my mind, I think what's healthy to do is to say that you're planning on not having him."

So the door isn't completely closed. If the Rams make a deep playoff run, and Puka heals well, maybe. But McVay is planning as if Puka won't be back. And I think fans should do the same. Anything else would be a bonus.

Part four: why the surgery decision makes sense

Let me give you my take on the surgery. This is not a report. It's my opinion.

I think this was the right call for Puka's career.

He's 25 years old. He has a long career ahead of him. An MCL and PCL injury plus turf toe is a lot. If he had tried to rush back without surgery, he might have played a few games late in the season, but he could have risked a bigger problem.

McVay made the same point. He said the decision was about "return to performance" not just this year, but "for his career moving forward." And he said Puka got several opinions. According to McVay, "He and his agent wanted to be able to get the full scope," so they could "make the right decision." And McVay said it was "the best decision for him and for the long term."

So yes, it hurts the Rams this season. But a healthy Puka for the next five or ten years is worth a lot more than a few weeks of a limited Puka in December.

Puka's 2026 season, in numbers

Let's take a moment to look at what Puka did this year, because it was incredible while it lasted.

In just seven games, Puka had 70 catches, 715 yards, and four touchdowns. All three numbers still lead the Rams.

And remember, he missed Weeks 2 and 3 earlier this season, after a sports hernia developed into a hip problem. Then he came back and produced at a pace most receivers never reach. Seventy catches in seven games is ten catches a game.

Then in Week 9 against Washington, he had one of the best games of his career. Fourteen catches for 150 yards and the game-winning touchdown, on 19 targets. And it was near the end of that game that he hurt his knee.

So his season ends with an incredible stretch of football. It's just too short.

What this means for 2027

Let's look ahead for a second. This is not a report. It's my take.

Because Puka had surgery now, in November, he should have the whole offseason to recover. If everything goes well, he could be fully healthy for training camp next summer.

That's actually one of the reasons the surgery makes sense. If he had waited until after the season, his recovery would have eaten into the offseason, and maybe even into next season.

But there's also the business side. ESPN's Dan Graziano had already raised a hypothetical about Puka's future contract, and that was before the suspension and the surgery. Injury history and conduct are both things teams think about when they decide how much to commit to a player long-term.

The Rams haven't said anything about changing their plans for Puka. And he's still only 25. But this offseason will be a big one for him, on and off the field.

Part five: McVay on the suspension

Of course, McVay was also asked about Puka's three-game suspension, which the NFL announced on November 19 for a violation of the personal conduct policy.

McVay was careful and brief. He said: "I think the league's done a really good job of being in good communication with us." And he said it was "something that we always knew was a possibility."

Then: "It's an unfortunate thing. It's a learning op. It's something that obviously happened a while ago that there's been a lot of discussion and dialogue behind the scenes, and we'll learn from it. We'll grow. But that's kind of really all I can add to that."

Later, another reporter asked about his conversations with Puka. McVay said the first thing was "the league making a decision." He said there were "a lot of conversations, a lot of discussions," and that Puka "understands what the expectations are and what we want to be about and how we learn and how we grow."

So McVay didn't go into details, and I don't think he should. The league made its decision. And McVay made it clear that the team expects Puka to learn from it.

With the surgery, the suspension now overlaps with games he was going to miss anyway.

Part six: good news, Kobie Turner is coming back

Now the good news.

A reporter asked about Kobie Turner, Davante Adams, and Terrance Ferguson. McVay said: "Kobie's a guy that he'll get activated. Those other two, we're not quite there yet."

And he said: "As of right now, our mindset and mentality is starting Kobie's clock, and hopefully he'll be ready to go this week."

That's big. Turner suffered a grade 2 groin strain in Week 6. Now the Rams are opening his practice window, and he could play against the Packers.

Another reporter asked what Turner's return would mean. McVay said: "Oh, it's big. He's obviously a great player. He's a great leader. I think he's been incredibly engaged in the time that he's missed."

He kept going: "Just the production in both phases, the leadership, his ability to just add value in a variety of spots, and just continue to really add to a group that's playing really well. And then you get one of your big-time players back. That's a big deal for us."

And remember why Turner matters so much right now. Poona Ford tore his triceps in Week 6 and is expected to miss the rest of the season. So the middle of the defensive line has been thinner. Getting Turner back next to Aaron Donald and with Myles Garrett on the edge? That's a scary front for the Packers' offensive line.

Part seven: the Davante Adams update

Now Davante Adams.

McVay said: "Don't think he's ready quite yet." But he also said: "I know he's been attacking his rehab like crazy and is excited to be able to get back out there, and there is a possibility for the game after the Packers."

Earlier in the press conference, he said something similar: "The hopeful that potentially there's a chance to get Davante back after this week. We'll see how he's feeling. I know he looks great, has been feeling good." But he added: "I want to be careful to not get too far ahead of myself."

And with Puka out for the season, Adams becomes even more important. He's going to be the most experienced receiver Stafford has for the rest of the year.

Part eight: Terrance Ferguson isn't ready yet

What about Terrance Ferguson?

McVay said Ferguson isn't ready to be activated yet. But when a reporter asked if the Rams might have to shut him down for the season, McVay said: "No, that's hopefully the plan." He meant the plan is to keep him around.

He explained: "The plan is to not shut him down. You want to leave out hope to be able to give him an opportunity to return to play and come back and be a part of this thing."

But he also admitted things could change: "There's a lot of things that we've planned on that you have to be ready to adjust and adapt." And: "Whether or not the situation allows us to be able to do that is still to be determined."

So the Rams want to keep Ferguson's season alive. But they need him healthy enough to be activated before his window runs out.

Part nine: the IR math just changed

Here's an interesting part of the press conference.

A reporter asked how many IR return spots the Rams would have left if both Turner and Ferguson were activated.

McVay said: "I'm not sure. You would probably know better." Then: "But obviously we won't need one for Puka now. So we have a little bit of a luxury."

That's a big detail. Remember, according to SB Nation's Turf Show Times, the Rams had only four return designations left. And the expectation was that Adams, Puka, and Turner would use three of them.

Now Puka won't need one. So that frees up a spot.

Here's how it looks now, based on McVay's comments. Kobie Turner is getting activated, so he'll use one. Davante Adams will likely use one when he's ready. Terrance Ferguson has already been designated, so he doesn't need a new one. That leaves some room for other players later in the season.

Part ten: Justin Dedich is coming back

And here's one of those players.

McVay said: "We'll bring Justin Dedich back off of IR with that spot that Dylan ended up taking up."

So McMahon goes to IR, and Dedich comes back. Dedich is an interior offensive lineman who's been on IR since late August with a hand injury.

And McVay said it was an easy decision once Puka chose surgery: "Kind of once we knew we were going to go the direction of the surgery with Puka, it made it an easy one for us there."

A reporter asked if Dedich would be the only offensive line move. McVay said: "Yes, other than just maybe adding a practice squad guy."

So the Rams are adding one more body to an offensive line that's lost Shelton, McMahon, and Avila. That's important, because the middle of the line is the biggest concern going into the Packers game.

Part eleven: Shelton in a couple of weeks

One more offensive line note.

McVay said about Coleman Shelton: "Got his hamstring pretty good. We'll reevaluate him in a couple weeks."

That lines up with the four-to-six-week timeline that was reported. And Shelton isn't on IR, so he won't need a return designation when he's healthy.

So for the Packers game, expect Beaux Limmer at center. He slid over from left guard in Arizona and graded 75.6 according to PFF, per Blaine Grisak of Rams On SI. McVay said after that game: "Can't say enough about Beaux Limmer."

Part twelve: confidence in the receivers

A reporter asked McVay how confident he is in the receivers now that Puka is out for the season.

McVay said: "I'm confident. I thought they did a really nice job last week, and you just take things a day at a time."

And he said: "A lot of guys getting a chance to be able to step up, and this year has required us to have great fluidity and flexibility on a week-to-week basis."

That's backed up by the Arizona game. Xavier Smith, Konata Mumpfield, Tutu Atwell, and CJ Daniels each had a catch longer than 20 yards. Smith graded 92.6 and scored his first career touchdown. Mumpfield graded 80.6 with three catches for 81 yards.

And now that Puka's out long-term, these guys aren't just filling in for a few weeks. This is their opportunity for the rest of the season.

Part thirteen: will the two-back look stay?

Another reporter asked about the two-back sets, the "pony" look with Kyren Williams and Blake Corum, which the Rams used for the first time in Arizona.

McVay didn't give away his plan. He said: "We're in the midst of that right now, so we'll figure it out."

He explained how he thinks about it: "It's partly you're thinking of your players, is where everything always starts. And then secondly, it goes to what do we think is some of the best ways to try to be able to be cognizant of the opponent's personnel and their scheme."

And: "Whether or not that's something that we lean on is to be determined."

So McVay isn't promising anything. It depends on what works best against the Packers. But that look gave the Rams 197 yards and a touchdown on 17 plays in Arizona, according to ESPN's Sarah Barshop and NFL Next Gen Stats. I'd be surprised if we don't see some version of it again.

Part fourteen: the hook-and-lateral

This was a fun part of the press conference.

A reporter asked about the hook-and-lateral, the trick play where a receiver catches a short pass and then pitches it to a teammate running behind him. Teams have been using it more often lately, even in the middle of games, not just as a last-second desperation play.

McVay said the Rams have it in their playbook. And he said they actually tried it against the Chargers: "We tried to run it against the Chargers. We dropped the initial part of it. It had a chance to be a pretty big play on a third down."

He also gave credit to other teams that have run it well, like Buffalo, Detroit, and Chicago. And he mentioned that the 49ers hit a big one against Arizona for a touchdown early in a game on a long third down.

So the Rams have it ready. And with Puka out and the offense looking for new ideas, don't be shocked if we see McVay call it again.
