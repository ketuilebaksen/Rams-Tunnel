Before we look at any team, let’s set the ground rules. Because every trade conversation about the 2026 Rams has to start with two facts.

Fact number one: the money. According to the latest reporting, the Rams have roughly one million dollars in salary cap space. That’s it. This front office went all-in this offseason. They traded for Myles Garrett. They traded for Trent McDuffie. They added Tre’Davious White. And when you build a roster like that, the cap fills up fast. So any player the Rams acquire before November 10th either has to be cheap, or the Rams have to send salary back the other way.

Fact number two: the draft capital. The Garrett trade cost the Rams their 2027 first-round pick, their 2028 second-round pick and their 2029 third-round pick — and that 2029 third becomes a first if Garrett is ever traded to an AFC North team. The McDuffie trade cost them the 29th pick in the 2026 draft, a fifth and a sixth in 2026, and a 2027 third-rounder. That’s a lot of premium picks already gone. The Rams still have picks to deal, but they’re not the kind of picks that land a superstar.

So when you hear somebody say the Rams should go get a big name, keep those two facts in your head. The question isn’t just “Would this player help?” It’s “Can the Rams actually afford him?”

Now let’s go team by team.

Team number one: the Philadelphia Eagles.

The Eagles are 2-3. They lost to the Rams in Week 4 at home — that 24-20 comeback win we all remember — and then they lost 19-17 to the Jacksonville Jaguars in London last Sunday. Trevor Lawrence threw for 331 yards and two touchdowns, and the Jaguars outgained Philadelphia 408 to 232. Philadelphia’s offense is struggling, and the injuries are piling up.

And the Eagles have already made one move that turned heads. On October 7th, they traded center Cam Jurgens — a two-time Pro Bowler — to the Baltimore Ravens. The Eagles sent Jurgens and a 2027 seventh-round pick to Baltimore for a 2027 fifth-round pick and a 2028 second-rounder. That came one day after right tackle Lane Johnson retired. So you can see why people are asking whether Philadelphia is about to tear things down.

But here’s the thing: the Eagles’ owner isn’t treating this as a rebuild. Jeffrey Lurie said the team is “gung-ho to win big and win our division first and go from there.” And he added: “Our expectations are very high.” According to Newsweek, the Eagles are also holding two first-round picks and two second-round picks in 2028 — which means Howie Roseman has ammunition to buy, not just sell. And with only four sacks through their first four games, the Eagles are more likely to be shopping for a pass rusher than giving one away.

So my verdict on Philadelphia: don’t expect a fire sale. And even if one came, the Eagles are a direct NFC rival. They’d much rather send a veteran to the AFC than help a team they could see in January.

And think about the Eagles’ best defensive players. Jalen Carter had two sacks and two forced fumbles in the loss to Jacksonville. That’s a cornerstone, not a trade chip. The Eagles’ young core is exactly the kind of group a team keeps when it’s trying to stay in the race. And Rams fans remember how good that defense can still be — the Rams didn’t take the lead in Philadelphia until 1:10 was left in the game. That’s a proud organization that’s going to fight to save its season, not hand pieces to the team that just beat it.

Team number two: the Houston Texans.

Houston started 0-4. Then in Week 5, the Texans beat the Tennessee Titans 26-3 to get to 1-4. And right after that game, they did something that tells you exactly which direction they’re heading. They traded for wide receiver Marvin Harrison Jr. from the Arizona Cardinals. According to the AP, Houston received Harrison and a 2027 seventh-round pick, and Arizona received a 2027 second-round pick and a 2027 sixth-rounder.

Harrison was the fourth overall pick in 2024, and he reunites with his former Ohio State teammate, C.J. Stroud. Stroud said: “I loved him in college. He’s probably the hardest worker I’ve ever been around in my life.” And DeMeco Ryans closed his press conference with a simple line: “We traded for Marvin Harrison.”

That’s not a team getting ready to sell. That’s a team trying to save its season. So my verdict on Houston: they’re buyers. If anything, they’re competition for the Rams on the trade market, not a trade partner.

And that actually matters for the Rams. When more teams decide they’re buyers, prices go up. The Harrison deal already set one benchmark: a former fourth overall pick who’d caught five passes for 85 yards and a touchdown through four games still cost Houston a second-round pick. If struggling teams like Houston are willing to pay that kind of price to save their seasons, sellers are going to ask for more — and a Rams team without much cap space or premium draft capital could get priced out of the biggest names quickly.

Team number three: the Los Angeles Chargers.

This is the one that’s getting the most attention around the league. The Chargers are 0-5. Nobody saw that coming. They made the playoffs the last two seasons under Jim Harbaugh, and they added Mike McDaniel as offensive coordinator. And now, according to ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler, the Chargers are drawing more interest from rival teams than any of the other 0-5 clubs.

Now, let’s get one thing out of the way: Justin Herbert is not going anywhere. ESPN’s Adam Schefter reported that a Chargers source shut that down completely: “The sentence should we trade Justin Herbert has never once been uttered in our organization, nor will it be.”

But according to Fowler, there are three Chargers who could become trade candidates: edge rusher Khalil Mack, safety Derwin James Jr., and Tuli Tuipulotu. And the biggest name of those three is Mack.

Let’s talk about Mack, because on paper he looks like exactly what the Rams need. He’s a nine-time Pro Bowler and a former Defensive Player of the Year. He’s 35 years old now, but he’s still productive — he had two sacks through the Chargers’ first three games. And the Rams need pass rush help. According to Fox Sports, the Rams have only seven sacks this season, which ranks 28th in the NFL. Myles Garrett has played in only one game because of his knee, and McVay said this week the Rams won’t open his 21-day practice window yet.

So why isn’t this a slam dunk? Because of that cap number. Mack signed a one-year, 18 million dollar deal with the Chargers this offseason. Ten million of that was a signing bonus that’s already been paid. So an acquiring team would owe the rest of his eight million dollar salary — prorated for the weeks remaining after the trade. Even prorated, that’s several million dollars. The Rams have about one. To make it work, they’d have to send a significant salary back to the Chargers, and every big contract on this roster belongs to someone the Rams need.

And there’s another problem. One executive from another team told Fowler: “I think it’s going to be really difficult for them to admit when and if they need to trade guys.” Harbaugh’s Chargers don’t sound like a team ready to wave the white flag. And even if they did, would they really send Khalil Mack across town to SoFi to help the team they share a stadium with?

So this is not a report — but if the Chargers do sell Mack, I’d expect a team with real cap space to get him. My verdict: Mack would be a dream fit for the Rams, but the money makes it close to impossible.

And what about Derwin James? He’s a star, but the Rams’ secondary is the one area of this defense that doesn’t need help. Quentin Lake and Trent McDuffie have been two of the best defensive backs in football this season. According to Pro Football Focus, Lake has a 90.0 grade and McDuffie has an 89.1, and the two of them have led the league in pass breakups. Jaylen Watson has been tough enough to play through a dislocated shoulder and still pick off Josh Allen. Spending resources on another defensive back would be solving the wrong problem.

Team number four: the Arizona Cardinals.

And this one is personal, because the Rams play them this Sunday.

Arizona is 1-4. Last week, the Cardinals lost 45-21 at home to the Detroit Lions. And then they traded Marvin Harrison Jr. to Houston. When you trade a former fourth overall pick in the middle of the season for a second-round pick, you’re telling everyone that you’re building for the future.

And here’s the connection every Rams fan should know: Arizona’s head coach is Mike LaFleur. Before he took that job on February 1st, LaFleur spent three seasons as the Rams’ offensive coordinator under Sean McVay. The Cardinals gave him a five-year contract and asked him to turn the franchise around after Jonathan Gannon went 15-36 in three years.

So could the Rams and Cardinals make a trade? I’d say it’s very unlikely. Division rivals rarely help each other, and LaFleur knows better than anyone how valuable any piece would be to McVay’s system. But that connection matters a lot more for Sunday than for the trade deadline.

Think about it. LaFleur knows the Rams’ offense inside and out. He helped run it. He knows McVay’s play-calling habits, the terminology, the tendencies. That’s an advantage for Arizona’s defensive staff this week. But McVay knows LaFleur, too. And McVay has the far better roster.

Here’s what the Cardinals bring into SoFi on Sunday. Quarterback Jacoby Brissett threw for 292 yards and three touchdowns against Detroit. Rookie running back Jeremiyah Love — the fourth overall pick out of Notre Dame — leads the team with 67 carries for 245 yards and a touchdown. Tight end Trey McBride had nine catches for 66 yards and a score in Week 5. And on defense, Budda Baker had 11 tackles and a sack, and Baron Browning forced two fumbles. This is not a team without talent. And Love is the player I’d watch most closely. The Rams’ run defense just gave up 234 yards to Buffalo, and every team on the schedule watched that film. I’d expect LaFleur’s game plan on Sunday to hand the ball to the rookie again and again and see if the Rams have fixed anything. That’s exactly why the linebacker story we’re about to get to matters so much. But it’s a team going through a transition, and the Rams are 12.5-point favorites according to FanDuel’s line as reported by Sports Illustrated.

So my verdict on Arizona: they’re sellers, but not to the Rams. The real opportunity against the Cardinals is the two games on the schedule — this Sunday and again in Week 10.

So that’s the four teams. And if you add it all up, here’s the honest picture: the Eagles and Texans are trying to win, the Chargers have the most interesting veteran on the list but he’s too expensive for this cap, and the Cardinals are a division rival. None of those four teams is a natural partner for the Rams right now.

Which brings us to the second story — and I think this one is much more realistic.

His name is Cedric Gray. He’s a linebacker for the Tennessee Titans. And according to an SB Nation article by Brendan Dzwierzynski, published on Yahoo Sports this week, he’s emerged as a potential trade deadline target for the Rams. Again — that’s an analyst’s suggestion, not a report of talks. But once you look at the details, it makes a lot of sense.

Let’s start with who he is. Gray was a fourth-round pick in 2024, 106th overall, out of North Carolina. In college, he was a tackling machine — 145 tackles in 2022, when he was a first-team All-ACC selection and a second-team All-American, and 121 tackles in 2023. As a rookie in Tennessee, he played sparingly. And then in 2025, he exploded. Sixteen starts. 164 tackles. He also added a sack, seven tackles for loss, four passes defended and two fumble recoveries. According to CBS Sports, that ranked fourth in the NFL and was the most tackles any Titan has ever recorded in a single season. A franchise record, in his first full season as a starter.

And then 2026 happened. The Titans hired a new coaching staff, with Robert Saleh as head coach and Gus Bradley as defensive coordinator. Gray missed the season opener with a concussion. And by the time he came back, rookie Anthony Hill Jr. had taken over the middle of the defense. Through four games, Gray has played just 44 percent of Tennessee’s defensive snaps and started two games — after starting all 16 games he played last year. In Week 4 at Baltimore, he played 30 of 64 snaps. Hill played all 64. Cody Barton played 53.

So what did Saleh say about it? He praised Gray, saying he “does a good job in the run game and pass game.” He also called Barton a very good linebacker and communicator. And he explained that the Titans have been using more dime packages — with only one linebacker on the field — and more looks with five defensive linemen. That naturally cuts down linebacker snaps. But here’s an important detail: Saleh’s staff didn’t draft Gray. They did draft Hill. And when a new coaching staff takes over, the players they picked often get the first opportunity.

And here’s a fun bit of history. This wouldn’t be the first time the Rams and Titans have done business at linebacker. Back in 2024, the Rams traded linebacker Ernest Jones to Tennessee. Now, two years later, the conversation could go the other direction, with a Titans linebacker coming to Los Angeles. Front offices remember who they’ve worked with, and the Rams and Titans have a recent history of making deals.

Now, here’s why Gray could make so much sense for the Rams.

First, the need. According to that same SB Nation piece, the Rams have invested very little in off-ball linebackers under McVay. Nate Landman is their best one, and he’s been a solid player for defensive coordinator Chris Shula. Against Buffalo, Landman forced two fumbles, including the one in overtime that Aaron Donald recovered. But he also played 89 percent of the defensive snaps. That’s a heavy workload. And the article pointed to one long Josh Allen run in overtime where Landman was caught in the middle of the field as Allen broke free.

Second, the run defense. Buffalo ran for 234 yards on Monday night. James Cook had 111 and two touchdowns. Allen ran for 121. According to Fox Sports, the Rams are allowing 128.4 rushing yards per game, which ranks 26th in the league. Gray’s strength is exactly what the Rams are missing. According to Pro Football Focus, he has a 74.8 run-defense grade this season, ninth among linebackers, and he hasn’t missed a tackle on 90 snaps.

Third — and this is the big one — the money. According to Yahoo Sports, Gray is still on his fourth-round rookie contract, which runs through 2027, at barely more than one million dollars a year. And after the deadline, the Rams would only owe the prorated portion of that salary. That’s the kind of number that fits under a cap with one million dollars of room. Unlike Khalil Mack, Gray is a player the Rams could actually afford. And because his contract runs through 2027, he wouldn’t just be a rental. He’d be a cheap, productive linebacker for this season and next.

And fourth, the fit. The Rams play a lot of dime defense, just like Tennessee. So Gray wouldn’t need to be an every-down player. He could be the run-down linebacker — the guy who comes in on first and second down, takes some of the load off Landman, and gives Shula another option against physical rushing teams. He doesn’t need to be Fred Warner. He just needs to tackle well and make plays in the run game, which is exactly what he did 164 times last season.

Now let me be fair about the obstacles, because there are real ones.

The Rams aren’t the only team that’s been linked to him. CBS Sports listed the Denver Broncos as a potential suitor, and the Broncos have about six million dollars in cap space — far more than the Rams. And according to Yahoo Sports, the Titans have no reason to sell him cheaply. He’s young, he’s productive, and he’s under contract through next season. Tennessee is in a rebuild, and rebuilding teams want draft picks.

The Rams also already made a move at linebacker. On October 6th, they signed veteran Eric Kendricks to the practice squad. Kendricks has years of starting experience, and he gives the Rams an emergency option if Landman or anyone else goes down. That might reduce the urgency.

And finally, the price. If the Titans want a mid-round pick, the Rams would have to decide whether they’re willing to part with more of their future after already trading so much for Garrett and McDuffie. My guess is that a late-round pick wouldn’t get it done.

So this is not a report — but if I were the Rams, Cedric Gray would be the first call I’d make before November 10th. He’s affordable, he’s under contract next year, he fixes a real weakness, and he’d cost far less than any big-name veteran on the market.

And there’s one more thing that makes the timing of this deadline so interesting: the Rams’ schedule. Between now and November 10th, the Rams play four games. Arizona at home this Sunday. Then at Las Vegas. Then the Chargers at SoFi in Week 8. Then at Washington on November 8th — just two days before the deadline. So the Rams will actually face the Chargers, the most talked-about seller on this list, before anybody knows whether Khalil Mack is moving. And by the time the Rams fly home from Washington, the front office will have a clear picture of whether this team is 6-3, 4-5, or somewhere in between. That record will decide how aggressive they are.

And the Garrett timeline lines up with that, too. According to NFL Network’s Ian Rapoport, Garrett is expected back around Week 9 — the Washington game. If his practice window opens in the next couple of weeks and he looks like himself, the Rams may decide they don’t need another pass rusher at all. If his return gets pushed back, the conversation about edge help gets louder. That’s why this deadline is about more than trades. It’s about the Rams’ own medical reports.

Now let me rank the options we’ve talked about from most realistic to least realistic.

Number one: Cedric Gray. Affordable, available and a clear fit. It still depends on Tennessee’s asking price, but this is the most realistic option on the board.

Number two: standing pat and waiting for Myles Garrett. This isn’t technically a trade, but it might be the biggest “addition” the Rams make all season. McVay said Garrett is “making good progress,” and that “there’s a difference between return to play and return to performance.” Getting a healthy Garrett back in November would do more for this pass rush than any deadline trade.

Number three: a cheap veteran depth piece from a seller. Not a headline move, just someone to help on special teams or in a specific role. After what happened with the kicking game against Buffalo, depth on special teams matters, too.

Number four: Khalil Mack. A dream fit on the field, but the money and the cross-town politics make it a long shot.

And number five: anything from the Eagles, Texans or Cardinals. The Eagles and Texans are buying, and the Cardinals are in the division. I’d be shocked.

But here’s my bottom line. The Rams don’t need a blockbuster at this deadline. They already made their blockbusters. What they need is to fix two specific problems that cost them against Buffalo: the run defense and the special teams. They’ve already addressed the kicker by signing Blake Grupe. A player like Cedric Gray could address the run defense. And Myles Garrett’s return could transform the pass rush. If the Rams handle those three things, this roster is good enough to make a run — even after a 2-3 start.

And the next step is Sunday. Beat the Cardinals at SoFi. Get to 3-3. And let the trade deadline conversation be about adding the final piece to a winning team, instead of patching up a struggling one.

Here’s my prediction for Sunday, on the record like always. Rams 33, Cardinals 17. The Rams hold Jeremiyah Love under 70 rushing yards, and the defense gets at least three sacks against a Cardinals offense that just lost its top young receiver. And McVay gets the win against his former offensive coordinator.
