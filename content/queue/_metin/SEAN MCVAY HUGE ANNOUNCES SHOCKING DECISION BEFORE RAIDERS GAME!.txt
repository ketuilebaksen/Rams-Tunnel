Let’s start with the Puka news, because it’s the story of the week.

According to the LA Daily News’ Adam Grosbard, as reported by The Big Lead: “Rams WR Puka Nacua left practice early with a calf injury. He was limited today.” ESPN’s Sarah Barshop confirmed it and said he would be listed as limited with a calf injury on the official report.

Now, here’s the important thing to understand about how injury reports work. Thursday’s “limited” designation is based on how much a player practiced. Friday is when teams release their final report with game designations — out, doubtful or questionable. So the key question isn’t what happened on Thursday. It’s whether Puka can practice on Friday, and what McVay says about him at his Friday press conference. If you’re watching this after that update, check the pinned comment — I’ll put the latest there.

And if Puka ends up questionable, the real answer may not come until Sunday. Teams announce their inactive players about 90 minutes before kickoff. For a 4:25 Eastern game, that’s around 2:55 Eastern, 11:55 in the morning Pacific. So if you’re a Rams fan on Sunday, keep an eye on pregame warmups. If Puka is out there running full-speed routes and cutting hard, that’s a great sign. If he’s only jogging, or he’s not out there at all, that tells you a lot.

Here’s why this matters so much. According to Heavy, Puka has 417 receiving yards and a touchdown in four games this season — and he’s already missed two games. When he missed Weeks 2 and 3, the Rams went 1-1, beating the Giants and then losing a close one in Denver, 30-26. And now, with Adams on IR, Puka isn’t just the number-one receiver. He’s almost the entire passing game. According to Newsweek, apart from Puka and Adams, the Rams’ wide receivers have 13 combined catches this season.

And calf injuries are tricky. For a receiver, the calf is everything — it’s what you push off of when you cut, when you accelerate, when you explode out of a break. A player can feel fine walking around and still not be able to run at full speed. That’s why teams are usually careful with them. If the Rams think there’s a real risk of making it worse, they might hold him out of one game to protect him for the next ten.

And Puka has dealt with a lot physically over his career. As a rookie in 2023, he set NFL rookie records with 105 catches and 1,486 receiving yards. Since then, injuries have cost him time — including a knee injury in 2024 — and earlier this season he dealt with hip and groin issues before missing those two games in September. He plays with a physical style, he takes hits after the catch, and when he’s healthy, he’s one of the most targeted receivers in football. He was targeted 24 times against Buffalo. That kind of workload is exactly why the Rams have to be smart with a calf injury.

So who plays if Puka can’t? According to the Rams’ official website, the receivers on the 53-man roster behind Puka and Adams are Konata Mumpfield, Jordan Whittington, Tutu Atwell, Alex Bachman and C.J. Daniels Jr. Brennan Presley is on the practice squad and could be elevated. Atwell is the veteran of the group — he’s been with the Rams since 2021 and has the most experience in McVay’s system. Whittington is a tough, physical blocker who can play the slot. And Mumpfield has been the most productive of the young group, with eight catches for 127 yards this season, according to the AP. None of them is a number-one receiver yet. But together, with the tight ends, they can keep the offense moving if the plan is built around quick throws and the run game.

So that’s the first big decision McVay faces: play Puka at less than 100 percent in a must-win game, or protect him for the long run. And honestly, there’s no easy answer.

Now let’s go through the other decisions McVay has already made this week, because there have been a lot of them.

Decision number one: no outside receiver — at least for now. On Wednesday, according to the Rams’ official website, McVay was asked about adding a receiver from outside the building. He said: “That’s not something that we’ve talked about right now.” He added: “We’ve got some depth on our roster. Maybe we talked about the practice squad, but not anything external.” That decision looks even more important now. If Puka can’t go, the Rams’ top receivers on Sunday would be Konata Mumpfield, Jordan Whittington and Tutu Atwell.

Decision number two: trust Mumpfield. According to The Sporting Tribune, McVay said this week: “He’s one of those guys that when he’s been counted on, he’s delivered.” And according to the AP, McVay also said: “What I do appreciate is he’s made the most out of his opportunities.” Remember, when Puka missed Weeks 2 and 3, Mumpfield had four catches for 93 yards and a go-ahead touchdown in the loss at Denver. If Puka is out or limited, Mumpfield could become Stafford’s number-one target this weekend.

Decision number three: Kobie Turner to injured reserve. According to Rams On SI, the Rams placed Turner on IR on Wednesday, which means he’ll miss at least four weeks. They also signed edge rusher Darryl Peterson III to the active roster and added Jalen Hunt to the practice squad.

Decision number four: a new kicker. The Rams signed Brandon McManus on Tuesday after a workout against Matt Prater. Sunday will be his first game as a Ram. That was a decision McVay and the front office made fast — Grupe got hurt on Sunday, went on IR on Monday, and McManus was signed by Tuesday. After two weeks where the Rams went from a released kicker to an injured kicker to a punter kicking the go-ahead extra point, getting a veteran with a Super Bowl ring in the building was the most important special teams decision of the season. McManus has kicked in big games before. On Sunday, the Rams just need him to make the routine kicks routine again.

And decision number five could come at linebacker. According to The Sporting Tribune, with Nate Landman in the concussion protocol, veteran Eric Kendricks — who joined the practice squad earlier this month — could be elevated to help fill in. Kendricks has been a starting linebacker in this league for a long time, and that experience could matter in a game where the Rams need someone to get the defense lined up correctly.

So no, McVay didn’t announce one shocking decision. He’s made a whole string of decisions just to get this team on the field healthy enough to compete. As McVay put it this week, according to The Sporting Tribune: “It’s building up pretty quickly.”

Let’s talk about how long that injury list really is, because it’s hard to believe.

According to Roundtable Sports via Yahoo, here’s where things stand for the Rams. Davante Adams: on IR with a ruptured hamstring, expected to miss six to 10 weeks, and according to the AP, he had surgery on Tuesday. Poona Ford: out for the season with a triceps injury. Blake Grupe: out for the season with a torn ACL. Kobie Turner: on IR, expected to miss four to six weeks. Myles Garrett: still on IR after knee surgery, and according to the AP, he hasn’t played since Week 1. Tight end Terrance Ferguson: still on IR with an ankle injury. Trent McDuffie: did not practice Wednesday or Thursday with a groin injury, and Roundtable Sports reported he’s tracking toward missing Sunday’s game. Nate Landman: did not practice Wednesday or Thursday and is in the concussion protocol. And now Puka, limited with a calf injury.

That’s a starting-caliber player at almost every level of the team. For a roster that entered the year as the Super Bowl favorite, it’s brutal.

Now, I want to be clear: injuries don’t explain everything. The Rams lost to Arizona with most of their offense healthy, and McVay has said over and over that the team is beating itself. But when you combine the mistakes with this many injuries, you start to understand why this season has gone the way it has.

There’s one piece of good news on defense, though. Aaron Donald is still there. In case you forgot, Donald came out of retirement in late August — according to theScore, on a one-year, 20 million dollar deal that can reach 30 million with incentives. He said at the time that he hoped he and Myles Garrett could “do some special things together.” Garrett got hurt after the opener, so we’ve barely seen that pairing. But with Turner and Ford out, Donald is more important than ever. According to The Sporting Tribune, Donald said recently: “It felt good just watching the film, looking more like myself.” If the best defensive player of his generation is starting to feel like himself again, that’s a big deal for a defense that’s lost so many bodies up front.

Now let’s talk about the man at the center of all of this: Matthew Stafford.

Stafford is the reigning MVP. According to the Rams’ official website, he won the AP MVP award for the 2025 season. And through six games this year, according to Sports Illustrated’s Iain MacMillan, he’s averaging 299 passing yards per game. But he’s also thrown six interceptions. MacMillan wrote that Stafford needs to play better with Adams out.

And the numbers show both sides of Stafford’s season. Against Buffalo, he went 40 of 54 for 383 yards and three touchdowns with no interceptions. Against Arizona, he went 25 of 33 for 222 yards and two touchdowns with no interceptions. And for three straight weeks, he’s led a touchdown drive in the final two minutes — in Philadelphia, against Buffalo and against Arizona. When the game is on the line, he’s still elite.

But according to FOX Sports, against Arizona, he averaged a season-low 4.8 air yards per attempt. And according to Rams On SI, his adjusted EPA per play through the first three quarters ranks 17th in the league, compared to seventh in the fourth quarter. So the issue isn’t whether Stafford can still play. It’s whether the Rams can get that fourth-quarter version of him from the first snap.

After the Arizona loss, Stafford said: “We are what our record is.” And this week, according to The Sporting Tribune, he talked about replacing Adams: “It’s a team effort.” According to Rams On SI, Stafford also said he feels more responsibility to help the team win with so many players out. That’s the right attitude. But if Puka can’t play, Stafford is going to have to make the most of an inexperienced receiver group, the tight ends and Kyren Williams out of the backfield.

So here’s the debate I want to put to you: does Stafford need to win this game, or does he just need to avoid losing it? I lean toward the second one. With this many injuries, the Rams don’t need Stafford to throw for 400 yards. They need him to protect the football, take the easy completions and let the run game and defense do the rest. No forced throws into double coverage. No turnovers that hand the Raiders short fields.

Now let’s look at the opponent, because the Raiders are not a team to take lightly.

According to the NFL’s official site, the Raiders are 3-3 and the Rams are 2-4. Kickoff is Sunday at 4:25 p.m. Eastern, 1:25 Pacific, at Allegiant Stadium, on FOX. The Raiders started 3-0, but according to the AP, they’ve now lost three straight, against Kansas City, a lopsided loss at New England, and a 33-30 loss to Buffalo last week.

First-year head coach Klint Kubiak put it bluntly, according to the AP: “That’s unacceptable, and that starts with me.” And right guard Caleb Rogers said: “We’re just a really good team who hasn’t closed in close games, and we’ve got to close.”

Sound familiar? These two teams are dealing with almost the same problem. They’re both losing close games, and they both know it. That makes this a dangerous game for the Rams. And McVay knows it. According to The Sporting Tribune, he said this week: “They’re well coached in all three phases.” And when he was asked about all the noise around his team, he kept it simple: “The game’s coming on Sunday.” A team that’s lost three straight, angry, playing at home and desperate to turn things around, is not going to roll over.

Here’s how the Raiders’ offense looks. Kirk Cousins has a 98.0 passer rating and 14 touchdown passes, tied for the league lead, according to the AP. Against Buffalo, he threw for 313 yards and two touchdowns. But according to the AP, the Raiders have scored touchdowns on only 12 of 23 red zone trips — and only four of 10 during this losing streak. Against Buffalo, they went just two for five. So they move the ball, but they stall near the end zone.

And there’s a fun connection here. McVay and Kirk Cousins know each other well. Before McVay became the Rams’ head coach, he was the offensive coordinator in Washington from 2014 to 2016 — and Cousins was his quarterback for much of that time. McVay helped build the offense that turned Cousins into a starter. Now he has to help his defense stop him. And of course, there’s the other storyline: Raiders head coach Klint Kubiak is the son of Gary Kubiak, who coached Brandon McManus to a Super Bowl title in Denver. So Sunday is full of old connections.

And here’s what the analytics say. According to Sports Illustrated’s MacMillan, the Raiders rank 26th in net yards per play, 25th in opponent EPA, 27th in opponent success rate, 27th in rush EPA and 29th in rush success rate. MacMillan called them a regression candidate and said: “It’s time to buy back in on the Rams.” He picked the Rams to cover.

And the betting market has been moving. According to Sports Illustrated, the Rams opened the week as 4.5-point favorites at BetMGM. According to Heavy, the line has since dropped to 3.5. That’s the market reacting to the Puka news. According to SI, the Rams are 5-1 against the spread in the last six meetings with the Raiders.

Let me also quickly mention Brock Bowers. According to the Raiders’ official injury report, he was limited on Wednesday with hamstring and calf issues. Bowers is one of the best tight ends in football, and if he’s healthy, he’s Cousins’ favorite target. With Landman possibly out, the Rams’ linebackers and safeties will have their hands full.

Now let’s zoom out to the big picture, because the standings make this game even more important.

According to Newsweek, the Rams are four games out of first place in the NFC West. The 49ers are 6-0. Arizona, at 2-4, sits ahead of the Rams because they beat them head-to-head. The Rams are 0-2 in the division. And according to NFL Next Gen Stats, the Rams’ playoff chances are down to 43 percent.

According to ESPN Research, as cited by TSN, the Rams are just the fifth team in the past 50 seasons to enter the year as the outright Super Bowl favorite and lose at least four of their first six games. None of the previous four made it to their conference championship game.

So what happens if the Rams lose on Sunday? At 2-5, the division is almost out of reach, and the margin for error in the wild-card race gets tiny. It wouldn’t be mathematically over, but it would be close to a must-win-every-week situation for the rest of the year. And after the Raiders, the Rams play the Chargers, then Washington, then one more game before their Week 11 bye — and after the bye come the Packers and a Christmas Day game in Seattle.

But if they win? At 3-4, with a home game against the Chargers next, the Rams are right back in the wild-card picture. That’s the difference one game can make in October.

And there’s a lot of pressure on McVay personally. After the Arizona loss, according to TSN, he said: “I don’t have those answers right now, otherwise it would’ve gotten fixed.” He also said: “It starts with me, and I got to be able to have some better answers.” I respect that. He hasn’t blamed the injuries, the officials or the players. But let’s also remember who we’re talking about. McVay has taken the Rams to two Super Bowls and won one. He turned around the 2021 season after a three-game losing streak and turned a 3-6 start into a playoff trip in 2023. If any coach can pull a team out of a hole like this, it’s him. But he has to start doing it on Sunday.

Now let’s talk about the matchup on the field, because there’s a clear path to a Rams win.

Start with the run game. MacMillan’s numbers show a Raiders team struggling on both sides of the ball — 25th in opponent EPA and 27th in opponent success rate on defense, and near the bottom of the league in rushing efficiency. According to the AP, Las Vegas ranks 26th in scoring defense at 27 points per game, and it’s allowed more than 415 yards per game over its last four games. If Puka is limited, the smartest thing McVay can do is lean on Kyren Williams and Blake Corum. Run the ball, shorten the game, protect Stafford, and keep the Rams’ shorthanded defense off the field.

That also fits what Kyren said after the Arizona game. When asked about the offense’s energy, he pointed to “self-inflicted wounds, not starting fast, not having that energy every single drive.” Give Kyren the ball early, and you address all three.

Next, the tight ends. With Adams out and Puka hurt, Colby Parkinson, Tyler Higbee and Davis Allen should be a big part of the game plan. Parkinson was a full participant all week despite a shoulder issue, and he caught the go-ahead touchdown against Arizona. Expect more two- and three-tight-end sets.

On defense, the Rams’ strength has been the pass defense. According to Fox Sports, the Rams allow only about 167 passing yards per game. Their weakness has been the run — about 127 rushing yards per game. And without Ford and Turner in the middle, stopping the run gets even harder. That’s where Donald comes in. And it’s not just about the run. According to the Raiders’ official injury report, left tackle Kolton Miller was limited on Wednesday with a shoulder injury. If Miller isn’t at full strength, that’s an opportunity for Donald, newly signed edge rusher Darryl Peterson III and the rest of the pass rush to get after Cousins. Cousins is a smart, accurate quarterback, but he’s never been a scrambler. Pressure up the middle is the best way to rattle him, and nobody in football has been better at creating pressure up the middle than Aaron Donald. If Donald has a big game, the Rams’ shorthanded defense suddenly looks a lot more dangerous.

And special teams. McManus is kicking in a dome with a fixed roof, which helps. But he’s working with a snapper and holder he’s only had a few days to practice with. One clean operation on Sunday — every extra point, every makeable field goal — could decide this game.

Here are my three keys for Sunday.

Key number one: win without forcing it. Whether Puka plays or not, Stafford has to be clean. No interceptions. If the Rams win the turnover battle, they win this game.

Key number two: run the ball and start fast. Twenty carries against Arizona wasn’t enough. Against a Raiders defense that’s been giving up big yardage, Kyren and Corum should combine for 25 carries or more. Score on the opening drive and make the Raiders chase the game.

Key number three: hold them in the red zone. The Rams held Arizona to zero touchdowns in five red zone trips. The Raiders have struggled in the red zone during their losing streak. If the Rams make Las Vegas kick field goals instead of scoring touchdowns, they’ll win.

Here’s my prediction. If Puka plays, I’m sticking with what I said earlier this week: Rams 27, Raiders 20. If Puka is out, I’ll lower it to Rams 24, Raiders 20 — a closer game, with Kyren and the tight ends carrying the offense and the defense making a late stop. Either way, I think the Rams win this one. This team is too talented and too desperate to lose to a team that’s struggling just as much as they are.

So here’s my bottom line.

McVay didn’t announce one shocking decision before the Raiders game. What he’s done is make a series of hard choices — no outside receiver, trusting Mumpfield, putting Kobie Turner on IR, signing McManus, and possibly elevating Eric Kendricks. And now the biggest decision of the week is still to come: whether Puka Nacua plays through a calf injury, or whether the Rams play without both of their top two receivers.

Whatever that decision is, the message for Sunday is simple. The Rams don’t need to be perfect. They need to be clean. Protect the football, run the ball, start fast, and stop beating themselves. Do that, and they leave Las Vegas at 3-4 with their season still very much alive.
