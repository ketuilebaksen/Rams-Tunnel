Let’s start with the injury report, because that’s how McVay opened his Friday press conference.

Here’s what he said, in order.

Coleman Shelton. McVay said the center “will be limited, listed as questionable for the game with his hip.” According to the Rams’ official website, Shelton had missed practice on Wednesday and Thursday, so being limited on Friday is actually a step in the right direction.

Colby Parkinson. McVay said the tight end would be a DNP — did not participate — and questionable. According to the Rams’ website, it’s a rib injury. Parkinson didn’t practice at all this week.

Joe Cardona. McVay said the long snapper “will be doubtful for the game with his ankle.” And then he added the line that tells you what’s going to happen: “That would mean Jake McQuaide would be up as the long snapper.”

Trent McDuffie. McVay said the cornerback would be limited and questionable with his groin. He added that the team is “hopeful, but you know, we’ll continue to see how he’s at.”

And then the surprise: Jordan Whittington. McVay said Whittington’s “back just locked up, you know, within the last 30 minutes.” He added: “He’s tough as it gets,” and said Whittington would be listed as questionable.

So that’s five players with a game status. One doubtful, four questionable. And on the good side of the report, according to the Rams’ website, linebacker Nate Landman and linebacker Desjuan Johnson were full participants and have no game status. Puka Nacua, Aaron Donald, Byron Young and Alaric Jackson were all listed with rest days on Friday — normal veteran rest, not injuries.

If that’s the “shocking decision,” it’s a pretty quiet one. But each of those names matters, so let’s go through them.

First, the long snapper.

According to Pro Football Talk, the Rams signed Jake McQuaide to the practice squad this week, along with kicker Joshua Karty and linebacker Jon Rhattigan. McQuaide was the Rams’ long snapper from 2011, back in St. Louis, through 2020, according to Pro Football Talk. He’s a two-time Pro Bowler, according to Heavy. And he came back for the last nine games of last season.

So when McVay says McQuaide “would be up,” he means the Rams would elevate him from the practice squad to the game-day roster. That’s a smart move. You never want a backup who’s never snapped in a game handling field goals in a close one. McQuaide has done this hundreds of times.

Now, there was a related question in the press conference that I want to be honest about. A reporter asked McVay whether the Rams would elevate “Josh.” From the audio, it isn’t completely clear who that refers to — it may have been a question about kicker Joshua Karty, but I can’t be 100 percent sure. McVay’s answer was about the game-day roster as a whole. He said: “Part of that is just being able to figure out our 48.” And he said that with Whittington’s back, he had to work through “how that affects some different things if he’s not able to go.”

Let me explain what he means by “our 48,” because it’s a detail most fans don’t think about. NFL teams can only dress a limited number of players on game day, and the rest are inactive. So every week, coaches have to decide who’s active. That’s my own explanation of the rule. If a player like Whittington, who also helps on special teams, can’t go, it affects more than just the receiver room. It changes who covers kicks, who returns kicks and who’s the backup at several spots. McVay said: “You also have to be ready for contingency plans if injuries occur within the game.” That’s the real puzzle the Rams are solving this weekend.

And Whittington’s injury hits a thin spot. With Davante Adams out with his hamstring, the Rams’ receiver room is already short. According to Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak, Colts receiver Josh Downs has more receiving yards this season than Tutu Atwell, Konata Mumpfield and Whittington combined — and that’s a sign of how little the Rams have gotten from that group. Against the Raiders, according to the AP, Mumpfield’s 15-yard catch was the only reception by a Rams wide receiver other than Puka. So if Whittington can’t go, Atwell and Mumpfield will have to play even more snaps, and the Rams may lean on their tight ends again — which is harder if Parkinson can’t play either. That’s why this injury report matters more than it looks. And it’s one more reason to keep an eye on the receiver trade market before the November 10th deadline.

Now, McDuffie. If he plays, it’s a big boost. According to the Chargers’ website, receiver Ladd McConkey is off the injury report after his concussion and is cleared to play. McConkey is the Chargers’ most dangerous young receiver, and McDuffie is exactly the type of cornerback you want matching up with him. If McDuffie can’t go, the Rams will need their backup corners to keep McConkey in front of them and tackle well after the catch. McVay said “hopeful,” which is about as positive as a coach usually gets on a Friday without making a promise.

And just as a reminder, two other names weren’t on this report because they’re still on injured reserve. According to the Rams’ website, the team decided this week not to open the 21-day practice windows for Myles Garrett or Terrance Ferguson. McVay said about Garrett: “I know it’s frustrating, but he’s making good progress, and we’re just on a week-to-week basis right now.” Back in September, according to Pro Football Talk, NFL Network’s Ian Rapoport reported that the Rams’ Week 9 game against Washington was Garrett’s most likely return date. We’ll find out next week whether that’s still realistic.

Now let’s talk about the center position, because this one could really matter on Sunday.

A reporter asked McVay who would start at center if Shelton couldn’t play. McVay said: “What’s been good is Dylan McMahon’s done a great job this week.” He added that McMahon has “gotten a lot of work this week because of the approach we’ve taken with Coleman,” and said: “He would be the guy that would step up.”

McVay also mentioned Beaux Limmer, who has played center for the Rams before, and said: “I don’t think you can have enough centers just because of the importance of that position.” He said the importance goes beyond just snapping the ball — it’s also “a lot of the communication that goes inside out.”

According to Heavy, Rams offensive coordinator Nate Scheelhaase also praised McMahon. Scheelhaase said: “I’m really encouraged by Dylan McMahon and what he’s been able to do since being here.” And he called Shelton a “tone setter for us.”

Stafford talked about Shelton, too. In his media session, he said Shelton is “obviously a really good player physically for us,” a “great communicator” and “mentally really sharp,” and that he “does a great job for us in both run and pass game stuff.”

Here’s why this matters so much on Sunday. The center is the one calling out protections — who blocks whom. And the Chargers have Khalil Mack. According to the Chargers’ website, Mack is off the injury report after full practices on Thursday and Friday. If the Rams have a backup center making the calls, the Chargers might try to confuse the protection with different blitzes and stunts. So if McMahon starts, watch the first few series closely. If the communication is clean, the Rams should be fine.

Now, the most interesting part of McVay’s press conference — at least for me — was what he said about the defense and about turnovers.

A reporter asked McVay what he’s seen from his defense over the last two weeks. McVay said: “Physicality, strain.” He talked about “playing 11 as one” and said the defense has been “really good situationally” in the red area and on third downs. And then he gave a stat: he said the defense went about seven quarters without allowing a touchdown over the last two games. In his words: “I want to say, seven quarters before we end up giving a touchdown over those last two weeks.”

He also gave credit to defensive coordinator Chris Shula and the defensive coaches for putting together plans that “accentuate our guys’ skill sets.”

And the numbers back up part of that. According to the Rams’ official website, opponents have failed on fourth down seven times this season. Against the Raiders, according to the AP, the Rams held Las Vegas to 3-of-14 on third down and stopped them on fourth down three times.

But then came the honest part. A reporter pointed out that the defense hasn’t forced as many turnovers as in past seasons. According to the Rams’ website, the Rams have just six takeaways and 10 sacks, both near the bottom of the NFL. McVay said turnovers “come in bunches,” and that the key is to “just keep committing to the fundamentals of attacking.”

He even joked about one of his players dropping an interception last week. He said: “We give” him “a hard time. He could have caught that one last week early on, but he’s got great ball skills.” I’m not going to guess the player’s name, because it wasn’t clear in the audio.

And then McVay said the line I think is the most important of the whole week: “I’m not a big stats guy, but the stats that do matter, obviously points is the most important, but then outside of that is your turnover margin and your explosive play differential.”

Now here’s why that’s so interesting. According to the Rams’ official website, the Chargers have the most giveaways in the NFL this season, with 15. And Justin Herbert is the only quarterback with an interception in every game. So the Rams’ defense, which has struggled to force turnovers, is facing the offense that gives the ball away more than anyone. If the turnovers really do “come in bunches,” Sunday might be the day the bunch arrives.

Now, McVay also talked about his offense, and his answer was really revealing.

A reporter asked about the play-action game. McVay said everything starts with being “efficient on early downs.” He said: “I think everybody in the league, all 32 teams would say that, hey, you want to be able to marry the run and the pass and the conflict that it can put defenses in.”

And then he said something I really liked: “I’m not necessarily as interested in like balance as much as how do we consistently move the ball and score points.” He explained that when an offense has “the threat of both” — run and pass — “you kind of keep people off balance,” and “that’s when we’ve been at our best offensively.”

He said the key against the Raiders was being efficient on early downs, and that it “was a huge factor for last week for sure.”

The numbers from Las Vegas show it. According to the AP, the Rams ran 38 times and threw just 29 passes, and they rushed for 199 yards. According to the Rams’ website, Stafford completed 79 percent of his passes, his highest as a Ram, and he’s completed at least 70 percent of his passes in three straight games. But here’s the other side: according to the Rams’ website, Stafford has averaged under five air yards per attempt in his last two games, which he hadn’t done in his previous 23 games. And Scheelhaase said: “There are some games, like the last one, where your biggest gain is 17 yards.”

So the Rams are efficient, but they aren’t explosive. And remember what McVay said about the stats that matter: turnover margin and explosive play differential. Right now, the Rams’ offense is winning with efficiency. At some point, they need a few big plays too.

Then a reporter asked about playing without injured players like Terrance Ferguson. McVay said: “It affects it a lot.” He explained: “You don’t replace playmakers like those guys.” It changes “some of the personnel groupings you’ll activate.” But he also said: “I think it’s cool to be able to see other guys step up.” He pointed to the tight ends making more plays, both running backs getting “a good amount of carries,” and a catch by Konata Mumpfield on second-and-10. And he said: “Obviously Puka will get his share as well.”

Stafford talked about the running backs, too. He said he trusts both Kyren Williams and Blake Corum “to execute any play we have in our offense at any time.” He said: “I think you feel really comfortable with both those guys doing everything we ask of a halfback in this offense.” And he said: “It’s an awesome feeling, you know, as a player knowing that you’re getting a guy who’s fresh and motivated and ready to rock and roll.”

Now let’s talk about Aaron Donald, because McVay had some great things to say.

A reporter said Donald seems looser and like he’s having more fun since coming back. McVay said Donald has “different perspective.” He said there’s “an appreciation, there’s a gratitude that he has having stepped away for a couple years.” And he said: “I think you’re just watching a guy that is maturing and continuing to mature.”

Then McVay told a funny story. A reporter asked whether Donald could be good in media one day. McVay pointed to Donald’s pregame work at last year’s Super Bowl and said that when Donald gets going on football, “he never shuts up.” He said people see Donald “as this introverted man of few words, but when he speaks, there’s a lot of efficacy behind his words.” And McVay said: “He’s an inspiration to be around and I’m grateful for him.”

Stafford was asked about Donald, too. He said: “He’s in the weight room every other time I turn the corner, look in there, he’s lifting, doing his thing.” He said it “feels like the same stuff to me.”

Now here’s the part people might not know. According to the Rams’ official website, Donald still doesn’t have a sack in five appearances this season. But he’s being double-teamed at the highest rate of his career — 76 percent of the time. That’s the hidden value. When Donald draws two blockers on most snaps, somebody else should be free. That’s why the Rams desperately need more from their other pass rushers — and why Myles Garrett’s return is so important.

And Donald’s own quote this week, according to the Rams’ website: “The only thing that excites me is winning.” That’s Aaron Donald.

Now let’s talk about what Stafford said about the Chargers, because he had some fun with it.

A reporter asked Stafford about playing the team he shares a building with. According to The Sporting Tribune, Stafford said: “I don’t run into those guys too often, to be honest with you.” And he joked: “I think it’s a big town to cross.” According to the AP, he was joking about the distance between the two teams’ training facilities.

On Khalil Mack, Stafford said: “Obviously, I used to be in the division with Khalil back in the day when he was in Chicago. He still looks like he’s running around making plays.” And that’s true — Stafford played Mack twice a year when Stafford was with the Lions and Mack was with the Bears.

Stafford also praised the Chargers’ defense. He said: “When you watch the tape, shoot, their defense as a whole runs around, flies around. I think they’ve been playing really good defense this year.” He added: “Obviously, they haven’t gotten probably some of the results that they’ve wanted, but really well coached group.”

In the full press conference, Stafford also said the Chargers’ defense is “attacking a bunch of different ways” and that they “mix their personnel really well.” He mentioned a “good mix of young guys up front that are playing at a high level.” And he talked about Derwin James, calling him a “perennial big-time player,” and said he “saw the news there” — a reference to James’ setback. According to the Chargers’ website, James has since been ruled out for Sunday. That’s a big loss for the Chargers, because James is the type of player who can line up anywhere and disguise what the defense is doing. Without him, the Chargers’ coverage becomes easier for Stafford to read before the snap.

And on the Chargers’ record, Stafford said: “We don’t really look at the other team’s record and go, ‘They’re good or they’re bad.’ It’s, ‘Hey, this is NFL football.’” He said: “You have to go out there and earn it and do it. That’s our mindset this week and will be for the rest of the year.”

Stafford was also asked about urgency, with the Rams sitting at 3-4. And his answer was very Stafford. He said: “I don’t think we look as big picture of that, maybe as some of the outside world does. We’re here on a Wednesday trying to figure out what we’re going to do on normal down and distance against the Chargers.” But he added: “I think the urgency anytime you step on a field, especially in the NFL, should be high.” And he said the team still wants to “enjoy our time out there” and “go have fun, playing the game that we love to play.” That’s a veteran quarterback who’s been through enough seasons to know that you don’t win in November by panicking in October.

McVay said something similar on Wednesday. According to The Sporting Tribune, he said: “I really couldn’t care less what the record says.” He said: “I see a team that’s really good. They’ve had a chance to win a lot of these games.” And he praised the Chargers’ offense, saying: “I know how good of a coach Mike McDaniel is and how good they are offensively with their personnel.”

So nobody in the Rams’ building is overlooking this team. And they shouldn’t. According to the AP, the Chargers are the NFL’s only winless team and its lowest-scoring team. But they’re coming off a bye. Jim Harbaugh said, according to the AP: “I feel like every stone, any part that we could look to better, that’s the way we responded.” And Justin Herbert said: “I think it was a very helpful and useful week.”

Here’s one more number that matters for the Rams’ pass rush. According to the AP, Herbert has taken 21 sacks this season. And according to the Rams’ official website, the Chargers have allowed 81 sacks since the start of last season, tied for the most in football. So if the Rams are ever going to get their pass rush going, this is the week.

Now a little history. According to the AP, the Rams lead the all-time series 7-6. The Chargers won the last meeting 31-10 on January 1st, 2023, in Inglewood. And this is only the third meeting since both teams moved to Los Angeles. The two teams only play about once every four years.

And there’s one more fun detail. According to Yahoo Sports, the game will be on FOX with Adam Amin on play-by-play, Drew Brees as the analyst and Kristina Pink as the reporter. That’s a great connection, because Brees played a big role in Stafford’s career. A reporter asked Stafford about it this week. Stafford said: “I just have a ton of respect for guys that did it for a long time at a high level.” And he said Brees did it “for an extremely long period of time at a really high level.”

Here’s the backstory. According to Forbes, Stafford said on Chris Long’s “Green Light” podcast that before the 2025 season, Brees asked him how old he was — he was 37 — and told him he might have the best five years of his career coming up. Stafford said: “Drew saying that kinda lit a fire under me.” And what happened next? Stafford won the 2025 MVP. So on Sunday, the man who lit that fire will be calling Stafford’s game.

Now let’s put it all together with my keys for Sunday.

Key number one: the center and the snapper. If Shelton can’t play, McMahon has to handle Mack and the Chargers’ pressure calls. And McQuaide has to be perfect on every snap. The Rams don’t need these two to be heroes. They need them to be invisible.

Key number two: early-down efficiency, plus a few explosive plays. McVay told you early downs were the key last week. Keep that going with Kyren and Corum. But the Rams also need to find some big plays. With Derwin James and Deane Leonard both out for the Chargers, according to the Chargers’ website, this is a good week to take a couple of shots down the field.

Key number three: win the turnover battle. McVay said turnover margin is one of the stats that matters most. The Chargers lead the league in giveaways, and Herbert has thrown an interception in every game. If the Rams can get two takeaways, I think this game is over by the fourth quarter.

My prediction: Rams 27, Chargers 17. The Rams are getting healthier on defense, the Chargers are missing key pieces in their secondary, and the turnover matchup favors the Rams. But a team coming off a bye with Herbert and Mack can make it close if the Rams’ backup center or backup long snapper has a rough day.

So here’s my bottom line.

There was no shocking decision from McVay this week. What we got was an honest injury report: Cardona doubtful, with Jake McQuaide likely to snap; Shelton, Parkinson, McDuffie and Whittington questionable; and Dylan McMahon ready to start at center if needed. We got McVay telling us exactly what he values — turnover margin and explosive plays — right before a game against the team with the most giveaways in the league. And we got Stafford saying the Rams aren’t looking at the Chargers’ record, they’re looking at the tape.

That’s not a shocking headline. But it’s the real story, and I think it’s a lot more useful.
