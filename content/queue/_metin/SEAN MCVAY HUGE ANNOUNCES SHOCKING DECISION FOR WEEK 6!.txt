Now, I want to be clear: nobody’s being benched, and nobody’s been ruled out on the Rams’ side. This isn’t a dramatic shake-up. Let’s start with the biggest name on the injury report: Trent McDuffie.

But first, a little context on this week, because it explains a lot of what we saw on the injury report. The Rams played on Monday night, which means a short week. They didn’t actually practice on Wednesday — the Wednesday report was only an estimate. So the team had just two real practices, Thursday and Friday, to get ready for Arizona. On a short week like this, every bit of soreness from Monday’s overtime game shows up later than usual. That’s exactly what happened with Curl, and it’s why McVay was so careful with his veterans on Friday.

Here’s how McVay explained it on Friday. McDuffie felt a little something in his groin at the end of Thursday’s practice. So the Rams held him out on Friday and listed him as questionable. And McVay said: “Trent felt a little something in his groin so we’re going to be smart with him.” He also said: “He has the right mindset and mentality and we’ll just see where we’re at with that.”

When a reporter asked whether this came from the Bills game or from practice, McVay explained that it was a practice issue — something McDuffie felt at the end of Thursday’s session — and pointed out how many snaps McDuffie has been playing. McVay called him “so tough” for the workload he’s carried.

Now, why does this matter so much? Because McDuffie has been one of the best cornerbacks in football this season. According to Yahoo Sports, through five weeks, McDuffie has allowed the lowest passer rating in coverage of any cornerback in the NFL: 21.1. Twenty-one point one. That’s lower than a quarterback would get by throwing every single pass into the dirt. Pro Football Focus had him at an 89.1 grade earlier this season, and he and Quentin Lake were leading the league in pass breakups.

And remember what the Rams paid to get him. They traded the 29th pick in the 2026 draft, plus a fifth and a sixth that year and a 2027 third-rounder, to Kansas City. Then they signed him to a four-year, 124 million dollar contract that made him the highest-paid cornerback in football at the time. He’s not just a starter. He’s a cornerstone of this defense for years to come.

And he’s been a huge part of why this defense has looked so different in coverage. Through the first four games, the Rams allowed just 990 total yards and only five pass plays of 20 yards or more. Defensive coordinator Chris Shula has been able to blitz far more aggressively than he did last season — his blitz rate jumped from under 14 percent in Week 1 to more than 50 percent against the Eagles, based on the numbers we’ve covered on this channel. You can only blitz like that when you trust your cornerbacks on an island. McDuffie is the reason Shula can trust them. Without him, I’d expect Shula to dial back the pressure a little and play more zone to protect the back end.

That’s why “be smart” is the right decision. You don’t risk a 124 million dollar cornerback with a groin injury against a 1-4 team in October. Groin injuries are tricky. Push one too early, and a one-week issue can become a four-week issue. McVay has been consistent about this all season — with Puka Nacua’s groin, with Aaron Donald’s back, and with Myles Garrett’s knee. When the Rams have a choice between short-term risk and long-term health, they’ve been choosing long-term health.

So what happens if McDuffie can’t go?

According to Yahoo Sports, Josh Wallace and Emmanuel Forbes Jr. could see bigger roles opposite Jaylen Watson. Wallace was limited earlier this week with a shoulder issue, but he practiced fully on Friday and has no game designation. And Watson has been playing through his own pain — he dislocated his shoulder in Denver and still came back to intercept Josh Allen on Monday night while wearing a harness. The Rams also have veteran Tre’Davious White, who they acquired this season.

Here’s the good news for the Rams: if there’s ever a week to be without your top cornerback, it’s this one. And I’ll explain why in a few minutes when we get to the Cardinals’ injury report.

Now, Kam Curl. McVay said the safety’s ankle issue was “soreness” from Monday night’s game. Curl was limited on Thursday and then didn’t practice on Friday, which is why he’s listed as questionable. But McVay sounded optimistic: “We feel good about him.”

So the Rams could be without two of their four starters in the secondary on Sunday — or they could have both. My read: Curl sounds more likely to play than McDuffie. McVay’s tone about Curl was confident. With McDuffie, it was cautious.

Both were listed as not injury related. That’s McVay managing his veterans’ bodies over a long season. It’s the same approach we’ve seen with Matthew Stafford. These aren’t injuries — they’re rest. Both players have no game designation and should be full go on Sunday.

So when you add it up, here’s the Rams’ injury picture: nobody ruled out, two starters questionable in the secondary, and almost everyone else trending up. That’s a pretty healthy team for Week 6.

And here’s one more matchup to keep an eye on without McDuffie: Trey McBride. Arizona’s tight end has 42 catches this season — he’s by far their most reliable target. That’s a job for Quentin Lake, the Rams’ versatile defensive back who’s been one of the best in football at breaking up passes. If Lake can limit McBride, Arizona doesn’t have many other places to go with the football, especially with Marvin Harrison Jr. now in Houston.

Now let’s talk about Myles Garrett, because McVay gave us a timeline for the next answer.

When he was asked whether the Rams expect to open Garrett’s 21-day practice window next week, McVay said he’ll talk with the medical staff and that he’ll have the answer on Monday. Here’s how he put it: “whenever we connect on Monday, I’ll have that answer for you.” And then he added: “He’s got a good look in his eye and he’s making good progress. So we’ll see.”

A reporter followed up by asking whether the Rams were in any hurry to get Garrett back. And this is where McVay gave one of his most honest answers of the season. He said it’s important to get Garrett back, and he didn’t want that misunderstood. But he said: “I’m not a doctor.” He said the plan is to trust the medical staff, and he even joked about how often he repeats himself. He said he’s “like Woody from Toy Story” — “just wind me up” — because he keeps coming back to the same idea: return to performance.

And then he explained what that means. Garrett has benchmarks he needs to hit in his rehab and recovery after knee surgery. According to McVay, Garrett is hitting all of those benchmarks. The question now is what it means to actually get back and play real football. That’s where McVay says the Rams are “truly week to week.” And he finished with this: “I’d love to have him back out there, but not at the expense of rushing him back.”

So here’s what that tells me. Garrett’s rehab is on track. He’s hitting his milestones. And on Monday, we’ll find out whether the Rams open his practice window next week, ahead of the Raiders game. Remember, NFL Network’s Ian Rapoport has reported that Garrett is expected back around Week 9. If the window opens on Monday, that timeline looks right on schedule. If not, it probably slides a little. Either way, Monday is the day to watch.

Remember the timeline here. Garrett played in Week 1 against San Francisco in Melbourne. Then he had knee surgery, and the Rams placed him on injured reserve on September 17th, three days after the procedure. Earlier this week, McVay made a point that stuck with a lot of people: “There’s a difference between return to play and return to performance.” He’s been saying the same thing ever since — which is exactly why he made the Woody joke on Friday. And think about what the Rams gave up for Garrett: Jared Verse, a 2027 first-round pick, a 2028 second and a 2029 third. When you make that kind of investment, you don’t rush the player back for a game in October. You make sure he’s fully himself for December and January.

Now let’s talk about Blake Grupe, because McVay made a decision here that a lot of coaches wouldn’t. He’s putting a kicker into a game after just two days of practice with the team.

A reporter asked McVay how he knows when he can trust Grupe in a game after such a short time. And McVay said the process is “very similar” to how the Rams brought in Harrison Mevis last year. He said Grupe “came in, he’s got a nice vibe and demeanor.” He said: “I’m sure when you guys talk to him, you know, there’s a quiet confidence.” And he said Grupe “did a great job yesterday.”

McVay also admitted that a practice session isn’t the same as a game. Kicking in practice, with wind and different conditions, isn’t the same as stepping into a stadium environment. But he said one of the reasons the Rams felt comfortable signing Grupe was his experience. He’s kicked in a lot of big settings. Now the focus is on the operation — the snap, the hold, and making sure the timing is synced up. McVay called Thursday “a good positive step,” said they’d do the same thing on Friday, and then said: “I do feel confident based on what I’ve seen and in his experience to put him out there.”

And that’s the right approach. Grupe has 105 career field goal attempts and a 60-yard field goal on his résumé. He went 11 for 11 with the Colts last season. He doesn’t need to learn how to kick. He needs to learn his new snapper and holder. Two days of reps is enough to get the timing right for a veteran.

And it’s worth remembering why Grupe is here. Harrison Mevis made five of nine field goals before he was released, and every one of his misses came from 40 yards or longer. Grupe’s career numbers are 86 of 105 on field goals and 96 of 98 on extra points. His 2025 season in New Orleans was rough — 18 of 26 — but after the Colts picked him up, he was perfect, including four for four from 50 yards and beyond and a 60-yard field goal in Seattle that set a Colts franchise record. The Rams are betting on that Colts version. Sunday is his first chance to prove them right. And as McVay said earlier this week, the ideal scenario is that Grupe kicks a lot more extra points than field goals on Sunday.

Now, the LaFleur question. Every reporter wanted to know whether the Rams have to change their play calls because Arizona’s head coach, Mike LaFleur, spent the last three seasons as the Rams’ offensive coordinator.

And McVay gave a really interesting answer. He said this has become “very normal” — the Rams have faced a lot of former coaches who’ve done a great job for them. Then he explained when familiarity really matters. He said that if the Rams were an operation where you stop at the line of scrimmage and communicate with code words — like they did earlier in his tenure — that “would be a different deal,” because the defense can hear it and react. In that situation, you’d adjust certain words that LaFleur might recognize. But he said that’s not really how the Rams operate right now. And he finished with the same phrase he used earlier in the week: they’re “not chasing a ghost.”

He also gave LaFleur a lot of credit. He said, “he’s a great coach,” and acknowledged that LaFleur “knows exactly” what the Rams have been trying to do. But he added that there are “always new iterations.” In other words: LaFleur knows the foundation, but McVay’s offense evolves every week. Knowing last year’s plays doesn’t mean you know this week’s plan.

And here’s the flip side. LaFleur is now running his own offense in Arizona — and the Rams’ defensive staff has spent three years in the same building as him. They know how he thinks about attacking defenses, too. On Sunday, LaFleur will be working with Jacoby Brissett at quarterback, and Arizona added backup quarterback Carson Beck to the injury report on Friday with a back issue, although Beck has no game designation. Without Love in the backfield, LaFleur will have to lean more on Brissett’s arm — and that’s exactly where the Rams’ pass rush can take over.

Aaron Donald has 16 career sacks against the Cardinals, his most against any team. Arizona’s offensive line has already allowed 12 sacks this season, and left tackle Paris Johnson Jr. is on injured reserve. Byron Young will be fresh after his rest day. If the Rams get after Brissett early, it won’t matter much what LaFleur knows about McVay’s offense.

Now, special teams. A reporter asked McVay what his conversations with special teams coordinator Bubba Ventrone have been like after the struggles of the last few weeks. And McVay’s answer showed you exactly how he’s handling this.

He said his job is “to really be able to coach the team,” and that he wants “to see this get fixed.” He said each play is “its own separate entity,” and that the staff is addressing things “head-on.” The questions they’re asking: could they put players in better spots, or are there execution errors in the fundamentals and techniques?

He said he wants Ventrone to know “I’m right there to help.” He said he’s very familiar with everything Ventrone is coaching, “as I should be.” And he said the approach is to be “solution-oriented.” Then he finished with a line that got a laugh: he’s hopeful the fix happens “so I don’t have to keep talking about it with you.”

That’s consistent with what McVay said on Tuesday, when he made it clear there would be no changes to the coaching staff: “I have a lot of confidence in Bubba Ventrone.” He changed the kicker. He kept the coordinator. And now he’s personally involved in fixing the unit.

Now — the Cardinals’ injury report. And this is where things get really interesting.

Arizona’s rookie running back Jeremiyah Love — the fourth overall pick in this year’s draft — has been ruled out with an ankle injury. He didn’t practice all week. Love leads the Cardinals with 245 rushing yards this season. Without him, Tyler Allgeier is expected to take over the bulk of the work. That’s a huge break for a Rams run defense that just gave up 234 rushing yards to Buffalo.

But the bigger story might be Arizona’s secondary. The Cardinals have ruled out Wydett Williams with a concussion and safety Andrew Wingard with a knee injury. Safety Kitan Crawford is questionable with an ankle. And Budda Baker — the Cardinals’ best defensive player, the safety Davante Adams singled out this week — is questionable too, after missing practice on Wednesday and Friday. Linebacker Zaven Collins was added to the report on Friday with a hip injury and is questionable. Even their punter, Blake Gillikin, is questionable with a back issue.

So think about this matchup. The Rams’ offense, led by the NFL’s leading passer, against a Cardinals secondary that could be missing as many as three or four safeties. Arizona’s pass defense already ranked dead last in the NFL in dropback EPA, according to WalterFootball’s matchup hub. Now it might be playing without its leader in the back end.

This is exactly why I said earlier that if the Rams had to pick a week to be without McDuffie, this would be it. Arizona just lost its best rookie weapon. Marvin Harrison Jr. is gone after the trade to Houston. And quarterback Jacoby Brissett’s top remaining target is tight end Trey McBride, who has 42 catches for 308 yards and three touchdowns this season. That’s a passing attack the Rams’ defense can handle even without McDuffie.

Let’s look at the numbers. According to Football Nation USA’s matchup guide, the Rams have allowed about 4.7 yards per play this season. The Cardinals have allowed about 6.1. The Rams have gained about 5.8 yards per play on offense. Arizona has gained about 4.4. Stafford has completed 139 of 216 passes for 1,572 yards, nine touchdowns and six interceptions. Brissett has completed 132 of 199 for 1,110 yards, nine touchdowns and five interceptions. And Arizona’s offensive line has allowed 12 sacks, compared to seven for the Rams.

The betting market agrees with the numbers. According to that same guide, the Rams opened as 14-point favorites, and the line has settled around 12.5. The total has climbed from 47.5 to 51.5, which tells you the market expects points — a lot of them.

And history backs the Rams up, too. The last two times these teams met, the Rams won 45-17 in December of last season and 37-20 in Week 18. Arizona is in the first season of a rebuild under LaFleur. The Rams are a 2-3 team that’s been within one score in its losses to Denver and Buffalo. Talent-wise, this is a mismatch. The only way the Cardinals keep this close is if the Rams beat themselves with turnovers, penalties or special teams mistakes — the same things McVay keeps talking about.

Before the grades, here are the three things I’ll be watching first on Sunday.

Number one: the inactive list, 90 minutes before kickoff. If McDuffie is active, watch his snap count in the first quarter. If the Rams are protecting him, you might see him rotate with Josh Wallace or Emmanuel Forbes Jr. If he’s inactive, watch how Shula adjusts the blitz rate.

Number two: Grupe’s first kick. Whether it’s an extra point or a field goal, that first snap, hold and kick will tell you a lot about the new operation. A clean first attempt calms everyone down — the kicker, the coaches and 70,000 fans who still remember the blocked kick from Monday night.

Number three: the opening drive. Stafford said the offense executed better in the second half against Buffalo. Against a Cardinals secondary that could be without multiple safeties, the Rams should test the deep middle early. A touchdown on the first drive would set the tone for the whole afternoon.

So now let me grade McVay’s decisions from Friday.

Holding McDuffie out of practice and being cautious: A. You protect a 124 million dollar cornerback in October. Every time.

The veteran rest days for Higbee and Byron Young: A. Smart, long-term roster management, and it costs nothing against a 1-4 opponent.

The Garrett approach: A-minus. Rams fans want him back yesterday, and so do I. But McVay’s “return to performance” message is the right one. A Garrett who comes back at 100 percent in November is worth far more than a Garrett who comes back at 80 percent in October.

Trusting Grupe after two days: B-plus. It’s the right call given his experience, but the proof will come on Sunday. If he hits his first kick from 40-plus, this grade goes up.

And the LaFleur answer: A. Clear, honest and confident. No paranoia, no conspiracy theories. Just execute.
