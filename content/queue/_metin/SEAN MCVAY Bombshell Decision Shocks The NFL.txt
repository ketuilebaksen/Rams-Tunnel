He explained why protecting Stafford matters even more against this Arizona defense. And he made fun of himself in a way I didn’t expect, when a reporter asked about the players’ shadow boxing league.

Part one: the Puka “decision”

McVay started with Puka, because he knew everyone would ask.

He said: “I’m sure you guys are wondering what changed with Puka. We needed the roster spot. We didn’t need it initially.”

Then he added the key point: “There was never going to be a time that he was going to be able to return prior to anyways, kind of what I had suggested to you guys.”

In other words, putting Puka on IR doesn’t change his timeline at all. He was always going to miss at least four games. IR just makes that official. And it frees up a roster spot.

McVay was clear that the spot was needed for a specific reason: “Some things changed where we needed that spot because of all the moving pieces. That was what we did to be able to get Brennan Presley up.”

And then the part I liked most: “He’s in good spirits. Start his rehab and take it a little bit at a time.”

So here’s the summary. On Wednesday, the Rams planned to keep Puka on the active roster. By Thursday, they needed the spot, so Puka went on IR and Presley came up. Puka’s knee hasn’t gotten worse. The timeline hasn’t changed. And he’s in good spirits as he starts rehab.

Per ESPN, Puka will miss at least the next four games: at Arizona, home against Green Bay, home against Kansas City, and at San Francisco.

Part two: Garrett will play

This is the big one for Sunday.

McVay said Garrett didn’t practice Friday and will be listed as questionable. But when a reporter asked directly, “Do you expect Myles to play?” McVay said: “I do.”

He explained the Friday rest: “He’s feeling good. It’s more of just getting him feeling as good as possible. He’s had a really good week. Played a bunch of snaps, and expect him to be able to build on that, but we’re being smart with him.”

That’s the same approach the Rams have used since Garrett came back. Limited practice, rest on Friday, play on Sunday. And it worked last week. Garrett had three sacks, three tackles for loss, and a strip-sack before halftime against Washington.

Garrett himself admitted he’s not all the way back. Per the Sporting Tribune, he said, “I felt like I was a little bit slow, and it picked up in the second half.” And: “You can only get in football shape by playing football and being on the field.”

So the Rams are building him up week by week. And on Sunday, he faces a rookie quarterback, Carson Beck, making his first NFL start. That’s a dream matchup for a pass rusher trying to get his timing back.

Part three: McDuffie and Curl

McVay said Trent McDuffie and Kam Curl would both be limited on Friday and listed as questionable.

But he sounded optimistic: “I feel good about Cam and Trent.” He said a lot of it would depend on “how does that limited work go today.”

He was especially positive about McDuffie: “He was rolling, doing a lot of good stuff yesterday. He’s chomping at the bit to play.” And: “I think if you asked him, he would say that he’s hopeful to be able to play. So are we.”

But McVay was also careful. He said McDuffie’s back “felt a little sore today,” but that he “was able to hit some really good numbers.” The challenge is testing it in game-like situations. McVay said the Rams would try to “mimic and emulate some of the different physical situations,” the kind where the back “ends up getting stressed more than just truly running,” like “redirecting on some unscripted movements.”

That makes sense for a cornerback. Running in a straight line is one thing. Reacting to a receiver’s cut, flipping your hips, and changing direction without warning is what really tests a back injury.

So McDuffie is a true game-time question. If he plays, the secondary is in much better shape. If not, Josh Wallace and Emmanuel Forbes keep carrying a bigger load, like they did against Washington.

Part four: who’s out

McVay was clear about the players who won’t play.

Steve Avila: out with a hamstring injury. That’s a starting guard, and a real loss for an offensive line that has been one of the best in the league at protecting Stafford.

Terrance Ferguson: out with a hamstring injury. McVay said earlier this week that Ferguson’s recovery is “probably a couple weeks at least,” and that the Rams hope to keep a roster spot open for him before his 21-day window expires around the Green Bay game.

Puka Nacua: on IR.

Davante Adams: on IR. McVay said Wednesday that “after Green Bay is a realistic timeline for him.”

If you’re keeping track at home, that’s two starting receivers, a starting guard, and a pass-catching tight end out for Sunday.

Part five: why Brennan Presley got the call

A reporter asked what McVay saw in Brennan Presley that made him promote him to the active roster. And his answer was a great description of the kind of player coaches love.

“It’s really just his consistency of how he shows up at practice,” McVay said. “As you guys have seen each of the last couple years, he’s just one of those guys that makes the plays when his number’s called.”

He kept going: “He’s a great teammate, really smart, where he can play a lot of different spots if need be. Can contribute on special teams, and he’s got some return ability as well.”

So that’s why Presley is on the 53-man roster. He’s versatile, reliable, and he can help on special teams. With Jordan Whittington on IR, the return game needed help. Whittington was averaging 26.3 yards per kickoff return, according to Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak.

And McVay said the Rams now have multiple return options: “We got X-Man back as well. So you could see a combination of either one of those guys back with kick returns, as well as Ronnie Rivers. And that’s a positive for us.”

So on Sunday, expect some mix of Presley, Xavier Smith, and Ronnie Rivers returning kicks. That’s a lot better than what the Rams had a week ago, when Kyren Williams was returning punts against Washington.

Part six: all four tight ends are ready

This might be the most important update of the press conference.

McVay said: “I think everybody’s going to make it to the game.” He gave a quick update on each tight end. Colby Parkinson is “making good steady progressions.” Tyler Higbee and Davis Allen “have been battling. Those guys have done a great job.” And Max Klare “will continue to get his work as well, and he’s really progressing.”

Then: “I expect all four of those guys to be ready to go.”

Why is that so important? Because with Puka, Adams, Whittington, and Ferguson all out, the tight ends are the most experienced pass catchers the Rams have. The Rams already use two- and three-tight-end sets more than almost anyone in the league. On Sunday, I expect even more.

Higbee is a veteran Stafford trusts. Davis Allen played through a back injury against Washington, and McVay called him “tough as hell” on Monday. Parkinson is a big target in the red zone. And Klare gives them a fourth option.

That’s a real strength for this offense right now.

Part seven: McVay on CJ Daniels

This was my favorite answer of the press conference, because it’s about a young player who’s about to get a real chance.

A reporter asked what McVay has seen from CJ Daniels over the last couple of days. McVay said: “Just maturity. The same things that I’ve seen from him, whether it was giving great looks to our first-string defense, whether it’s handling things in training camp. I’ve said to you guys from the jump, he just had a mature presence. Kind of a no-nonsense, but it’s not too big for him.”

Then he described what Daniels does on the field: “He’s so secure to be able to handle coaching or corrections, and he plays big at the catch point. He’s got a physicality, a lot of the things that you’re looking for for our receivers. He’s checking those boxes.”

And then: “He’s very curious. That’s what you really want from these guys, is the humility and the passion for the game. Stay curious.”

So in my last video, I mentioned that the original video reported Stafford had praised Daniels, and I said I couldn’t confirm Stafford’s exact words. Now we have McVay’s own words, and they match what we talked about: Daniels “plays big at the catch point,” and he has the physicality the Rams want. That’s exactly what Stafford needs with Puka out, a receiver who can win in tight coverage.

And here’s a fun detail. McVay said, “It’s been cool watching him kind of just be in Robert Woods’ back pocket.” Remember earlier this week when McVay joked about being “tempted” to bring Robert Woods back and said it would be “a violation of the CBA”? Now we know why Woods was on his mind. Woods has been around the team, and Daniels has been learning from him. McVay also gave credit to the Rams’ receiver coaches for their work with Daniels.

A young receiver learning from Robert Woods, one of the best route runners and blockers McVay ever coached. That’s a pretty good teacher.

Part eight: the shadow boxing league, and McVay roasting himself

Now the fun part.

A reporter asked McVay about the players’ shadow boxing league in the locker room. Apparently, it’s pretty organized.

McVay said he’s aware of it, and he likes it: “I think it’s whatever they like to do to be able to have camaraderie and enjoy, and a little competition. And they definitely do some funny things right there.”

Then he explained why it matters: “What I like is when I hear laughter. I hear guys enjoying. I think it’s always about when can you find those times to lock in, but when can you do those things that organically occur that create a camaraderie and a connection that is something that only the players can really be able to do. And if it’s forced, it’s not natural and organic.”

He added: “We got a bunch of great guys that I think enjoy being around each other. And when you’re hearing laughs, I think that’s a good sign of a good culture.”

But then a reporter asked if McVay would ever join in, and he roasted himself. He mentioned seeing the Seahawks do it on Hard Knocks last year, and said, “I don’t think I’d be very good at that. I’m too stupid to understand it.”

And when asked about the weirdest place he’s seen players do it, he said: “Things always get quiet when I come around.” Then: “Like the coaches are having fun, you walk up, it gets quiet. Players are walking up, nobody likes me. So, you know, it’s just 10 years running, and, oh man, I’m lonely.”

The room laughed, and I did too. That’s a coach who’s comfortable with his team, even in a week full of injuries. And honestly, a team that’s laughing and shadow boxing in the locker room after losing its two best receivers is a team that hasn’t given up on anything.

Part nine: Stafford’s steadiness

A reporter asked about the value of having a quarterback like Matthew Stafford in a week with so many moving parts at receiver.

McVay said: “His steadiness is the key thing. It’s always beneficial to have a quarterback that has the mastery, the ownership, that’s truly an extension of the coaching staff.”

He explained what that looks like in practice: “His ability to communicate and just have everybody stay nice and calm, understand what they’re doing before they break the huddle. It’s tremendous, and it’s a big deal to us.”

And he described how Stafford helps young players: “When you hear him talk about all the different things as he’s breaking the huddle, or little coaching points, not exclusive to just a play call, but little tips and reminders. You can give him information, and he’s able to echo and articulate it to the other 10.”

Then: “I think there’s just a confidence that he breathes because of how calm he is, and that rubs off on everybody else in the huddle.”

That matters a lot this week. When CJ Daniels, Brennan Presley, Xavier Smith, and Konata Mumpfield are in the huddle together, it helps to have a 38-year-old former MVP telling them exactly what to do. Stafford isn’t just throwing the ball. He’s coaching on the field.

Part ten: protecting Stafford against Arizona’s blitz

This was the most important football answer of the day.

A reporter pointed out that the last time the Rams played Arizona, the Cardinals blitzed a lot and hit Stafford hard. With Puka and Adams out, does protecting him matter even more?

McVay said: “It always does, regardless of who he’s playing. You always want to try to be able to keep your quarterback clean.” He said blitzing has “been part of their M.O.,” and “they did a good job on some of those.”

He talked about the answers: “Philosophically, what is your approach? Do you want to be able to throw quick? What are some of the different answers?” He said Arizona’s blitz-heavy approach was “not necessarily anything that surprised us, but they do a good job.” And: “I got a lot of respect for what they do defensively, how they mix it up.”

And then the key line: “Protecting Matthew will always be at a premium and at the forefront of our minds, regardless of whether you have two of those premier players or not.”

Here’s why that matters. The Rams lost that October game 18-16. Arizona’s pressure was a big reason. Now the Rams go back without Avila on the offensive line and without Puka, who’s usually Stafford’s quick-throw safety valve against the blitz.

So what are the answers? Based on what McVay said, my guess is quick throws, more running backs and tight ends staying in to block, and screens to slow down the rush. The tight ends being healthy helps a lot here. Higbee and Allen can block a blitzer or slip out for a quick catch. That’s my analysis, not McVay’s plan.

Part eleven: McDuffie, “the blueprint”

The last question of the press conference was about Trent McDuffie. A reporter said some of the Rams’ defensive backs call McDuffie “the blueprint.” What makes him that kind of example?

McVay said it’s about “the consistency, his competitive stamina, and his consistency of his vibe, his demeanor no matter what.” He said McDuffie wants to be out there “even when nobody wants to be out there as much as him.”

But McVay said it’s more than his play: “How is he influencing, affecting positive change? Not exclusive to getting out there and doing a great job as a star playing field or boundary corner. But how can you uplift and elevate? How can you encourage?”

And he pointed to the Washington game, where McDuffie didn’t play: “You look at some of the plays that guys made last week, on the sidelines.” He mentioned Emmanuel Forbes making “that great tackle for a loss on the second down.”

So even while McDuffie was sidelined with his back injury, he was coaching and encouraging the younger corners. Forbes, Washington’s former first-round pick, made a big play against his old team, and McDuffie was there for it.

That’s why the Rams want McDuffie on the field. But it’s also why they’ll be careful with him. A player who leads like that is too important to risk.

Reading between the lines

McVay never tells you his game plan directly, but this press conference gave us some strong hints. Here’s what I picked up. This is my analysis, not McVay’s words.

The tight ends will be the focus. McVay went out of his way to say all four tight ends are ready. When a coach gives a detailed update on every player at one position, that position usually matters a lot that week.

Quick throws against the blitz. McVay asked, “Do you want to be able to throw quick?” That’s a coach thinking out loud about how to beat Arizona’s pressure. Expect short, fast throws and screens.

CJ Daniels is getting a role. You don’t spend that long praising a young receiver’s maturity and physicality right before a game unless he’s about to play a lot.

Multiple returners. McVay named Presley, Smith, and Rivers as kick return options. That suggests the Rams might rotate based on matchups, or use different players for kickoffs and punts.

Garrett’s snaps will go up. “Played a bunch of snaps and expect him to be able to build on that.” That’s a coach telling you Garrett’s role is growing.

Put that together, and you get a game plan built around protecting Stafford, using the tight ends, getting a young receiver involved, and letting the pass rush take over against a rookie quarterback.

The culture in a hard week

I want to spend a minute on something McVay said that goes beyond Sunday.

Think about what this team has gone through. Davante Adams ruptured his hamstring in October. Poona Ford was lost for the season. Kobie Turner went to IR. Jaylen Watson is done for the year. Jordan Whittington went to IR. Now Puka Nacua is on IR. Steve Avila and Terrance Ferguson are out. And McDuffie and Curl are banged up.

And yet, the Rams have won three straight. And the players are running a shadow boxing league in the locker room.

McVay said, “When you’re hearing laughs, I think that’s a good sign of a good culture.” I think that’s exactly right. A team that’s falling apart doesn’t laugh together. A team that’s falling apart goes quiet. This team is still competing and still enjoying each other.

Earlier this week, McVay summed up the mindset with two words: “No flinch.” Then he quoted Bill Parcells: “There’s a way to win all of them.” And now, he’s joking that the locker room goes quiet when he walks in because “nobody likes me.” That’s a coach who’s relaxed, and a team that’s together.

That doesn’t win you games on its own. But it helps you survive weeks like this one.

Part twelve: what it all means for Sunday

Let’s put all of McVay’s updates together.

The good news. Garrett is expected to play. All four tight ends are ready. McDuffie and Curl have a real chance. The return game has three options in Presley, Smith, and Rivers. And Arizona is starting rookie Carson Beck in his first NFL game.

The concerns. No Puka, no Adams, no Ferguson, and no Avila. Arizona likes to blitz, and they hit Stafford hard last time. The Rams lost that game 18-16.

The plan, based on McVay’s answers. Protect Stafford with quick throws and extra blockers. Feature the tight ends. Give CJ Daniels a real role, because he “plays big at the catch point.” Run the ball with Kyren Williams and Blake Corum, who combined for 192, 129, and 124 rushing yards in the last three games. And let Garrett and Donald go after a rookie.

The stakes, one more time

Before the prediction, a quick reminder of why this game matters.

The Rams are 5-4 with three straight wins. A win in Arizona gets them to 6-4 heading into the bye, with Garrett getting stronger and Adams expected back “after Green Bay.” A loss drops them to 5-5, with Green Bay, Kansas City, and San Francisco coming up, all while Puka is out.

And there’s revenge on the line. Arizona beat the Rams 18-16 in October, in the same game where Adams got hurt.

So this isn’t just another game before the bye. It’s the game that decides how the Rams feel for the next two weeks.
