Let’s start with exactly what Kyren Williams said, because the full answer is a lot more complicated than the clip that went viral.

According to ESPN’s Sarah Barshop, as published by TSN, Kyren was asked why the Rams’ energy seemed to change from drive to drive. His first answer was: “I can’t tell you, bro.” Then he added: “Probably because I’m not out there.”

When he was asked whether he should be getting a bigger share of the snaps, he said: “I can’t really call that.” And then he said something that didn’t make the viral clip: “It’s not on me, but I just think no matter what, we got to continue to have the energy.”

And according to Rams Newswire’s Matt Borelli, Kyren also described the team’s problems this way: “Self-inflicted wounds, not starting fast, not having that energy every single drive.”

So read that whole answer together. Yes, he’s clearly frustrated with his role. Yes, “Probably because I’m not out there” sounds like a shot at the coaching staff. But he also said it’s “not on me” to decide playing time, and he pointed to the same problems McVay has been talking about for weeks — self-inflicted wounds and slow starts. That’s not a player tearing the team apart. That’s a frustrated captain saying something emotional after one of the worst losses of the McVay era.

Now, why is Kyren frustrated? Let’s look at the numbers, because they tell an interesting story.

According to Barshop’s report, Kyren played 40 offensive snaps against Arizona, compared to 16 for Blake Corum. So he was on the field a lot. But according to Pro Football Network, he had just 10 carries — his fewest of the season — for 50 yards, plus three catches for 17 yards. Corum had nine carries for 42 yards and caught a touchdown pass. And as a team, the Rams ran the ball only 20 times.

Over the full season, according to Barshop, Kyren has played 287 of the Rams’ 410 offensive snaps — about 70 percent. Corum has played 114, about 28 percent. Among running backs, only Jonathan Taylor and Jaylen Warren have played more snaps than Kyren this season. According to Rams Newswire, Kyren has 80 carries for 393 yards and four touchdowns, plus 30 catches for 217 yards and another touchdown.

So here’s what’s interesting. Kyren isn’t being benched. He’s on the field for most of the plays. What’s changed is how often he gets the ball. According to Pro Football Network, McVay said back in late September that the running backs would split carries for the rest of the season. And this is a player who, according to Rams Newswire, is coming off three straight 1,000-yard rushing seasons. When you’re used to being the engine of the offense, and suddenly you’re getting 10 carries in a close game, you’re going to feel it.

And remember where Kyren came from. He was a fifth-round pick out of Notre Dame in 2022. Nobody handed him anything. He earned the starting job in 2023 and ran for more than 1,100 yards that season, then followed it up with nearly 1,300 yards in 2024. He’s the kind of player who plays with an edge because he’s always had to. That edge is part of what makes him great — and it’s also why a 10-carry game in a close loss would hit him hard.

And Blake Corum isn’t a random backup, either. The Rams took him in the third round in 2024 out of Michigan, where he was a national champion. He’s earned his role, and he’s been productive in it — that 15-yard touchdown on a screen pass against Arizona was a great play. So this isn’t a story about a coach benching his star for no reason. It’s a story about two good running backs, one football, and a coach trying to balance them during a losing streak. That’s a harder problem than the headlines make it sound.

I think the real point Kyren was trying to make is this: when he gets the ball early and often, the Rams play with energy. When the run game disappears, the offense goes flat. And honestly, against Arizona, that’s exactly what happened.

Now, let’s talk about the reaction, because this is where things got out of control.

According to Pro Football Network, one fan posted, “Wow McVay actually lost the locker room.” Chat Sports’ Jackson Durham called the quote “a very bad sign.” Sleeper’s NFL account said Kyren “might have a point.” And Pro Football Network’s own account posted a one-word reaction: “Yikes.”

But not everyone saw it that way. Alexis Kraft of Downtown Rams argued that Kyren was answering the question literally, and that his full interview was mostly positive.

And FOX Sports’ Henry McKenna made an important point: Rams players rarely criticize the coaching staff publicly over playing time. That’s what made this moment stand out. Under McVay, the Rams have usually kept these things inside the building.

So how did McVay respond? On Monday, according to Rams Wire’s Cameron DaSilva, McVay said this:

“He brought it up to me. I wasn’t aware of it.”

“He was emotional after the game. I know this guy’s heart.”

“He wants to make sure that he’s influencing and affecting positive change.”

“I think what he said it was more of, ‘I just always want to try to be that spark.’”

“He was frustrated after the game, but his teammates know what he’s about.”

And: “When he explained it to me, I totally understood where he was coming from. I think the way that it was interpreted was not what his intentions were.”

So according to McVay, Kyren went to him on his own — before McVay had even heard about the comments — to make sure there was no misunderstanding. That’s important. In a locker room that’s truly broken, players don’t walk into the head coach’s office to clear the air. They go to the media, or to social media, or they stay silent and let it fester. Kyren did the opposite.

And remember, according to Rams Wire, Kyren was named a team captain this offseason. A captain who’s frustrated after a loss and then takes responsibility for how it came across is not the same as a player who’s lost faith in his coach.

So is the locker room breaking? Here’s my honest answer: there’s frustration — of course there is. This team started the season as the Super Bowl favorite and is 2-4. But I don’t see the signs of a locker room that’s falling apart.

Look at what the leaders have said this week. Stafford said: “We are what our record is.” That’s honest, and it’s accountable. Safety Quentin Lake said there’s “no sense of panic,” but there is a “sense of urgency.” Puka Nacua said: “We’re still a connected group.” And McVay keeps putting the blame on himself. After the Arizona game, according to TSN, he said: “It starts with me, and I got to be able to have some better answers.”

When a locker room is truly gone, you usually see players blaming each other, leaks about who’s arguing with whom, and coaches pointing fingers. We’re not seeing that. What we’re seeing is a frustrated team that keeps losing close games — and keeps saying the same thing: we’re beating ourselves.

But here’s the part we can’t ignore. Frustration doesn’t stay quiet forever. If the Rams lose in Las Vegas and drop to 2-5, comments like Kyren’s could become more common. Winning is the best cure for every locker room problem. And losing is how small cracks become big ones. That’s why Sunday matters so much.

And here’s the funny thing about the word “fight” in all these headlines. The only fight anybody on this team has actually talked about is the one McVay described before the Arizona game. According to The Sporting Tribune, via Yahoo Sports, McVay said: “There’s no moral victories.” And he added: “And you got to be able to get up, stand up and ready to fight again.” That’s the fight this team needs — not with each other, but with the slow starts, the penalties and the mistakes that keep costing them games. And before that game, Stafford said: “We’re trying like hell every single time.” Nobody in that building is questioning anybody’s effort. The question is execution. And that’s something a team can fix together — if it stays together.

Now let’s get to the question that actually worries me more than Kyren’s comment: identity.

According to Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak, after the Arizona loss, McVay was asked to describe this team’s identity. His answer was: “We’re figuring it out. I can’t answer that right now.”

For a coach with McVay’s track record, that’s a stunning admission. For years, you always knew what the Rams were. In 2021, it was Stafford and Cooper Kupp lighting up defenses. In 2024 and 2025, it was a balanced offense with Kyren running hard and Puka making catches all over the field. This year, it’s harder to say.

And look at the evidence from the Arizona game. According to FOX Sports, Stafford averaged a season-low 4.8 air yards per attempt against a Cardinals defense that had been giving up 236.8 passing yards per game. According to Rams On SI, Arizona’s defense ranked 29th in EPA per play coming into the game. The Rams had fewer than 100 total yards at halftime. And yet, they only ran the ball 20 times.

So this wasn’t a pass-first team attacking downfield, and it wasn’t a run-first team grinding it out. It was somewhere in between — and that “in between” is exactly what McVay meant when he said he can’t answer the identity question right now.

To be fair to McVay, some of the Arizona decisions were forced on him. Once Grupe got hurt, the Rams had no placekicker. They ran out the clock before halftime because, as McVay put it, “We didn’t have a field goal operation.” They went for two after Corum’s touchdown, and the try failed when Stafford was sacked and fumbled. And they went for it on fourth-and-1 at the Arizona 27 instead of kicking. A lot of what looked like confusion was really a coach trying to win without a kicker. With McManus signed, those situations should look completely different on Sunday. But the slow start, the low air yards and the 20 runs had nothing to do with the kicker. Those were choices.

Kyren’s quote fits right into this. “Not starting fast.” That’s the pattern. And it shows up in a stat we’ve talked about on this channel: three straight weeks, Stafford has led a touchdown drive in the final two minutes. With 1:10 left in Philadelphia. With five seconds left against Buffalo. With 47 seconds left against Arizona. Great for highlights. But think about what that means. This offense keeps waking up at the end of games instead of the beginning.

According to Rams On SI, Stafford’s adjusted EPA per play ranks 17th in the league through the first three quarters — but seventh in the fourth quarter. That’s the same quarterback, the same receivers, the same offensive line. The difference is urgency. When the Rams are forced to play fast and aggressive, they look like a contender. When they’re not, they look lost.

Now let’s talk about the defense, because the Arizona game was a strange one on that side of the ball.

On one hand, the defense was on the field forever. According to the box score, Arizona held the ball for 34 minutes and 52 seconds, compared to 25 minutes and 8 seconds for the Rams. That’s a gap of almost 10 minutes. Backup running back Bam Knight ran 15 times for 80 yards. Jacoby Brissett completed 24 of 35 passes for 218 yards, even after Quentin Lake intercepted him early.

But on the other hand, the defense did its job when it mattered most. According to Rams On SI, Chris Shula’s unit held Arizona to zero touchdowns in five trips to the red zone. Zero for five. And the defense made stops in the fourth quarter that gave the offense a chance to win.

So when people say the defense collapsed against Arizona, I’d push back on that. The defense bent a lot. But it didn’t break until the final drive. The bigger problem was an offense that couldn’t stay on the field and kept handing Arizona the ball back.

And that brings us to the mistakes. McVay said it after the game, according to TSN: “A lot of different guys are taking their turn.” He’s right. Let’s list them.

According to Rams On SI, Warren McClendon’s false start turned a fourth-and-3 into a fourth-and-8, and that drive ended without points. Stafford took an 11-yard loss in the red zone, and on the very next play, Tyler Higbee fumbled. On a fourth-and-1, the Rams handed the ball to tight end Davis Allen on a fullback dive — a player who, according to Grisak, had never taken a handoff in his NFL career. That’s an interesting call when you have Kyren Williams, a three-time 1,000-yard rusher, on your roster. And on Arizona’s game-winning drive, according to Rams Newswire, an unnecessary roughness penalty in the final minute moved the Cardinals 15 yards closer to field goal range.

Look at that list again. A false start. A sack. A fumble. A questionable play call. A late penalty. That’s five different kinds of mistakes from five different parts of the team. That’s what McVay meant when he said, “We won’t win a game unless we figure out how not to beat ourselves.”

And honestly, this is where Kyren’s frustration makes sense. On fourth-and-1, the Rams didn’t give the ball to their captain and best short-yardage runner. They gave it to a tight end. If I’m Kyren, I’m frustrated too.

Let me give one more moment from that game its due, because the transcript going around got this part right. On the kickoff after his 44-yard field goal, kicker Blake Grupe — playing in his very first game as a Ram — ran down the field and made a tackle on the return. He tore his ACL on that play, according to the AP, and his season is over. That was his first and last game as a Ram. You can argue about whether a kicker should be making that tackle. But nobody can question his effort. And the Rams have already moved on at kicker: Brandon McManus signed on Tuesday and will make his Rams debut this Sunday.

So if you’ve seen videos this week listing Graham Gano, Justin Tucker, Rodrigo Blankenship and others as kicker options, those are out of date. The Rams worked out McManus and Matt Prater, and they chose McManus.

Now, let’s put this season in historical context, because the numbers are scary — but not hopeless.

According to ESPN Research, as cited by TSN, the Arizona loss was the biggest upset of the season by point spread. The Rams were 13.5-point favorites. ESPN Research also found that the Rams are just the fifth team in the past 50 seasons to enter the year as the outright Super Bowl favorite and lose at least four of their first six games. None of the previous four reached their conference championship game. And according to NFL Next Gen Stats, the Rams’ playoff chances are down to 43 percent.

That’s the bad news. Here’s the good news: McVay’s Rams have climbed out of holes before. In 2021, they lost three straight games in November — and then won the Super Bowl. In 2023, they started 3-6 and still made the playoffs. Rams On SI’s Grisak pointed to those same turnarounds, though he admitted it’s hard to feel confident right now. I understand that. But a 43 percent chance is not zero. And three of the Rams’ four losses came by a combined nine points.

So what would I do to fix this? Here’s my opinion — not a report, just what I’d want to see.

First, give the offense an identity on the opening drive. Script the first series around Kyren and the run game. Get him five or six touches in the first quarter. Let the offense set the tone instead of chasing the game.

Second, keep using Corum, but in specific roles. The split doesn’t have to disappear. But Kyren should be the one on the field in short-yardage situations and in the red zone. No more tight-end dives on fourth-and-1.

Third, let Stafford attack downfield earlier. A 4.8-yard average depth of target against a 29th-ranked defense isn’t good enough. Without Davante Adams, that’s harder — but Puka, Parkinson and the young receivers need chances to make plays before the fourth quarter.

And fourth, clean up the operation. False starts, late penalties, fumbles in plus territory — those are fixable. McVay said himself, according to TSN: “This year we are doing it, and so we have to be able to fix it, and it’s my job to do that.”

Now let’s look ahead to Sunday in Las Vegas, because a win would quiet a lot of this noise.

According to an AP preview, the Raiders are 3-3 and have lost three straight after a 3-0 start. Those losses came against Kansas City and Buffalo, plus a lopsided loss at New England. First-year head coach Klint Kubiak didn’t hold back: “That’s unacceptable, and that starts with me.” And right guard Caleb Rogers said: “We’re just a really good team who hasn’t closed in close games, and we’ve got to close.” Sound familiar? These two teams have the same problem.

Here’s the matchup to watch. According to the AP, Las Vegas has scored touchdowns on just 12 of its 23 trips inside the red zone, and only 4 of 10 during its losing streak. Kirk Cousins has a 98.0 passer rating and 14 touchdown passes, tied for the league lead. So the Raiders can move the ball, but they’ve been stalling near the goal line. If the Rams’ defense can repeat what it did against Arizona in the red zone, the Rams have a real chance.

And the Raiders’ defense has been struggling. According to the AP, Las Vegas ranks 26th in scoring defense at 27 points per game, and it has allowed more than 415 yards per game over its last four games. According to Fox Sports, the Raiders have allowed more than 237 passing yards per game this season. That’s a defense the Rams should be able to attack early — if they actually come out with a plan to start fast. This is the perfect week to prove that the slow starts were a problem they can fix, not a permanent part of who they are.

The Rams will be short-handed, though. According to the AP, linebacker Nate Landman entered the concussion protocol and missed Wednesday’s practice. Landman leads the Rams with 51 tackles and has forced two fumbles. He’s also the defense’s primary signal-caller. Trent McDuffie also missed Wednesday’s practice with a groin injury, and his status will be decided later in the week. Myles Garrett, Kobie Turner and Poona Ford are out. And according to the AP, Davante Adams had surgery on Tuesday.

On offense, McVay said this about Konata Mumpfield, according to the AP: “What I do appreciate is he’s made the most out of his opportunities.” Expect more snaps for Mumpfield with Adams out.

Here are my three keys for Sunday.

Key number one: start fast. Score on the opening drive. Kyren said it himself — “not starting fast” has been a problem. If the Rams can take an early lead, they can control the game instead of needing another two-minute miracle.

Key number two: feed Kyren. Twenty carries for the team isn’t enough. I want to see Kyren with at least 15 carries and a few catches. Give your captain a chance to be the spark he says he wants to be. And the matchup supports it. Arizona’s backup running back averaged more than five yards a carry against the Rams. The Raiders’ defense has given up big yardage for weeks. A steady run game would shorten the game, protect a short-handed defense and keep Stafford out of third-and-long. Sometimes the simplest answer to an identity crisis is to hand the ball to your best runner and let him set the tone.

Key number three: no self-inflicted wounds. No false starts on fourth down. No fumbles in the red zone. No 15-yard penalties in the final minute. Let McManus kick his field goals and extra points, and let the Rams win a game without beating themselves.

My prediction stays the same: Rams 27, Raiders 20. And I’ll add one more detail for this video — Kyren Williams scores a touchdown and goes over 100 total yards. If that happens, I think the locker room “fallout” story disappears pretty quickly.

So here’s my bottom line.

There was no fight in the Rams’ locker room. What happened is that a frustrated team captain said something emotional after a brutal loss, fans turned it into “McVay lost the locker room,” and then Kyren went to McVay on his own to clear the air. McVay says he understood where Kyren was coming from. That’s not a breaking point. That’s a team going through a hard stretch and trying to handle it the right way.

But don’t let that hide the real issue. McVay couldn’t describe this team’s identity after the Arizona loss. The offense keeps starting slow and waking up late. And the mistakes keep coming from everywhere. Those are the problems that could actually sink this season. And they’re the problems the Rams have to fix starting Sunday in Las Vegas.
