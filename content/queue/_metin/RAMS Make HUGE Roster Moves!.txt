Let's start with the injury report, because McVay opened with it and it sets the stakes for everything else. Colby Parkinson did not practice and is officially questionable — that AC joint sprain in his shoulder is lingering, and with the tight end room already thin, his pregame status is one to watch. Terrance Ferguson is out, as expected, with that medial ankle sprain — the breakout tight end watches this one from the sideline. Jaylen Watson is out with the dislocated shoulder, continuing the harness-and-strengthen plan we broke down this week. Josaiah Stewart popped up on the report with a groin issue and carries a questionable tag — keep an eye on that one, because this pass rush cannot afford another subtraction. And then McVay said the name nobody expected: Aaron Donald. Out.

Let's sit with the Donald news properly, because the way McVay explained it matters more than the headline. The timeline: Donald came out of the Denver game with soreness — not an injury on a specific play, but a body that didn't bounce back the way they hoped as the week went on. McVay was asked for details and kept it simple: he's working through it, he's as tough as it gets, and he's just not feeling good enough. And then came the sentence that defines how this organization is handling its legend: "Part of our job is to protect these tough dudes like him from themselves." McVay admitted it openly — if they asked Donald to play, he would say yes, and he'd probably even be productive. That's exactly why they're not asking. After consulting with the performance staff, the decision was made FOR him, because the man himself would never make it.

And then a reporter asked the awkward question — the business question. Donald's contract reportedly includes per-game bonuses. Does that factor into whether he's active? McVay's answer was one of the best moments of the entire press conference. No — and here's why. This guy wants to EARN everything he gets. He's such a man's man. There are times you talk about the business side, McVay said, but when you're talking about Aaron Donald, this isn't one of those conversations. "We have to protect him from him." He represents everything that's right about how this team wants to operate — and McVay called him an exception that everyone would agree on. Think about what that exchange tells you: the team is voluntarily sitting a player whose contract pays him to be active, purely on medical caution, and the player is reportedly fighting to play anyway. Whatever cynicism exists in this league, this situation is the opposite of it.

Now my honest analysis of what losing Donald means Sunday, because this channel doesn't sugarcoat. It hurts. Specifically, it hurts in the exact place the Eagles like to fight: the interior line of scrimmage. Donald's value — even at thirty-plus snaps a game, even shaking off rust — is gravitational. Guards slide to him, centers point at him, and everyone else gets cleaner looks. Take him off the field, and Kobie Turner and Braden Fiske inherit both the snaps and the double teams, while the young edge committee — Byron Young, Josaiah Stewart if that groin cooperates, Desjuan Johnson, and Wesley Bailey — has to win more one-on-ones without the inside help. Against a Philadelphia front that mauls people, in a building where silent counts and crowd noise already tax your communication, that's a real handicap. I won't pretend otherwise. But here's the frame that keeps me sane: this is week four, Donald is thirty-five years old and three weeks removed from a thousand-day layoff, and the entire point of bringing him back was January, not October. A soreness week now — handled with this much caution — is the system working. The Rams are playing chess with his body. Short-term pain, long-term legend.

And notice the bigger pattern, because the injury carousel this season has been almost comical: the week Puka finally returns is the week Donald sits. The week Kinchens comes back is the week Watson's shoulder keeps him out. This roster has not been whole for a single game all season — which, as I'll argue in a few minutes, is exactly why nobody should be drawing permanent conclusions about a 1-2 record.

Before the good news, the hard news — because overnight, the Rams made another move in the special teams war we've been documenting all week. Xavier Smith has been waived. The punt returner is gone, and the timeline here is remarkable: reporters talked to the special teams staff just yesterday and were told there was no thought of making a change. Then the staff sat with the film last night, the decision got made late, and McVay met with Smith this morning. That's how fast conviction forms in this building right now. And I want to spend a moment on HOW McVay delivered this news, because it was genuinely one of the most human answers I've heard from an NFL podium. He started with appreciation — Xavier Smith's journey, the odds he overcame, the person he is. He called this one of the worst parts of the job: using your values and principles to make decisions for the collective, even when they hurt people you've developed real relationships with. And then the honesty: "We just need better production out of that position." There were decisions last week — fielding decisions, judgment calls — that simply have to be better. "It doesn't mean I love him any less. But we need better production." No hiding behind injury designations, no coach-speak fog. A man told another man the truth, then told the public the same truth. That's rare.

So who returns punts in Philadelphia? McVay named his guy: Alex Bachman — the practice squad receiver who's been in this organization grinding, has played meaningful snaps, and has done, in McVay's words, a really good job. Kyren Williams remains the break-glass-in-emergency option — McVay has tremendous trust in him back there but flatly said he doesn't want his starting running back fielding punts in an ideal world. And when asked about bringing in outside help long-term, McVay left the door open — they'll always explore improving the roster — but said he's optimistic we see good things from Bachman on Sunday. Now connect the week's full arc, because it's remarkable: Connors signed to the 53 for kick returns. Pulley signed from the Eagles' practice squad for coverage. Ingle recycled to the practice squad as insurance. And now Smith out, Bachman in on punt returns. That is a complete, top-to-bottom renovation of the special teams operation in six days — one week after hidden yards helped lose a game, one year after special teams helped lose in this exact Philadelphia building. The organization identified its leak and attacked it with four separate transactions. Whatever happens Sunday, nobody can say they ignored the problem.

And now — finally — the sentence Rams fans have waited a month to hear. Puka Nacua is good to go. Officially. On the final injury report, no tag, no "questionable," no warmup drama pending: good to go, and Kam Kinchens right there with him. And McVay's description of Puka this week should get you genuinely excited, because this doesn't sound like a player being squeezed back at eighty percent. Physically, he's getting in and out of his breaks. He looks strong. Sturdy. Explosive. He was sharp in Thursday's practice — which McVay noted is the heaviest workload day of the week — and he handled all of it. The only honest caveat McVay offered: overall conditioning. You can't fully simulate game shape after a month off, so the snap count is something they'll navigate — and he joked about hoping for "the magic of the jersey on Sundays." Translation for the Rams House: Puka plays, they'll manage his volume intelligently, and the staff that protected him through four careful weeks is the same staff that gets the benefit of the doubt on the plan.

Think about what his return actually changes against this Eagles defense, because it's everything we've written about for a month finally becoming real. For three games, defenses played the Rams with a cheat code: shade coverage toward Davante Adams, dare everyone else to win, and tee off on obvious passing downs. Adams beat it anyway — 195 against the Giants, 137 against Denver — but the Denver tape showed the cost: fifty-five dropbacks, volume without efficiency, no rhythm throws, no easy outs. Puka Nacua is the easy out. He's the third-down separator, the middle-of-the-field problem, the yards-after-catch machine that turns second-and-nine into second-and-three. Put him and Adams on the same field — for the first time since the Melbourne opener — and Philadelphia's coordinator has to answer a question no one has had to answer yet this season: who do you double? Whichever way he answers, Matthew Stafford — the reigning MVP, coming off 390 yards in a loss — gets the matchup he wants. And with Kinchens restoring the back end on defense at the same time, this is the closest to whole the 2026 Rams have ever been on a game day... even with the legend resting.

Quick update on the other legend, because a reporter asked and the answer was encouraging: Myles Garrett's rehab is going well. McVay said he physically looks good, has "a good look in his eye," and has been in and out of meetings while attacking his rehab. No timeline yet — McVay was careful there — but remember the math we've tracked for weeks: the Bills game after this one completes the four-game minimum on injured reserve. The record-breaker's window is opening right as the calendar flips. Help is not hypothetical anymore. It's scheduled.
