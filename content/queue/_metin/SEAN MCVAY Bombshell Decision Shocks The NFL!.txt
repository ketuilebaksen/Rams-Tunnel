Part two: the big decision, Myles Garrett is back

Let’s talk about the real news.

Myles Garrett is expected to play Sunday for the first time since Week 1. Ian Rapoport and Mike Garafolo of NFL Network reported Friday that he’ll be activated from injured reserve before the game.

Quick refresher on how we got here. The Rams traded for Garrett on June 1st, sending Jared Verse, a 2027 first-round pick, a 2028 second, and a 2029 third to Cleveland. He missed most of training camp with knee irritation. He played the opener against the 49ers in Australia, but afterward he said he was “not 100 percent healthy.” Then he had arthroscopic knee surgery in mid-September and went on IR. He’s missed seven games.

Garrett talked to reporters this week, and his quotes were great. On sitting out: “It was very difficult.” On why he wanted to get back: “This is what I love to do. I love being around my teammates, being able to just help the whole group shine.” On how the injury happened, he said there “wasn’t a specific moment or time.” It “cascaded” after the Australia game: “There were things I was dealing with mechanically that just only continued to worsen.”

And on coming back: “I’m happy to be back and make the most of my time off and the rest of the time I got.”

Now, here’s the part that should get every Rams fan excited. Garrett was asked about playing alongside Aaron Donald, and he said: “I think offenses really have to pick their poison. Who’s going to be freed up this play?”

That’s the whole point of this defensive front. You can’t double-team Donald inside and Garrett outside on the same snap without leaving someone else open, like Byron Young, Josaiah Stewart, or Braden Fiske.

What to expect on Sunday. McVay wouldn’t commit to a snap count. When he was asked, he basically said they’re still working through it. Earlier in the week, he said, “Let’s just see how today goes, and then let’s see how tomorrow goes.” Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak projects Garrett at around half of the defensive snaps, maybe a little more. For reference, he played 61.5 percent of the snaps in the opener while not fully healthy. Grisak also expects the Rams to build up his workload gradually, with the goal of having him full-time after the bye.

So don’t expect 60 snaps. Expect a rotation, with Garrett on the field in the obvious passing downs and Stewart and Wesley Bailey still getting real work.

Why it matters. According to ESPN, the Rams are tied for 26th in the NFL with just 12 sacks this season. And the guy they’re adding was the 2025 Defensive Player of the Year, when he set the NFL single-season sack record with 23 for the Browns. Even at half speed, that’s a massive upgrade.

Part three: McVay on the Garrett surgery question

There was one exchange in this press conference I really want to highlight, because it was the most honest moment of the day.

A reporter asked McVay, in a respectful way, whether there was any second-guessing about how the Garrett situation was handled. Basically: should the knee have been taken care of back in the summer instead of trying to play through it in the opener?

McVay’s answer was thoughtful. He said: “I think you always want to reflect back on, all right, what would we do differently if things like this come up, but I’m not living in the past in that regard. I’m just grateful that he’s back and he’s feeling really good.”

Then he explained the thinking at the time: “I think our intentions were all in the right place.” The decision was to “give it a shot in San Fran,” because that was “the only way that you would really get an opportunity to see how he would feel.” And when it didn’t work, “let’s do the next right thing. He gets the surgery. He’s rehabbed great.”

Here’s my take, and this is opinion, not reporting. That’s a fair answer. In hindsight, if Garrett had surgery in the summer, maybe he’s healthy by Week 3 or 4 instead of Week 9. But the Rams had just paid a first-round pick and Jared Verse for him, and they wanted to see if he could play through it. They tried, it didn’t work, and they fixed it. The cost was seven games. With Garrett back and the Rams at 4-4, there’s still plenty of season left for that bet to pay off.

And I liked how McVay finished: he’s excited for Garrett “to just go play within himself. Doesn’t have to do anything other than be Myles.” In other words, he doesn’t need to be a hero on Sunday. He just needs to be himself.

Part four: the plan for Jayden Daniels

The next question was about the defensive line, and McVay’s answer was basically a mini coaching clinic on how to rush a mobile quarterback.

Here’s what he said. When you face “a player like Jayden Daniels, you got to do a great job, depending upon how many we’re rushing. If you’re rushing four, you’re short two escape lanes. You got to make sure that you’re great overlap, rushing four as one.”

Let me break that down. When you rush four, every pass rusher has a lane he’s responsible for. If one guy gets too far upfield or too far inside, he opens a gap, and a quarterback like Daniels runs right through it. “Rushing four as one” means all four rushers stay coordinated so the pocket collapses evenly, without a hole.

But McVay also said you can’t just sit back: “You can’t just sit there and watch. He’ll pick you apart because he does such a great job.” And when the Rams blitz, he said, they have to be careful about “not giving any sort of vertical seams for him to be able to escape.”

He also said something that I think is the real key to this game: “Hopefully we earn the right to be able to get into some of those known passing situations.” In plain English, the defense has to stop the run and win on first and second down. Then on third-and-long, Garrett and Donald get to pin their ears back.

Later in the presser, McVay put it another way: “If teams are able to be efficient in 50-50 situations on first and second down, it makes it a little bit easier to be able to kind of play the game on your terms. But that’s our job.”

And when a reporter asked how hard it will be for opposing play callers to scheme against Donald and Garrett, McVay said, “It presents real challenges,” because there aren’t many easy answers for an offense “if these guys are playing the way that I know that they’re capable of.”

Washington’s quarterback is healthy, by the way. Daniels was listed with a left elbow issue but practiced fully all week and has no injury designation.

Part five: Stafford’s elbow, explained

Stafford was added to the injury report this week with a right elbow issue, even though he was a full participant. A reporter asked McVay about it, and McVay basically said it was a paperwork thing.

“Really, it’s just because you guys asked me about it earlier in the week,” he said. “It’s more semantical than anything else. It’s normal, like what he told you guys.” He added that Stafford “hasn’t missed any sort of reps,” and that it’s “normal management stuff that he’s been doing over the last few years.”

When he was asked whether it affects how Stafford throws, McVay said: “I haven’t seen a bit of that.” Then: “He’s been a full participant, been awesome. He’s spinning the ball. He’s been able to activate all parts of the field with great timing, rhythm, and accuracy.”

That last line is interesting, because “activate all parts of the field” is exactly what the Rams haven’t been doing lately. Grisak’s numbers this week showed that the Rams’ explosive pass play rate through eight weeks is 4.1 percent, compared with 17.5 percent at this point last year. Stafford’s average depth of target over the last four weeks ranks 25th of 27 quarterbacks.

So here’s what I’ll be watching. If McVay is right and the elbow isn’t an issue, we should see Stafford take some deep shots Sunday against a Washington team that CBS listed as needing cornerback help. If we see another game of short throws and screens, people are going to keep asking about the elbow, whatever the injury report says.

And remember what Stafford said himself this week: “I haven’t missed a rep at practice. I haven’t missed a rep in a game, and I feel good.” And: “Listen, I’m 38 years old, trying to keep up with these young bucks.”

Part six: the two-kicker decision

This might be the closest thing to an actual “decision” in this press conference, so let’s talk about it.

On Thursday, the Rams signed kicker Joshua Karty from the practice squad to the 53-man roster. So now the Rams have two kickers on the active roster: veteran Brandon McManus, who handles field goals and extra points, and Karty, who handles kickoffs.

Karty was a Rams draft pick in 2024. Last week against the Chargers, he was elevated from the practice squad to kick off. He had five kickoffs, and two of them didn’t reach the landing zone, which gave the Chargers the ball at their own 40-yard line twice.

McVay talked about it honestly. He said the balls Karty did land in the landing zone were “beneficial for our coverage unit” and “made a difference in the yards.” But he added: “We’ve got to consistently land it in that landing zone. We don’t want to put the ball on the 40-yard line twice.”

He also explained the roster side. You only get a limited number of practice squad elevations, so signing Karty to the 53 means they don’t have to keep burning those. As McVay put it, having two kickers up on game day is “a luxury.”

And he gave McManus some credit too: “I think McManus has done a good job.”

Is this a bombshell? No. Is it unusual? A little. Most teams don’t carry a kickoff specialist. But under the new kickoff rules, where the landing zone decides field position, the Rams clearly think a dedicated kickoff guy is worth a roster spot. That’s a real decision, just not a shocking one.

Part seven: what McVay said about Aaron Donald

This was my favorite part of the whole press conference.

A reporter mentioned that Nate Scheelhaase, who joined the Rams’ staff after Donald’s first retirement, said what stood out to him was how much Donald affected the team even when he was gone. The reporter asked McVay whether he remembered talking to the team about Donald during training camp.

McVay said he’s talked about Aaron in team meetings so often that he doesn’t remember which one. But he explained what he usually uses Donald as an example for: “controlled aggression.” He said Donald does a great job “being totally and completely present, but making sure that you don’t let any of the extracurriculars outside the whistle affect your ability to hurt the team.”

He was also honest that Donald “had some growth early on in his career,” and said what resonated with players was “his accountability, his ownership, but most importantly his ability to model the way.” McVay joked that he doesn’t use Donald’s stories as much “when he’s sitting in the room.”

Then a reporter brought up something Donald himself said this week, that the season hasn’t been what he expected. Here’s Donald’s actual quote, from his media session: “No, it’s not what I expected, but we here.” And: “Ain’t what I expected, but I’m enjoying it.” On Garrett coming back, he said: “It’s good to have the pieces back to the puzzle.”

McVay’s response was one of the best things he said all week. He said: “That’s the risk that you take in sports. That’s why people love it, because there’s always the unpredictability of the outcome.” And then: “What I have loved is even though there have been some things that haven’t gone ideal, what a stud he’s been, and the growth mindset that he’s possessed.”

He described how Donald shows up to practice, works in the weight room, and how “intentional” and “locked in” he is in meetings. And then he said this: “Once he commits to something, he’s all in. It’s not like looking back like, oh, what would I do differently. That’s not how he’s wired. That’s not how he was raised. That’s not what he’s about.”

He also pointed out what Donald is doing physically: “getting used to how does your body adjust and acclimate with two years off, taking on the workload that you do as an inside defensive lineman. And he’s played a lot of snaps, and I think he’s only getting better.” And he finished with Donald’s own motto: “Hard work pays off, as he would say.”

Think about that for a second. A future Hall of Famer came out of retirement after two years, the season started 2-4, his star teammate got hurt in Week 1, and he’s still showing up every day and getting better. And now, in Week 9, he finally gets the teammate he came back to play with.

Part eight: the young guys McVay praised

At the end of the presser, McVay went through the players who’ve stepped up on the defensive front, and I want to give them their credit.

On the edge, he said, “I thought Josaiah Stewart played great last week. Byron Young has been outstanding. It was awesome to see Wes Bailey make an impact.” That’s Josaiah Stewart, the second-year outside linebacker, and Wesley Bailey, who forced the strip-sack against the Chargers that bounced right to Donald.

On the inside, McVay admitted, “losing Poona and Kobie Turner has been a big deal.” Poona Ford is out for the season with a ruptured triceps, and Kobie Turner is on IR with a groin injury, though McVay said they look forward to “getting Kobie back at some point.” But he praised Braden Fiske, said he’s been “really pleased with Ty Hamilton,” and noted that Larrell Murchison is “playing meaningful snaps for us.”

That’s the hidden story of this defense. Without Garrett, Ford, and Turner, this group still held the Chargers without a touchdown, pressured Justin Herbert on 17 of 37 dropbacks, and picked him off twice. Now you add Garrett back to a group that’s already playing well.

Part nine: the secondary, the real worry

I keep coming back to this, because I think it’s the biggest question for Sunday.

McVay said he’s “bummed” for Jaylen Watson, who’s out for the season. Josh Wallace takes his spot in the starting lineup, and Emmanuel Forbes, Washington’s former first-round pick, becomes the number three cornerback against his old team.

And Trent McDuffie didn’t practice all week with a back injury. He’s officially questionable, but a player who misses Wednesday, Thursday, and Friday is usually closer to doubtful than probable. If McDuffie can’t go, the Rams could be starting Josh Wallace and Emmanuel Forbes at cornerback, with Kam Curl, also questionable, behind them at safety.

So here’s how I see it. The pass rush has to win this game. If Garrett, Donald, Young, and company can make Daniels get rid of the ball fast, the secondary won’t be exposed as much. If Daniels has time, this could get uncomfortable.

That’s also why I keep saying cornerback has moved up the Rams’ list of deadline needs. Which brings us to the deadline.

Part ten: trade deadline check-in

Here’s where things stand. The trade deadline is Tuesday, November 10th, at 4 p.m. Eastern.

The Rams have not made a trade. Not for Jerry Jeudy, not for Keenan Allen, not for Khalil Mack, no matter what you’ve seen in fake clips this week.

Around the league, here’s what’s actually happened recently, per ESPN’s trade tracker. On November 4th, Arizona traded quarterback Jacoby Brissett to Baltimore. Before that, the Bears got Jeffery Simmons from Tennessee, the 49ers got DeForest Buckner from the Colts, and Seattle got Quenton Nelson from the Colts. Notice that two of the Rams’ NFC West rivals already made moves.

As for the Rams, here’s the situation. They have about $206,000 in cap space. Their best remaining picks are a 2027 second, fourth, fifth, and sixth, and a 2028 third. The names connected to them in real reporting and analysis are Josh Downs and Brian Thomas Jr., and the Colts reportedly plan to wait until after this weekend before making more deals.

And McVay’s own words from earlier this week still say a lot: “Hopefully, some of those trades will be guys getting back from injury.” This week, that “trade” is Myles Garrett. Ronnie Rivers is coming off IR too. And Davante Adams is trending toward the shorter end of his six-to-ten-week timeline.

So my read, and this is not a report: if the Rams do anything, it’ll be small, it’ll probably be at receiver or cornerback, and history says it’ll be on Tuesday. Les Snead has made four deadline-day trades over the years.

Part eleven: Washington preview

Rams at Commanders, Sunday, 1 p.m. Eastern, 10 a.m. Pacific, on FOX, at Northwest Stadium. The Rams are 4-4, tied with Seattle for second in the NFC West. Washington is 2-4-1, coming off a 27-10 win over the Eagles. The Rams are 5.5-point favorites, and the over/under is 47.5.

Washington’s final injury report. Receivers Treylon Burks and Dyami Brown are both out. Defensive tackle Jer’Zhan Newton is questionable with a pectoral injury. They already put defensive tackles Daron Payne and Javon Kinlaw on IR this week, along with running back Jeremy McNichols. That’s two receivers and two starting-caliber defensive tackles gone.

Keys for the Rams.

One: run the ball at a thin interior. Without Payne and Kinlaw, and with Newton questionable, Washington’s middle is vulnerable. Kyren Williams and Blake Corum combined for 192 rushing yards against the Raiders and 129 against the Chargers. Expect a heavy dose.

Two: protect the football. The Rams have 12 giveaways in eight games, and Stafford has eight interceptions. That’s the one thing that could flip this game.

Three: earn the passing downs. That’s McVay’s phrase, and he’s right. Win first and second down, and Garrett and Donald get to hunt on third.

Four: survive in the secondary. If McDuffie is out, the corners will be tested. With Burks and Dyami Brown out, Washington is thin at receiver too, which helps.

Five: start fast. The Rams went five drives without a first down to open the Chargers game. On the road, they can’t spot Washington points.

My prediction. I’m going to bump it up a little now that Garrett is confirmed and Washington is missing two receivers: Rams 26, Commanders 19. Garrett gets his first sack as a Ram, the run game takes over in the second half, and Puka has another 9-catch day. If McDuffie and Curl are both out, the margin could tighten.
