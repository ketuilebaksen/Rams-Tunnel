Let’s start with Puka, because that’s the question every Rams fan is asking.

Here’s how McVay opened his Friday press conference: “Trent will be a DNP. He’ll be questionable for the game. Nate Landman with his concussion, he’ll be out for the game. He’s making good progress though. And then Puka, I’m sure you guys are aware, got the calf yesterday in practice. He’ll be questionable for the game.”

When a reporter asked if Puka would practice Friday, McVay said simply: “He will not practice today.”

Then he was asked what happened — whether Puka went down on a specific play. And this is the most interesting part of the whole press conference. McVay said: “I don’t think he knows exactly when it occurred.” He explained that Puka “felt it” and “wasn’t able to have some of the power and full explosion.” According to the Rams’ official website, McVay described it as “a little minor thing in there,” and said Puka “took part in a majority of the practice, left, got it checked out.”

And when he was asked if it would be a pregame decision, McVay said: “It will be. Yeah.”

So let’s break down what that means.

First, the good news. McVay used the word “minor.” There was no dramatic moment where Puka went down holding his leg. He practiced most of Thursday, felt something, and the team shut him down to get it checked. That’s a lot better than a player being carted off the field.

Second, the not-so-good news. The specific problem McVay described — not having “the power and full explosion” — is exactly what a receiver needs. Puka’s whole game is about physical routes, breaking tackles and accelerating after the catch. If he can’t explode, he’s not the same player. And the Rams have been here before this season. When Puka missed Weeks 2 and 3, Konata Mumpfield stepped up, and in the Week 3 game at Denver, he had four catches for 93 yards and a go-ahead touchdown. So the Rams know they can move the ball without Puka for a game. It’s just much harder without Davante, too.

And third, the timing. Kickoff in Las Vegas is 1:25 p.m. Pacific. Teams announce their inactive players about 90 minutes before kickoff, so we should know Puka’s status by around 11:55 a.m. Pacific on Sunday. If you’re watching this after that, check the pinned comment — I’ll update it.

Here’s how I see it. With Davante Adams already out six to 10 weeks, the Rams cannot afford to lose Puka for a long time. A calf strain that gets aggravated can turn one missed game into four. So if there’s any doubt on Sunday morning, I’d expect the Rams to be careful. But if Puka can run and cut in warmups, he plays. That’s who he is.

According to the AP, Puka has 37 catches for 417 yards and a touchdown in four games this season, and he already missed the Giants and Broncos games with hip and groin injuries. He’s been the heart of this offense when healthy. That’s what makes Sunday’s decision so hard.

Now let’s talk about what happens if Puka can’t go, because McVay gave a really thoughtful answer about that.

A reporter asked what McVay is looking for from the other receivers if Puka is out, with Davante already sidelined. Here’s part of his answer: “You don’t ever ask anybody to replace those guys. They’re unique guys that have meant so much to us. I want those players to be their best selves.”

He then named the players who would step up: Konata Mumpfield, Tutu Atwell, Jordan Whittington and C.J. Daniels. And he said: “it’s by committee with all of your eligibles, tight ends, your backs.”

And he finished with this: “It’s a great opportunity for other guys to step up if that’s the situation that we’re dealing with.” He also said he wants those players to “enjoy the opportunity” and to make sure that “when you look yourself in the mirror, no regrets afterwards.”

He also said something that showed how much this season’s injuries are weighing on everyone. He said “you feel for players that have put so much into it that aren’t able to compete at their craft that they love and be out there with their teammates.” That’s McVay talking about Davante, about Puka, about Kobie Turner and Poona Ford. This team has lost a lot of good players in a short time, and you can hear it in how he talks.

I love that answer, because it tells you exactly how the Rams will try to win if Puka sits. No one player is going to be asked to be Puka. Instead, McVay will spread the ball around — to the receivers, to tight ends Colby Parkinson, Tyler Higbee and Davis Allen, and to running backs Kyren Williams and Blake Corum.

And there’s a debut possibility here. According to the AP, if Puka sits, rookie C.J. Daniels — a sixth-round pick — could make his NFL debut. That would be a big moment for a young player, on the road, in a must-win game.

McVay also took time on Friday to praise one of his tight ends, calling him “a complete tight end” with “great leadership traits,” someone who “can be an inline blocker, can play across the formation.” That’s a reminder of how much this offense will lean on the tight end group this week, especially Parkinson, who caught the go-ahead touchdown against Arizona and was a full participant in practice all week.

Now, decision number two: Eric Kendricks.

According to the LA Times’ Gary Klein, McVay said Kendricks will be promoted from the practice squad to replace Landman. And when he was asked about Kendricks taking over, McVay was full of praise. Here’s what he said: “If you didn’t know anything, you’d say this guy’s played a lot of football. He’s got a veteran presence. Real good concept understanding, can play a variety of schemes, has had success in a variety of schemes, understands football from a big picture perspective, has a good vibe about himself.”

And then he added: “Coaching against him for all those years, there was a lot of respect. Then you get around him and the consistency of how he shows up, and we’re lucky to have him.”

Let me tell you why that matters. According to Heavy, Kendricks was a second-round pick by the Minnesota Vikings in 2015. He’s played for the Vikings, the Dallas Cowboys, the Chargers and the San Francisco 49ers. He has 1,180 career tackles, 21.5 sacks, 11 interceptions and eight forced fumbles. And in 2019, he was a first-team All-Pro with Minnesota. This isn’t a random practice-squad player. He’s one of the most experienced linebackers in the league.

And he’s replacing a big piece. According to the AP, Landman had 51 tackles and two tackles for loss through six games. Landman is the defense’s main communicator — the player who gets everyone lined up. That’s why it matters that Kendricks understands “a variety of schemes.” He’s seen just about everything an offense can throw at him. And he’s been the communicator before. For years in Minnesota, Kendricks was the linebacker who made the calls and got the defense set. That’s exactly the job the Rams need filled on Sunday. Against a well-coached offense with a lot of motion and shifts, having a veteran who’s seen it all can prevent the busted coverages that lead to easy touchdowns.

According to Heavy, the Rams can elevate Kendricks two more times this season before they’d have to sign him to the 53-man roster. So if Landman’s recovery takes longer, the Rams might have another decision to make soon.

There’s also a fun detail here. Kendricks spent time with the 49ers, and Raiders head coach Klint Kubiak has coached in San Francisco too. So Kendricks may know some of the concepts Kubiak likes to use. That’s exactly the kind of small edge you want from a veteran.

Now, decision number three — and this is the big one for the rest of the season: Myles Garrett.

A reporter asked McVay to confirm that Garrett might start his 21-day practice window next week. McVay said: “That’s a possibility.” He added that he’d wait to talk to Garrett, the team’s doctors and head athletic trainer Reggie Scott before deciding — but he said again, “that is a possibility for next week.”

And it lines up with other reporting. According to Pro Football Talk, NFL Network’s Ian Rapoport said on Friday that Garrett is “likely to return to practice next week.” If that happens, Garrett could be available for the Chargers game in Week 8 or the Washington game in Week 9.

Let me remind you how big that would be. According to Pro Football Talk, Garrett was the 2025 AP Defensive Player of the Year with a single-season record 23 sacks for the Browns. The Rams traded big draft picks to get him this offseason. But he played through knee discomfort in Week 1 against the 49ers in Australia, then had a procedure and went on injured reserve. He’s barely played for the Rams.

And let me explain how that practice window works, because it matters. When a player on injured reserve returns to practice, the team opens a 21-day window. During those 21 days, the player can practice with the team, and the Rams can activate him to the 53-man roster at any point. If they don’t activate him by the end of the window, he stays on injured reserve for the rest of the season. So opening the window doesn’t mean Garrett plays right away. It means the clock starts, and the Rams get up to three weeks to see how his knee responds to full practices before they decide. If the window opens next week, the realistic best-case scenario is Garrett playing against the Chargers or Washington — and the realistic middle case is a return right around the deadline or the bye.

And with Poona Ford and Kobie Turner both on injured reserve, the Rams’ defensive line badly needs reinforcements. Getting Garrett back alongside Aaron Donald would be like adding a blockbuster trade at the deadline — without giving up a single pick. Remember, when Donald came out of retirement in August, according to theScore, he said about Garrett: “Hopefully, we do some special things together.” And McVay said about that defensive front: “The beauty of the unit is the unit.” We’ve barely seen that unit together. If Garrett comes back healthy, the Rams’ pass rush could go from a weakness to a strength almost overnight.

McVay also talked about what Garrett has been like behind the scenes while rehabbing. He said: “He’s been very present,” and “he’s got a lot of respect from his teammates.” He also said: “Guys like him. He’s got a nice humility about himself.” And according to the Rams’ defensive staff on Friday, Garrett has been building a strong relationship with outside linebackers coach Joe Coniglio, bonding with the outside linebackers, and even having those guys over to his place. For a player who’s been injured almost the whole season, that’s a great sign. He’s not just waiting to come back. He’s already leading.

Now, there was another really interesting part of Friday’s media session, and it came from the defensive side.

The Rams’ defensive coaches talked about losing Kobie Turner and Poona Ford, and the answers were emotional. About Turner, the staff said: “He’s a great communicator. We put a lot on those guys up front, and he’s obviously the lead dog in it.” They also called him “one of the hardest playing guys I’ve ever been around.” According to the AP, Turner will miss at least four games with a groin injury.

And about Poona Ford, there was this: when Ford got hurt, the team thought it might be “a 50/50 shot” after the MRI. “You were just praying that it wasn’t anything.” Then the news came back that he was done for the season. The staff said Ford had “never had a serious injury,” that he “doesn’t like taking vet days off,” and that he still practices on Fridays even after so many years in the league. That tells you the kind of player the Rams lost.

And then the question turned to who steps up. Defensive coordinator Chris Shula was asked about defensive lineman Tyler Davis, who now has a much bigger role. The answer: “He’s one of the kind of like quiet leaders on that D-line. He’s a great communicator just like Kobie was.” The staff noted that Davis has played a ton of snaps since he was a rookie, and that he’s “definitely ready for it.”

Shula also admitted something important. A reporter pointed out that the defense has done a good job limiting yards, but hasn’t created as many sacks and turnovers as last year. Shula agreed: “You’re exactly right. You want to create pressure on the offense, you want to create turnovers, you want to get a lot of sacks.”

That’s a fair point. According to Fox Sports, the Rams allow only about 167 passing yards per game — one of the best marks in football. But the splash plays haven’t been there as often. And with a struggling offense, the defense creating turnovers could be the difference in close games.

Now here’s a fun part of the press conference. Shula was asked about the “peanut punch” — the technique of punching the ball out of a ball carrier’s arms, made famous by former Bears cornerback Charles Tillman. Shula told a story about showing his players Tillman’s YouTube videos about 12 years ago, when he was the defensive coordinator at John Carroll University. Back then, he said, “you didn’t see anybody else doing it.”

Now, Shula said, the Rams chart fumbles around the league every week, and “aside from run reach on the quarterback, the peanut punch is the number one thing that’s causing fumbles.” He compared a perfect punch to “hitting a home run.” And who’s the best on the Rams at it? Shula said: “Landman is kind of our leader in the clubhouse at doing it.” That’s another reason losing Landman this week hurts.

But Shula was very clear about one rule: “You never sacrifice the tackle for the punch.” And against Arizona, missed tackles were a real problem. So expect the Rams to go for the ball — but only after they’ve wrapped up the ball carrier.

Shula also gave a scouting report on Raiders head coach Klint Kubiak. He pointed out that Kubiak has coached in Seattle, San Francisco, Denver and New Orleans, and said: “He does one of the best in the league at matching the offense to the personnel.” He mentioned Brock Bowers and the Raiders’ running backs, and called it “a huge challenge.”

Now let’s look at the final injury report for both teams, because this game is going to be shaped by who’s on the field.

According to the Rams’ official website, here’s the Rams’ final report. Puka Nacua: questionable, calf. Trent McDuffie: questionable, groin — he didn’t practice at all this week. Nate Landman: out, concussion. Josh Wallace and Colby Parkinson were full participants all week and have no designation. And three starters — Aaron Donald, Byron Young and offensive lineman Alaric Jackson — had non-injury rest days on Friday and have no designation. So Donald will play. That’s normal for veterans late in a week — teams give them a day off on Friday to keep their legs fresh for Sunday. Donald is 35 now, and after coming out of retirement, managing his workload is smart. With Turner and Ford out, the Rams need him at his best for four quarters.

McDuffie’s status is a big one, too. According to the AP, before missing the Arizona game, McDuffie had 16 tackles, two tackles for loss, seven passes defended and an interception in five games. The AP called him arguably the Rams’ best defender. If he can’t go, the secondary will be tested by Kirk Cousins, who has 14 touchdown passes, tied for the league lead, according to the AP. And remember what happened last week: according to Rams On SI, McVay said he felt good about McDuffie heading into the Arizona game, but McDuffie ended up inactive. So “questionable” with no practices all week is not a great sign. I’d prepare for the Rams to play without him and be pleasantly surprised if he’s out there.

For the Raiders, according to the Rams’ official website, tight end Carter Runyon and receiver Dont’e Thornton Jr. are out. Rookie running back Mike Washington Jr. is questionable with foot and back injuries — he didn’t practice Thursday or Friday. And here’s the key one: Brock Bowers, who was limited earlier in the week with hamstring and calf issues, has no game designation. That means Bowers is expected to play. Left tackle Kolton Miller and linebacker Quay Walker also have no designation.

And Shula’s point about Kubiak matters here. If Washington Jr. can’t play, Kubiak will adjust — that’s what Shula meant when he said Kubiak is one of the best at “matching the offense to the personnel.” Expect more Bowers, more play-action and more motion to create easy throws for Cousins. The Rams’ defense has to stay disciplined and communicate well — which is exactly why losing Landman, the main communicator, hurts so much.

So the Rams will likely face Bowers — one of the best tight ends in football — without their best linebacker in Landman and possibly without their best cornerback in McDuffie. That’s the biggest challenge for Shula’s defense on Sunday.

And there’s one more factor working in the Rams’ favor. According to the AP, the Raiders rank 26th in scoring defense, allowing 27 points per game, and they’ve given up more than 415 yards per game over their last four games. According to Sports Illustrated’s Iain MacMillan, Las Vegas also ranks 27th in opponent success rate. MacMillan’s take: “It’s time to buy back in on the Rams.” Even with all the injuries, the Rams are still 3.5-point favorites, according to Heavy. So the experts still believe this roster has enough to win — if it plays clean.

And don’t forget the kicker. Brandon McManus makes his Rams debut on Sunday, after signing on Tuesday. There’s a nice storyline there: Raiders head coach Klint Kubiak is the son of Gary Kubiak, who coached McManus to a Super Bowl title in Denver. And McManus will be kicking indoors at Allegiant Stadium, under a fixed roof. After two weeks of chaos at kicker, the Rams just need him to make the routine kicks.

Before my keys, here’s what I’ll be watching on Sunday morning. First, Puka in warmups — is he running full speed and cutting hard? Second, McDuffie — is he on the inactive list? Third, how Kendricks looks in his first game since joining the Rams. And fourth, McManus’ first few kicks in warmups. Those four things will tell us a lot about how this game will go before the first snap. If Puka is active, McDuffie is active and McManus looks sharp, the Rams’ chances go way up. If two or three of those go the wrong way, this becomes a grind-it-out game where the run game and the defense have to carry everything.

Now, here are my three keys for Sunday.

Key number one: the eligibles by committee. McVay said it himself — “it’s by committee with all of your eligibles, tight ends, your backs.” Whether Puka plays or not, the Rams need Parkinson, Higbee, Kyren and Corum to be heavily involved. Start fast, run the ball, and take the easy throws.

Key number two: Kendricks and the safeties against Bowers. Bowers is Cousins’ favorite target. Kendricks has to diagnose quickly, and the safeties have to help. If the Rams limit Bowers, they take away the Raiders’ best weapon.

Key number three: create a splash play. Shula admitted the sacks and turnovers haven’t been there. With Donald on the field and the Raiders’ red zone offense struggling, one forced fumble or interception could swing this game. The Raiders have scored touchdowns on just 12 of 23 red zone trips, according to the AP. Make them kick field goals, and take the ball away when you can.

Here’s my prediction. If Puka plays, Rams 27, Raiders 20. If he doesn’t, Rams 24, Raiders 20. And I’ll add one more: Eric Kendricks leads the Rams in tackles in his first game back in the lineup.

So here’s my bottom line.

McVay didn’t announce anything shocking on Friday. But he made three decisions that matter. Puka Nacua is questionable, with a “minor” calf issue that will be a pregame decision. Eric Kendricks, a former All-Pro, replaces Nate Landman. And Myles Garrett’s return to practice next week is now “a possibility” — backed up by Ian Rapoport’s report that it’s likely.

Short term, Sunday is about surviving the injuries and playing clean. The Rams are 2-4, last in the NFC West, and according to NFL Next Gen Stats, their playoff chances were down to 43 percent after the Arizona loss. A win puts them at 3-4 with the Chargers coming to town. A loss drops them to 2-5 and makes every week after that a must-win. Long term, the Garrett news might be the most important thing McVay said all week. If the Rams can win in Las Vegas and get Garrett back for the Chargers or Washington, this season could look very different by the November 10th trade deadline.
