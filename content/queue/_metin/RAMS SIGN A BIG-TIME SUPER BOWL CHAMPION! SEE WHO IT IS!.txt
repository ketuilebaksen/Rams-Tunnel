Let’s start with how we got here, because the Rams’ kicking situation over the last three weeks has been almost hard to believe.

Week 5, Monday night against Buffalo. Harrison Mevis misses a 50-yard field goal on the opening drive, then has a 48-yard game-winning attempt blocked in overtime. The Rams lose 37-34. Mevis is released the next day.

Week 6, against Arizona. Blake Grupe, signed off the Jets’ practice squad, makes a 44-yard field goal on his very first attempt as a Ram. On the very next kickoff, he hurts his knee making a tackle. According to the AP, he tore a knee ligament and is out for the season. The Rams play the rest of the game without a placekicker. They run out the clock before halftime, go for two after their first touchdown, go for it on fourth-and-1 at the Cardinals’ 27 — and lose 18-16. The go-ahead extra point late in the game? Kicked by punter Ethan Evans, with backup quarterback Stetson Bennett holding.

And now, Week 7. Brandon McManus.

According to Yahoo Sports, McManus is the Rams’ third official kicker in three weeks — and the fourth, if you count Evans’ emergency extra point. Since Greg Zuerlein left after the 2019 season, the Rams have used 11 different kickers, including McManus and Evans. And according to StatMuse, as cited by Yahoo Sports, McManus is the 14th different kicker of Sean McVay’s tenure. Heavy put it even more bluntly: since the start of last season, the Rams have changed kickers three times, changed long snappers, and changed special teams coordinators. Ethan Evans is the only returning piece of that operation.

That’s why this signing matters. The Rams don’t need a miracle at kicker. They need stability.

And it’s worth remembering how the Rams got here. For years, Greg Zuerlein — “Greg the Leg” — was one of the most reliable kickers in football for this franchise. According to Yahoo Sports, when he left for Dallas as a free agent in 2020, the carousel started. Matt Gay brought stability for a while and kicked for the Super Bowl LVI champions, but he left after the 2022 season. Since then, the Rams have cycled through one option after another. According to Heavy, Joshua Karty was a 2024 sixth-round pick who was replaced by Mevis during the 2025 season. Mevis lasted five games into 2026. Grupe lasted one kick. That’s the context McManus walks into.

And notice how quickly the front office moved this time. Grupe got hurt on Sunday. He was placed on injured reserve on Monday. McManus and Prater worked out on Tuesday, and McManus agreed to terms the same day. According to the LA Daily News’ Adam Grosbard, McManus “agreed to terms with the Rams pending a physical,” and Grosbard described him as a “12-year vet hoping to bring some stability to the position.” There was no long search and no project signing. The Rams went straight to experience. After two weeks of chaos, that’s the right instinct.

So who is Brandon McManus?

He entered the NFL as an undrafted free agent with the Indianapolis Colts in 2013. He found his home in Denver, where, according to Yahoo Sports, he spent nine seasons. And in the 2015 season, he was part of one of the great championship runs in recent memory. According to Pro Football Talk, during that postseason, McManus made all 10 of his field goal attempts and all three of his extra points on the way to winning the Super Bowl. Ten for ten in the playoffs. That’s what clutch looks like.

And think about the team he won it with. That Broncos team beat the Carolina Panthers 24-10 in Super Bowl 50 at Levi’s Stadium, in what turned out to be Peyton Manning’s final game. The Super Bowl MVP that night was Von Miller — who, years later, came to the Rams in a trade and helped them win Super Bowl LVI. So there’s a little bit of Rams history connected to McManus’ championship, too. He’s been in a locker room that knew how to win the biggest game in the sport. That kind of experience matters when everything around you feels like it’s falling apart. And here’s a twist for Sunday: the head coach of that Super Bowl 50 Broncos team was Gary Kubiak. The head coach of the Raiders team McManus will face in his Rams debut is Gary’s son, Klint Kubiak. So McManus’ first game as a Ram comes against the son of the coach he won a ring with. You can’t script this stuff.

After Denver, he kicked for Jacksonville and then spent two seasons in Green Bay. According to Heavy, he’s also had stops with the New York Giants and the Washington Commanders. And according to Over the Cap, as cited by Heavy, he’s earned more than 37 million dollars in his career — which is where you might have seen the “37 million dollar Super Bowl champion” headlines this week.

Here are his career numbers, according to Pro Football Talk. In 186 regular-season games, McManus has made 82 percent of his field goal attempts and 97.4 percent of his extra points.

When I first talked about McManus as an option, I said he went 10 for 10 with Green Bay last season, based on one report I’d seen. That wasn’t the full picture. According to Pro Football Talk, McManus played 14 regular-season games for the Packers in 2025 and went 24 of 30 on field goals and 32 of 33 on extra points. I want to be accurate with you, so I’m correcting that right here. And to be fair to McManus, 24 of 30 is still 80 percent — right in line with his career average. It was a normal McManus season, not a disaster.

Now let’s be honest about the concerns, because there are real ones.

First, last season’s playoffs. According to Pro Football Talk, in Green Bay’s playoff loss to the Chicago Bears, McManus missed two field goals and an extra point. After that game, the Packers moved on from him in the offseason. For a Rams fan base that just watched missed and blocked kicks cost them a game against Buffalo, that history is going to make people nervous. I understand that.

Second, the long kicks. According to Yahoo Sports, McManus went 3 of 5 from beyond 50 yards last season. That’s not bad, but it’s not automatic either. The Rams’ kickers this season have struggled on longer kicks — every one of Mevis’ four misses came from 40 yards or longer. So McManus’ accuracy in that 40-to-50 range will be one of the biggest things to watch.

And third, his age. McManus is 35. That’s not old for a kicker — Matt Prater, the other kicker the Rams worked out, is 42. But it does mean the Rams aren’t signing a long-term answer. They’re signing a veteran to get them through this season.

So why McManus over Prater? According to Yahoo Sports, McManus beat out Prater after a Tuesday workout. Prater went 18 of 20 for Buffalo last season, according to ClutchPoints. Both are experienced. Both have kicked in big games. But McManus is seven years younger, and the Rams clearly liked what they saw in person. When you’ve watched two kickers go down in two weeks, you want the guy who looked best on the field that day.

And here’s a fun detail: both of these kickers are former Broncos. Prater kicked in Denver before McManus, and back in 2013 he set what was then the NFL record with a 64-yard field goal in Denver’s thin air. McManus took over in Denver after that and kicked there for nine seasons. So the Rams essentially held a tryout between two eras of Broncos kicking — and chose the younger one.

Here’s how I see it. McManus isn’t a perfect kicker. His career numbers are solid, not elite. But he’s been in high-pressure moments. He’s won a Super Bowl. He’s kicked in cold weather, in big stadiums and in the playoffs. After a week where a punter had to kick the go-ahead extra point, the Rams needed someone who won’t be overwhelmed by the moment. McManus gives them that.

Let me put the last three Rams kickers side by side so you can see why this matters. Mevis: five of nine on field goals this season, with every miss from 40 yards or longer, before he was released. Grupe: one for one, a 44-yarder, before his season ended on the very next play. McManus: an 82 percent career kicker over 186 regular-season games, with a Super Bowl ring and a perfect postseason run on his résumé. On paper, McManus is easily the most accomplished kicker the Rams have had this season. Now he has to prove it.

And honestly, kicking is as much mental as it is physical. A kicker who’s been cut, criticized and doubted — like McManus was after last season’s playoff loss — can either crumble under that pressure or use it as fuel. The best kickers in the league all have one thing in common: a short memory. McManus has kicked in Super Bowls and in disappointing playoff losses. He knows both sides of it. That experience could be exactly what helps him settle into a new team so quickly.

And there’s one more thing that should help him this week. The Rams play at Allegiant Stadium in Las Vegas, which has a fixed roof. No wind, no rain, no weather. For a kicker learning a brand-new snapper and holder in just a few days, a dome is about as friendly a debut as you can get.

Now, let’s talk about the operation, because a kicker doesn’t kick alone.

McManus will be working with long snapper Joe Cardona and holder Ethan Evans. Cardona was brought in this offseason by special teams coordinator Bubba Ventrone, who coached him before. Evans has been the one constant through all of this — he’s held for Mevis, for Grupe, and now for McManus. And according to Yahoo Sports, the timing between snapper, holder and kicker usually develops through repetition. McManus will have only a few practices to get that timing right before Sunday.

Ventrone’s philosophy all season has been simple: “Let’s eliminate the bad football. Let’s stop from losing.” He’s also said: “We just have to get back to no self-inflicted wounds to a certain degree.” According to NFL.com, the Rams entered last week ranked dead last in special teams DVOA, and they were on pace for the worst special teams season in the history of that metric. McManus alone won’t fix all of that. The coverage units still have to stop giving up big returns. But a reliable kicker is the foundation of everything else.

And think about how much points matter right now. The Rams have lost three games by a combined nine points — four to Denver, three to Buffalo and two to Arizona. Against Buffalo, the kicking game cost them a 50-yard miss and a blocked 48-yarder. Against Arizona, the lack of a kicker cost them a field goal attempt before halftime, a two-point try instead of an extra point, and a fourth-and-1 decision at the 27. If the Rams simply had reliable kicking in those games, it’s easy to imagine at least one or two of those losses turning into wins. That’s how much this position matters.

And having a real kicker changes how McVay can coach. Against Arizona, without a kicker, every drive near midfield was “touchdown or nothing.” With McManus, McVay can take the points when a drive stalls at the 25 or 30. He can kick the extra point instead of being forced to go for two. And in the final two minutes of a half, he can try to get into field goal range instead of running out the clock. That’s a huge strategic difference — and it’s one reason this signing matters more than the headline suggests.

It also matters because of how often the Rams’ games have come down to the final seconds. Three straight weeks, Stafford has led a touchdown drive in the final two minutes: the go-ahead touchdown in Philadelphia with 1:10 left, the game-tying touchdown against Buffalo with five seconds left, and the go-ahead touchdown against Arizona with 47 seconds left. This team plays a lot of close games. In close games, kickers decide outcomes.

Now let’s get to the second story: Brian Thomas Jr.

With Davante Adams out six to 10 weeks with a ruptured hamstring, the Rams’ receiver room is thin. According to Sports Illustrated, Adams had 49 targets in six games before his injury. According to the AP, Konata Mumpfield has eight catches for 127 yards — the third-most by a Rams receiver this season. That’s a big drop-off after Puka Nacua and Adams. And according to the AP, last season Matthew Stafford threw to his depth receivers less often than any other quarterback on a playoff team. So the Rams’ passing game has been built around two receivers — and now one of them is out.

That’s why trade proposals involving Jacksonville’s Brian Thomas Jr. keep coming up. One proposal circulating this week would send Thomas and a 2027 seventh-round pick to the Rams, in exchange for a 2027 second-round pick and a 2027 fifth-round pick. Let me be very clear: that’s a hypothetical proposal. It is not a report of a trade, an offer or even talks.

And this isn’t the first time Thomas’ name has been connected to the Rams. Back in April, Bleacher Report’s Gary Davenport proposed a deal that would have sent the Rams’ 2026 first-round pick — number 13 overall — plus a third-rounder and a 2027 fourth-rounder to Jacksonville for Thomas. Davenport said adding Thomas “would give the Rams a terrifying trio.” Of course, the Rams used that 13th pick on quarterback Ty Simpson instead.

Let’s quickly review who Thomas is. He was the 23rd overall pick in 2024, and as a rookie, he caught 87 passes for 1,282 yards and 10 touchdowns. In 2025, he dropped to 48 catches for 707 yards and two touchdowns. And this season, through six games, he has nine catches for 105 yards and one touchdown, according to RotoWire. Last Sunday against Houston, he played 15 snaps in the first half and none in the second half, according to ESPN Research, after a dropped pass. Jaguars head coach Liam Coen — McVay’s former offensive coordinator — said: “Guys have to step up and make plays.”

So is Jacksonville selling? According to Tom Pelissero, as reported by Bleacher Report on October 10th, the Jaguars have “shown no interest” in trading Thomas — although, as Pelissero put it, “there continue to be calls periodically.” And back in March, Jaguars general manager James Gladstone called the trade rumors “fraudulent.” So even after the benching, there’s no sign Jacksonville is ready to move him.

Now let’s talk about that proposal — a 2027 second-round pick and a fifth for Thomas and a seventh. Is that worth it for the Rams?

Here’s the case for it. Thomas is 2024’s 23rd overall pick, he had a 1,282-yard season, and he’s still on a cheap rookie contract that runs through 2027. According to Spotrac, his 2026 base salary is about 1.075 million dollars, so the Rams could realistically fit him under their tight cap. And his speed would give Stafford a vertical threat to pair with Puka.

And here’s the case against it. The Rams have already traded away their 2027 first-round pick, their 2027 third-rounder, their 2028 second-rounder and their 2029 third-rounder in the Myles Garrett and Trent McDuffie deals. Giving up a second-round pick for a receiver with nine catches this season — and who was just benched — would be a huge gamble. And at 2-4, the Rams have to be careful about trading away even more of the future for a player who might not fix the real problems.

My verdict hasn’t changed from our last video. I like the idea of Brian Thomas Jr. I don’t like a second-round price. If the Jaguars ever drop their asking price to a Day 3 pick, the Rams should absolutely make that call. Until then, I’d keep looking — Jerry Jeudy in Cleveland, with a 1.5 million dollar base salary, is still the more realistic option if the Rams decide to add a receiver.

And remember, the Rams also have options already in the building. Tutu Atwell had 105 catches for 1,535 yards and five touchdowns in his first five seasons with the Rams. Jordan Whittington, Konata Mumpfield and rookie CJ Daniels are all ready for bigger roles. And the tight ends — Colby Parkinson, Tyler Higbee and Davis Allen — can carry more of the load, too. Parkinson just caught the go-ahead touchdown against Arizona with 47 seconds left. And don’t forget Kyren Williams, who’s become a real weapon as a receiver out of the backfield this season. Without Adams, Kyren’s role in the passing game could grow even more — especially on third downs, where Stafford needs a reliable target who can make defenders miss.

The biggest risk is that the Rams lean too heavily on Puka. He was targeted 24 times against Buffalo, and he’s already dealt with a hip and groin injury this season. Spreading the ball around isn’t just smart strategy. It’s how the Rams keep their best player healthy for December.

Now let’s look ahead to Sunday, because this is where McManus’ Rams career begins.

The Rams travel to Las Vegas to face the Raiders. According to Fox Sports, Las Vegas is 3-3 under head coach Klint Kubiak, with Kirk Cousins at quarterback. Cousins has thrown for 1,582 yards, 14 touchdowns and five interceptions. The Raiders score 27 points per game and allow 27 points per game, and they’re giving up more than 237 passing yards per game. The Rams are 5.5-point favorites, according to Fox Sports. It’s also a homecoming that won’t happen: Davante Adams spent parts of three seasons with the Raiders, and he’ll miss this game with his hamstring injury. And the Raiders have a new head coach this year in Klint Kubiak, so the Rams’ defense is facing an offense with some new wrinkles — led by a veteran quarterback in Cousins who’s seen just about everything.

For the Rams, this game is about two things. First, can the offense finish drives without Davante Adams? Puka Nacua will see a lot of attention, so the tight ends and the young receivers have to step up. Second, can the special teams finally have a clean, quiet game? With McManus at kicker, Cardona snapping and Evans holding, the Rams need every extra point and every makeable field goal.

There’s more injury news to keep in mind, too. According to the AP, defensive tackle Poona Ford is out for the season with a ruptured triceps, and Kobie Turner is on injured reserve with a groin injury. Myles Garrett’s practice window still hasn’t opened. And Trent McDuffie is “feeling much better,” according to McVay, but the Rams won’t decide on his status until later in the week. So the Rams’ defense will be shorthanded against a Raiders offense that scores 27 points a game.

That’s why points matter so much on Sunday. If the Rams get into field goal range, they have to come away with points. If they score touchdowns, they have to convert the extra points. That’s McManus’ job: make the kicks that don’t make the highlights, so the Rams never have to go for two again because they don’t have a kicker.

Here are my three keys for Sunday.

Key number one: a clean operation. Snap, hold, kick. If McManus makes his first kick and the operation looks smooth, the whole team can exhale.

Key number two: Puka and the tight ends. With Adams out, Stafford needs to spread the ball around, especially to Parkinson and Higbee. The Raiders’ pass defense has been vulnerable, giving up more than 237 yards a game.

Key number three: no self-inflicted wounds. No false starts on fourth down. No fumbles in plus territory. No 15-yard penalties in the final minute. McVay has said it again and again: “We won’t win a game unless we figure out how not to beat ourselves.”

Here’s my prediction, on the record. Rams 27, Raiders 20. McManus goes perfect on extra points and makes two field goals, including one from 40-plus. Puka goes over 100 yards. Parkinson catches a touchdown. And the Rams get to 3-4 heading into the Chargers game. I’ve missed my last two predictions, so hold me to this one.

So here’s my bottom line. Yes, the Rams signed a Super Bowl champion — but he’s a kicker, and that’s exactly what they needed most. Brandon McManus isn’t perfect, and last season’s playoff loss will follow him until he proves it was just one bad day. But he’s experienced, he’s been clutch in the biggest games, and he gives the Rams something they haven’t had in weeks: a real, veteran kicker. As for Brian Thomas Jr., the idea is interesting, but a second-round pick is too much for a receiver who was just benched. The Rams’ best path forward is simple: stable kicking, cleaner execution, and players already in the building stepping up while Davante Adams recovers.
