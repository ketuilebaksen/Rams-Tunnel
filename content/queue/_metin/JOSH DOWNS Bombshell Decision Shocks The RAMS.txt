So when you hear the Rams’ name at this deadline, think fifth-round pick, not first-round pick. That’s where Downs lives.

Now let’s talk about who Josh Downs is, because he’s a good player in a tough situation.

According to ESPN, Downs is 25 years old and in his fourth NFL season, and he’s in the final year of his rookie contract. He came into the league in 2023 out of North Carolina — that’s my own knowledge of his background. And he got off to a strong start. According to Heavy, Downs topped 700 receiving yards in each of his first two seasons. In his career, he has 224 catches for 2,447 receiving yards and 11 touchdowns, according to Heavy, and with return yards included, he’s over 2,700 total yards.

But the last two seasons haven’t been as good. According to Pro Football Talk, Downs had 58 catches for 566 yards and four touchdowns last season. And this season, according to ESPN, he has 26 catches for 307 yards and no touchdowns through seven games. His catch rate is 56.5 percent of his targets, which ESPN says is a career low.

So why does he want out? Look at what’s happening in Indianapolis. According to ESPN, the Colts are 2-5, they’ve gone 3-12 since Week 10 of last season, and they fired general manager Chris Ballard on October 19th. Ed Dodds is the interim general manager. And on Tuesday, the Colts traded two of their best veterans — Quenton Nelson to Seattle and DeForest Buckner to San Francisco. Steichen said, according to ESPN: “Anytime you lose guys like that, it hurts.”

And the Colts may not be done. According to ESPN, multiple teams have expressed interest in running back Jonathan Taylor, too. Steichen pushed back on that. He said: “Those are hypothetical things right now.” And he added: “JT’s on our football team.” According to ESPN, Indianapolis also doesn’t have a 2027 first-round pick, which is one more reason a front office in rebuild mode would want to collect as many picks as it can. When a team is in that position, a receiver on an expiring contract who has asked to leave is one of the easiest pieces to move.

Think about it from the Colts’ side for a second. If they keep Downs, he plays out the season and becomes a free agent, and they likely get nothing back for him. If they trade him, they get a draft pick. So the Colts’ leverage is limited. That’s why the expected price — a fifth- or sixth-round pick, according to Atkins — is so low for a 25-year-old who has already had two 700-yard seasons. For a team with very few picks and almost no cap space, that’s the kind of deal that actually fits.

And on Wednesday, the day after the Nelson and Buckner trades, Downs asked to be traded. When reporters asked him about it, according to ESPN, he said: “I’m open to whatever. Whatever comes my way, come my way and we go from there.” And according to Pro Football Talk, he added: “My feet are here right now, so I’ve got to be right here. It could be different tomorrow, could be different the next day — I don’t know.”

He’s still showing up, though. According to Heavy, Downs was still practicing with the Colts on Friday, and he’s expected to play Sunday against the Jaguars. The Colts don’t plan to sit him unless a deal gets close.

And here’s the timeline from the insiders. According to Heavy, NFL insider Jordan Schultz reported that the Steelers are among the AFC teams interested in Downs. Schultz added: “While a deal is not considered likely this week, the expectation is a trade will eventually happen, per sources.”

So the most likely outcome is that Downs gets traded before November 10th. The question is where.

Now let’s look at why the Rams make sense, because there are real reasons.

Reason number one: the Rams’ receiver room needs help. According to Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak, Downs has more receiving yards this season than Tutu Atwell, Konata Mumpfield and Jordan Whittington combined. Grisak also pointed out that Mumpfield and Whittington have had negative EPA per target in the Rams’ three-receiver sets. EPA stands for expected points added. In simple terms, it means the offense has been getting less than expected when the ball goes to them. And we saw it against the Raiders. According to the AP, Mumpfield’s 15-yard catch was the only reception by a Rams wide receiver other than Puka Nacua.

Reason number two: Davante Adams is out. According to the Rams’ official website, Adams ruptured his hamstring, and McVay said he’s projected to be “on the shorter end” of a six-to-10-week timeline. So the Rams need help for at least the next month. Downs could step in right away as a third receiver and then become valuable depth once Adams returns.

Reason number three: the price. According to Grisak, Downs would cost the Rams about 2.4 million dollars for the rest of the season. According to The Athletic’s Nate Atkins, as cited by Heavy, Downs is the most obvious Colts player who could fit in Los Angeles, and a trade might cost just a fifth- or sixth-round pick. That’s a price the Rams can actually pay. Compare that to Jeffery Simmons, who cost two first-round picks. The Rams are shopping at the other end of the market.

Reason number four: the McVay connection. Grisak made an interesting comparison this week. He compared Downs to Jamison Crowder, the slot receiver McVay coached when he was Washington’s offensive coordinator. According to Grisak, Crowder had 126 catches for 1,471 yards and nine touchdowns over two seasons with McVay. And McVay said about Crowder back then: “It gives us the ability to kind of move him around and use him in a variety of ways.” Grisak described Downs as a “polished, quick-separating, high-volume slot receiver.” That’s the type of player McVay has used well before.

Reason number five: punt returns. According to Atkins, Downs’ punt-return ability would help the Rams. And if you’ve watched the Rams this season, you know the return game has been a problem. According to Rams Wire, back in September, the Rams ranked last in the NFL on special teams by DVOA, and Rams Wire called the return game the area most in need of change. A receiver who can also return punts helps in two places at once.

And let me explain why a slot receiver like Downs matters more than his stats might suggest. On third down, the defense knows you have to throw. The pass rush comes harder and the coverage gets tighter, and the quarterback has very little time. A slot receiver who can get open quickly over the middle — in a small window, against a linebacker or a nickel cornerback — is often the difference between a first down and a punt. That’s my own explanation, but it’s exactly the job Grisak was describing when he called Downs a “quick-separating” receiver. And with Stafford at 38, getting the ball out fast and avoiding hits is a big deal. Quick, easy throws on third down keep him upright for January.

Now, let’s be fair. There are real reasons this might not happen.

Reason number one: the fit with the Rams’ formations. Atkins, as cited by Heavy, wrote: “Downs is a slot-only player, which makes him a harder fit with the three-tight end sets the Rams love.” That’s a fair point. Against the Raiders, the Rams leaned on their tight ends — Davis Allen, Colby Parkinson and Tyler Higbee combined for 11 catches, 82 yards and two touchdowns, according to the AP. If the Rams keep using three tight ends, a slot receiver might not see the field as much as you’d think.

Reason number two: McVay’s own words. On Wednesday, when McVay was asked whether there had been any new trade discussions, according to the LA Times, his first answer was: “No.” Then he said, according to the New York Post: “Hopefully, some of those trades will be guys getting back from injury that haven’t played for us.” He did add, “You always think about it,” according to the New York Post. But McVay is clearly leaning toward patience.

Reason number three: the money. Even at 2.4 million dollars, Downs costs more than the Rams have. According to Athlon Sports, citing Over The Cap, the Rams have 206,406 dollars in cap space. According to Grisak, the most realistic way to make room would be restructuring Kyren Williams’ contract, which could free about 3.5 million dollars. That’s doable — but it’s another step.

Reason number four: the competition. According to Schultz, the Steelers are interested. ESPN named the Chiefs and Steelers as fits. And Grisak wrote that “the market for Downs appears to be competitive at this point.” If a team like Pittsburgh offers a slightly better pick, the Colts will take it. The Rams’ best 2027 picks after their second-rounder are a fourth, a fifth and a sixth, according to Grisak.

And reason number five: his down year. A career-low catch rate and no touchdowns through seven games is a fair concern. Some of that is the Colts’ situation — according to Heavy, he’s been the Colts’ best receiver with Alec Pierce out, which means defenses have been focused on him. But it’s still something the Rams would have to weigh.

Now here’s something funny. On that same ESPN list, the Rams were named as a fit for two players — and neither of them was Downs. According to ESPN, the Rams were listed as a fit for Vikings receiver Jauan Jennings, who had a 50 percent trade chance, and Buccaneers defensive tackle A’Shawn Robinson, who had a 30 percent trade chance. So if the clip wanted to quote ESPN accurately, the Rams name should have been attached to Jennings, not Downs.

That doesn’t mean Downs to the Rams is a bad idea. Rams On SI and The Athletic both think it makes sense. It just means nobody at ESPN has reported it — and Schefter certainly didn’t “confirm” it.

Let’s compare those two receivers side by side, because it’s a real choice. Jennings is the bigger, more physical outside receiver. According to Athlon Sports, citing Pro Football Focus, he has just four catches for 43 yards this season, but in 2025 he had 55 catches for 643 yards and nine touchdowns with the 49ers. His base salary is 8 million dollars, so he’d cost the Rams roughly 4 million for the rest of the season — that’s my own rough math — and he plays for a 6-0 Vikings team that might not want to sell.

Downs is the smaller, quicker slot receiver. He has 26 catches for 307 yards this season, he’d cost about 2.4 million according to Grisak, and he plays for a 2-5 Colts team that is clearly selling. Downs is younger, cheaper and more available. Jennings fits the Rams’ physical, run-first identity a little better and can play outside while Adams is out.

If I’m the Rams, Downs is the easier deal to make. Jennings might be the better fit for how McVay wants to play right now. That’s the debate.

Now let me walk through what a Downs trade would actually require, step by step.

Step one: create cap space. The Rams would need about 2.4 million dollars, according to Grisak. They have about 206,000. A restructure of a contract like Kyren Williams’ — which Grisak estimated could free about 3.5 million — would cover it.

Step two: agree on a price. Atkins suggested a fifth- or sixth-round pick. If another team offers a fourth, the Rams would have to decide whether to match it with their 2027 fourth-rounder.

Step three: timing. According to Schultz, a deal wasn’t considered likely this week. The Colts play the Jaguars on Sunday, and Downs is expected to play. The Rams play Washington on November 8th, and the deadline is two days later, at 4 p.m. Eastern on November 10th. So the most likely window for any Downs trade is the week of the deadline itself.

None of those steps has been reported as happening. But none of them is impossible either.

So here’s my honest take, and remember, this is not a report. I think Downs is the most realistic receiver the Rams could add before the deadline. He’s young, he’s cheap, he wants out and the Colts are selling. If the Rams make a receiver move, I’d bet on someone like Downs over a big name like George Pickens or Brian Thomas Jr.

But I’d also put the odds of the Rams getting him below 50 percent. McVay is talking about patience. The Rams are short on cap space. And at least two AFC teams are in the mix. If I had to guess, I’d say Downs gets traded — but there’s a good chance it’s to a team like Pittsburgh or Kansas City.

According to the Rams’ official website, receiver Jordan Whittington didn’t practice on Friday because of a back injury and is questionable for Sunday’s game. If Whittington misses time and the Rams struggle to get anything from their receivers besides Puka again, the pressure to add someone like Downs will go up.

Now let’s look at the full final injury report for Sunday, because there’s a lot to cover.

According to the Rams’ official website, long snapper Joe Cardona is doubtful with an ankle injury. McVay said that “would mean Jake McQuaide would be up” from the practice squad as the long snapper on Sunday. McQuaide was the Rams’ long snapper from 2011 to 2020, according to Pro Football Talk, so the Rams are in good hands.

Trent McDuffie is questionable with a groin injury. He was limited in practice all week. McVay said the Rams are “hopeful” about McDuffie, “but we’ll continue to see” where he’s at before the game.

Offensive lineman Coleman Shelton is questionable with a hip injury, but he was limited on Friday after missing practice Wednesday and Thursday. That’s a good sign. Tight end Colby Parkinson is questionable with a rib injury after not practicing all week. And Whittington is questionable with his back.

The good news: linebacker Nate Landman and linebacker Desjuan Johnson were full participants and have no game status, so they’re set to play. And Puka Nacua, Aaron Donald, Byron Young and Alaric Jackson were all listed with rest days on Friday — that’s normal veteran rest, not injuries.

On the Chargers’ side, according to the Chargers’ website, safety Derwin James, guard Kayode Awosika, defensive back Deane Leonard and receiver Brenen Thompson are all out. Cornerbacks Donte Jackson and Rodney Shelley are questionable. And Khalil Mack is off the injury report after full practices on Thursday and Friday. So Mack will play, and Ladd McConkey and Tre’ Harris are cleared, too.

That Chargers secondary is banged up. No Derwin James, no Deane Leonard, and two questionable cornerbacks. If the Rams can protect Stafford, there should be chances to throw the ball down the field.

Here are my three keys for Sunday.

Key number one: protect Stafford from Mack. If Shelton can play, that helps. If not, the Rams need to run the ball and use quick throws. The Rams ran 38 times against the Raiders, according to the AP, and that formula works.

Key number two: attack the Chargers’ secondary. With James and Leonard out, this is the week to take a few deep shots. Puka is the obvious target, but the Rams need someone else to step up — maybe Tutu Atwell or Konata Mumpfield. If one of them has a big game, it might even change the Rams’ thinking about the deadline.

Key number three: a clean special teams game with McQuaide snapping. McQuaide is a veteran, but he hasn’t worked with this kicker and holder in a game this season. The Rams need every snap to be perfect. No blocked kicks, no fake punts, no long returns.

My prediction: Rams 27, Chargers 17. The Chargers are 0-6 and missing key pieces in their secondary. The Rams are coming off their best game of the season. But a winless team coming off a bye, with Mack and Justin Herbert, can make this close if the Rams make mistakes.

So here’s my bottom line.

Josh Downs did make a big decision this week — he asked the Colts to trade him. That part is real. But the clip claiming Adam Schefter confirmed the Rams are “closing” on him is not. ESPN’s own rankings listed the Chiefs and Steelers as fits for Downs, and Jordan Schultz reported Pittsburgh’s interest. Nobody has reported the Rams are in talks.

Still, Downs makes sense for the Rams. He’s 25, he’s cheap, he can return punts, and he’d fill the third receiver spot that Rams On SI and The Athletic both say the Rams need. If the Rams make a receiver trade before November 10th, I’d bet on someone like Downs. But between McVay’s patience, the Rams’ cap space and the competition, I’d still call it less likely than not.

And keep an eye on two things over the next 10 days. First, Whittington’s back. If the Rams’ receiver depth takes another hit, a move like Downs goes from “nice to have” to “need to have.” Second, how the Rams’ receivers play against a Chargers secondary missing Derwin James. If Atwell or Mumpfield break out on Sunday, the Rams might feel comfortable waiting. If they disappear again, I think Les Snead will at least pick up the phone.
