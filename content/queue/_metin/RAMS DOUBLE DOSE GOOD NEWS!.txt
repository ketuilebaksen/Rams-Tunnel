Part one: Puka goes on IR after all

Let’s start with the correction.

On Wednesday, McVay said, “We’re not going to put him on IR just because of some semantics with the roster and things like that.” I reported that in my last video.

On Thursday, the Rams placed Puka on injured reserve anyway.

On Friday, McVay explained the change, and it’s simple: “We needed a roster spot. We didn’t need it initially.” And then: “Some things changed where we needed that spot because of all the moving pieces.”

So here’s the key point. This isn’t about Puka’s knee getting worse. McVay was clear that it was about roster space. The Rams needed an open spot on the 53-man roster, and the easiest way to get it was to move a player who can’t play for weeks anyway.

Remember, McVay has been talking all week about “roster mechanics.” On Wednesday, he said, “This is as much as I can remember, spending time with Les in the early parts of the week, of just the roster mechanics.” Puka’s IR move is part of that puzzle.

Quick note on the injury itself. ESPN’s report described Puka’s knee injury as a torn MCL. McVay described it Monday as “an MCL, PCL sprain, also turf toe.” I’m going with McVay’s description, since it came straight from the head coach, but I want you to know the reports don’t perfectly match.

Part two: when can Puka come back?

Being on IR means Puka has to miss at least four games. According to ESPN, those four games are: at Arizona this Sunday, home against Green Bay after the bye, home against Kansas City, and at San Francisco.

So the earliest Puka could return is the game after San Francisco, which lines up with the Week 15 timeline the original video mentioned. That fits what McVay said on Monday, “at least a month,” and what he said Wednesday, that Puka will be “out for an extended period of time.”

And that’s the right call. Puka is dealing with a knee injury and turf toe. For a receiver who depends on quick cuts, balance, and changing direction, that’s a tough combination. Rushing him back would risk something worse. The goal should be a healthy Puka for the stretch run and the playoffs, not a limited Puka in late November.

Here’s a stat that shows how much the Rams will miss him. Per ESPN, Puka has 70 catches for 715 yards and four touchdowns in just seven games. He missed Weeks 2 and 3 with a hip injury. Seventy catches in seven games is ten a game. That’s an incredible pace.

Part three: the two new receivers

So who did the Rams add with that roster spot?

Brennan Presley was promoted from the practice squad to the active roster. The original video pointed out that his value is on special teams, especially as a returner. That gives the Rams another option alongside Xavier Smith, who they just brought back from the 49ers’ practice squad.

Tyler Scott was signed to the practice squad. He’s not arriving with big receiving numbers, but he has NFL experience, and right now the Rams need bodies who know how to run routes at this level.

Let’s be realistic. Neither player replaces Puka. Nobody does. But together, they give McVay a few more options for the next four weeks, especially on special teams, where the Rams lost Jordan Whittington to IR. Whittington was averaging 26.3 yards per kickoff return, according to Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak, so the return game matters.

Part four: the final injury report for Arizona

Here’s where things stand going into Sunday, according to the Sporting Tribune’s Friday report.

Myles Garrett: questionable, but expected to play. He sat out Friday’s practice after carrying a heavy snap load in his return against Washington. McVay expects him to go.

Trent McDuffie: questionable. He was limited Friday. He felt good after Thursday’s practice but was a little sore on Friday, and the Rams planned to test him with less predictable movements. That’s exactly what McVay talked about Wednesday, when he said the issue is “mirroring, redirecting, your lateral quickness, your short space.”

Kam Curl: questionable. Limited Friday.

Steve Avila: out with a hamstring injury.

Terrance Ferguson: out with a hamstring injury.

Tyler Higbee, Colby Parkinson, Davis Allen, and Max Klare: expected to be available. That’s important. Earlier in the week, there were concerns. Higbee showed up on the injury report with an illness, Davis Allen was banged up, and Parkinson was limited. But all four tight ends are expected to be ready. With Puka out, that might be the biggest piece of good news of the week.

Puka Nacua: on IR.

Davante Adams: on IR. McVay said Wednesday, “I would think after Green Bay is a realistic timeline for him.”

So the double dose of good news is real: Garrett is expected to play, and all four tight ends are expected to be available. McDuffie might make it a triple.

Part five: Garrett is honest about his knee

Let’s talk about Garrett, because he said something really interesting this week.

In his first game back against Washington, Garrett had three sacks, three tackles for loss, and a strip-sack right before halftime that led to points. That’s an incredible return. But Garrett himself was honest that he’s not all the way back.

He said, “I felt like I was a little bit slow, and it picked up in the second half.”

He also said the knee isn’t at full strength yet, and he’s working to get his first-step timing back. And he said: “You can only get in football shape by playing football and being on the field.”

Think about that. Three sacks, and he thinks he was slow. That’s a scary thought for the rest of the NFC. If that was Garrett at less than full speed, what does he look like in December?

And the reason he sat out Friday makes sense. He played a lot of snaps against Washington, more than a lot of people expected in his first game back. The Rams are managing his workload carefully. McVay said Monday that Garrett’s snap count will keep increasing “as he’s feeling better and getting back to being able to play that workload that he’s been accustomed to throughout his whole career.”

Part six: the rookie quarterback problem for Arizona

Now the matchup that has Rams fans excited.

Arizona is starting rookie quarterback Carson Beck, and it will be his first NFL start.

The original video put it perfectly: imagine you’re a young quarterback in your first start, and you’ve got Myles Garrett and Aaron Donald coming straight at you. That’s not the welcome anyone wants.

And it’s not just Garrett and Donald. Byron Young has been outstanding on the edge, McVay said. Braden Fiske has held up inside. Josaiah Stewart and Wesley Bailey have made plays. This pass rush can come from anywhere.

McVay talked about this approach before the Washington game. He said the Rams have to “earn the right to be able to get into some of those known passing situations.” In other words, stop the run on first and second down, and then let the pass rush hunt on third and long. Against a rookie quarterback making his first start, that plan gets even more dangerous.

But here’s the warning, and the original video said it too. The Rams already lost to Arizona this season, 18-16 on October 18th. That’s the game where Davante Adams got hurt. So nobody should treat this as an automatic win. Divisional games are always close, and a team with a rookie quarterback can sometimes play loose and surprise you.

Part seven: CJ Daniels’ opportunity

The original video highlighted one young receiver, and I think it’s worth talking about: CJ Daniels.

According to the original video, Matthew Stafford has spoken positively about Daniels. I haven’t been able to confirm Stafford’s exact words, so I’ll just say the video reported it. What I can tell you is what Daniels showed in college at Miami: he can make contested catches. He doesn’t always get wide open, but when the ball is in the air, he fights for it.

That’s a useful skill right now. With Puka out, Stafford doesn’t have a receiver who gets open easily on every play. He needs someone he can trust in tight coverage, someone he can throw to when the pocket gets messy.

Before the season, there was talk that Daniels could be the Rams’ third receiver. Instead, his role stayed small. Against Washington, he wasn’t even targeted. But injuries change everything. McVay said Wednesday it will be “a great opportunity for some new guys to get chances that they haven’t gotten yet.”

The original video mentioned one prediction of Daniels going over 50 receiving yards against Arizona. That’s not a guarantee, but it’s a fair bar. If he can do that, he might earn a bigger role even after Adams and Puka come back.

Part eight: the offensive game plan without Puka

So what does the Rams’ offense look like on Sunday?

The run game comes first. Kyren Williams and Blake Corum combined for 192 rushing yards against the Raiders, 129 against the Chargers, and 124 against Washington. Ronnie Rivers, who came off IR, could get some work too. The original video mentioned a prediction of more than 150 combined rushing yards against Arizona. That’s a high bar, but if the Rams get there, they’ll probably win.

Two backs on the field? The original video raised the idea of using two running backs with two tight ends. McVay was asked about this on Wednesday and said, “I think you explore everything.” He also quoted Bill Parcells: “There’s a way to win all of them.” So I wouldn’t be surprised to see some new looks. But to be clear, McVay didn’t confirm any specific plan.

The tight ends are the passing game. With Higbee, Parkinson, Allen, and Klare all expected to play, the Rams have their deepest position group on offense available. I expect a lot of two- and three-tight-end sets. The Rams already use those more than almost anyone in the league.

Atwell is the deep shot. Tutu Atwell had the Rams’ biggest pass play since Adams got hurt, a 40-yard catch against the Chargers. He’s the one real speed threat left.

Protect Stafford. With Avila out, the offensive line is missing a starter. The line has been one of the best in football at protecting Stafford. It needs to hold up again.

And one more thing. The original video made a smart point. Arizona’s coaches have already seen McVay’s offense once this season. But with so many new players on the field, the Rams might show the Cardinals things they haven’t prepared for. Sometimes injuries force creativity.

Part nine: Puka’s absence on the schedule

Here’s something worth noticing about Puka’s four-game IR stint. Look at those four games: Arizona, Green Bay, Kansas City, and San Francisco.

That’s a tough stretch.

Arizona already beat the Rams once this season.

Green Bay is a strong opponent coming out of the bye.

Kansas City just traded for Josh Downs at the deadline, giving Patrick Mahomes another receiver. And yes, that means the Rams will see Downs, the player four fake clips said was coming to Los Angeles, in a Chiefs uniform.

San Francisco just added Keenan Allen for a sixth-round pick, after adding DeForest Buckner earlier.

So the Rams will face two teams that traded for receivers at the deadline, while playing without their own best receiver. That’s the reality of the stretch ahead. The good news is that Davante Adams is expected back “after Green Bay,” per McVay, so he could be back for the Kansas City and San Francisco games.

Part ten: the bigger picture, 6-4 or 5-5

Here’s why Sunday matters so much.

The Rams are 5-4 with three straight wins. A win in Arizona makes them 6-4 heading into the bye, with momentum and a chance to get healthier. A loss makes them 5-5, with a tough four-game stretch coming.

The original video said it well: there’s “virtually no margin for error left.” I wouldn’t go quite that far, since there’s still plenty of season left. But a loss to Arizona, a team starting a rookie quarterback in his first game, would be a real setback.

And there’s revenge on the line. The Rams lost 18-16 to Arizona in October. They lost Adams in that game. This is a chance to settle that score.

Part eleven: Tyreek Hill and the restructures, quick update

Quick update on a story we covered this week. The Rams restructured the contracts of Kyren Williams and Kam Curl, creating about $3 million in cap space, per Heavy. That got people talking about free agent Tyreek Hill, whose agent, Drew Rosenhaus, said he’ll let teams know when Hill is medically cleared.

Nothing new on that front. Nobody has reported Rams interest in Hill, and he hasn’t been cleared. But it’s worth remembering that the restructures also gave the Rams the room to make roster moves like promoting Brennan Presley. So the cap space is already being used, just not on a star.

Looking back at the October loss

Let’s remember what happened the last time these two teams met, because it matters for Sunday.

On October 18th, Arizona beat the Rams 18-16. It was close the whole way, and it came down to the end. McVay mentioned this week that three of the Rams’ four losses this season looked a lot like that one: tight games decided late. And he said the experience of those losses helped the Rams finish the Washington game.

That October game was also the worst day of the season for the Rams’ offense. Davante Adams pulled up in the end zone on a noncontact play on the second-to-last offensive snap and ended up with a ruptured hamstring. The next day, McVay announced Poona Ford was out for the season and Kobie Turner was going on injured reserve. The Rams fell to 2-4.

Since then, they’ve won three straight. So this game is about more than one win. It’s about proving the team that came back from 2-4 is real.

The Rams’ receiver depth chart for Sunday

Let’s look at exactly who will be catching passes from Stafford.

Tutu Atwell. He has 99 receiving yards this season, per Rams Wire’s Cameron DaSilva, but he’s the deep threat. His 40-yard catch against the Chargers is the Rams’ longest pass play since Adams went down.

Konata Mumpfield. He leads the healthy receivers with 162 yards this season, per DaSilva. He had two catches for 20 yards against Washington.

CJ Daniels. The contested-catch guy. He wasn’t targeted against Washington, but he should get real snaps now.

Xavier Smith. Back from the 49ers’ practice squad. Mostly a returner, but he knows the offense.

Brennan Presley. Just promoted. Likely special teams first.

The tight ends. Tyler Higbee, Colby Parkinson, Davis Allen, and Max Klare, all expected to be available. McVay called Allen “tough as hell” after he played through a back injury against Washington.

That’s not a scary group on paper. But it’s a group that will be motivated, and McVay has shown over the years that he can scheme players open. This is where coaching matters most.

The secondary against a rookie

On the other side of the ball, the Rams’ secondary is still banged up. Jaylen Watson is out for the season. McDuffie and Kam Curl are both questionable. Josh Wallace has been starting in Watson’s place, and Emmanuel Forbes has moved up to the number three cornerback.

But here’s the thing. That group held Washington to 13 points last week without McDuffie. And now it faces a rookie quarterback in his first start, not Jayden Daniels.

If McDuffie plays, the Rams’ secondary gets a lot more comfortable. If he doesn’t, the Rams will lean even harder on the pass rush to make Beck’s day short. Either way, a rookie making his first start is more likely to make mistakes than a veteran. The Rams need to be ready to take advantage.

Garrett and Donald, game two

Last week was the first time Garrett and Aaron Donald played together. McVay said Monday that their combination was “unscouted,” meaning Washington had no film of how the Rams would use them. Now every opponent has a tape.

So Arizona’s coaches will have spent the week studying it. They’ll try to slide protection toward Garrett, or chip him with a running back, or get the ball out quickly. And that’s fine, because McVay said the Rams’ plan “may or may not be the same or different” going forward.

Here’s what I’m watching. If Arizona focuses on Garrett, does Donald get one-on-ones again? McVay said Donald “had a couple awesome things that allowed him to be able to get some one-on-ones” against Washington. And if Arizona tries to handle both of them, do Byron Young and Josaiah Stewart get free shots?

That’s the beauty of this front. Garrett said it best before last week’s game: “Offenses really have to pick their poison.” And McVay said Wednesday that Donald is having fun: “You can tell when he’s having fun and when he’s not.” A rookie quarterback is not who you want to face when those two are having fun.

The numbers I’ll be watching

Here’s my stat sheet checklist for Sunday.

Rams rushing yards. Over 130 would be a great sign. The original video’s prediction of 150-plus would be even better.

Targets to tight ends. I expect at least 12 targets combined for Higbee, Parkinson, Allen, and Klare.

Sacks. The Rams were tied for 26th in the league with 12 sacks before Garrett returned, per ESPN. They had a big day against Washington. Three or more against a rookie would be a statement.

Turnovers. Win the turnover battle against a rookie, and the Rams win the game.

Starting field position. With Smith and Presley returning kicks, the Rams need to start drives with good field position.

Stafford’s yards per attempt. It was about 5.1 against Washington. Without Puka, I’d be happy with anything over six.

Part twelve: keys to the game

Let me give you my keys for Sunday.

One: rattle the rookie. Carson Beck is making his first NFL start. Get pressure early, make him uncomfortable, and force mistakes. Garrett, Donald, Young, and Fiske should have chances.

Two: run the ball. The Rams don’t need 300 passing yards. They need 30-plus carries and a clock-controlling game plan.

Three: feature the tight ends. All four are expected to play. Use them.

Four: win the turnover battle. The Rams have had 12 giveaways in their first eight games, and Stafford has eight interceptions. Against a rookie quarterback, protecting the ball and forcing turnovers is the formula.

Five: special teams. With Smith and Presley in the return game, the Rams need to win field position.

Six: don’t let Arizona hang around. The October loss was a close one. If the Rams get a lead, they need to keep pushing.

What still worries me

The headline says good news, and there is some. But let me be honest about what still worries me going into Sunday.

The offensive line without Avila. The Rams’ line has been one of the best in the league at protecting Stafford. Losing a starting guard changes that. Arizona will try to attack the interior, and Stafford doesn’t have Puka as a quick safety valve anymore.

Garrett’s knee. He’s expected to play, but he sat out Friday and admitted the knee isn’t at full strength yet. If the Rams have to limit his snaps, the pass rush won’t be quite as dangerous.

McDuffie’s back. He was sore again on Friday after feeling good on Thursday. That back-and-forth is what makes back injuries tricky. If he can’t go, the secondary is thin.

The October game. Arizona knows how to play the Rams. They won last time, and they’ll come in confident at home, even with a rookie quarterback.

Turnovers. Twelve giveaways in eight games is too many. Against a defense that will be trying to help its rookie quarterback with short fields, the Rams can’t give the ball away.

None of that means the Rams will lose. It just means this isn’t the easy win some fans think it is. The Rams have to earn it.
