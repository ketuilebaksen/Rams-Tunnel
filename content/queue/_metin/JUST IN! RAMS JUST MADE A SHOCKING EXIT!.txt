Less than twenty-four hours after a blocked field goal ended one of the wildest Monday Night Football games of the season, the Los Angeles Rams made their move. Harrison Mevis is gone. The kicker they called “The Thiccer Kicker” — the undrafted underdog who went six for six in the playoffs last January, who never missed a single extra point in his NFL career — was released on Tuesday. In his place, the Rams signed Blake Grupe off the New York Jets’ practice squad, a kicker who has already been cut twice in the last eleven months. That’s the shocking exit. And it’s real — Sean McVay confirmed it himself. But here’s the question nobody in Los Angeles can answer yet: did the Rams just fix their biggest weakness, or did they simply change the name on the back of the jersey? Because if you look at the full history of this special teams unit, the kicker might be the smallest part of the problem. Today I’m going to walk you through exactly why Mevis was cut, who Blake Grupe really is, what McVay said about the special teams coordinator everybody’s pointing at, and the other surprise exit that hit the Rams this week. Let’s start with the timeline, because it moved fast.

Monday night, October 12th. The Rams lose 37-34 in overtime to the Buffalo Bills at SoFi Stadium. Harrison Mevis misses a 50-yard field goal on the opening drive. Then in overtime, after Nate Landman forces a fumble and Aaron Donald recovers it, the Rams drive into field goal range and line up for a 48-yard game-winner. Greg Rousseau gets a hand on it. Blocked. Buffalo takes over, and Tyler Bass wins it from 45 yards. After the game, Sean McVay doesn’t hide his frustration: “In the kicking game, especially with our PAT, field goal, not even close to good enough. It cost us the game tonight.” And when he was asked about possible changes: “I’m going to evaluate everything.”

Tuesday morning, October 13th. NFL Network’s Ian Rapoport and Mike Garafolo report that the Rams are releasing Mevis and signing Blake Grupe. Later that day, McVay confirms it at his press conference.

Wednesday, October 14th. The moves become official. Grupe is signed. Mevis is released. And the Rams make a couple of smaller moves alongside it: edge rusher Garrett Nelson is added, cornerback Damarion Williams joins the practice squad, and outside linebacker Jamie Sheriff is released from the practice squad.

From blocked kick to pink slip in less than a day. That’s how fast the NFL moves when a contender starts 2-3.

And think about how unusual that is for McVay. He’s not a coach who usually makes knee-jerk moves after one game. When the Rams brought Mevis in to challenge Karty last season, McVay let the two of them compete before making the call. This time, there was no competition and no waiting. That tells you how seriously he takes where this team is right now — 2-3, three games behind San Francisco, with the Super Bowl being played in their own stadium this February.

Now, before we get to Grupe, I want to give Harrison Mevis his due, because his story deserves more than a one-line transaction.

Mevis went undrafted in 2024 out of Missouri, where he was one of the most famous kickers in college football. He led the SEC with 26 made field goals in 2021, and he made 89 of 106 field goal attempts in his college career. And in 2023, he hit a 61-yard walk-off field goal to beat Kansas State — one of the most famous kicks in Missouri history. This was a kicker with a big leg and a big moment already on his résumé. But undrafted kickers don’t get many chances. He went to training camp with the Carolina Panthers and got waived. He kicked in the UFL for the Birmingham Stallions. He spent the summer of 2025 with the New York Jets and got released again. Then the Rams brought him onto their practice squad.

And midway through last season, with Joshua Karty struggling, McVay made the switch and promoted Mevis. What happened next was close to perfect. Mevis made 12 of his 13 field goals in the regular season and all 39 of his extra points. His only regular-season miss came in that painful one-point overtime loss to Seattle in December. And in the playoffs, when every kick mattered, he went six for six. Rams fans loved him. The nickname caught on. He became one of those underdog stories that makes a locker room believe in each other.

Even after Monday’s loss, his teammates stood behind him. Kyren Williams said it plainly: “We don’t change up on nobody because of a bad game.” And Mevis himself handled it like a pro. “Go back to work and find ways to fix it. Next-play mentality, right?” He also said, about the overtime kick: “I felt like I hit it good. That’s all I can tell you.”

So why did the Rams move on so quickly? Because the numbers this season were impossible to ignore.

Here’s where Mevis stood after five games. Five of nine on field goals. According to the AP, his four missed field goals were the most in the NFL this season. According to Fox Sports, his 55.6 percent success rate was the worst in the league. And every single miss came from 40 yards or longer — three from 40 to 49 yards and one from 50-plus. Now, to be completely fair, he was still 14 for 14 on extra points. Short kicks were never the issue. But in a season where the Rams have already lost two one-score games — by four points in Denver and by three in overtime against Buffalo — the longer field goals are exactly the kicks that decide games.

And there’s one more detail about the blocked kick that matters. According to NFL.com, the offensive line let Rousseau through — but the kick also appeared low and to the left. In other words, it wasn’t just one thing that went wrong. It was protection and trajectory together. And that’s going to be important when we talk about whether replacing the kicker actually solves the problem.

So the Rams decided they couldn’t wait. Five games in, three games behind the undefeated 49ers in the NFC West, they decided the kicking position couldn’t be a question mark for one more week.

Now — who is Blake Grupe?

Grupe is 27 years old. He played his college football at Arkansas State and Notre Dame, and he entered the NFL in 2023 as an undrafted free agent with the New Orleans Saints. And here’s the thing a lot of people don’t realize: he won the job right away. As a rookie in 2023, he made 30 of his 37 field goals. In 2024, he got better, making 27 of 31. That’s a kicker who looked like he’d found a home.

Then 2025 happened. Grupe missed eight kicks over his first twelve games, finishing his time in New Orleans 18 of 26 on field goals. The Saints released him last November. And that could have been the end of his story as a starter.

Instead, the Indianapolis Colts picked him up a few days later — and he was perfect. Eleven of eleven on field goals over five games. Ten of ten on extra points. Four for four from 50 yards and beyond. And in Week 15, he drilled a 60-yarder in Seattle that set a Colts franchise record. After he got to Indianapolis, Grupe said something that tells you a lot about how a kicker’s confidence works: “To come here and all of a sudden everything’s right down the middle, it just makes you thankful for this team.”

But the roller coaster wasn’t over. This past summer, Grupe lost a training camp competition in Indianapolis to Spencer Shrader, and the Colts cut him in August. The Jets claimed him off waivers, but he never kicked in a game for them — they moved him to their practice squad. And that’s where the Rams found him.

Here are his career numbers. Eighty-six of 105 field goals. That’s 81.9 percent. A long of 60 yards. And 96 of 98 on extra points.

So what did McVay say about why Grupe was the choice? Here’s the quote: “He did a great job for Indy last year, has kicked in a lot of big-time professional settings.” And he added: “We felt like he was the best guy available that had real game experience.” McVay was also asked whether he considered bringing in multiple kickers for a competition, the way the Rams did with Karty and Mevis last season. And he chose not to. The plan is to give Grupe the job and let him do his thing.

I actually think that’s the right call, and here’s why. Kicking is as mental as it is physical. If you bring a kicker in and tell him he’s auditioning every single day, you’re adding pressure to a position that already lives on pressure. Grupe knows he’s the guy. He’s going to step onto the SoFi field on Sunday knowing the job is his. That matters.

Now let me be honest about the risk, because I promised you the whole story.

There’s a fascinating parallel here that I don’t think enough people are talking about. Last season, Harrison Mevis came in midseason and was nearly perfect: 12 of 13. Everyone thought the kicking problem was solved. Then this season, he went five of nine. Blake Grupe is coming off his own nearly perfect stretch — 11 of 11 in Indianapolis. But that’s a small sample. Before that, he went 18 of 26 in New Orleans. So the Rams are making a bet. They’re betting that the Colts version of Grupe is the real one, and the 2025 Saints version was the outlier. That’s a reasonable bet. His first two seasons in New Orleans support it. But it is still a bet.

And there’s one more thing to remember. An 81.9 percent career field goal rate is solid. It’s not elite. This isn’t a kicker who’s going to make you forget about the problem overnight. What he gives the Rams is experience — 105 career attempts, a 60-yard leg, and three-plus seasons of NFL pressure kicks. Mevis had 22 regular-season attempts in his career. Grupe has nearly five times that. That’s the trade the Rams made: proven experience for upside and youth.

Now let’s get to the part of this story that everyone online is debating: Bubba Ventrone.

Ventrone is the Rams’ special teams coordinator. He was hired this past offseason after spending 2023 through 2025 as the special teams coordinator for the Cleveland Browns, and before that, 2018 through 2022 with the Indianapolis Colts. And yes, here’s an interesting connection: Ventrone and Grupe both have Colts history — though not at the same time. Ventrone left Indianapolis after 2022, and Grupe didn’t arrive there until late 2025.

The Rams hired Ventrone because of what happened last season. In December of 2025, after that 38-37 overtime loss in Seattle — a game where the Rams gave up a fourth-quarter punt-return touchdown to Rashid Shaheed — the Rams fired special teams coordinator Chase Blackburn. Ben Kotwica took over for the rest of the season. Then in the offseason, McVay brought in Ventrone to fix the unit.

So after Monday night, a lot of fans started asking whether Ventrone should be next. And McVay shut that down immediately. Here’s exactly what he said: “I have a lot of confidence in Bubba Ventrone. There’s no changes that are gonna be made there. We’re gonna keep working.” He also said: “This isn’t the first time that we’ve had adversity,” and that it’s “the first time that we’ve had some different things that I have to do a better job of.”

Notice what McVay did there. He took responsibility himself. He protected his coordinator. And he made one decisive personnel move instead of blowing up the whole unit five games into the season. You can agree or disagree with that, but it’s a clear plan. The kicker changes. The coaching staff stays.

But now we have to talk about the bigger picture, because this is where the story gets uncomfortable for the Rams.

According to the AP, Blake Grupe will be the Rams’ fifth kicker in four seasons since Matt Gay left — the same Matt Gay who kicked for the Super Bowl-winning team in 2021. According to Fox Sports, Grupe is the ninth kicker of the McVay era. And according to ESPN, the Rams have had 81 kicks blocked or missed since McVay took over in 2017 — including three blocked kicks since the start of the 2025 season. Two of those came in Philadelphia last year, when the Eagles blocked two Rams field goals. The third was Rousseau’s block on Monday night.

And it’s not just kicks. Sheil Kapadia of The Ringer pointed out that the Rams’ special teams came into Week 5 ranked 32nd in DVOA — dead last in the NFL. And he added that the Rams have been 26th or worse in four of the last five seasons. His conclusion: special teams are “Maybe the one thing McVay can’t figure out, and it continues to cost them games.”

Look at this season alone. Week 3 in Denver: special teams mistakes helped cost the Rams in a 30-26 loss. Week 4 in Philadelphia: the Rams won, but a long punt return set up an Eagles touchdown. Week 5 against Buffalo: Ray Davis returned the opening kickoff 70 yards — punter Ethan Evans had to make the tackle to save a touchdown — and the Rams allowed 219 kick return yards on seven returns. Then the missed 50-yarder. Then the block.

And look at last season. The 49ers beat the Rams 26-23 in overtime in October with the help of a string of Rams special teams mistakes. The Seahawks beat them 38-37 in overtime in December with a punt-return touchdown. And in the NFC Championship Game in Seattle — a 31-27 loss — a muffed punt was one of the biggest swings of the game.

So when you put it all together, here’s the honest truth. Harrison Mevis didn’t create this problem. He was part of it this season, absolutely. But the Rams’ special teams struggles started long before he arrived, and they include kick coverage, punt coverage, protection and ball security. Replacing the kicker addresses one piece. It doesn’t address the rest.

Now, to be fair, the Rams aren’t just swapping kickers and hoping for the best. According to reporting this week, they’ve signed special-teams-focused players like linebacker Grant Stuard, and they’ve moved special teams practice to the start of the day — when players are fresh and focused, instead of tacking it on at the end. That’s a small detail, but it sends a message. McVay is telling his roster that the third phase is not an afterthought anymore.

And there’s a real argument that Grupe himself makes the whole operation better. Think about the blocked kick for a second. A 48-yard attempt needs height to clear the line. If the trajectory is low, even a small breakdown in protection becomes a disaster. Grupe has hit a 60-yarder in an NFL game and went four for four from 50-plus in Indianapolis. A strong, consistent leg gives the protection more margin for error. It doesn’t solve everything, but it helps.

Now let’s talk about the other surprise exit this week — because the kicker wasn’t the only Ram who won’t be on the field Sunday.

After the game on Monday night, during the postgame jersey exchange, left tackle Alaric Jackson struck Bills linebacker Bradley Chubb in the head after a confrontation. Teammates separated them quickly, but the cameras caught everything. The NFL suspended Jackson two games for unsportsmanlike conduct. He appealed. And on Wednesday, according to the Rams’ official website, hearing officer Derrick Brooks reduced the suspension to one game.

So Jackson will miss Sunday’s game against Arizona, and David Quessenberry will start at left tackle. That’s a real loss. Jackson is the Rams’ starting left tackle, the man protecting Matthew Stafford’s blind side. On a night when Stafford just threw 54 passes without an interception, losing your left tackle is not ideal. And I’ll say this clearly: whatever was said on the field, you can’t do that. The Rams already lost the game. They didn’t need to lose a starter too.

The good news is that it’s only one game, and the matchup helps. Arizona’s defense has struggled this season, and Quessenberry is a veteran who’s been in this system. But keep an eye on Stafford’s protection on Sunday. If the Cardinals can get pressure on the left side, that’s where it’ll come from.

And one more injury note while we’re here. McVay confirmed that the Rams will not open Myles Garrett’s 21-day practice window this week. Here’s what he said: “We will not, not this week.” He said Garrett is “making good progress,” that the knee surgery “went really well,” and that his status is “week-to-week.” He also added an important line: “There’s a difference between return to play and return to performance.” The good news is that offensive lineman Keagen Trost, who’s been on injured reserve with a hamstring injury since the preseason, is expected back this week. McVay said he expects him “to be back out there this week.”

Now let’s look at Sunday, because this is where Blake Grupe’s Rams career begins.

The Arizona Cardinals come to SoFi Stadium on Sunday, October 18th, at 1:05 Pacific time on FOX. Arizona is 1-4. The Rams are 2-3. And according to FanDuel’s line as reported by Sports Illustrated, the Rams are 12.5-point favorites, with an over/under of 52.5.

But here’s the twist that every Rams fan should hear. Sports Illustrated’s Iain MacMillan actually picked the Cardinals to cover that spread. And his reason? The Rams’ special teams. He called them the worst in the league and said that’s exactly the kind of weakness that could give Arizona a chance to keep it close. That’s the reputation this unit has right now. When a betting analyst picks a 1-4 team to stay within two scores because of your special teams, you know it’s a national storyline.

So what do I want to see from Grupe on Sunday? Three things.

Number one: the extra points. The Rams scored five touchdowns on Monday night. If this offense keeps scoring like that, Grupe could have four or five extra-point attempts on Sunday. Those need to be automatic. He’s 96 of 98 in his career — so this should be his strength.

Number two: one confident kick from 40-plus. Every one of Mevis’s misses this season came from 40 yards or longer. That’s the range that’s been haunting the Rams. If Grupe drills one from 45 early, the whole sideline exhales. Confidence is contagious, and so is doubt.

Number three: the operation. Clean snap, clean hold, clean protection, good height on the ball. If the Rams go a full game without a single special teams question, that’s a win in itself. That’s how you start rebuilding trust.

And the offense can make Grupe’s first game a lot easier. Matthew Stafford leads the NFL with 1,572 passing yards through five games, according to Fox Sports, and on Monday night he finally played a game without an interception. If the Rams keep finishing drives the way they did against Buffalo — five touchdowns, every one from five yards or closer — Grupe’s day might be mostly extra points. That’s the ideal debut for a new kicker: a few routine kicks to find his rhythm with the snapper and holder before the pressure kicks arrive.

And it’s not just Grupe on display Sunday. Ventrone’s coverage units need a clean game too. After allowing 219 kick return yards on Monday, the Rams can’t afford to hand Arizona short fields. The Cardinals’ best path to an upset is exactly what MacMillan said: special teams.

Let’s zoom out on the schedule, too, because it matters for how much pressure Grupe faces right away. According to Fox Sports, the Rams’ next five opponents — Arizona, Las Vegas, the Chargers, Washington and Arizona again — have a combined record of 5-14-1. That’s a stretch where the Rams should be favored most weeks. In theory, that gives Grupe a chance to settle in before the bye, without a playoff-level kick on the line every Sunday. But the Rams also can’t afford to drop any of those games. The 49ers are 5-0 and have already beaten every other team in the NFC West. If the Rams want to get back into the division race, this five-game stretch has to go their way. And close games in that stretch might come down to — you guessed it — a field goal.

Now let me give you my verdict on the move itself.

Was releasing Mevis the right decision? I think it was. I’ll admit it’s painful, because Mevis was a great story and his playoff run last season was clutch. But the Rams are a team with Super Bowl expectations — the Super Bowl is at SoFi this season — and they can’t spend the next month hoping a kicker who’s five of nine finds his rhythm. The fact that every miss came from 40-plus is a pattern, not bad luck. And Grupe brings more experience, a stronger long-range track record, and a recent perfect stretch. On paper, it’s an upgrade at the position.

Was it enough? No. And I don’t think McVay believes it is either. The kicker change was the fastest, most visible move the Rams could make. The bigger work — coverage, protection, consistency across the whole unit — has to happen on the practice field, week after week. McVay is betting that Ventrone can lead that work. That bet will be judged over the next five games.

Let me also give you my grades on the week’s moves. Releasing Mevis and signing Grupe: B-plus. It’s decisive and reasonable, though it doesn’t fix the larger problems on its own. Keeping Ventrone: incomplete. It might be the right call, but it depends entirely on what the unit does next. Moving special teams practice to the start of the day: A. It’s a small change that shows real priority. And Alaric Jackson’s postgame incident: an F. A starting left tackle can’t put himself in that position, especially after a loss when the team needs everyone available.
