And second — this is the big one — Aaron Donald sounds closer to his season debut than any of us expected. McVay basically laid out exactly what needs to happen over the next few days, and if the legend checks those boxes, we could see number 99 on the field against the Giants on Monday Night Football. Oh, and McVay also told us the Rams are never playing in Australia again — his words, not mine. There is a LOT to unpack today, so let's get into all of it.

Let's start where McVay started: the injury report. And I'm not going to lie to you — for a defense that just lost its superstar pass rusher, this list is uncomfortably long.

Here's the rundown from practice. Kam Curl is limited with the ankle he's been nursing since the opener. Omar Speights is limited with his hip. Nate Landman is limited with his shoulder. Kam Kinchens did not practice at all with a hamstring injury. And Davante Adams got his normal veteran rest day — that one's routine, nothing to read into.

But look at the pattern there. Curl and Kinchens are two of your key safeties. Landman and Speights are your inside linebackers. That is the entire middle of this defense — the communication hub, the tackling core, the guys responsible for cleaning up everything — all banged up at the same time, in a week where the pass rush is already missing Myles Garrett. The limited tags aren't panic-worthy on their own; limited on Thursday often becomes full-go by the weekend. But Kinchens sitting out completely with a hamstring is worth monitoring all week, because soft-tissue injuries and short turnarounds are a bad combination, and safety depth gets thin very fast.

Now — the Myles Garrett update. And honestly, this was the most encouraging thirty seconds of the entire press conference. McVay said the surgery went well and Garrett is feeling good. Then he gave us the detail that tells you everything: Garrett was on crutches one day, and walking without them the next. And McVay, with a smile, said he's healing fast — "like Wolverine."

Now let's keep our feet on the ground, because McVay immediately did exactly that. When he was asked for a timeline, he said he's reluctant to put one on it. The four-game minimum on injured reserve stands — that hasn't changed, and it was never going to. And McVay was clear about the philosophy: they are not going to rush him back. They want Garrett back on the practice field able to do everything necessary to perform at the level he's accustomed to — not at eighty percent, not managing the knee week to week like they tried before Melbourne. We saw how that experiment ended. Zero tackles, zero pressures, and a trip to the operating room. This time they're doing it right, and as frustrating as patience is, that's the correct call for a player this franchise gave up Jared Verse and three premium picks to get. If you missed our full breakdown of that trade and the Maxx Crosby story behind it, go watch it after this video.

Alright — now the part of this press conference that had me leaning toward my screen. Aaron Donald.

A reporter asked McVay directly: what are the benchmarks for deciding whether Donald plays this week or next? And McVay's answer was refreshingly simple. It comes down to how he feels. He said Donald is "chomping at the bit" — that's a direct quote. The holdup hasn't been desire, and it hasn't been doubt about his level. Remember, McVay already said publicly that he expects Donald to play at the same level he did before retirement. The holdup has been reps — specifically, one-on-one reps. Because of the chaos of this early schedule — the Australia trip, the travel recovery, the shifted practice week — there just haven't been many opportunities for Donald to get those competitive settings against real bodies.

But here's why my ears perked up. McVay said the team is hopeful Donald gets good work in these next two practices. And then he said this: if Donald says he's ready to go, "we'll be excited to see him rock."

That is not a coach preparing you for another absence, Rams fans. That is a coach leaving the door wide open. Read the sequence: good practice work Thursday, good work Friday, Donald tells them he's ready — and Aaron Donald makes his 2026 season debut on Monday Night Football, in prime time, with this defense desperate for exactly what he provides. Interior pressure. Pocket-collapsing, double-team-eating, game-wrecking interior pressure. The one thing that makes life easier for every edge rusher trying to replace Myles Garrett's production. I'm not going to promise you it happens, because the decision genuinely sits with Donald and his own readiness. But everything about McVay's tone said this is close.

And think about what Donald's presence would mean for the young edge group we broke down earlier this week. Byron Young, Josaiah Stewart, Desjuan Johnson — maybe even Wesley Bailey — are all being asked to replace Garrett's impact by committee. Every one of those jobs gets easier the moment offenses have to slide their protection toward Aaron Donald. One-on-ones open up. The math changes. That's why this isn't just a feel-good storyline; it's the single biggest schematic swing available to this defense right now.

Now let's talk about the mindset stuff, because McVay gave us some genuinely funny and genuinely revealing moments. Asked how he wants the team to respond to the loss, he said he just wants the team to be present and move forward — and then he joked that he's trying to pull the Men in Black routine on Melbourne. Flash the little memory wiper, "I don't remember what happened, that was so long ago." And then came the line of the day: "We're not playing in Australia ever again, I hope." He immediately caught himself and clarified — it's not about the atmosphere, which he loved, and he knows the quote will get taken out of context. But the message underneath the joke was serious: chest out, chin up, eyes forward. Great focus, great energy, an edge in the preparation. That's the reset.

And there's a scheduling detail buried in there that tells you how weird this week actually is: McVay said it's Thursday, "but it's a Wednesday in our mind." Because of the Monday night game, the entire practice week is shifted back a day. Combine that with the Australia recovery and you understand why Donald's reps have been so hard to come by — this team hasn't had a normal week yet.

Now, the opponent. And I want you to pay attention to how McVay talked about this Giants defense, because there was zero coach-speak fluff in it — he sounded like a man who watched the film twice.

He said the Giants have great players across the board and do a great job schematically. He name-checked the pass rush first: Kayvon Thibodeaux, Brian Burns, and Abdul Carter — and said you feel their presence. Then he praised the rookie inside linebacker Arvell Reese, saying he was an excellent player in college and you can see why he was picked where he was. But the line that stuck with me was this one: McVay said he saw a defense that "played with their hair on fire." Great energy, great effort, a real style of play, connected across all three levels. And when someone suggested the Giants might have everyone's attention now, McVay pushed back with a phrase that might as well be the Rams' season motto after Melbourne: "Humility is a day away." The film speaks for itself, he said — playmakers all over the field, and excellent in the kicking game too.

So no, the Rams are not sneaking into an easy bounce-back spot. A fast, angry, connected front seven with three legitimate edge threats is exactly the wrong matchup for an offense that just failed to protect its rhythm for sixty minutes. Which brings us to the offensive questions.

McVay was asked about the communication problems both he and the staff mentioned after the opener — guys not being on the same page. His answer was honest: there are things about that night he'll never fully have answers on. But his focus is on the operation — the clarity coaches provide, the consistency that gives players a chance to bring the plan to life. Confidence, he said, is earned from preparation. That's where his whole concentration is this week.

On the run game, there was actually real positivity. Asked about Kyren Williams and the carry distribution, McVay said the backs did a good job with what they could control, that the line created good space and movement — and he specifically praised the interior trio of Coleman Shelton, Steve Avila, and Kevin Dotson, plus Alaric Jackson and Warren McClendon on some of the inside combinations, along with the tight ends. But he didn't sugarcoat the bigger issue: the offense never got any continuity or rhythm. So few plays in the first half, then the missed opportunity at the goal line, and suddenly you're one possession into the second half in a completely different game. His words: consistency is the truest measurement of performance. Stringing positive plays together, run and pass — that's what good offenses do, and that's what they're chasing this week.

And on Matthew Stafford, McVay shut the door on any concern before it could even form. He said that game was so unique that digging into Stafford's performance is wasting emotional energy. There is nobody he believes in more to lead and play the quarterback position. And then he zoomed out into something bigger: whether you're Matthew Stafford or any great in any arena, you're going to have off days — how you handle adversity is what separates successful people from everyone else. And Stafford, he said, has been successful precisely because he handles those moments. That's your franchise quarterback's coach telling you: the film is burned, we move on.

One more human moment before we wrap, because I loved this. McVay was asked about Mike LaFleur — his former offensive coordinator and one of his closest friends — getting his first win as head coach of the Cardinals. McVay lit up. He said he wasn't surprised, that he watched the whole game, that the Cardinals looked good in all three phases — and that seeing LaFleur's parents nervous in the stands, and his wife and kids celebrating, made it an awesome day. Yes, the Cardinals are a division rival, and yes, McVay joked he hopes LaFleur doesn't tear his plantar fascia celebrating like McVay himself did last year. But it was a genuine window into how connected this coaching fraternity is — he even mentioned how surreal it was watching the Vikings and Packers play each other, two more of his closest friends on opposite sidelines.

So here's where everything stands heading into Monday night. The Garrett news is as good as it could possibly be four days after surgery — but he's still out for at least four games, so it changes nothing about the immediate challenge. The middle of the defense is banged up, with Kinchens the name to watch on that hamstring. The Giants front is legitimately dangerous and playing with confidence. And hovering over all of it is the Aaron Donald question — two good practices and a yes from the man himself, and prime time gets a whole lot more interesting.
