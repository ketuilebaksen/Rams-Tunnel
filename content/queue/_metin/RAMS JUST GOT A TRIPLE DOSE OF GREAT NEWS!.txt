Part one: the clip, piece by piece

If you’ve watched any of our fake-clip breakdowns, you’ll recognize the template immediately.

The opening hook. It starts with a “countdown” of numbers: six to ten weeks, four 2027 draft picks, a million dollars of cap space, zero first-round picks. Some of those numbers are real, and we’ll get to them. It’s a smart opening, because real numbers make the rest sound real.

The fake date. It says this happened “live on ESPN First Take, Friday, November 6th.”

The family story. This time, the narrator was sweeping his porch, with his phone leaning against a flower pot, and swept dust onto his own shoes when he heard the news.

The running joke. This time it’s a school pickup line. Downs is “the kid at the curb” holding a sign with his own name on it. The Colts are the school. The Rams are “a small hatchback at the back of the line” with no first-round pick in the trunk. And reality is a crossing guard with a whistle.

The Stephen A. Smith bit. As always, Stephen A. supposedly “shouted” that Schefter confirmed it and told the Rams to “close the purchase on Monday.”

The fake quote. And of course, it ends with the line every one of these clips uses: “I can confirm this trade is going to happen.”

The hedge. And then, just like the last several clips, the narrator keeps admitting it isn’t real: “expected, not official,” “nothing is signed,” “nothing is announced,” and “nobody reported that the Colts have agreed to anything at all.”

Here’s the simple test. If Adam Schefter had confirmed a Rams trade on live national TV, it would be on his X account in seconds, on ESPN’s homepage in minutes, and in every Rams beat writer’s column by the afternoon. I checked. It isn’t there.

And notice something else. The clip even admits that the Rams part of the story comes from “Schefter and through Schefter alone.” Then it says Schefter’s “reads have a habit of turning into headlines a week later.” That’s a clever way of saying, “I can’t show you this anywhere, but trust me.” Don’t.

Part two: what’s real in the clip

As always, the fake is built around real facts. Let’s give them credit, because this is how these clips trick people.

Josh Downs did ask to be traded. That’s real. ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler reported it, and Downs told ESPN he’s “open to whatever.” He’s 25 and in the final year of his rookie contract.

The Colts are in a fire sale, and they fired their GM. Also real. Indianapolis fired general manager Chris Ballard on October 19th after a 2-4 start. Since then, they’ve traded Quenton Nelson to Seattle and DeForest Buckner to San Francisco.

The Colts are waiting until Monday. Real, more or less. Jordan Schultz reported that the Colts plan to wait until after Week 9 to make more trades, because they think offers will go up closer to the deadline. So “back at the table Monday” is a fair way to describe it.

Teams have called about Downs. Real. ESPN’s reporting has the Steelers and Chiefs as the main interested teams. Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak also wrote that the Rams have called about Downs and Brian Thomas Jr., though he didn’t say where that came from. Calling about a player isn’t negotiating, but it’s the one real thread connecting the Rams to Downs.

Davante Adams is out six to ten weeks. Real. McVay said that after the hamstring injury on October 18th. The clip calls it a “thigh injury,” which is technically true, since your hamstring is in your thigh. And the latest is good news: McVay said Adams’ surgery “went really well” and that his recovery is trending toward the shorter end.

The Rams have four 2027 picks and no first. Real. The Browns own the Rams’ 2027 first from the Garrett trade, and the Chiefs own the 2027 third from the McDuffie trade. The Rams still have a 2027 second, fourth, fifth, and sixth, plus a 2028 third.

The Rams’ cap space is tiny. Real, and the clip actually overstates it. It says about a million dollars. The most recent number I’ve seen from Athlon Sports, citing Over The Cap, is only a few thousand dollars. Either way, it’s the least room in the league or close to it.

Garrett is back. Real. McVay said Friday he expects Myles Garrett to play against Washington.

So, a pile of real facts, then a fake Schefter quote on top. Same recipe as always.

Part three: what’s wrong in the clip

Now let’s go through what doesn’t hold up.

The Schefter quote. There’s no record of Schefter saying this anywhere. The real Schefter reporting on Downs is about his price, not his destination: the Colts want a third-round pick, though a fourth might get it done. That’s it. No Rams, no Forbes, no “I can confirm.”

The Stephen A. Smith reaction. No record of that either.

The Raiders. At the end, the clip says Downs is “also linked to the Raiders” and that it covers that “in a separate video.” That’s another tell. These channels make the same fake clip for multiple teams, so every fan base thinks its team is about to land the player. The real reporting has named the Steelers and Chiefs as the main suitors, not the Rams or the Raiders.

The salary contradiction. And here’s the best part. The clip itself caught this one. The fake Schefter quote says Downs’ “rookie contract fits in the Rams’ cap without moving other salaries.” Then, in the very next sentence, it says Forbes “goes along to balance the salary.” Those two things can’t both be true. If Downs fits without moving salary, you don’t need to send Forbes to balance it. If you need Forbes to balance it, then Downs doesn’t fit without moving salary.

The narrator even admits he “cannot make them hold hands.” That’s because a real insider wouldn’t contradict himself in back-to-back sentences. It’s a sign the script was stitched together from different articles.

Part four: where the Forbes idea actually came from

The Forbes part of this fake trade isn’t random. It comes from real analysis, and that’s worth explaining.

A few days ago, ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler named Emmanuel Forbes Jr. as a possible trade candidate. His reasoning was that Forbes “has lost his starting job due to the additions of Trent McDuffie and Jaylen Watson.” Then Athlon Sports ranked possible Rams moves, and trading Forbes came out as the most likely. Forbes has a $2.85 million cap hit, and per Athlon’s math, trading him would raise the Rams’ cap space to about $2.86 million. That’s room for a cheap receiver.

So the idea of “trade Forbes, get a receiver” is a real analyst idea. The fake clip took that idea, attached it to Downs, added a fourth-round pick, and put Schefter’s name on it.

And here’s the real problem with it. Fowler’s reasoning was based on Forbes being a backup behind McDuffie and Watson. That’s no longer the situation.

Jaylen Watson is out for the season with a shoulder injury and headed to injured reserve. McVay called it “a big blow.” Trent McDuffie missed every practice this week with a back injury and was listed as questionable for Washington. Safety Kam Curl was questionable too. Josh Wallace was set to start in Watson’s place, and Forbes moved up to the number three cornerback, against Washington, the team that drafted him in the first round in 2023.

So the Rams would be trading away one of their few healthy cornerbacks in the same week their secondary got thinner. That’s solving one problem by creating another.

My take, and this is not a report: if the Rams trade Forbes this week, they’d almost have to bring in another cornerback at the same time. And with almost no cap space, that’s a lot of moving pieces for a team that’s barely made a move all season.

Part five: the real Josh Downs, and whether he fits

Now let’s set the fake clip aside, because Downs himself is a legitimate trade target, and he’s worth understanding.

Who he is. The Colts drafted Downs in the third round in 2023. As a rookie, he caught 68 passes, a Colts record for a first-year player. His best season was two years ago: 72 catches for 803 yards. For his career, he has 228 catches, 2,470 yards, and 12 touchdowns.

This season. Through eight games, he has 30 catches for 330 yards and a touchdown. His catch rate entering Week 8 was a career-low 56.5 percent. The Colts are 2-6, so the whole offense has struggled.

What he does well. The clip is right about one thing: Downs runs quick routes inside. He’s a slot receiver who separates fast, wins against man coverage, and gives his quarterback an easy throw when the pressure is coming. He’s also returned punts.

The concern. Blocking. McVay asks his receivers to block, a lot. ESPN’s Ben Solak, who proposed a Downs-to-the-Rams trade earlier this week, wrote that his “biggest hang-up in trading Downs to anyone is that he has to be protected from blocking responsibilities.” Solak thinks the Rams’ heavy use of three-tight-end sets could let Downs line up where he isn’t asked to block.

The real proposal. Solak’s deal was Downs plus a 2027 fifth-round pick to the Rams, for a 2028 third. That’s a real ESPN analyst’s idea, not a report. Notice it doesn’t include Forbes at all.

The real price. Schefter reported the Colts want a third, maybe a fourth. With the Steelers and Chiefs interested and the Colts waiting until Monday to drive up the bidding, my guess is the price goes up, not down.

Does he fix the Rams’ problem? Here’s my concern, and this is my analysis, not reporting. The Rams’ biggest offensive issue right now isn’t short completions. It’s explosive plays. Per Grisak, the Rams’ explosive pass play rate through eight weeks was 4.1 percent, compared with 17.5 percent at this point last year. Since Adams got hurt, they’d had exactly one completion over 20 yards. Stafford’s average depth of target over the last four weeks ranked 25th of 27 quarterbacks.

Downs helps you move the chains on third down. He doesn’t really stretch the field. So if the Rams pay a third-round pick for him, they’re getting a reliable slot receiver, but not the deep threat they’re really missing. That’s why I think Brian Thomas Jr. is the better fit on paper, even though he’d cost more.

Part six: can the Rams even afford Downs?

Let’s do the money one more time, because it’s the most important part of any Rams trade this year.

The Rams have almost no cap space, a few thousand dollars per the most recent Athlon number. Downs is on a third-round rookie contract, so his salary is small. I couldn’t find the exact amount left on his 2026 salary, so I’m not going to make one up. But even a small rookie salary is likely more than a few thousand dollars, so the Rams would still have to create room.

They have a couple of ways to do it. A Kyren Williams restructure has been estimated to free up around $3.5 million. Or they could trade a player with salary, like Forbes. But as we just covered, trading Forbes now has a real football cost.

And there’s one more consideration. Downs is in the last year of his rookie deal. If the Rams trade a third-round pick for him, they’d want to keep him beyond this season, which means paying him a new contract next year. Adams is 33. Puka Nacua is the long-term number one. A young slot receiver could make sense as the long-term number two or three. But it’s a commitment, not just a rental.

So could the Rams do it? Yes, with a restructure. Would it be a “cheap and already in motion” move like the fake clip says? No. Nothing about the Rams’ cap situation is cheap or easy right now.

Quick detour: the rest of the Rams’ real options

Since Downs is only one name, here’s a fast look at the rest of the real board, based on actual analysis this week. Again, none of these are reports of talks.

Dyami Brown, Commanders. ESPN’s Aaron Schatz named him as a possible Rams target, and Athlon pointed out he’s cheap, with a $1.22 million base salary and only about $250,000 in dead money for Washington. He’s got speed, with a 48-yard catch this season, and he returns kicks. But he was ruled out of today’s game with a calf injury.

Brian Thomas Jr., Jaguars. The deep threat, and the one ESPN’s Bill Barnwell called the “exciting” option. Benched and struggling this year, and likely the most expensive in picks.

Jauan Jennings, Vikings. Physical and a willing blocker, which McVay loves. But per Athlon, he has just six catches for 54 yards in six games, and his contract is complicated.

Jerry Jeudy, Browns. Not available. Tom Pelissero reported Thursday that Cleveland expects to keep him unless it gets a “truly crazy offer.” And the Browns own the Rams’ 2027 first, so they have no reason to help the Rams win.

Donte Jackson, Chargers. A veteran cornerback idea from CBS Sports’ JP Acosta. Experienced, with 23 career interceptions, but he’s missed three straight games with a hamstring injury, and the Chargers have said they aren’t sellers.

And don’t forget what’s happening around the NFC West. The 49ers already added DeForest Buckner, and Seattle added Quenton Nelson, both from the Colts. That’s real pressure on the Rams to do something. But McVay’s public stance, according to ESPN’s Sarah Barshop, is that the Rams would rather count on players coming back from injury. As he put it earlier this week: “Hopefully, some of those trades will be guys getting back from injury.”

So here’s my realistic ranking for Tuesday. Most likely: no major move. Next most likely: a small move for a cheap receiver or a healthy cornerback, paired with a restructure. Least likely: anything that looks like the fake clip.

Part seven: answering the clip’s skeptic questions

The clip set up some skeptic objections, so let me give you my honest answers to the real questions here.

“Isn’t it a good sign that Downs asked to be traded?” For the teams interested in him, yes. A player who wants out gives the buyer leverage. But the Colts are using that leverage too, by waiting until Monday and letting teams bid against each other. A trade request doesn’t make him cheap. It makes him available.

“Isn’t it urgent because Adams is hurt?” Less urgent than it looks. Adams is trending toward the short end of six to ten weeks, which could put him back around the end of November. A trade for Downs gives you a few weeks of a fill-in, then a third receiver for December and January. That’s useful, but it’s not a “close the purchase on Monday” emergency.

“Doesn’t the McVay fit make sense?” Partly. He’s a good route runner and gets open fast. But the blocking concern is real, and he doesn’t solve the deep-ball problem.

“So will it happen?” Nobody credible has reported that it will. The real reporting has the Steelers and Chiefs as the main suitors. If the Rams make a move by Tuesday, my guess is it’ll be small, it’ll come with a restructure, and it might even be at cornerback instead of receiver.
