So let’s ask the question that actually matters now: was standing still at the deadline the right call? Let’s grade it.

Part one: quick update on the original video

The original video is based on a November 13 article. A lot has changed since then, so let me update a few things.

It said Puka wouldn’t be placed on injured reserve. That changed. The Rams placed Puka on IR on November 12. McVay explained it, saying, “We needed the roster spot,” and that Puka was never going to come back before then anyway.

Then, on November 19, the NFL suspended Puka for three games for violating the personal conduct policy. He’s serving it while on IR, so he isn’t expected to miss any extra games. His first possible game back is December 20 against Dallas, if he’s healthy.

Davante Adams is still on IR. McVay said, “After Green Bay is a realistic timeline.”

Jordan Whittington is on IR with a back issue.

So the receiver situation is actually more set now than when that article came out. The Rams know Puka is out through the 49ers game. They know Adams is probably out for the Packers. And they’re planning around that.

Part two: the McConkey inquiry, one more time

Let’s quickly go back over the McConkey story, so everyone’s on the same page.

According to ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler, at least one NFL team asked the Chargers about McConkey before the deadline. People around the league believed it was the Rams. The Chargers reportedly weren’t interested.

Why would the Rams call? It makes sense. McConkey is a precise route runner who can get open all over the field. In McVay’s offense, he’d give Stafford another reliable target. And at the time, Puka had just gone down with MCL and PCL sprains plus turf toe, and Adams was already out with his hamstring.

But a good reason to call doesn’t mean the other team wants to sell. And the Chargers apparently didn’t. McConkey is one of their best young players, on a rookie deal, and they’re competing. There was never much chance they’d trade him to the team that shares their stadium in the middle of the season.

So the inquiry was real, or at least widely believed. The trade was never close.

Part three: why the Rams couldn’t go all-in

Here’s why standing still wasn’t just about the Chargers saying no.

The Rams had already gone all-in. They made big trades for Myles Garrett and Trent McDuffie. Those deals were about winning now, and they cost future draft picks.

Here’s what the Rams no longer have: their 2027 first-round pick, which went to Cleveland. Their 2027 third-round pick, which went to Kansas City. Their 2028 second-round pick. And their 2027 seventh.

What they still have: a 2027 second, plus fourth-, fifth-, and sixth-round picks, and a 2028 third.

And the money was tight. Before the deadline, Over The Cap had the Rams about $600,000 over the salary cap. Restructures for Kyren Williams and Kam Curl created about $3 million in space. That’s enough to keep running the team, but not enough to take on a big salary without sending one back.

So even if the Chargers had been willing, a deal for a young star receiver would have cost a lot more than the Rams could easily give. And it could have hurt the team for years.

McVay said it best earlier this month, talking about trades: “It kind of takes two.” You can make the call. But if the price is too high, or the other team won’t sell, there’s no deal.

Les Snead’s deadline history

To understand why the Rams made the call, you have to understand Les Snead.

Snead is one of the most aggressive general managers in the NFL. He traded for Jalen Ramsey in 2019. He traded for Matthew Stafford in 2021. He traded for Von Miller at the 2021 deadline, and that move helped the Rams win the Super Bowl that season. And this year, he went and got Myles Garrett and Trent McDuffie.

So when a star player might be available, Snead almost always checks. That’s who he is. The McConkey call fits that pattern perfectly.

But Snead also knows when to stop. He’s not going to pay any price just to make a splash. If the other team won’t sell, or the cost would hurt the team too much, he moves on.

That’s what happened here. And honestly, that’s what you want from a general manager. Aggressive, but not reckless.

What a McConkey trade might have cost

Let’s play a quick hypothetical. This is not a report. No price was ever reported, and the Chargers reportedly weren’t interested at all.

But if they had been, what would it have taken?

A young, productive receiver on a rookie contract is one of the most valuable things in the NFL. Teams usually want a first-round pick, or more, for a player like that.

And the Rams don’t have their 2027 first. It’s already gone to Cleveland. So the best they could have offered was a future first, like 2028, plus other picks or players.

Think about what that would mean. The Rams would be without two first-round picks in back-to-back years. For a team with a 38-year-old quarterback, that’s a big risk. When Stafford eventually retires, the Rams will need young talent and draft picks to rebuild around a new quarterback.

So even in the best-case scenario, the price would have been very high. That’s part of why standing still makes sense.

Part four: what happened after the deadline

Now here’s the best evidence we have.

Five days after the deadline, the Rams went to Arizona without Puka, without Adams, without Terrance Ferguson, and without Steve Avila. Then they lost two centers during the game.

And they won 34-6.

Here’s how:

The run game dominated. The Rams ran for 202 yards. Blake Corum had 100 yards and a touchdown. Ronnie Rivers had 81 yards and closed the game.

McVay changed the offense. According to ESPN’s Sarah Barshop and NFL Next Gen Stats, the Rams had used 11 personnel on 53% of their plays coming in. Against Arizona, they used it only three times. Instead, they ran 17 plays from 20 personnel, the “pony” look with two running backs, for 197 yards and a touchdown.

Stafford spread the ball. He went 18 of 26 for 249 yards and three touchdowns, throwing to nine different receivers.

The young receivers made plays. According to Blaine Grisak of Rams On SI, Xavier Smith, Konata Mumpfield, Tutu Atwell, and CJ Daniels each had a catch longer than 20 yards. Smith graded 92.6 and scored his first career touchdown. Mumpfield graded 80.6 with three catches for 81 yards.

The defense smothered Arizona. Just 123 total yards allowed, five sacks, and two interceptions.

So the Rams answered the question that the McConkey inquiry was trying to solve. They found a way to move the ball without their top receivers. At least for one game.

The rivals who did make moves

Here’s an interesting twist. Two of the Rams’ upcoming opponents actually did add receivers at the deadline.

The Kansas City Chiefs traded for Josh Downs. They sent the Colts a 2027 fourth-round pick and a 2027 fifth-round pick, and they got Downs plus a 2027 seventh. The Rams play the Chiefs right after the Packers.

The San Francisco 49ers traded a 2027 sixth-round pick for Keenan Allen. But Allen is facing a pending suspension of at least three games. The Rams play the 49ers on December 13.

So the Rams will face two teams that got stronger at receiver while the Rams stood still. That makes the next few weeks even more interesting.

If the Rams beat both of those teams with their current receiver group, the “standing still” decision will look great. If they lose to both, people will point to the deadline.

That’s why I keep saying the grade is incomplete.

Part five: would McConkey have changed Arizona?

Here’s a fun question. If the Rams had somehow landed McConkey, would the Arizona game have looked different?

This is not a report, it’s just my opinion.

Probably not by much. The Rams won by 28. The run game and the defense controlled everything. And McVay’s offense was built around two running backs and three tight ends, not around a star receiver.

Where McConkey would have helped is in a close game. A third-and-7 late in the fourth quarter, when you need a receiver who can beat man coverage. That’s when not having Puka or Adams can really hurt.

So the Arizona win doesn’t prove the Rams don’t need another receiver. It proves they can win without one against a team like Arizona. The bigger tests are coming: the Packers, the Chiefs, and the 49ers.

Part six: the case for standing still

Let me give you both sides. First, the case that the Rams made the right call.

One: the price. A young star receiver would have cost a big package. The Rams already traded their 2027 first. Giving up more future picks could have hurt them for years.

Two: the cap. The Rams were over the cap before restructures. Adding salary would have been very hard.

Three: help is coming. Adams’ realistic timeline is after Green Bay. Kobie Turner could be back for the Chiefs, according to SB Nation’s Turf Show Times. Terrance Ferguson’s practice window has been opened. And Puka could return December 20.

Four: the results. Four straight wins, including 34-6 in Arizona after the deadline.

Five: McVay’s track record. Over the previous three seasons, his teams went 23-7 after their bye weeks. He’s good at making do with what he has.

Part seven: the case against standing still

Now the other side.

One: the schedule. ESPN Analytics says the Rams have the toughest second-half schedule in the NFL. Arizona was 3-7. The Packers, Chiefs, and 49ers are a lot tougher.

Two: the receiver depth is young. Smith, Mumpfield, Atwell, and Daniels played well in Arizona. But now they’re on film, and better defenses will be ready for them.

Three: injuries keep coming. Shelton is out four to six weeks. McMahon is on IR. Avila is hurt. If the offensive line struggles, the run game could slow down, and then the passing game becomes even more important.

Four: the IR math. According to Turf Show Times, the Rams have only four IR return designations left. They need some of those for Adams, Puka, and Turner. If another receiver gets hurt, the depth gets very thin.

Five: Puka’s situation is more complicated now. With the suspension on top of the injury, his return isn’t just a medical question anymore.

So I understand fans who wanted the Rams to be more aggressive.

Part eight: my grade

So what’s my grade for the Rams’ deadline decision?

This is not a report, it’s my opinion: I’m giving it a B-plus, with an “incomplete” label.

Why a B-plus? Because the Rams protected their future, stayed within their cap situation, and still won convincingly right after the deadline. And they did make the call on McConkey. They didn’t just sit back. They tried, and when the price wasn’t there, they moved on.

Why “incomplete”? Because the real test hasn’t happened yet. If the Rams beat the Packers and the Chiefs with this receiver group, the grade goes up to an A. If the offense struggles and they drop two of the next three, people will look back at the deadline and wonder.

Part ten: now, the real good news before the Packers game

Okay, the headline promised good news. Here’s what’s real.

Good news one: the suspension doesn’t cost the Rams any games. Puka is serving it while on IR, during games he was already going to miss. On the field, the plan doesn’t change.

Good news two: help is on the way. Adams’ realistic timeline is after Green Bay, which could mean the Chiefs game. Turner could be back around then too. Ferguson’s window is open. And Garrett, Rivers, McDuffie, and Curl are already back.

Good news three: McVay has a new weapon. The pony formation gave the Rams almost 12 yards per play in Arizona. And McVay has had extra time during the bye to build new plays off it.

Good news four: the run game is historic. Over the last four games, the Rams have outrushed opponents 653 to 175. According to ESPN Research, that’s their biggest four-game rushing differential since 1984, when Eric Dickerson ran for 2,105 yards.

Good news five: the playoff odds look good. ESPN’s Football Power Index gives the Rams a 47.1% chance to make the playoffs. The Packers are at 33.3%.

That’s a lot of real good news. No fake trade required.

Part eleven: the bad news we can’t ignore

To be fair, here’s the bad news too.

The offensive line is thin. Coleman Shelton is out four to six weeks with a hamstring strain. Dylan McMahon is on IR with a partial patellar tear. Steve Avila is dealing with a hamstring injury. Beaux Limmer will likely play center.

The receivers are still missing their top two. Puka is out through at least the 49ers game. Adams is likely out for the Packers.

The kickoffs need work. Special teams got a B-minus in Arizona after two kickoffs missed their landing zone or the field of play. McVay said that “wasn’t a positive for us today.”

So it’s not all sunshine. But the good outweighs the bad right now.

Do the Rams already have their own McConkey?

Here’s a question I’ve been thinking about. This is not a report, just my opinion.

What made McConkey attractive was his route running. He’s quick, he’s precise, and he gets open in tight spaces.

Is there anyone on the Rams roster who could play a similar role?

Konata Mumpfield might be the closest. He’s a smart route runner who’s earned McVay’s trust. In Arizona, he had three catches for 81 yards and an 80.6 PFF grade. He won one-on-one matchups down the field.

Xavier Smith brings something different. He’s great after the catch, as we saw on his touchdown screen. His 92.6 grade in Arizona was the best of any Rams receiver.

Brennan Presley, on the practice squad, was Oklahoma State’s all-time leader in receptions. He’s small and quick. But he’s only played nine NFL snaps, so we don’t know yet.

None of them is McConkey. But together, they might be enough until Adams and Puka come back. And if one of them breaks out against the Packers, the whole deadline conversation changes.

Part twelve: how McVay can keep the passing game alive

Here’s what I expect McVay to do against the Packers to keep the passing game working. This is not a report. It’s my analysis.

More formations, not more receivers. Instead of asking young receivers to win one-on-one, McVay will use the pony look, three-tight-end sets, and motion to create easy throws.

Spread the ball. Stafford threw to nine different players in Arizona. Expect the same. Nobody needs ten targets. Everybody needs a few.

Lean on the run. The best way to help a thin receiver group is to make the defense worry about the run first.

Use the backs and tight ends as receivers. Corum caught three passes in Arizona. Higbee and Davis Allen both scored. Colby Parkinson graded 83.5.

Take a few deep shots off play-action. Mumpfield’s 44-yard catch and Atwell’s 31-yarder on fourth-and-6 both came when the defense was worried about the run.

That’s how you survive without a McConkey. You don’t replace one player. You change the whole plan.

The Stafford factor

There’s one more reason the Rams could afford to stand still: Matthew Stafford.

A lot of quarterbacks need a star receiver to look good. Stafford doesn’t. He’s 38, he’s in his 18th season, and he’s spent his whole career making receivers around him better.

In Arizona, his expected points added per pass was 0.63, which ranked second in the league that week behind only Brock Purdy, according to Blaine Grisak. He did that throwing to a group that didn’t include Puka or Adams.

McVay said before that game that Stafford’s “steadiness is the key thing,” and that he’s “truly an extension of the coaching staff.” When your quarterback can coach up young receivers in the huddle, you don’t need to trade for a star every time someone gets hurt.

That’s a big part of why I think the Rams’ decision made sense. They have a quarterback who can make it work.

Part thirteen: the defense makes it easier

One more thing the original video got right: the defense can take pressure off the offense.

The Rams invested a lot in Garrett and McDuffie. And it’s paying off. Against Washington, Garrett had three sacks. Against Arizona, McDuffie returned from his back injury and graded 85.3.

And it’s not just the stars. Undrafted rookie Wesley Bailey graded 93.0 in Arizona. According to Grisak, he’s the first undrafted rookie since at least 2000 with an interception, multiple sacks, and multiple forced fumbles in one season. Kamren Kinchens had a huge interception. Aaron Donald got his first sack of the season.

If the defense keeps games close, or keeps giving the offense short fields, the Rams don’t need a superstar receiver. They just need the offense to not make mistakes.

How the deadline will be judged in January

Let me zoom out one more time.

Trade deadline decisions are usually judged at the end of the season, not right after the deadline.

If the Rams make the playoffs and Adams, Puka, and Turner are healthy for the run, nobody will remember the McConkey call. They’ll say Snead was smart to keep his picks and trust his roster.

If the Rams miss the playoffs because the passing game fell apart in December, people will say the Rams should have done more.

And remember, if the Rams make the playoffs, they get two extra IR return designations, according to Turf Show Times. That could help them bring back players for the postseason, which makes making the playoffs even more valuable.

So really, the deadline grade will come down to one thing: do the Rams make the playoffs? Right now, ESPN gives them a 47.1% chance. The Packers game could push that number a lot in either direction.
