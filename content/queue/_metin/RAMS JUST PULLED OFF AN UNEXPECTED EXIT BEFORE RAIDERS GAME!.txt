Now let’s talk about the trade market, because this is where things get interesting.

ESPN’s Dan Graziano reported this week that the Rams “will at least explore the possibility of trading for a wide receiver in the wake of the Davante Adams injury.” According to Pro Football Rumors’ Sam Robinson, the Rams appear to be considering “short-term help.” That’s real reporting. But it’s not a report of a trade — and McVay himself said on Wednesday that adding an outside receiver is “not something that we’ve talked about right now.”

Now here’s the part that changes everything. According to Pro Football Rumors, citing Over the Cap, the Rams have just under 990,000 dollars in cap space.

Let me explain why that matters so much. When you trade for a player in the middle of the season, you take on the rest of his salary for that year. So if a player is making a big salary, the Rams would need cap room to absorb it. And right now, they basically don’t have any.

That means the Rams have three options if they want to add a receiver. One: trade for a player with a small salary. Two: send salary back to the other team in the trade — a player-for-player deal. Or three: create cap room by restructuring someone’s contract. Pro Football Rumors mentioned all three possibilities.

And here’s a detail a lot of fans don’t know. NFL players are paid their base salary in weekly checks over the 18 weeks of the regular season. When a player is traded in the middle of the year, his new team only pays — and only counts against the cap — the checks that are left. So a player’s full salary isn’t what matters at the deadline. What matters is the part of it that’s still left to pay.

Let’s do some quick math with Jerry Jeudy, because his number is public. According to Pro Football Rumors, his 2026 base salary is 1.5 million dollars. Divide that by 18 weeks, and you get roughly 83,000 dollars per week. If he were traded with about 10 weeks left in the season, the new team would owe him roughly 830,000 dollars. That’s under the Rams’ 990,000 dollars in space. So a player like Jeudy could fit without the Rams moving any money at all. That’s my own rough math, not a team figure — but it shows why low-salary players are the realistic targets.

And restructures are the other tool. When a team restructures a contract, it turns part of a player’s base salary into a signing bonus. That bonus gets spread out over future years of the contract, which frees up cap space right now. The Rams have done it before, and they could do it again if they find the right player. But every restructure pushes money into future years, and this team has already pushed a lot of chips into the middle of the table. So Les Snead has to be careful.

And that money problem makes some of the names you’ve been hearing a lot less realistic than they sound.

Let’s start with the biggest one in this week’s transcripts: Chris Godwin.

Godwin is a Super Bowl champion, and at his best, he was one of the best slot receivers in football. And Tampa Bay is in trouble. According to Athlon Sports, via Yahoo, the Bucs are 1-5. Baker Mayfield has missed three straight games with a thumb injury, and undrafted rookie Jalon Daniels has been starting. Mike Evans is no longer with the team. So it’s easy to see why people think Tampa Bay might sell.

Here’s what Godwin’s season looks like. According to Athlon, he’s healthy, and through six games he has 23 catches for 216 yards on 30 targets. His 2024 season ended with an ankle injury, so the fact that he’s playing every week is good news. But according to Athlon, he’s no longer the top target in Tampa Bay. Emeka Egbuka leads the Bucs with 49 targets. So the Rams would be trading for a receiver who’s become the second option on a 1-5 team.

But here’s the problem. According to Athlon, citing Spotrac, Godwin signed a three-year, 66 million dollar contract in March 2025, and his cap hit this season is 33.7 million dollars. Next year, it’s 29.9 million.

Now think about the Rams’ cap space: under one million dollars. Even halfway through the season, Godwin’s remaining salary would be far more than the Rams can absorb. To make it work, the Rams would have to send a big contract back to Tampa Bay or restructure deals all over the roster. And then they’d be stuck with a nearly 30 million dollar cap hit in 2027 for a receiver who had 216 yards in six games this year.

Some videos are saying the Rams could get Godwin for a “bargain fifth-round pick.” Even if Tampa Bay agreed to that, the money doesn’t work — unless Tampa Bay agreed to pay most of his remaining salary. And that does happen sometimes. Remember the Von Miller trade in 2021? Denver agreed to pay most of Miller’s remaining salary to make the deal work for the Rams, and in return the Broncos got a second- and a third-round pick. Teams will eat salary if they get better draft picks back. But the Rams don’t have many good picks left to offer, and Godwin’s 29.9 million dollar cap hit next year would still be a problem. That’s why I’d put Godwin at the bottom of the realistic list.

Now let’s talk about Brian Thomas Jr., because his name keeps coming up.

Here, the money is actually much easier. According to Spotrac, his 2026 base salary is about 1.075 million dollars, and his cap hit is about 4 million. That’s still more than the Rams’ current cap space, so they’d need a small adjustment somewhere. But it’s workable.

The problem with Thomas is the price in draft picks. As a rookie in 2024, he had 1,282 yards and 10 touchdowns. But according to RotoWire, through six games this season he has nine catches for 105 yards and one touchdown. And according to Tom Pelissero, as reported by Bleacher Report, the Jaguars have “shown no interest” in trading him, even though they keep getting calls.

But for a former first-round pick who had 1,282 yards as a rookie, I can’t see Jacksonville accepting a third-round pick two years from now — especially when they don’t seem to be shopping him. Now, Josh Downs — and this one has a fun Rams connection.

According to Heavy, Downs’ uncle is Dre’ Bly, the Super Bowl-winning cornerback. Bly won that ring with the St. Louis Rams after the 1999 season. And Downs’ brother, Caleb Downs, is a first-round rookie with the Dallas Cowboys. So football runs in that family.

Here’s why Downs is realistic. According to Heavy, the Colts are 2-4 and recently fired general manager Chris Ballard, with Ed Dodds now serving as interim GM. When a front office changes, players drafted by the old regime can become available. Downs is in the final year of his rookie deal, and according to Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak, as cited by Heavy, the Rams would only take on about 2 million dollars in salary in a trade. Grisak’s quote: “Cheap enough where they could make the salary work.”

On the field, Downs has 24 catches for 289 yards this season and 2,429 career receiving yards. According to Grisak, he had a 75.3 percent success rate against press coverage last season. Heavy proposed a 2027 fourth-round pick and a 2028 fourth-round pick. He was the 79th overall pick in 2023, he’s only 25, and The Athletic’s Dane Brugler wrote in his 2023 scouting report: “He is obviously undersized, but he has the catch radius of a bigger player.” Grisak suggested the Rams might even try to get it done with a 2027 fifth and a conditional Day 3 pick in 2028.

And there’s another Colt worth mentioning. According to Pro Football Rumors, Keenan Allen is now on that Colts team in transition. He’s 34, and he’s on an expiring, low-cost deal. And here’s a fun twist: Allen spent most of his career with the Chargers — the Rams’ opponent next week. He’s not the player he was five years ago. But he’s a smart, sure-handed route runner who knows how to beat zone coverage — and he’d cost very little.

The same goes for Calvin Ridley in Tennessee. According to Pro Football Rumors, he’s 31 and also on an expiring, low-cost contract. Veterans like Allen and Ridley might not move the needle in the headlines, but they fit the Rams’ cap situation.

Now here’s a twist that most people haven’t considered: what if the Rams trade away a player to make room?

According to Pro Football Rumors, Fox Sports’ Eric Williams named tight end Colby Parkinson as a possible trade option. Parkinson is on a three-year, 22.5 million dollar deal with a 5.5 million dollar base salary this season, and his contract expires after the year. The Rams drafted tight end Max Klare in the second round this spring, and he’s seen as Parkinson’s eventual replacement.

But I’d be very careful here. Parkinson just caught the go-ahead touchdown against Arizona with 47 seconds left. With Terrance Ferguson on injured reserve, and with Adams out and Puka hurt, the tight ends are a huge part of this offense right now. Trading Parkinson would solve a money problem and create a football problem. Unless the Rams get a clearly better player back, I’d keep him.

There’s also the question of the receivers already on the roster. According to Pro Football Rumors, Tutu Atwell was reacquired from Miami in August, and he has three catches for 31 yards since coming back. Konata Mumpfield, a 2025 seventh-round pick, has eight catches for 127 yards and a touchdown. Pro Football Rumors suggested the Rams may not yet see Mumpfield as a reliable number-two receiver. But McVay clearly believes in him. According to The Sporting Tribune, McVay said this week: “He’s one of those guys that when he’s been counted on, he’s delivered.”

And remember what Mumpfield did the last time Puka was out. When Puka missed Weeks 2 and 3, Mumpfield stepped in, and in the Week 3 loss at Denver, he had four catches for 93 yards and a go-ahead touchdown. That’s the kind of game the Rams need from him this Sunday if Puka can’t play.

And McVay also mentioned the practice squad when he talked about receiver depth. According to the Rams’ official website, Brennan Presley is on the practice squad, and receivers Alex Bachman and rookie C.J. Daniels Jr. are on the active roster. Bachman was promoted from the practice squad earlier this month. None of them is a star. But in McVay’s system, receivers who know exactly where to be can make plays — especially when defenses are focused on stopping the run and the tight ends. If Puka is out, expect McVay to use more tight ends and more motion, and to spread the ball around to whoever is open rather than forcing it to one receiver.

That’s why the trade talk and the Sunday game are connected. If Mumpfield, Whittington, Atwell and the tight ends play well against the Raiders, the pressure to make a deal drops. If they struggle — especially if Puka misses time — Les Snead’s phone gets a lot busier before November 10th. In a way, this game is an audition for the whole receiver room.

So let me rank the realistic options, considering both the draft picks and the cap.

Number one: Josh Downs. About 2 million dollars in salary, a Day 3 price, and a front office in transition that might be ready to sell. He’s the cleanest fit.

Number two: Jerry Jeudy. A 1.5 million dollar salary and a Day 3 asking price. The only problem is that Cleveland is winning and may not want to sell.

Number three: a cheap veteran like Keenan Allen or Calvin Ridley. Low cost, low risk, and real experience.

Number four: Brian Thomas Jr. The salary works, but the draft price is too high and the Jaguars don’t seem interested.

And last: Chris Godwin. A great player at his best, but with a 33.7 million dollar cap hit and the Rams under one million in space, it just doesn’t make sense.

Now let me be clear about one more thing. One of the transcripts going around this week said a blockbuster trade feels “like an absolute certainty.” It isn’t. Nobody has reported that the Rams are close to any deal. Graziano said they’ll “explore the possibility.” McVay said they haven’t talked about it “right now.” And the trade deadline isn’t until November 10th. Anything can happen, but nothing is certain.

And think about the calendar. The Rams play the Raiders this Sunday, the Chargers on November 1st and Washington on November 8th. The deadline comes two days after that Washington game. So the front office will get to watch three more games before it has to decide anything. If the Rams win two or three of those, they’re back in the race, and a small move makes sense. If they lose two or three, they might decide to hold on to what’s left of their future picks. That’s why I don’t expect anything to happen quickly — unless an injury, like Puka’s calf, forces their hand.

Now let’s look ahead to Sunday, because whatever happens with trades, the Rams have to beat the Raiders.

According to the AP, the Raiders are 3-3 and have lost three straight after a 3-0 start. First-year head coach Klint Kubiak said: “That’s unacceptable, and that starts with me.” Kirk Cousins has 14 touchdown passes, tied for the league lead, and a 98.0 passer rating. But the Raiders have scored touchdowns on only 12 of 23 trips inside the red zone, and just four of 10 during their losing streak.

There are some Raiders injuries to watch, too. According to the Raiders’ official injury report, star tight end Brock Bowers was limited on Wednesday with hamstring and calf issues, and left tackle Kolton Miller was limited with a shoulder injury. Defensive tackle Tonka Hemingway didn’t practice because of a concussion. And receiver Dont’e Thornton Jr. returned to practice from injured reserve. If Bowers is limited, Cousins loses his favorite weapon. If Miller can’t go, that’s a big opportunity for Aaron Donald and the Rams’ pass rush.

According to Sports Illustrated’s Iain MacMillan, the Raiders rank 26th in net yards per play and 27th in opponent success rate, and MacMillan wrote: “It’s time to buy back in on the Rams.” According to Heavy, the Rams are now 3.5-point favorites — the line has moved since the Puka news.

The Rams’ injury situation is the big question. Puka is uncertain. Trent McDuffie didn’t practice Wednesday or Thursday with a groin injury, and Roundtable Sports reported he’s tracking toward missing the game. Linebacker Nate Landman is in the concussion protocol. According to The Sporting Tribune, veteran linebacker Eric Kendricks could be elevated from the practice squad to help.

But there’s good news, too. Brandon McManus makes his Rams debut in a dome. Colby Parkinson was a full participant in practice all week. And Aaron Donald, who came out of retirement in August, leads a defensive front that badly needs him with Turner and Ford out. According to The Sporting Tribune, Donald said recently: “It felt good just watching the film, looking more like myself.”

Here are my three keys for Sunday.

Key number one: protect the football. Stafford has thrown six interceptions this season, according to Sports Illustrated. With a thin receiver group, the Rams can’t afford to give the Raiders short fields.

Key number two: lean on the run game and the tight ends. If Puka is out or limited, Kyren Williams, Blake Corum, Parkinson and Tyler Higbee have to carry the offense. Kyren said after the Arizona loss that the team needs to stop “not starting fast.” Give him the ball early.

Key number three: make Las Vegas kick field goals. The Rams held Arizona to zero touchdowns in five red zone trips. If they do that again, they win.

My prediction: if Puka plays, Rams 27, Raiders 20. If he doesn’t, Rams 24, Raiders 20. I still think the Rams find a way.

So here’s my bottom line.

There was no shocking release or trade before the Raiders game. The real “exits” this week were injuries — Adams, Ford and Turner to injured reserve, and Puka leaving practice early on Thursday. And while the Rams are expected to explore the receiver market before November 10th, the biggest obstacle isn’t just draft picks. It’s money. With under a million dollars in cap space, the Rams can’t simply trade for a big contract like Chris Godwin’s. The realistic targets are cheap ones — Josh Downs, Jerry Jeudy or a low-cost veteran — or a creative deal where salary goes both ways.

So the next time you see a video promising a blockbuster, ask one question: how would the Rams pay for it? That question rules out a lot of the noise.
