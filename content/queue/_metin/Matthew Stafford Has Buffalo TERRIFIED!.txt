On Thursday, Stafford was asked how he handled the adversity in Philadelphia — two interceptions, a ten-point deficit, a road crowd in the rain. Here’s what he said: “Draw on experience. Obviously have a lot of that. Have been a part of games where I’ve had to keep going and find a way to try to win a game, and that was obviously the occasion last Sunday as well.” Then he explained his philosophy: “Just like I say it all the time, just trust my preparation and live with the results.”

He also talked about the man on the other sideline with real respect. “I got a ton of respect for Josh, what he brings to their team,” Stafford said, calling Monday night “a big-time challenge for us as a team, as a unit.” And think about the history between these two: Josh Allen won the MVP in 2024. Stafford won it in 2025. The last two MVPs, under the lights at SoFi. That’s not just a regular-season game. That’s a heavyweight bout. And there’s another layer: Stafford will face Bills defensive coordinator Jim Leonhard for the first time as a coach. The two overlapped in the league as players — Leonhard was a safety from 2005 to 2014. Now Leonhard is in his first season running Buffalo’s defense, and it hasn’t been smooth. A veteran quarterback who’s seen everything against a first-year coordinator whose defense ranks near the bottom of the league? That’s a matchup Stafford should be able to exploit — if he stays patient.

And then came the part I love. He didn’t pretend everything was fine. He said: “Are there areas I want to be better that y’all don’t even pick up on and talk about? Absolutely. Are there the obvious ones? For sure.” That’s a 38-year-old MVP admitting he has things to fix — including things the cameras don’t catch. “But I knew the best thing for me was to go out there and continue to play as best I could. Crawling in a hole, not an option at that point. Just trying to make sure that I do everything I can to pull our team out of that one and give us a chance.”

And he made sure to give credit where it belonged: “Our defense made a hell of a stop on the two-yard line, gave us an opportunity, and then a great third-down stop gave us the ball back. So it takes everybody.” That’s a leader talking. Not “I won the game.” Not “I’m fine, stop asking.” Just: I made mistakes, I kept playing, and my teammates had my back.

Now listen to what McVay said when he was asked to evaluate Stafford’s first four games, because this is where it gets really interesting. McVay didn’t hide from the mistakes either. But he focused on something else: “The cool thing about him... he was at his best in the most important moments. That’s the competitive greatness. To find a way in spite of some different things that maybe didn’t go our way throughout the course of the day, to go 98 and to go 78. That is everything you need to know about Matthew.”

Ninety-eight yards. That was the drive after the goal-line stand, when the Rams started at their own two-yard line, down ten, and Kyren Williams punched it in at 3:59. Seventy-eight yards. That was the game-winning drive, ending with Kyren’s seven-yard touchdown with 1:10 left. Two long drives, in the rain, against a Vic Fangio defense, with the season on the line. McVay is right: that’s what great quarterbacks do.

And then McVay said something I think is really important — he spread the blame. “It seems like we’ve kind of all taken our turn,” he said. “Whether I’m not putting us in a good situation and can provide better clarity... maybe there’s a decision that I’d want to make... the ball was a little bit more slick on the one that kind of sailed on the play action.” In other words: some of those mistakes are on the play-caller, some are on the conditions, and some are on the quarterback. And he finished with this: “There’s nobody that I’d rather have leading us... The steadiness of his demeanor to be able to handle the ebbs and flows has been exactly what we want.” He even pointed back to Denver: “Even in the Denver game where we came up short, he gave us every chance to find a way to kind of finish that out.”

And if you watched our Stafford-versus-Fangio breakdown, you know why McVay’s words matter. Before Sunday, Stafford was 0-5 against Vic Fangio defenses. He beat that streak not by playing perfectly, but by adjusting — taking the checkdowns, trusting Kyren Williams, staying patient when the deep shots weren’t there. That’s the same quarterback McVay is describing: not a flawless one, a resilient one. And resilience is exactly what you need against a quarterback like Josh Allen, because Monday night is not going to be a clean game. It’s going to be a fight.

Now let’s talk about the stat that explains this whole Rams season so far, because it’s one of the strangest numbers in the NFL. The Rams have piled up 1,668 total yards — the second-most in the league. They’ve topped 400 yards in each of their last three games. And yet they rank just 19th in scoring, at about 21 points per game. How does that happen? Turnovers and self-inflicted wounds. The Rams have turned the ball over twice in every single game — eight giveaways in four weeks. Stafford’s six interceptions are a big part of that. And then there are the penalties.

This week, the Rams’ offensive staff was asked about the gap between how good the offense looks between the 20s and how often it stalls. And the answer was blunt: “We’re just hurting ourselves more than what we are accustomed to doing.” The staff pointed out that around this time last year, the Rams were one of the least penalized teams in the league. Now, they’ve had a lot of penalties in the “fringe area” — that zone just outside the red zone and inside it — over the last few weeks. And that changes everything. “That changes the way you can call plays,” the answer went. “It becomes a lot more difficult to call plays and obviously becomes a lot more difficult to execute.” A holding penalty on first down inside the 20 turns a manageable drive into second-and-20. That’s how 400-yard games turn into 21-point games.

And it’s not just the offense. Special teams keep leaking points too. Harrison Mevis is five of seven on field goals this season after missing a 48-yarder wide left in Philadelphia. He also kicked a ball out of bounds that gave the Eagles the ball at their own 40-yard line. And a big Britain Covey punt return set up an Eagles touchdown two plays later. Add the turnovers, the penalties and the special teams mistakes together, and you understand why a team with the second-most yards in the league is only scoring 21 points a game. The talent isn’t the problem. The margins are.

And McVay talked about the balance he wants. He doesn’t want to over-coach players and take away their aggression. The staff explained it this way: you don’t want a lineman who’s afraid to block hard on the next snap after getting a holding call. There are going to be some “competitive penalties” — the kind that come from playing hard. The goal is limiting the avoidable ones. That’s the line the Rams have to walk on Monday night: stay aggressive, stop beating yourself.

Now — the other side of the ball, because this is where the Rams have a real edge. Through four games, the Rams’ defense has allowed just 990 total yards — the second-fewest in the NFL. They allow 4.2 yards per play, second-best in the league. They’ve given up only five pass plays of 20 yards or more — tied for the fewest in football. And here’s a crazy one: since the 49ers put up 379 yards on them in the opener in Melbourne, no Rams opponent has reached 260 yards. Not the Giants. Not the Broncos. Not the Eagles. That’s a defense that doesn’t give up chunk plays — and that’s exactly what you want against Josh Allen.

And the individual numbers back it up. According to PFF, Quentin Lake and Trent McDuffie have the two highest grades on the Rams’ defense — Lake at 90.0 and McDuffie at 89.1. McDuffie has allowed just five catches on 18 targets this season. That’s why Lake’s questionable tag matters so much, and why McVay’s comment that Lake is “trending in the right direction” is such good news. And the pass rush has been producing even without the stars: the Rams generated 26 pressures in Philadelphia, and Byron Young alone has 21 of the team’s 75 pressures this season, with three and a half sacks. Now add Aaron Donald back into that mix.

Because Buffalo’s offense is explosive. The Bills lead the NFL in scoring at about 32 points per game, and their offense has 17 pass plays of 20-plus yards, tied for second-most in the league. Remember the last time these teams met, in December of 2024? Allen threw for 342 yards and three touchdowns and ran for three more touchdowns — and the Rams still won 44-42, with Stafford throwing for 320 yards and two scores. Allen is capable of scoring 40 on anybody. But on the other side, the Bills’ defense is allowing about 27 points per game, 25th in the league. That’s why the total for this game is up in the mid-50s.

So here’s the matchup that decides Monday night: the Rams’ defense that never gives up big plays, against a Bills offense built on big plays. And the Rams’ offense that racks up yards but keeps shooting itself in the foot, against a Bills defense that can’t stop anybody. If the Rams’ offense cleans up the turnovers and penalties, the yards finally turn into points — and Buffalo can’t keep up. If they don’t, Josh Allen is exactly the kind of quarterback who makes you pay for every gift.

And the national media is leaning toward the Rams. One major national host picked the Rams to win and cover at minus three and a half this week, saying the Rams’ explosive offense against a Buffalo defense that’s been “allowing big chunk plays” is a bad matchup for the Bills. But that same host also made a point that I think Rams fans should take seriously, even if it stings. Talking about the NFC West, he called the 49ers and Seahawks physical, punishing teams — and then he said: “The Rams are kind of pretty.” Not soft. Pretty. Big plays, star receivers, a quarterback who throws beautiful passes. His point was that in January, against teams that want to punch you in the mouth, pretty can get pushed around.

Here’s my answer to that. Go watch the fourth quarter in Philadelphia. In the rain. Against a physical Eagles front. Kyren Williams broke three tackles at the goal line and said his motto is “by any means.” The defense stuffed a fourth down at the two-yard line. Byron Young strip-sacked Jalen Hurts. Nothing about that was pretty. That was a fight — and the Rams won it. And with Donald back in the middle, Garrett working his way back from his knee surgery, and a defense that hasn’t allowed 260 yards since Week 1, I’d argue this Rams team has more toughness than people want to give it credit for. Whatever happens in this weekend’s 49ers–Seahawks showdown, the Rams will see both of those teams again. Monday night is the first chance to prove the “pretty” label wrong.

Let me also add one Stafford stat that gets lost in the interception talk: he’s thrown for 300-plus yards in three straight games. Puka Nacua had nine catches for 125 yards in his return. And the usage numbers show something interesting — when Puka is on the field, Davante Adams’ target share drops, which is part of why Davante had a quieter day in Philly. That’s not a problem; that’s balance. Against a Bills secondary that’s been giving up a lot to receivers, with Dee Alford questionable, both Puka and Davante should have chances to eat. And Kyren Williams — 5.4 yards per carry this season and the highest PFF grade on the offense — gives Stafford a safety valve when the pressure comes. If Stafford takes the checkdowns, the explosive plays will come.

So here are my three keys for Stafford on Monday night. One: zero turnovers. The Rams have had two giveaways in every game. Break that streak and they win. Two: clean football in the red zone. No holding penalties on first down, no false starts in the fringe area. Turn yards into touchdowns, not field goals. Three: start fast. SoFi will be electric under the lights. Score early, make Josh Allen chase, and let a defense that doesn’t allow big plays make him work for every yard.

My prediction hasn’t changed: Rams 31, Bills 27. I said it in our preview, and after this week’s injury report, I feel even better about it. Stafford plays a clean game, Donald and the defense make one big play in the second half, and the Rams go over .500 for the first time this season.
