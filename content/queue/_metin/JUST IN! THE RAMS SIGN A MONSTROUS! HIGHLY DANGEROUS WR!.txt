Now, story number one: Sean McVay’s trade deadline plan.

On Tuesday, both of the Rams’ biggest division rivals made big trades. According to the Seahawks’ official website, Seattle traded a 2027 third-round pick and a 2028 fifth-round pick to the Colts for All-Pro guard Quenton Nelson. According to Pro Football Rumors, San Francisco traded a 2027 fourth-round pick to the Colts to bring back defensive tackle DeForest Buckner. The 49ers are 6-1, the Seahawks are 4-3 and the Rams are 3-4, according to ESPN.

So on Wednesday, the obvious question for McVay was: are the Rams going to respond?

According to the LA Times’ Gary Klein, when McVay was asked whether there had been any new trade discussions, his first answer was simple: “No.” Then he explained. According to the New York Post, McVay said: “Hopefully, some of those trades will be guys getting back from injury that haven’t played for us.” And he added: “That’s kind of the way that we look at it.”

According to the LA Times, McVay said the Rams are counting on some of their key injured players being able to “return within a month.” According to the New York Post, he said: “That’s kind of where that value lies for us.” And he said many of the missing players are ones “we do expect to get back at some point, just not exactly sure when that is.”

But McVay didn’t completely close the door. According to the New York Post, he said: “Like I’ve told you in years past, you always think about it.” And according to the LA Times, he acknowledged that the conversation could be different if more players were out for the season.

So here’s how I read it. McVay is telling you the Rams’ plan is patience. They believe their best “additions” are already on the roster — they just haven’t been on the field. And unless something changes, like another major injury, they’re not planning to chase a big trade just because Seattle and San Francisco did.

Now let’s look at who those “trades” actually are, because the list is long.

Myles Garrett. The 2025 Defensive Player of the Year has been on injured reserve since September 17th after knee surgery, according to the Rams’ official website. On Wednesday, the Rams said they would not open his 21-day practice window this week. McVay said: “I know it’s frustrating, but he’s making good progress, and we’re just on a week-to-week basis right now.” According to the AP, Garrett will miss his seventh straight game on Sunday.

Terrance Ferguson. The tight end has been on injured reserve since October 2nd with an ankle injury, according to the Rams’ website. He was eligible to return to practice this week, but the Rams didn’t open his window either.

Davante Adams. He ruptured his hamstring against Arizona, and the original timeline was six to 10 weeks. But there’s good news there. According to Heavy, McVay said Adams’ surgery “went really well,” and on the timeline, he said: “I think it’s probably closer to the shorter term.”

Trent McDuffie. He’s missed the last two games with a groin injury. But according to the Rams’ injury report, he was a limited participant in practice on both Wednesday and Thursday.

Nate Landman. The linebacker was a full participant on Wednesday and Thursday after clearing the concussion protocol, according to the Rams’ injury report and Yahoo Sports.

And Kobie Turner, who’s on injured reserve, according to Rams On SI.

That’s a lot of talent. If even half of those players come back healthy in November, the Rams will look like a different team. And that’s McVay’s point.

Let me quickly explain how the injured reserve return process works, because it explains why the timeline keeps feeling slow. When a player is on injured reserve, he has to miss a minimum number of games before he’s eligible to come back. Then the team can “open his 21-day practice window.” Once that window opens, the player can practice with the team for up to 21 days. At any point during that window, the team can activate him to the roster. But if the 21 days run out and he hasn’t been activated, he has to stay on injured reserve for the rest of the season. So teams are careful. They don’t want to open the window until they’re confident the player can be ready within those three weeks. That’s why the Rams haven’t opened the window for Garrett or Ferguson yet — not because something has gone wrong, but because once the clock starts, there’s no pausing it.

And that also tells you something about the math. Even if the Rams open Garrett’s window next week, he could practice for a week or two before being activated. That’s why “within a month” is a realistic estimate — and why the deadline comes first.

Now here’s my honest take, because I think there’s a real risk in this plan.

Expecting players back and actually getting them back are two different things. And Garrett is the perfect example. Rams fans have been hearing for weeks that he’s getting close. Ian Rapoport reported last week that Garrett was likely to return to practice “next week.” Then this week, the Rams decided not to open his window. That doesn’t mean anything is wrong. It just means the timeline keeps moving.

And here’s the part I don’t think enough people are talking about: the calendar. McVay said he hopes the key players can return “within a month.” McVay said that on October 28th. A month from then is late November. But the trade deadline is November 10th. That means the Rams have to decide whether to trade before they know whether their injured players will actually come back on time.

So if the Rams wait, and Garrett or Adams has a setback after November 10th, there’s no fixing it with a trade. That’s the gamble. McVay and Les Snead are betting on their medical staff and their players. They’ve earned some trust — they built a Super Bowl winner. But it’s still a bet.

That’s why I don’t think the plan has to be all-or-nothing. The Rams can trust their injured players and still make one small, affordable move for depth. That’s not panic. That’s insurance.

Before we get to Jennings, there’s one more area the transcript brought up, and it’s one McVay himself keeps talking about: special teams.

According to Rams Wire’s Cameron DaSilva, back in September, FTN Fantasy’s DVOA rankings put the Rams last in the NFL on special teams at minus 15.7 percent. And it hasn’t gotten much better. According to Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak, writing in mid-October, through five games the Rams had one of the worst special teams starts in DVOA history. Grisak listed the problems: a 45-yard kickoff return in Week 1, a missed 43-yard field goal against the Giants, a 41-yard punt return touchdown against the Eagles, and a missed 50-yard kick and a blocked potential game-winner in overtime against the Bills. After that game, the Rams cut kicker Harrison Mevis and signed Blake Grupe.

Grisak also found a stat that tells you how long this has been a problem. Since 2023, the Rams have signed six kickers. And over eight years, they’ve had five special teams coordinators.

Even after the Raiders win, McVay wasn’t happy. According to the AP, he said the special teams were “not good enough,” and added: “We’ve talked about this a lot, and it’s just got to get fixed.” The Raiders converted a fake punt and had kickoff returns of 42 and 36 yards.

And now there’s another problem. According to the Rams’ injury report, long snapper Joe Cardona didn’t practice on Wednesday or Thursday because of an ankle injury. According to the Rams’ website, the team signed veteran Jake McQuaide to the practice squad. If Cardona can’t play, a new long snapper on Sunday adds even more pressure to a unit that’s already struggling.

Here’s the thing, though: you don’t fix special teams with a blockbuster trade. You fix it with coaching, a reliable returner and better execution. So when people say “the Rams need to make a move,” special teams is a reminder that not every problem gets solved at the deadline.

Now, story number two: Jauan Jennings.

According to Heavy, ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler and Matt Bowen named the Rams as a team fit for Jennings. Bowen wrote that “Jennings has seen very limited opportunities in Minnesota this season,” and called him “a consistent route runner who plays through contact.” ESPN gave him a 50 percent chance of being traded.

Rams fans know Jennings well, and not always fondly. He spent years with the 49ers, catching big third-down passes against the Rams. He was a seventh-round pick by San Francisco in 2020, and he worked his way up from the bottom of the roster to become one of Kyle Shanahan’s most trusted receivers. He even had a big moment on the biggest stage: in Super Bowl LVIII against the Chiefs, Jennings threw a touchdown pass to Christian McCaffrey and also caught a touchdown pass. That’s my own recollection of that game, not something from this week’s reporting — but it tells you what kind of player he is. He’s a gamer. And his production in San Francisco was real. According to Athlon Sports, via Yahoo, Jennings had 55 catches for 643 yards and nine touchdowns in 2025, and his 19 contested catches ranked among the top five in the NFL, according to the Vikings’ website. And in 2024, he had the best season of his career — 77 catches for 975 yards and six touchdowns.

Then he left. According to Athlon Sports, Jennings signed with Minnesota in May on a one-year deal with an 8-million-dollar base salary and a chance to reach 13 million with incentives. And it hasn’t worked out.

According to Athlon Sports, via Yahoo, citing Pro Football Focus, Jennings has just four catches on eight targets for 43 yards this season. He’s run 137 routes, and his passer rating when targeted is 26.6. Through his first five games, he had only two catches for nine yards, according to CBS Sports. Two of those four catches came last week, for 34 yards in Minnesota’s 16-10 win over the Colts.

That’s a huge drop from where he was a year ago. And there’s been some frustration. According to Heavy, Justin Jefferson publicly said Jennings “wants that ball.” And according to Heavy, CBS Sports’ John Breech wrote that Jennings “might show some patience, but if he doesn’t start getting the ball soon, it wouldn’t be shocking to see him quietly ask for a trade.” But let me be careful here: there’s no report that Jennings has actually asked for a trade.

So why would he fit the Rams?

Reason number one: the receiver room. According to the AP, in the Raiders game, Konata Mumpfield’s 15-yard catch was the only reception by a Rams wide receiver other than Puka Nacua. With Adams out, the Rams need someone who can win on the outside right now.

Reason number two: his style. Bowen called him a receiver who “plays through contact.” That’s exactly what you want in tight windows and on third downs. And remember, the Rams are leaning into the run game. Against the Raiders, they ran 38 times and threw just 29 passes, according to the AP. Jennings came from Kyle Shanahan’s offense in San Francisco, where receivers are expected to block. That’s my own observation, not something ESPN said — but in a run-first offense, a physical receiver who blocks is valuable.

Reason number three: the connections. According to Heavy, Jennings plays for Kevin O’Connell, the former Rams offensive coordinator, so he already knows a lot of the language of McVay’s offense. And he knows the NFC West inside and out. He’s played against the Rams, the Seahawks and the Cardinals twice a year for most of his career.

And yes, there’s a fun part, too. A former 49er catching touchdowns for the Rams against the 49ers? Rams fans would love that.

But now let’s be honest about the problems, because there are several.

Problem number one: money. According to Athlon Sports, the Rams have just 206,406 dollars in cap space, citing Over The Cap. Jennings has an 8-million-dollar base salary. Players are paid over the 18 weeks of the regular season, so a team that trades for him at the deadline would take on roughly half of that — around 4 million dollars. That’s my own rough math. Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak estimated earlier this month that Jennings would cost the Rams about 4 million in cap space. Either way, it’s far more than the Rams have right now. According to Athlon, the Rams would have to send money back or restructure a contract to make it work.

Grisak has already shown how that could happen. In a separate article, he wrote that restructuring Kyren Williams’ contract could free up about 3.5 million dollars, and that Alaric Jackson’s deal is another option. So it’s possible — but it takes work.

Problem number two: the Vikings might not want to sell. According to Athlon Sports, Minnesota is 6-0. A team that’s unbeaten and chasing the No. 1 seed doesn’t usually trade away depth, even if that depth isn’t playing much. If Justin Jefferson or Jordan Addison gets hurt in December, the Vikings would want Jennings there. So “50 percent” might be optimistic.

Problem number three: price. According to Grisak, Jennings would only cost a Day 3 pick, possibly as a pick swap. That’s reasonable. But the Rams are already missing their 2027 first and third, their 2028 second and their 2029 third, according to Pro Football Rumors. Every pick they give up now is one less pick for a roster that will need cheap young talent soon.

Problem number four: the competition. Jennings has been linked to other teams, too. CBS Sports named the 49ers, Raiders, Chiefs, Steelers and Packers as possible landing spots. The 49ers reunion idea got shot down, though. According to Yahoo Sports, NFL Network’s Tom Pelissero wrote on his Substack: “I don’t see a Jauan Jennings reunion being an option at this time.”

And problem number five: McVay. Remember, when McVay was asked about adding an outside receiver earlier this month, he said, according to the Rams’ official website: “That’s not something that we’ve talked about right now.” And this week, he said the Rams’ “trades” are the players coming back from injury. If Adams really is on the shorter end of his timeline, the Rams might decide they only need a receiver for four or five games — and that might not be worth the trouble.

So is Jennings realistic? I’d say he’s possible, but not likely. He fits the football side. The money and the Vikings’ record make it complicated.

But let’s play it out anyway, because I know some of you are wondering what a Jennings deal could even look like. And I want to be very clear: this is not a report. This is just me walking through the pieces.

Step one, the Rams would have to create cap space. The most realistic way, according to Grisak, is restructuring Kyren Williams’ contract to free up about 3.5 million dollars. That alone still might not cover all of Jennings’ remaining salary, so the Rams would probably need a little more room — or the Vikings would have to agree to keep part of his salary, the way the Colts did with DeForest Buckner in the 49ers trade.

Step two, the pick. Grisak suggested a Day 3 pick, maybe as a swap. The Rams do have their 2027 fourth, fifth and sixth-round picks, according to Grisak. So something like a late-round pick swap is the type of price that would fit.

Step three, Minnesota has to say yes. And that’s the hardest step. A 6-0 team doesn’t need draft picks to win now. It needs depth. So the Vikings would have to decide that Jennings is more useful as a late-round pick than as insurance for Jefferson and Addison.

Put those three steps together, and you can see why I’m skeptical. It’s not that any one step is impossible. It’s that all three have to happen in the next 11 days — and the head coach just told reporters his main plan is waiting on his injured players.

Now let me compare him to the other names, because Jennings isn’t the only option.

Colts receiver Josh Downs has requested a trade, according to ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler, as reported by Pro Football Talk. According to Grisak, Downs would cost the Rams about 2.4 million dollars for the rest of the season and maybe a fifth- or sixth-round pick. He’s cheaper than Jennings, he’s on a team that’s selling, and he wants out. That makes Downs more realistic, even if he’s a slot receiver and not a physical outside guy like Jennings.

Jaguars receiver Brian Thomas Jr. is the high-upside option. According to Grisak, he’d only cost about 600,000 dollars this season, but the price could be a second-round pick. That’s a big ask for a team that’s already short on picks.

And on defense, there’s A’Shawn Robinson. According to Heavy, Fowler noted that Robinson is still productive and has connections around the league, including with the Rams. He played for the Rams from 2020 to 2022 and was part of the Super Bowl LVI team. With Poona Ford out for the season and Kobie Turner on injured reserve, a defensive tackle might help more than a receiver. ESPN gave him a 30 percent chance of being traded.

So if I had to rank the realistic options, it would be Downs first, Jennings second, and Thomas third. And honestly, “no trade at all” is right up there with Downs, given what McVay said this week.

Now let’s look at Sunday, because the Rams have a game to win before any of this gets decided.

The Rams host the Chargers at SoFi Stadium on Sunday at 1:05 p.m. Pacific time, according to Football Nation. The Chargers are 0-6 and coming off a bye. According to Football Nation, the Rams are favored by about six and a half to seven points.

Here’s the Rams’ injury report as of Thursday, according to the Rams’ official website. Offensive lineman Coleman Shelton, tight end Colby Parkinson and long snapper Joe Cardona didn’t practice on Wednesday or Thursday. Trent McDuffie was limited both days. Nate Landman and linebacker Desjuan Johnson were full participants.

On the Chargers’ side, according to the Chargers’ website, Khalil Mack sat out Wednesday’s practice for his usual veteran rest day, so expect him to play. Safety Derwin James Jr. didn’t practice with a hamstring injury, and Jim Harbaugh said James had a setback and could go on injured reserve.

Here are my three keys for Sunday.

Key number one: protect Stafford. If Shelton can’t play, the offensive line has to adjust, and Mack will be looking for a mismatch. Use the run game, quick throws and the tight ends.

Key number two: get a receiver other than Puka involved. One catch from the rest of the receivers isn’t going to cut it. If Tutu Atwell, Konata Mumpfield or Jordan Whittington can make a few plays, it takes pressure off the whole trade conversation — and it backs up what McVay said about the Rams’ depth.

Key number three: survive special teams. With a possible new long snapper and a unit that’s been a problem all year, the goal is simple: no disasters. No fake punts, no long returns, no blocked kicks.

My prediction: Rams 27, Chargers 17. The Rams are healthier on defense than they’ve been in weeks, and they’re coming off their best game of the season. But a winless team off a bye can be dangerous, so this one won’t be easy.

So here’s my bottom line.

No, the Rams did not sign a “monstrous” wide receiver. There’s no signing and no trade. What’s real is that McVay told reporters the Rams’ biggest “trades” will hopefully be players coming back from injury within a month — and that you “always think about” other moves. And it’s real that ESPN’s Fowler and Bowen named the Rams as a fit for Jauan Jennings, with a 50 percent chance he’s traded somewhere.

Jennings fits the Rams on the field. He’s tough, he catches the ball in traffic, and he knows the NFC West. But with about 206,000 dollars in cap space, a 6-0 Vikings team that might want to keep him and McVay leaning on his injured players, I’d call it possible — not likely.

The bigger question is the calendar. The Rams have to decide by November 10th, but their injured stars might not be back until late November. That’s the gamble McVay and Snead are taking.
