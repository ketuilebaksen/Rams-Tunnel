Part one: the Puka injury, what we actually know

Let’s start with the facts, because there’s a lot of noise out there.

How it happened. On the Rams’ final offensive snap against Washington, a third-down jet sweep, Puka ran for five yards and took a hard hit. He stayed down briefly, then walked off the field. Earlier in the game, in the third quarter, he’d also visited the blue medical tent and said afterward that he was checked for a concussion and cleared.

What was reported Sunday night. ESPN’s Adam Schefter and NFL Network’s Mike Garafolo reported that Puka would have an MRI on Monday, and that the Rams’ initial belief was that he did not tear his ACL.

What McVay said Monday. The diagnosis came in: MCL and PCL sprains, plus turf toe. McVay said Puka is likely going on IR, and that the team is “thinking it’s going to be at least a month.”

So here’s how I’d read that. This is not a season-ending injury, and that’s the good news. No ACL tear. But it’s not a one-week thing either. If he goes on IR, he’ll have to miss at least four games. The Rams play Arizona this Sunday, then have their bye, then play Green Bay. So “at least a month” could mean Puka is out until sometime in December.

And this hurts even more because of what Puka was doing. Against Washington, he caught 14 passes for 150 yards and the game-winning touchdown. Quick correction from my last video: I said he was targeted 21 times. ESPN’s count is 19 targets on Stafford’s 43 attempts. Either way, it was nearly half of Stafford’s throws.

ESPN also pointed out that Puka has 25 catches over his last two games, the second most by a Rams player over a two-game span in the Super Bowl era. And it was his ninth career regular-season game with at least 150 receiving yards, which ties him with Jerry Rice and Randy Moss for the second most by any player in his first four seasons since the 1970 merger.

That’s the guy the Rams are losing for at least a month.

Part two: the play call question

The original video raised a fair question, and I want to address it. Why was Puka running a jet sweep on the final offensive play of a game the Rams were trying to close out?

Here’s my honest take, and it’s opinion. I understand the frustration. Puka was carrying an enormous load. He takes hits on screens, crossing routes, and contested catches every single week. When you see your most important player get hurt on a late jet sweep, of course you ask why he was out there.

But I also understand why McVay called it. It was third down, the Rams needed a first down to help close the game, and your best player is your best option. Puka gained five yards on the play. Injuries on plays like that are bad luck as much as anything else. If McVay had called a different play and the Rams had punted and Washington had driven for the tying score, people would be asking why Puka didn’t get the ball.

So I don’t think the play call was the real problem. The real problem is the one we’ve been talking about for three weeks: the Rams’ passing game has run almost entirely through one player. When one player gets that much work, the injury risk goes up. The fix isn’t calling fewer plays for Puka. It’s having more players who can carry part of the load.

Part three: the receiver room right now

And that’s exactly the problem. Look at what’s left.

Against Washington, outside of Puka, the receivers barely touched the ball. Konata Mumpfield caught two passes for 20 yards. Tutu Atwell had two catches for 16 yards. Jordan Whittington didn’t have a catch. CJ Daniels wasn’t targeted.

And the season numbers are even more worrying. According to Rams Wire’s Cameron DaSilva, Mumpfield has 162 receiving yards on the season, Atwell has 99, and Whittington has 12.

Then add the injuries. Davante Adams is on IR after hamstring surgery, though McVay said the surgery “went really well” and he’s trending toward the shorter end of his six-to-ten-week timeline. Tight end Terrance Ferguson hurt his hamstring in practice last Friday and missed the Washington game. And now Puka.

So right now, the healthy pass catchers for the Rams are Atwell, Mumpfield, Whittington, Daniels, and the tight ends, led by Tyler Higbee. That’s not enough. Not for a team that wants to make the playoffs, and definitely not for one with championship hopes.

Part four: McVay’s stance and the Rams’ money

A week ago, McVay’s public position was clear. According to ESPN’s Sarah Barshop, he indicated the Rams didn’t plan to add outside players and would rather count on injured players coming back. As he put it then: “Hopefully, some of those trades will be guys getting back from injury.”

That made sense a week ago, when Garrett was about to return and Puka was healthy. And Garrett did return, with three sacks in his first game back. But the math changed on Monday. You can’t wait for Adams to replace Puka when you’re also waiting for Adams to replace himself.

Here’s the hard part, though: the money hasn’t changed.

The Rams have almost no cap space. The most recent number I’ve seen, from Athlon Sports citing Over The Cap, is just a few thousand dollars. They don’t have their 2027 first-round pick, which Cleveland owns from the Garrett trade, or their 2027 third, which Kansas City owns from the McDuffie trade. They still have a 2027 second, fourth, fifth, and sixth, and a 2028 third.

There is one piece of cap news worth mentioning, though. If Puka goes on IR, his salary still counts against the cap. IR doesn’t create space. So the Rams still need a restructure, or to send salary back, to make any deal work. A Kyren Williams restructure has been estimated to free up about $3.5 million.

So the Rams need a receiver more than ever, and they’re still one of the hardest teams in the league to make a trade with. That’s the box Les Snead is in today.

Part five: the cheap option, Dyami Brown

Now let’s look at the two trade ideas from the video, starting with the cheap one.

ESPN analyst Aaron Schatz proposed a trade sending Washington receiver Dyami Brown to the Rams. Here are the terms: the Rams would get Brown and a 2027 seventh-round pick, and Washington would get a 2027 sixth-round pick. So the Rams basically move down one round at the end of the draft to add a receiver. That’s about as cheap as a trade gets.

There’s a nice bonus too. The Rams traded their 2027 seventh-round pick to Baltimore in the Tre’Davious White deal, so this would also get them a seventh back.

Who is Dyami Brown? He’s 27, a former third-round pick, and his best trait is vertical speed, which is exactly what the Rams’ passing game has been missing. This season, through seven games, he has nine catches for 94 yards, including a 48-yard gain. He’s also returned kickoffs. Those regular-season numbers aren’t impressive. But during Washington’s playoff run two years ago, Brown caught 14 passes for 229 yards and a touchdown in three games. He’s shown he can show up in big moments.

Schatz also made a point I really like: Brown is a willing, physical blocker. McVay asks a lot of his receivers in the run game, and the run game has carried the Rams for three straight weeks.

The money. Per Athlon Sports, Brown’s base salary is $1.22 million, his cap hit is $1.72 million, and Washington would only carry about $250,000 in dead money if it traded him. So he’s cheap, but the Rams would still need to clear some room first.

The problem. Brown hurt his calf in practice last Thursday and missed Sunday’s game against the Rams. And Washington’s receiver room is banged up, with Treylon Burks and Terry McLaurin dealing with injuries too. That could make the Commanders reluctant to trade another receiver.

My take, and this is not a report. Of every option out there, this is the one that makes the most sense for the Rams’ budget. It costs almost nothing in draft capital, the salary is small, and Brown brings speed and blocking. The question is his calf. If the medical checks out, I’d do this deal in a heartbeat. But it’s an analyst’s proposal. Nobody has reported that the Rams and Washington are actually talking.

Part six: the big swing, Brian Thomas Jr.

Now the bigger idea: Brian Thomas Jr.

Thomas was a star as a rookie in 2024, with 1,282 receiving yards. He’s 24, he’s 6-foot-3, and he ran a 4.33 40-yard dash. On paper, he’s exactly the kind of deep threat the Rams have been missing. Imagine him stretching the field with Puka working underneath when Puka gets back, and Adams returning too. That would be three dangerous receivers for the playoff run.

But this season has been a real struggle. Through eight games, according to the video, he has just 10 catches for 117 yards. He was benched earlier this season. And against Baltimore, he played only 19 offensive snaps and didn’t catch a pass.

The reporting. The video says Schefter reported that the Jaguars may be more willing to listen to trade offers after turning down interest earlier. I couldn’t confirm that exact report, so I’m treating it carefully. What I can tell you is that ESPN’s Bill Barnwell called Thomas the “exciting” trade option for the Rams last week, and Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak wrote that the Rams have called about Thomas and Josh Downs, though he didn’t name a source.

The contract. Thomas is still on his rookie deal, which runs through 2027. That’s the big appeal. He wouldn’t just be a rental. He’d be a long-term piece next to Puka.

The competition. The Chiefs are reportedly interested too. If Kansas City gets serious, the price goes up.

The cost. Here’s the hard part. A young receiver with a 1,282-yard season on a cheap rookie deal won’t come cheap. The Rams’ best pick is a 2027 second-rounder. If the Jaguars want that, plus more, it would be a huge bet on a player who’s having one of the worst seasons of his young career.

My take, and this is not a report. I love the upside. But I’d only do it at a reasonable price. If the Jaguars want the Rams’ 2027 second-round pick plus more, I’d walk away. The Rams already gave up a lot for Garrett and McDuffie. Betting the 2027 second on a player who has to “recapture” his rookie form is risky, especially with Puka and Adams both expected back later this season.

Part seven: the other names on the board

Rams Wire listed a few more possible targets: Josh Downs, Keenan Allen, Jerry Jeudy, and Calvin Ridley. None of them are confirmed Rams targets, but let’s run through them quickly, because we’ve covered most of them this week.

Josh Downs, Colts. He requested a trade. Schefter reported the Colts want a third-round pick, maybe a fourth. The Steelers and Chiefs are the main reported suitors, and the Colts said they’d wait until after Week 9 to see if offers improve. He’s a quick slot receiver, but blocking is a question. We’ve now seen three fake “Schefter” clips about Downs to the Rams. None of them were real.

Keenan Allen, Colts. He’s 34, on a one-year deal, and cheap in draft picks. But he’s facing a pending minimum three-game NFL suspension from his August arrest. Trading for him means that suspension could land at any time.

Jerry Jeudy, Browns. Not available. Tom Pelissero reported that Cleveland is expected to keep him unless it gets a “truly crazy offer.” And Cleveland owns the Rams’ 2027 first, so it has no reason to help.

Calvin Ridley. His name is on the Rams Wire list. I don’t have any new reporting about his availability, so I’ll leave it at that rather than guess.

So here’s my board for today, ranked by how realistic I think each move is. This is opinion, not reporting.

One: Dyami Brown. Cheap, fast, blocks, and an ESPN analyst already proposed a price. The calf is the question.

Two: Josh Downs. A selling team and a player who wants out. But the price may have gone up.

Three: Brian Thomas Jr. The best upside, but the most expensive, and there’s competition.

Four: Keenan Allen. Cheap, but the suspension risk is real.

Five: Jerry Jeudy. Not happening.

Part eight: does the Puka injury change everything?

Let me answer the big question directly.

A week ago, I said that if the Rams did anything, it’d be small. The Puka injury makes me think the odds of a move just went up a lot. You can’t go into Arizona on Sunday with Atwell, Mumpfield, and Whittington as your top three receivers and expect the passing game to work. Stafford threw 43 times for 219 yards against Washington, and that was with Puka.

But it doesn’t change the reality that the Rams can’t overpay. Here’s why. Puka is expected back in about a month. Adams is trending toward a return around the end of November or early December. So when the playoffs start, the Rams could have both of them back. Whatever receiver they trade for today is really a bridge for the next four to six weeks, and then a depth piece.

That’s why Dyami Brown makes so much sense to me. A sixth-for-seventh swap is a perfect price for a bridge receiver. A second-round pick for Brian Thomas Jr. is a franchise decision. Those are two very different kinds of moves.

And remember one more thing. The Rams are 5-4 with three straight wins. The defense is playing great. Garrett had three sacks in his first game back. This team doesn’t need a superstar today. It needs somebody who can catch the ball and keep Stafford from throwing into double coverage every play.

What life without Puka looks like for Stafford

Let’s think about what this means on the field, because it’s not just about one player.

For the last three weeks, defenses have known exactly where the ball was going, and Puka beat them anyway. Fourteen catches against Washington. Eleven of 12 targets against the Chargers. When your best receiver can win even when everyone knows he’s getting the ball, the offense can survive.

Without him, Stafford loses that safety valve. Coming into the Washington game, Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak had the Rams’ explosive pass play rate at 4.1 percent through eight weeks, compared with 17.5 percent at this point last season. Stafford’s average depth of target over the previous four weeks ranked 25th of 27 quarterbacks. And that was with Puka.

So here’s my concern, and this is analysis, not reporting. Without Puka, defenses can play the Rams’ run game more aggressively, crowd the line, and dare Atwell, Mumpfield, and Whittington to beat them. If nobody can, the run game that’s carried this team for three weeks gets harder too.

That’s why the deadline matters so much today. The Rams don’t need someone to replace Puka. Nobody can. They need someone who forces defenses to respect a second option, so the run game and the tight ends still have room to work.

The silver lining

I want to end this part on a positive note, because there is one.

First, no torn ACL. The first fear on Sunday night was that this could be season-ending. It isn’t. MCL and PCL sprains and turf toe are serious, but players come back from them. “At least a month” means the Rams should get Puka back for the stretch run.

Second, the timing of the bye helps. The Rams play Arizona, then get a week off. So a four-game IR stint costs Puka fewer real weeks of football than it would at another point in the season.

Third, Adams. If he’s back around the end of November or early December, as McVay’s timeline suggests, he could be back before Puka. That gives Stafford a veteran number one again for the December stretch.

And fourth, the defense. With Garrett back and playing like that, the Rams can win games 20-13. They don’t need to win shootouts right now.

Part nine: the other injury updates from Monday

A few more updates from McVay on Monday.

Steve Avila left the Washington game with a hamstring strain. McVay said, “It’s going to be a little bit of time for him, unfortunately, but hopefully we’ll get him back at some point.” He also said putting Avila on IR is “not something we’re trying to do right now.” That’s a hit to an offensive line that’s been one of the best in football at protecting Stafford.

Trent McDuffie missed the Washington game with his back injury. But McVay said he’s “making good progress,” and that the hope is to “take it a day at a time and continue to progress and hopefully be able to get him back this week.” That’s important, because the secondary is thin without Jaylen Watson, who’s out for the season.

The IR return slots. This one’s a little technical, but it matters. Teams only get a limited number of players they can bring back from IR each season. Garrett used one last week. Adams and Kobie Turner are both candidates to use more. And now Puka would need one too. So the Rams have to be careful about how they manage those slots for the rest of the season.

Part ten: what to watch today

Here’s what I’ll be watching between now and 4 p.m. Eastern.

A restructure. If the Rams restructure a contract, like Kyren Williams’, that’s the clearest signal a trade is coming. No restructure, and any deal has to include salary going the other way.

The Puka IR move. If the Rams officially place Puka on IR today, it confirms how long they think he’ll be out. Watch for whether they also make a corresponding roster move.

Washington’s decision on Brown. If Washington is worried about its own receiver injuries, it might hang on to Brown. If it’s willing to sell, the Schatz proposal could be close to what a real deal looks like.

Kansas City. The Chiefs are reportedly interested in both Thomas and Downs. If they land one of them early in the day, the Rams’ options shrink.

The final hour. Les Snead has made four deadline-day trades over the years. If something happens, it might come late. Deals can be agreed right up until 4 p.m. Eastern and announced a bit after that, so don’t panic if nothing happens by lunch.

And when a real trade happens, it’ll show up on Adam Schefter’s or Ian Rapoport’s actual X accounts first, in a sentence or two: team, player, picks. Not in a 10-minute YouTube clip with a story about sweeping a porch. We’ve seen fourteen of those fake clips already.

Part eleven: Arizona this Sunday

Whatever happens today, the Rams play the Arizona Cardinals this Sunday, and then they finally get their bye.

Arizona beat the Rams 18-16 back on October 18th. That’s the game where Davante Adams got hurt. Now the Rams will face them without Adams and without Puka. That’s a huge challenge for the passing game.

Here’s how I think the Rams have to approach it.

Lean on the run. Kyren Williams and Blake Corum have carried this team for three weeks, with 192 rushing yards against the Raiders, 129 against the Chargers, and 124 against Washington. That has to continue.

Use the tight ends. Tyler Higbee, Davis Allen, and maybe Colby Parkinson or Terrance Ferguson, depending on health. The Rams already use more two- and three-tight-end sets than almost anyone. With no Puka, the tight ends could become the main targets.

Let the defense win it. Garrett, Aaron Donald, Byron Young, and the rest of the front just held Washington to 13 points. If they can do that again, the Rams don’t need 300 passing yards.

Get to the bye at 6-4. That’s the goal. Win Sunday, rest up, get healthier, and come back for the stretch run with Adams, Turner, McDuffie, and eventually Puka.

Wrapping it up

Let’s bring it home.

The Rams have not signed two new stars, and they haven’t traded for anyone yet. But deadline day just became a lot more interesting, because Puka Nacua is likely headed to IR with MCL and PCL sprains and turf toe. McVay said it’ll be “at least a month.”

With Puka and Adams both out, the Rams’ receiver room is thin: Atwell, Mumpfield, Whittington, and the tight ends. The cheapest real idea out there is ESPN’s Aaron Schatz proposal: Dyami Brown and a 2027 seventh for a 2027 sixth. The biggest swing would be Brian Thomas Jr., but that would cost real draft capital and the Chiefs are reportedly interested. And the Rams still have almost no cap space, so any deal needs a restructure or salary going back.

My take: get a bridge receiver at a fair price, don’t mortgage the future, and trust that Puka and Adams will be back for the playoff push.
