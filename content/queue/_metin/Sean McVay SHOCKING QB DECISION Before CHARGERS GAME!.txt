Fake: Schefter saying “I can confirm the Rams are in this auction, and their argument is unique.” I couldn’t find it anywhere. When Schefter confirms a team is in on a player, it’s on ESPN and on his X account within minutes, and every NFL website picks it up. This didn’t happen.

Fake: “This trade is going to happen.” No trusted reporter has said that about Mack and the Rams.

Fake: “Bears and Lions have more picks, but the Rams have the urgency.” I couldn’t find that quote either.

Unverified: the claim that CBS Sports called a Mack trade to the Bears “the most likely trade of this deadline.” I couldn’t find that article, so I’m not going to repeat it as fact.

Wrong: the clip says “nobody has linked the Rams to Khalil Mack before today.” That’s actually not true — and it’s kind of funny, because the clip is trying to sound careful. According to Heavy, Bleacher Report’s Kristopher Knox floated Mack as a fit for the Rams back on October 1st, in a trade big board, with a 2027 fourth-round pick as the suggested price. So the idea isn’t new. It’s just an idea.

Now, what’s real?

Real: Khalil Mack is a trade candidate. The Chargers are 0-6, according to the official NFL game center, as cited by Football Nation. Teams in that position usually listen on veterans with expiring contracts.

Real: Schefter has talked about Mack. According to Yahoo Sports, on October 25th, Schefter said: “You have to wonder if a team like the Chicago Bears will keep calling.” Notice — the Bears, not the Rams.

Real: Bill Barnwell did propose a Mack trade to the Lions. According to ESPN, on October 22nd, Barnwell pitched a deal sending Mack and cornerback Donte Jackson to Detroit for a 2027 fourth-round pick and a 2027 sixth-round pick, with the Chargers paying about half of the remaining money.

Real: the Rams are missing their 2027 first-round pick, their 2027 third, their 2028 second and their 2029 third, according to Pro Football Rumors. The clip got that list right.

Real: Myles Garrett has been on injured reserve since early in the season. According to the Rams’ official website, he’s been on IR since September 17th after knee surgery.

Now, let’s talk about Khalil Mack himself, because he’s a legend, and he deserves a real breakdown.

According to Heavy, Mack is 35 years old and in his 13th NFL season. He’s a nine-time Pro Bowler, a three-time first-team All-Pro and the 2016 Defensive Player of the Year. According to Bettors Insider, he has 116 career sacks and 36 forced fumbles. In 2023, he had 17 sacks for the Chargers, which according to Heavy is a franchise record. And last season, even after missing four games with an elbow injury, he still finished with five and a half sacks.

This season, according to Bettors Insider, he has three sacks and 19 total tackles through six games. And according to ESPN’s Bill Barnwell, Mack’s quick pressure rate is 5.0 percent, which is still well above the league average of 3.9 percent. So at 35, he can still get to the quarterback.

Here’s his contract. According to Heavy, citing NFL Network’s Ian Rapoport, Mack returned to the Chargers in March on a one-year deal worth 18 million dollars, fully guaranteed. But according to Pro Football Rumors, his base salary is only 8 million dollars, which makes him easier to trade. And according to Barnwell, Mack is owed about 5.3 million dollars for the rest of this season, and he becomes a free agent after the year.

And what has Mack said about the rumors? According to Heavy, on October 15th, he told reporters: “I’m focused on this team and what I’m doing right now.” He added: “I feel like the outside noise is the outside noise.” And when he was asked why he wasn’t chasing a ring somewhere else, he said: “We’re still ring chasing here.” Earlier this year, according to the Chargers’ training camp preview, he said: “Ultimately, this is where me and my family want to be this year.”

So Mack isn’t asking out. He’s saying all the right things. But the Chargers are 0-6, and that changes the conversation whether a player wants it to or not.

And here’s a piece of history the clip completely missed — and it’s a great one. The Rams have actually tried to get Khalil Mack before. Back in September 2018, when Mack was holding out with the Raiders, the Rams made a real offer. According to ESPN, and this was reported by Adam Schefter himself, the Rams offered the Raiders a first-round pick and a third-round pick for Mack. Rams executive Kevin Demoff said: “We offered a pretty aggressive package and (the Raiders) came back and said, ‘We just think you’re going to pick too low.’” In other words, the Raiders thought the Rams would be so good that their first-round pick would be near the bottom of the round. According to that same ESPN report, the Rams’ plan was to “go all in” that season, pairing Mack with Aaron Donald, Ndamukong Suh and Michael Brockers. Mack went to Chicago instead.

So when the clip says nobody has ever connected the Rams and Khalil Mack, it’s wrong twice. A writer floated it this month, and the Rams really did chase him eight years ago. And think about this: Aaron Donald is back on the Rams’ roster this season after coming out of retirement. The Donald-and-Mack pairing the Rams dreamed about in 2018 is technically still possible in 2026. That’s a fun thought. But a fun thought isn’t a report.

Now let’s look at the real market for Mack, because it’s crowded — and the Rams aren’t at the front of the line.

The Bears are the team mentioned most. Mack played for Chicago from 2018 to 2021, and according to Yahoo Sports, Schefter wondered if Chicago “will keep calling.” NBC Sports’ Pro Football Talk also wrote that Mack “could return to the Bears.”

The Lions are another team that’s been mentioned. Barnwell’s ESPN proposal sent him to Detroit, and Barnwell argued that Detroit’s edge group is thin beyond Aidan Hutchinson, who often draws double teams.

And here’s the one that should worry Rams fans: the 49ers. According to Bettors Insider, NFL insider Jordan Schultz said on The Pivot that the 49ers and Bears are “the two teams he is watching most closely.” According to Pro Football Rumors, 49ers CEO John Lynch said on KNBR that George Kittle asked him whether the team was trying to trade for Mack. Pro Football Rumors called that “hardly confirmation,” but think about it. The 49ers just traded for DeForest Buckner on Tuesday. If they add Mack too, that’s a scary defensive line in our own division.

So the real Mack market looks like Chicago, Detroit and maybe San Francisco. The Rams were floated by one writer in early October, but there’s no report of the Rams being involved in any talks.

Now let me be fair to the idea. Does Mack make sense for the Rams?

On the field, yes. There’s a real need. According to Last Word on Sports, the Rams’ pass rush ranks 27th in the league with just 10 sacks. Myles Garrett hasn’t played since September. And on Wednesday, the Rams announced they won’t open his practice window this week. McVay said, according to the Rams’ official website: “I know it’s frustrating, but he’s making good progress, and we’re just on a week-to-week basis right now.” According to the AP, Garrett will miss his seventh straight game on Sunday.

So the clip’s basic argument — Mack could fill in until Garrett returns, and then play next to him — makes sense on paper. A Garrett-and-Mack pass rush in December would be fun to watch. I get the appeal.

But now let’s talk about why it almost certainly won’t happen.

Problem number one: money. According to Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak, citing Over The Cap, the Rams have only about 206,000 dollars in cap space. Mack is owed about 5.3 million for the rest of this season, according to Barnwell. That means the Rams would have to send more than 5 million dollars in salary back to the Chargers, or restructure contracts to make room, or ask the Chargers to eat some of the money. Barnwell’s own Lions proposal had the Chargers paying about half. It’s not impossible — Mack is much cheaper than someone like George Pickens — but it’s not simple either.

Let me explain why the money works this way, because it confuses a lot of fans. NFL players are paid their base salary in weekly checks over the 18 weeks of the regular season. So when a player is traded in the middle of the season, his new team only pays the checks that are left. That’s why Mack’s 8-million-dollar base salary turns into roughly 5.3 million for the rest of the year, according to Barnwell. The rest of his 18-million-dollar deal was most likely structured as bonus money, which stays on the Chargers’ books — that’s my reading of the reported numbers, not an official breakdown. For the Rams, though, even 5.3 million is about 25 times more than the cap space they have right now. So before the Rams could even make an offer, they’d have to create room — and every restructure pushes money into future years, when the Rams will need cap space to pay players like Puka Nacua. According to The Athletic’s Nate Atkins, Puka is one of five key Rams players in a contract year.

Problem number two: draft picks. The Rams are already missing their 2027 first and third, their 2028 second and their 2029 third. According to Grisak, what they do have in 2027 is a second, a fourth, a fifth and a sixth. Now, to be fair, Barnwell’s proposal only had the Lions giving up a fourth and a sixth. So the price for Mack might not be huge. But the Bears and Lions can easily match that — and they have more picks to sweeten a deal if there’s a bidding war.

Problem number three: the “trade of players” idea. The clip says the Rams would pay mostly with players. But which players? Every starter the Rams send away hurts a 3-4 team trying to climb back into the race. And a winless team that’s selling usually wants picks, not veterans. So that part of the clip doesn’t really fit how a rebuilding team thinks.

Problem number four: the Rams’ other needs. Remember, the Rams also need a wide receiver with Davante Adams out, and they need help at defensive tackle with Poona Ford out for the season and Kobie Turner on injured reserve. With almost no cap space, the Rams probably get one move at most. Is an edge rusher the right one when Garrett is expected back eventually? I’m not sure it is.

And problem number five: what McVay actually said. On Wednesday, according to Heavy, McVay was asked about the trade deadline, and he said: “What you look at is, hopefully, some of those ‘trades’ will be guys getting back from injury that haven’t played for us.” And he added: “A lot of the guys that are big, huge parts that we’re missing, we do expect to get back at some point.”

That’s about as clear as McVay ever gets about the deadline. He’s telling you the Rams’ biggest additions are supposed to be Garrett, McDuffie, Adams, Ferguson and Turner coming back healthy — not a 35-year-old from the building next door.

Now, one thing the clip did get right is that the Rams and Chargers share SoFi Stadium. And I’ll admit, the idea of a player walking down the tunnel and turning the other way is a fun picture. But here’s the thing nobody’s talking about: the Rams play the Chargers on Sunday. In that building. And Khalil Mack is expected to be on the other side.

So instead of daydreaming about Mack in a Rams jersey, let’s talk about how the Rams deal with Mack in a Chargers jersey — because that actually matters this weekend.

According to the Chargers’ official website, Mack was listed on Wednesday’s injury report as not practicing for rest, which the team called “his usual veteran rest day.” So expect him to play.

And the Chargers have some injury problems of their own. According to the Chargers’ website, safety Derwin James Jr. didn’t practice on Wednesday with a hamstring injury, and Jim Harbaugh said James had a setback in his rehab and could potentially go on injured reserve. Guard Kayode Awosika didn’t practice with a fibula injury. Cornerback Donte Jackson — the same player Barnwell proposed trading with Mack — was limited with a hamstring injury. Receiver Ladd McConkey was a full participant after a concussion.

The Chargers are 0-6, and according to Football Nation, they lost 22-6 at Kansas City before their bye week. Justin Herbert has struggled. According to Football Nation, he has 1,141 passing yards, five touchdowns and eight interceptions. That’s not the Herbert we’re used to seeing.

But a winless team coming off a bye is still dangerous. They’ve had two weeks to prepare for this game, and they’ll treat it like their Super Bowl. And remember what Harbaugh said earlier this month, according to Heavy: he’s “hellbent” on turning the season around. A Harbaugh team doesn’t quit.

Now the Rams’ side. According to Yahoo Sports, linebacker Nate Landman has cleared the concussion protocol and should be good for Sunday. Trent McDuffie is trending in the right direction after missing the last two games with a groin injury. According to the Rams’ official website, he was a limited participant on Wednesday, his first practice since getting hurt in Week 6.

But there are a few new concerns. According to Yahoo Sports, offensive lineman Coleman Shelton missed practice with a hip injury. Tight end Colby Parkinson was held out with a rib injury. And long snapper Joe Cardona has an ankle injury. According to the Rams’ website, the team signed veteran Jake McQuaide to the practice squad because of it.

That Shelton injury matters a lot this week. If the Rams are missing an offensive lineman, Khalil Mack will be looking to take advantage. And that’s the real story of the week: not trading for Mack, but blocking him.

Now let’s put this in context with the rest of the NFC West, because it’s been a crazy week.

On Tuesday, the Seattle Seahawks traded for All-Pro guard Quenton Nelson from the Colts, sending a 2027 third-round pick and a 2028 fifth-round pick, according to the Seahawks’ official website. The same day, the 49ers traded a 2027 fourth-round pick to bring back defensive tackle DeForest Buckner, according to Pro Football Rumors. According to ESPN, the 49ers are 6-1, the Seahawks are 4-3 and the Rams are 3-4.

The clip mentions the Nelson trade, and that part is real. But it doesn’t change the Rams’ Mack math. The Rams still have almost no cap space and few picks. And if the 49ers are the team that ends up with Mack, that’s a much bigger story for the Rams than anything in that clip.

So what should Rams fans actually watch before the November 10th deadline? According to Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak, the most realistic move is a smaller one — like Colts receiver Josh Downs, who requested a trade this week, according to ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler, and who would cost the Rams about 2.4 million dollars for the rest of the season. That kind of move fits. A Mack trade is possible in theory, but it would require the Rams to clear real money and beat three other teams in a bidding war.

Now let’s preview Sunday, because that’s where the season actually gets decided.

According to Football Nation, the Rams host the Chargers on Sunday, November 1st, at 1:05 p.m. Pacific time at SoFi Stadium, on FOX. The Rams are favored by about six and a half to seven points, and the total is 43.5, as of Monday’s numbers.

Here are my three keys.

Key number one: protect Stafford from Mack. If Coleman Shelton can’t play, the Rams’ offensive line will have to adjust. Mack is still a dangerous pass rusher, especially against backups. Use the run game, use quick throws and use the tight ends to chip him. Don’t let the Chargers’ best defender take over the game. A veteran like Mack lives for this kind of matchup — a crosstown rival in a shared building and a chance to remind every contender watching exactly what he can still do before the deadline.

Key number two: keep the run game going. Kyren Williams and Blake Corum combined for almost 200 yards against the Raiders. According to the AP, the Rams ran 38 times and threw just 29 passes. McVay called that balance “the identity that we’re hunting.” Do it again, and you take the pressure off the offensive line and keep Mack from pinning his ears back.

Key number three: get after Herbert. Herbert has eight interceptions, according to Football Nation. The Rams’ defense forced the Raiders into 3-of-14 on third down last week, according to the AP. If McDuffie is back and the pass rush gets home, the Rams can force mistakes. And with Derwin James possibly out for the Chargers, the Rams’ passing game should find some room, too.

My prediction: Rams 27, Chargers 17. The Rams are the better team, they’re healthier on defense than they’ve been in weeks, and they’re playing with some confidence after Las Vegas. But don’t sleep on Mack. He might be the reason this game is closer than the spread says.

No, Sean McVay did not make a shocking quarterback decision before the Chargers game. Matthew Stafford is the starter, he’s playing well, and he just made franchise history. The video with that headline doesn’t even talk about quarterbacks.
