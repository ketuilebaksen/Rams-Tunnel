Part four: why June 1 matters

Stephen A mentioned "after June 1" for a reason, so let me explain it in plain terms.

When an NFL team trades or releases a player, any bonus money it already paid him but hasn't counted against the cap yet usually hits the salary cap right away. That's called dead money.

But if the move happens after June 1, the team can spread that dead money across two seasons instead of one. That frees up more cap space in the current year.

So when Stephen A says the Steelers would "save $26.5 million" by moving Metcalf after June 1, he's talking about that kind of cap math. I can't confirm his exact number, but the June 1 idea itself is a real thing teams do.

And here's the key point for Rams fans: June is a long way away. Even in the best case, this rumor has nothing to do with the 2026 season. It's about 2027.

Who is DK Metcalf?

For younger fans, or anyone who doesn't follow the Steelers, here's a quick background on Metcalf.

He was a second-round pick by the Seattle Seahawks in 2019. He spent his first six seasons in Seattle, which means Rams fans saw him twice a year in the NFC West.

In 2025, the Seahawks traded him to the Pittsburgh Steelers, and he signed a big contract there.

So when people talk about Metcalf, they're talking about a player with real talent and real production. That's why his name comes up. But they're also talking about a player whose last couple of years have come with questions, both about consistency and about his temper on the field.

What a catch rate really tells you

Let me add one thing about that 48% catch rate, because numbers like that can be misleading.

Catch rate is just catches divided by targets. But it depends on a lot more than the receiver.

The quarterback matters. If the passes are off target, the receiver's catch rate drops.

The type of route matters. Deep receivers usually have lower catch rates than slot receivers, because deep passes are harder to complete. A player who runs a lot of go routes will almost always have a lower catch rate than a player who runs short routes.

The defense matters. If a receiver is getting double coverage, his catch rate goes down.

So if Stephen A's number is right, 48% is low. But it doesn't automatically mean Metcalf has bad hands. It could also reflect his role, his quarterback, or how defenses play him.

That said, McVay's offense is built on timing and accuracy. Stafford is one of the most accurate deep passers in the league. So if Metcalf ever did come to LA, his catch rate might look very different. That's just my opinion, not a prediction.

Stafford and big receivers

Here's a fun connection. Matthew Stafford has a history with big receivers.

In Detroit, Stafford's top target for years was Calvin Johnson, "Megatron," one of the biggest and most physically gifted receivers in NFL history. Stafford loved throwing to a big target who could win jump balls down the field.

With the Rams, his best receivers have mostly been route technicians. Cooper Kupp. Puka Nacua. Davante Adams. Players who win with precision, not size.

So adding a big body like Metcalf would be a change. But it's a style Stafford knows well from earlier in his career.

This is not a report. It's just an interesting thought. And remember, Stafford's own future beyond this season is something the Rams will have to think about too. He's 38 and in his 18th season.
