First, the quick facts, so we’re all on the same page. Bills 37, Rams 34 in overtime. The Rams are now 2-3. The Bills are 4-1. Harrison Mevis missed a 50-yard field goal on the opening drive and had a 48-yard attempt blocked by Greg Rousseau in overtime, and Buffalo won it on a Tyler Bass field goal. Sean McVay said the kicking game was “not even close to good enough” and that it “cost us the game tonight.” That’s the headline everybody’s talking about, and it’s fair. Now let’s start with the man who stole the show.

Puka Nacua. Seventeen catches on 24 targets, 138 yards and a touchdown. Let’s put that in perspective, because it’s hard to even process. According to CBS Sports, 17 receptions is the most ever by any player in a single Monday Night Football game. Ever. In more than five decades of Monday Night Football. According to the Buffalo Bills’ own recap, it’s also the most catches any player has ever had in a game against the Bills. Puka fell just one catch short of the Rams’ franchise record of 18, set by Tom Fears all the way back in 1950. And he became only the fifth player in Rams history with at least 15 catches, 100 receiving yards and a touchdown in the same game — the first since Danny Amendola.

Now think about the context of that performance. Two weeks ago, Puka was coming back from a groin injury that cost him two games. An outside sports doctor publicly raised the possibility of a “pre-hernia” and suggested surgery might be needed down the road. People were asking whether the Rams should be managing his snaps, limiting his workload, even looking for another receiver at the trade deadline. And what did Puka do? In his first game back, in the rain in Philadelphia, he caught nine passes for 125 yards. And in his second game back, he caught seventeen. Twenty-six catches in two games. If that’s a player who’s hurting, I’d hate to see him healthy.

And this isn’t the first time he’s done this to Buffalo. The last time the Bills came to SoFi, in December of 2024, Puka caught twelve passes for 162 yards in a 44-42 Rams win. So in his last two games against the Bills, Puka Nacua has 29 catches for 300 yards. Buffalo simply has no answer for him. They tried. Christian Benford was cleared to play after missing the Patriots game. The Bills knew the ball was going to Puka. And they still couldn’t stop it.

Here’s what makes Puka so impossible to defend in a game like this. He’s not a one-trick receiver. He wins on short routes, catching the ball and breaking tackles. He wins in the middle of the field, where he’s fearless in traffic. He wins on contested catches — he set a Pro Football Focus record last season with 27 contested catches. And he’s a nightmare after the catch. When a quarterback has a receiver like that, every option becomes a good option. Stafford threw to him 24 times because every time he looked, Puka was open — or close enough that it didn’t matter.

And Puka’s dominance opened up everything else. Remember the stat we talked about before the game: Davante Adams’ target rate drops when Puka is on the field. That’s exactly what happened. Davante didn’t need a huge volume night. He just needed to win in the biggest moments — and he did, catching two touchdown passes from four and five yards out. When the defense is collapsing on Puka, Davante gets the one-on-ones near the goal line. That’s the definition of a complementary receiving duo.

And let’s talk about Davante for a second, because his role in this offense is different now than it was a month ago. When Puka was out, Davante was the whole show — seven catches for 137 yards in Denver, with defenses rolling coverage his way and still not stopping him. Now, with Puka back, he’s become the closer near the goal line. He’s a veteran who’s been doing this at an elite level for more than a decade, and he understands that his job changes week to week. Some nights he’s the focal point. Some nights he’s the guy who catches the touchdown after the defense has spent all game chasing Puka. Monday night was the second kind — and it was exactly what the Rams needed.

Now — Matthew Stafford. And I want to spend real time here, because this was a statement game.

Forty completions on 54 attempts. 383 yards. Three touchdowns. Zero interceptions. Coming into Monday night, Stafford had thrown at least one interception in every game this season — according to ESPN Research, the longest streak like that to open a season in his career. He had six interceptions in four games. The criticism was loud. National panels called it “yolo ball.” And what did he say during the week? “Crawling in a hole — not an option.” He said he trusts his preparation and lives with the results. Then he went out on national television and played his cleanest game of the season against one of the league’s most aggressive pass rushes, led by Greg Rousseau, who came into the night leading the NFL in sacks.

That’s what a reigning MVP does. That’s what a 38-year-old veteran in his eighteenth season does when everyone is questioning him. He doesn’t argue on social media. He doesn’t make excuses. He completes 40 passes.

And remember the storyline coming into this game. According to the Rams’ official preview, this was only the second time in Monday Night Football history that the two starting quarterbacks were the two most recent MVPs: Josh Allen in 2024, Matthew Stafford in 2025. On the stat sheet, Stafford won that duel easily — 383 passing yards and three touchdowns, while Allen threw for just over 200 yards through the air. Allen beat the Rams with his legs, and his team won the game. But as a passer, on that night, Stafford was the better quarterback. That matters for the rest of the season, because it shows the reigning MVP is still capable of carrying this offense against elite competition.

And look at the last drive of regulation. Down 34-27 with 1:39 left. Sixty-two yards to go. Stafford moved the Rams down the field in just over a minute, hit Kyren Williams over the middle to set up the goal-line situation, and Kyren punched it in with five seconds left to tie the game. Last week McVay pointed to the 98-yard and 78-yard touchdown drives in Philadelphia and said Stafford “was at his best in the most important moments.” Monday night was more of the same. And in overtime, after the defense forced a fumble, Stafford immediately hit tight end Davis Allen for 21 yards and Puka for another 21 to get the Rams into field goal range. The offense did its job. Every time it needed a big play in the fourth quarter and overtime, it got one.

And there’s one more Stafford number worth highlighting. Coming into this game, according to Sharp Football Analysis, he had thrown for more than 300 yards in three straight games. With 383 on Monday night, that’s now four in a row. The yards were never the problem. The turnovers were. Take the turnovers away, and you’re looking at a quarterback putting together an MVP-caliber stretch — against Denver, Philadelphia and Buffalo, three of the toughest opponents on the schedule so far. If the interception problem is truly behind him, Stafford’s numbers over the rest of the season could be special.

After the game, Stafford said: “I love the fight that we had as a team.” And he added something that tells you exactly where his mind is: “Tough to find a perfect game. We’re gonna try to go out there and score every time we touch the ball.” That’s the mentality of a quarterback who knows this offense can be elite — and is finally starting to prove it.

And if you think about last season — when Stafford won the MVP and Puka led the NFL with 129 catches — this is what that offense looked like at its best. A quarterback getting the ball out on time, a receiver who wins every kind of route, a second receiver who punishes single coverage, and a running game that keeps defenses honest. For the first four weeks of this season, injuries kept that version of the offense off the field. Puka missed two games. The tight ends were hurt. Stafford was forcing throws to the one healthy receiver defenses were sitting on. Monday night was the first time in weeks that all the pieces were together — and the result was the best offensive performance of the season.

Now here’s the part of Monday night that I think matters most for the rest of the season: the red zone.

Coming into this game, the Rams had a strange problem. They ranked second in the NFL in total yards but just 19th in scoring. They moved the ball between the 20s better than almost anyone, and then stalled. Penalties in what Davante Adams called the “strike zone” or “fringe area” — right around the red zone — kept killing drives. The Rams had 21 accepted penalties over the previous two games, the most in back-to-back games in McVay’s ten seasons. Davante said before this game: “We have to make sure we’re not beating ourselves.”

Now look at Monday night. Five Rams touchdowns. Davante from four yards. Blake Corum from one yard. Puka from two yards. Davante again from five yards. Kyren from one yard. Every single touchdown came from five yards or closer. When the Rams got close, they finished. And the man who called out the problem during the week — Davante Adams — caught two of those touchdowns himself. That’s leadership by example.

And it was a team effort near the goal line. Tyler Higbee drew a pass interference penalty that set up Corum’s touchdown. Corum also had a 34-yard catch, showing he’s more than just a downhill runner. Kyren had the tying score with five seconds left. The two-back system with Kyren and Corum continues to give the Rams two different weapons — and both of them scored on Monday night.

Thirty-four points is the most the Rams have scored all season. Before Monday, their season high was 28 against the Giants. And they did it against a defense that, for all its struggles, has one of the best pass rushers in the NFL. This is what the Rams’ offense was supposed to look like when the season started: a healthy Puka, a productive Davante, the two-back rotation, tight ends back in the lineup, and a quarterback protecting the football.

And notice something else about this team: it doesn’t quit. In Philadelphia, the Rams came back from ten points down in the fourth quarter to win. On Monday night, they came back from seven points down with 1:39 left to force overtime. Two weeks in a row, this offense has delivered in the final minutes against good teams. You can’t teach that. Some teams fold when they fall behind late. This group has shown, twice now, that it gets sharper. If the Rams can carry that late-game edge into the rest of the season — and get a kicking game that finishes the job — they’ll win a lot of close games.

Let me also give credit to the tight ends, because their return was a big part of this. Colby Parkinson was back after missing the Eagles game with knee and shoulder injuries. Tyler Higbee made his impact with that pass interference drawn near the goal line. And Davis Allen made one of the biggest catches of the night — the 21-yard reception on the first play after the overtime fumble recovery. When the Rams were missing their tight ends earlier in the season, Stafford had fewer safe options in the middle of the field, and that’s part of why the interceptions piled up. With the tight ends back, the middle of the field opened up again. And notice: no interceptions.

Now, let’s be fair, because I promised you both sides. Buffalo’s defense came into this game ranked 30th in the NFL in opponent EPA per play, and it was missing Ed Oliver, one of its best interior defenders. The Bills also committed 13 penalties for 112 yards, which helped extend Rams drives. So yes, the matchup favored the Rams’ offense. But you still have to execute — and the Rams did. Against a weaker defense, the 2026 Rams had sometimes still stalled because of their own mistakes. This time, they didn’t.

And this performance matters because of what’s coming next. The Rams host the Arizona Cardinals this Sunday at SoFi. Then they go to Las Vegas, host the Chargers, and travel to Washington. If this offense keeps scoring 30-plus points with clean football, the Rams can win a lot of games in a row — and they need to, because they’re now three games behind the undefeated 49ers in the NFC West.

So why did the Rams lose a game in which their offense played this well? We covered it in detail in our last video, so I’ll keep it short. The kicking game failed: Mevis missed from 50 and had a 48-yarder blocked in overtime, and he’s now five of nine on field goals this season. The run defense struggled: Buffalo ran for over 230 yards, with James Cook going for 111 yards and two touchdowns and Josh Allen running for 121 yards — most of it on scrambles. And McVay admitted he regretted not being more aggressive before the overtime kick: “I wish I would have had a better play call and gone for it, got it a lot tighter.” All of those things are real, and all of them have to be fixed.

The national reaction tells you how good this game was, even in a loss. NBC News called it “the thriller of the NFL season — and perhaps a Super Bowl preview.” That’s not how people talk about a team that’s falling apart. It’s how they talk about two contenders who went blow for blow under the lights. Before the game, Mike Florio said this wouldn’t be a true Super Bowl preview unless the Rams started winning more consistently — and that’s the challenge now. The Rams proved they can play with anybody. Now they have to prove they can finish.

But here’s the key distinction. Those are problems in specific areas — the kicking game, the run defense, one late-game decision. The offense, the part of the team that had been the biggest question mark for weeks because of the turnovers and penalties, just played its best game of the year. If you had told Rams fans before the game that Stafford would throw zero interceptions, Puka would catch 17 passes, and the Rams would score 34 points, every single one of them would have expected a win. The foundation is there. The finishing pieces aren’t — yet.

Let me also mention the context on the other side, because Buffalo deserves credit. Josh Allen earned his 100th career win on Monday night — and his first career overtime win after being winless in his previous seven overtime games, including the playoffs. Khalil Shakir caught three passes for 80 yards, with most of those coming in the fourth quarter and overtime, including a 57-yard catch in the extra period. Greg Rousseau blocked the kick that changed the game. That’s a good football team. And the Rams went toe-to-toe with them for 60 minutes and more.

And here’s something else I’m taking from this game: the defense still made plays when it mattered, even on a night it struggled against the run. Jaylen Watson, playing in a shoulder harness after dislocating his shoulder in Denver, intercepted Josh Allen. Nate Landman, who missed practice early in the week, forced two fumbles — including the one in overtime that Aaron Donald recovered. Josaiah Stewart had a third-down sack that held Buffalo to a field goal. Allen had just 60 passing yards and 21 rushing yards at halftime. The defense needs to tighten up against the run, but it’s not broken — and with Myles Garrett expected back around Week 9, according to reporting from Ian Rapoport, help is coming.

Now let’s think about what this means for the big picture. A week ago, the biggest question about the Rams was whether Stafford could stop turning the ball over and whether this offense could turn yards into points. Monday night answered both questions. Zero interceptions. Thirty-four points. Five touchdowns from inside the five. The questions now are about special teams and run defense — areas that can be addressed with coaching, personnel decisions and, potentially, small moves before the November 10th trade deadline. McVay already said he’s going to “evaluate everything.” That’s a better list of problems to have than a quarterback who can’t stop throwing interceptions.

And for Puka, this game might be the moment he officially entered a new tier. Last season, he led the NFL with 129 catches and finished third in Offensive Player of the Year voting. This season, injuries slowed his start. But two games back, he has 26 catches and 263 yards. If he stays healthy, there’s no reason he can’t put together another historic season — and help carry the Rams back into the playoff race.

The one thing to watch this week is his workload. Twenty-four targets is a massive number for any receiver, let alone one who was dealing with a groin injury two weeks ago. Keep an eye on the practice reports leading up to the Cardinals game. If Puka is a full participant, that’s a great sign. If the Rams limit him early in the week, don’t panic — that’s smart management for a player this important. The Rams need Puka in January, not just in October. But after what he showed on Monday night, it’s hard to imagine any defense on the schedule that can contain him when he’s on the field.

Let me grade the offense from Monday night. Stafford: A. Clean, efficient, clutch. Puka: A-plus — historic. Davante: A-minus — limited volume, but two touchdowns exactly when they were needed. Running backs: B-plus — two touchdowns and big plays, though the run game wasn’t the focus. Tight ends: B — important contributions in key spots. Offensive line: B — Stafford threw 54 times against one of the league’s best pass rushers and came out with zero interceptions, though the line allowed the sack on the two-point attempt. Overall offense: A-minus. The only thing missing was the win.

And let me add a word about McVay’s play-calling, because it deserves credit even after the overtime regret. Fifty-four pass attempts is a lot, but it made sense: Buffalo’s pass defense had been one of the most generous in the league, and the Rams had a receiver the Bills couldn’t cover. McVay attacked the weakness relentlessly. He also mixed in Corum and Kyren near the goal line, which is how the Rams turned so many drives into touchdowns. The one decision he wants back is the third-down call before the overtime kick — and he owned it publicly. For most of the night, the play-calling put the offense in a position to win.

And McVay’s message to the team says it all: “This stings, and it’s going to hurt. But we can’t let this team beat us twice.” In other words: don’t let this loss steal the confidence this offense just earned. Take what worked — the clean football, the red zone execution, Puka’s dominance — and carry it into Sunday against Arizona.

And the Cardinals game is the perfect chance to do exactly that. Arizona came into this past weekend at 1-3, winless since its Week 1 victory. The Rams host them on Sunday at 1:05 p.m. Pacific at SoFi, and then face them again on the road in Week 10. Two games against the team at the bottom of the division in a span of five weeks. If this offense plays the way it played on Monday night, the Rams should be heavy favorites in both. And those are exactly the games a team three games behind in the division has to win.

Here’s my bottom line, Rams House. The scoreboard says the Bills won. But if you watched this game closely, you saw an offense that destroyed one of the AFC’s top teams for four quarters and overtime. Puka Nacua made Monday Night Football history. Matthew Stafford answered every critic with his cleanest game of the season. Davante Adams practiced what he preached in the red zone. And the Rams scored more points than they had all year. The kicking game and the run defense have to be fixed — immediately. But the offense? The offense looks like it’s back. And if the rest of the team catches up to it, this season is far from over.
