Let’s start with who Jerry Jeudy is, because a lot of people only remember the headlines.

Jeudy was a star at Alabama, and the Denver Broncos took him 15th overall in the 2020 draft. At the 2020 combine, he ran a 4.45 40-yard dash. But the thing scouts loved most wasn’t his speed. It was his route running. Coming out of college, he was called one of the best route runners in that draft class — sharp breaks, quick feet, the ability to create separation without needing to be the biggest guy on the field.

His time in Denver was up and down. The talent was obvious, but the production never fully matched it. Denver went through quarterbacks and coaches, and Jeudy never became the true number one many expected. According to Pro Football Rumors, Denver even turned down an offer of a third- and a fifth-round pick for him at the 2023 deadline. Then in March 2024, the Broncos traded him to Cleveland for a fifth- and a sixth-round pick.

And here’s where the story gets interesting. In his first season in Cleveland, Jeudy broke out. In 2024, he caught 90 passes for 1,229 yards and four touchdowns, and he made the Pro Bowl. Cleveland rewarded him with a three-year, 52.5 million dollar extension.

Then 2025 happened. According to Bleacher Report, his production dropped to 50 catches, 602 yards and two touchdowns. And in 2026, his role in Cleveland has shrunk even more.

So let me be clear on one detail from that clip: Jeudy is not 25. He’s 27. Still in his prime for a receiver, but not the young prospect that clip makes him sound like.

Now, why has Jeudy become available? It comes down to the Browns’ draft.

In the 2026 draft, Cleveland took receiver KC Concepcion in the first round and Denzel Boston in the second round. According to CBS Sports’ Carter Bahns, those two rookies — along with tight end Harold Fannin — became the face of Cleveland’s passing game right away. Bahns wrote that the Browns are “clearly committed to its newest weapons as the present and future of the offense.” Through the first three games, according to CBS, Jeudy had just two catches on five targets.

Through five games, according to Bleacher Report, Jeudy had eight catches for 81 yards and no touchdowns. Then last Sunday against Baltimore, he had his best game of the season. According to the Browns’ official website, he led Cleveland with 69 receiving yards, and his 33-yard catch on the final drive set up Andre Szmyt’s game-winning 37-yard field goal as time expired. The Browns won 13-10. According to Draft Sharks, he caught all four of his targets in that game.

So add it all up: through six games, Jeudy has 12 catches for 150 yards and no touchdowns. For a 2024 Pro Bowler, those are tiny numbers. But that Ravens game also showed something important — when he gets the ball in a big moment, he can still make the play.

Now here’s the part a lot of people miss: why Jeudy’s contract makes him one of the easiest receivers in the league to trade for.

According to Pro Football Rumors, Jeudy’s 2026 base salary is only 1.5 million dollars. That sounds like a minimum deal, but it isn’t the whole story. The rest of his money is structured elsewhere in the contract, and according to Heavy, Cleveland would carry a large dead-cap hit if it moved him. So the low salary isn’t a sign that nobody values him. It’s just how the deal was built.

Then there’s 2027. According to Pro Football Rumors, Jeudy has a non-guaranteed 21.49 million dollar option bonus due next year. If a team doesn’t want to pay it, it can move on after this season. So for an acquiring team, Jeudy can be a low-cost rental: 1.5 million dollars for the rest of 2026, and then a decision.

For the Rams, that matters a lot. They don’t have much salary-cap room after the big moves this year. A receiver who costs about a million and a half dollars is one of the few kinds of players they can actually afford without moving other money around.

And it matters for draft picks, too. According to Sports Illustrated, the Rams don’t have their 2027 first-round pick or their 2028 second-round pick because of the Myles Garrett trade. They’ve also moved other picks in the Trent McDuffie deal. So the Rams can’t go around the league offering second-round picks for every receiver who becomes available. If they’re going to add someone, it has to be a Day 3 price. And Jeudy might be the best receiver available at that price.

There’s also a relationship angle here. Geitheim pointed out that the Rams and the Browns just worked together on the Myles Garrett trade. When two front offices have already completed a major deal, the next phone call is a lot easier. That doesn’t mean a trade will happen. But Les Snead and Andrew Berry already know how to do business together.

Okay, so why would Jeudy make sense for the Rams on the field?

Start with the obvious: Davante Adams is out. Through six games, according to Gridiron Heroics via Yahoo Sports, Adams had 30 catches for 480 yards and four touchdowns. And according to the AP, last season Matthew Stafford threw to his depth receivers less than any other quarterback on a playoff team. This offense was built around two receivers — Puka Nacua and Adams. Now one of them is gone for at least six weeks.

Behind Puka, the Rams’ receivers have been quiet. According to the AP, Konata Mumpfield has eight catches for 127 yards — the third-most by a Rams receiver this season. Jordan Whittington, Tutu Atwell, Alex Bachman and rookie CJ Daniels are all options, and Colby Parkinson and Tyler Higbee give Stafford reliable tight ends. But none of them has done what Jeudy did in 2024 — a 1,229-yard season.

Here’s where the fit gets interesting. Jeudy’s best skill has always been route running. And Sean McVay’s offense is built on receivers winning with timing, footwork and understanding the defense — not just raw speed. The clip was right about that part. A receiver who can run every route on the tree, line up outside or in the slot, and get open quickly is exactly what Stafford likes.

And Jeudy wouldn’t have to be the star. Puka would still be the number one. Jeudy would be the second option — the guy who wins when defenses roll their coverage toward Puka. That’s a much easier job than carrying an offense by yourself.

There’s also the workload issue. Puka was targeted 24 times against Buffalo, and he’s already dealt with a hip and groin injury this season. If the Rams keep forcing the ball to Puka every single play with Adams out, they risk wearing down their best player before December. A veteran like Jeudy would take some of that pressure off.

Now let me be fair and give you the case against it, because there are real concerns.

Concern number one: the Browns are winning. According to the Browns’ official website, Cleveland is 4-2 and in first place in the AFC North after beating Baltimore. Teams in first place usually don’t sell veteran receivers in October. And after that game, cleveland.com’s Ellis L. Labbe argued that the trade talk should end, pointing to how much Deshaun Watson trusts Jeudy. Browns head coach Todd Monken has also said Jeudy has earned more opportunities. If Cleveland believes it can win the division, it might want Jeudy’s experience for the stretch run.

Concern number two: depth. Earlier this season, according to Draft Sharks, Concepcion left a game early with a concussion, and Jeudy’s targets went up right away. That’s exactly why a contender keeps a veteran third receiver around. Injuries happen. Cleveland knows that.

Concern number three: the production. Twelve catches in six games is not a lot. Some of that is his role in Cleveland, but some of it is fair to question. His numbers dropped from 1,229 yards in 2024 to 602 yards in 2025. The Rams would be betting that the 2024 version is still in there.

And concern number four: timing. The deadline is November 10th. Even if the Rams traded for Jeudy, he’d need to learn the offense quickly. McVay’s system is complex. A receiver who arrives in November might need a couple of weeks before he’s fully comfortable. That’s not a reason to avoid a trade, but it’s a reason not to expect miracles overnight.

So where does that leave us? A trade is possible, but it isn’t close as far as anyone has reported. And with Cleveland winning, it might be harder to pull off than it looked two weeks ago.

Now, the clip compared a Jeudy trade to the Randy Moss deal, so let’s talk about that, because the history is real.

In 2007, the New England Patriots traded a fourth-round pick to the Oakland Raiders for Randy Moss. Moss had fallen out of favor in Oakland. In his first season in New England, he caught 23 touchdown passes, which was a single-season record. It’s one of the best value trades in NFL history.

But let’s be honest. Moss was a future Hall of Famer, and he was catching passes from Tom Brady in his prime. That’s not a fair comparison for any receiver, including Jeudy.

Here’s a better comparison, and it’s Jeudy himself. In March 2024, Cleveland traded a fifth- and a sixth-round pick to Denver for Jeudy. Most people shrugged. Then he went out and had 1,229 yards and made the Pro Bowl. That’s the real precedent. Jeudy has already been a Day 3 trade once — and it worked. The question is whether it can work twice.

And there’s a twist: Jeudy’s 1,229-yard season came with a rotating cast of Browns quarterbacks. Imagine what he could do with a quarterback like Stafford, who knows exactly where to put the ball on timing routes. That’s the upside the Rams would be betting on.

That’s really the key to this whole story. The Rams’ young receivers are auditioning right now — not just for playing time, but for whether the front office spends a draft pick.

Here are my three keys for Sunday.

Key number one: spread the ball around. Stafford needs at least four or five different players with three or more catches. If it’s Puka or nothing, the Raiders will double him and dare anyone else to beat them.

Key number two: protect Stafford. Without Adams, Stafford may have to hold the ball a little longer while receivers get open. The offensive line has to give him that time.

Key number three: no self-inflicted wounds. McVay said it after the Arizona loss: “We won’t win a game unless we figure out how not to beat ourselves.” No false starts on fourth down, no fumbles in plus territory, no costly penalties in the final minute.
