Let’s start with Davante Adams, because this is the injury that changes everything.

Late in Sunday’s loss to the Cardinals, on the Rams’ final drive, Adams pulled up suddenly while running a route, hopped in the back of the end zone and limped off. There was no contact. On Monday, McVay confirmed the diagnosis: a ruptured hamstring. The timeline is six to 10 weeks. According to Raiders Wire, Adams has been placed on injured reserve. And according to Sports Illustrated, if he needs the full 10 weeks, he’d return with only a couple of regular-season games left.

Let’s be clear about what the Rams are losing. Adams played in all six games this season. According to Sports Illustrated, he caught 30 of his 49 targets for 480 yards and four touchdowns, and he was on the field for 75.5 percent of the offensive snaps. According to Yahoo Sports, he led the Rams in receiving yards. And remember, when Puka Nacua missed two games with a hip and groin injury earlier this year, Adams carried this passing game almost by himself.

He’s 33 years old, in his 13th NFL season, and a six-time Pro Bowler. He’s also the veteran voice in that receiver room — the guy who called out the penalties in the red zone and then caught two touchdowns against Buffalo to back it up. Losing him hurts on the field and in the locker room.

And the timing is brutal. The Rams were just starting to see what this offense looks like with both Puka and Adams healthy. Since Puka came back from his injury, he’s caught nine passes for 125 yards against the Eagles, 17 for 138 against the Bills, and six for 80 against the Cardinals. That’s 32 catches in three games. A big reason defenses couldn’t just double Puka was that Adams was on the other side. Now that balance is gone. Every defensive coordinator on the Rams’ schedule is going to roll coverage toward Puka and dare someone else to beat them.

And the betting markets reacted immediately. According to Sports Illustrated, the Rams opened the season as the betting favorite to win the Super Bowl at plus-650 on DraftKings. After the Adams news, their odds moved to plus-1000, then plus-1200. They’re now minus-120 just to make the playoffs. That tells you how much the market thinks this one injury matters.

So when could Adams be back? Let’s do the math on McVay’s timeline. Six weeks from the Cardinals game would put us around the end of November. The Rams’ bye comes in Week 11, and their first game after the bye is against the Green Bay Packers. So in the best-case scenario, Adams could return right around that Packers game. Ten weeks would push it to late December, close to the Christmas Day game in Seattle. That means the Rams will likely have to play at least five or six games without him — including the stretch that decides whether they’re buyers at the trade deadline. If they can stay afloat until he returns, a healthy Adams in December could feel like a deadline acquisition of its own.

But Adams wasn’t the only bad news on Monday. According to the Los Angeles Times, this was a rash of injuries.

Defensive lineman Kobie Turner has been placed on injured reserve with a groin injury he suffered against the Cardinals. Turner has 22 tackles and two pass breakups this season. Defensive tackle Poona Ford is also sidelined. That’s two starters on the defensive line. According to the Times, Aaron Donald and Braden Fiske are now the remaining starters up front, with Tyler Davis, Larrell Murchison and Ty Hamilton taking on bigger roles.

Myles Garrett? McVay said the Rams still won’t open his 21-day practice window this week. According to RotoWire, there’s no indication of a setback — he simply needs more rehab time. But that means no Garrett against the Raiders either.

Blake Grupe, the kicker the Rams signed last Tuesday, was placed on injured reserve after hurting his knee on the kickoff following his 44-yard field goal on Sunday. According to Heavy, that means he’ll miss at least four games.

And Trent McDuffie, who missed the Cardinals game with a groin injury, is “progressing,” according to the Times, with a decision on his status for Las Vegas expected late in the week.

So let me put that in perspective. In one week, the Rams lost a Pro Bowl receiver for most of the season, two defensive line starters, and their new kicker — while their All-Pro pass rusher and their best cornerback are still waiting to return. That’s a lot for any team to absorb.

And the defensive line injuries matter a lot for Sunday. The Rams’ run defense has already been a weak spot — according to Fox Sports, they’re allowing 127 rushing yards per game. Now they’ll face the Raiders without Kobie Turner and Poona Ford in the middle. The good news is that Las Vegas hasn’t run the ball well, averaging just 79.2 rushing yards per game. But the Rams will need Aaron Donald, Braden Fiske and Byron Young to carry a heavier load, and the young backups will have to hold up against the run. And if McDuffie is cleared to return, that would be a huge boost against a quarterback like Kirk Cousins, who has thrown 14 touchdown passes already this season.

And here’s how McVay responded. He said: “We’ve taken some shots this year.” He said: “These are adverse moments and we’ve got to be able to push through and do hard things.” And then: “We’ve got to be able to get up and keep swingin’.” He even called the stretch ahead “a good test.” And safety Quentin Lake put it perfectly: there’s “no sense of panic,” but “there has to be a sense of urgency.”

Now, the kicker. Because the Rams have to solve this before Sunday.

According to NFL Network’s Mike Garafolo, the Rams are working out two veteran kickers: Brandon McManus and Matt Prater. Combined, they have more than three decades of NFL experience. Prater is 42 years old, and he last kicked for the Buffalo Bills in 2025, making 18 of 20 kicks with a long of 52. McManus is 34 and last kicked for the Green Bay Packers in 2025, where he made 10 of 10 kicks with a long of 56.

And that’s exactly what I said the Rams needed in our last video: a veteran with real game experience. After going from Mevis to Grupe to punter Ethan Evans kicking a go-ahead extra point in the same week, the Rams can’t afford another learning curve. Whichever of these two they choose, he’ll be the Rams’ sixth kicker in less than 60 games, according to the AP’s count that Grupe was the fifth over the past 57 games.

And both of these kickers bring real résumés. McManus won a Super Bowl with the Denver Broncos in the 2015 season and has made some of the biggest kicks of the last decade. Prater, also a former Bronco, set the NFL record at the time with a 64-yard field goal in Denver back in 2013, and he’s kicked for more than 15 seasons. One brings a strong, recent track record — 10 for 10 last year. The other brings unmatched experience. Either way, the Rams are finally choosing proven veterans over projects. After the last two weeks, that’s exactly the right call.

Now let’s get to the receiver question.

Without Adams, here’s what the Rams’ receiver room looks like, according to the Los Angeles Times: Puka Nacua, Jordan Whittington, Konata Mumpfield, Tutu Atwell and rookie CJ Daniels. According to Sports Illustrated, Puka and Whittington have both missed time this season too. That’s not a lot of proven depth behind Puka. And Atwell and Daniels were both inactive on Sunday.

So it makes sense that, according to the Times, the Rams may add a receiver. But “may add” is not the same as “have added.” That’s the honest state of things right now.

And that brings us to Brian Thomas Jr.

On Monday, SB Nation’s Brendan Dzwierzynski wrote a piece titled, “Should the Rams go after Brian Thomas Jr.?” Let me be very clear about what that article is: it’s an analyst asking whether the Rams should make a call. It does not say a trade is happening, that an offer has been made, or that the teams are talking. But it raises a fascinating idea. So let’s take it seriously.

Who is Brian Thomas Jr.? He was the 23rd overall pick in the 2024 draft out of LSU, and his rookie season was spectacular. According to RotoWire, he caught 87 passes for 1,282 yards and 10 touchdowns. He looked like one of the brightest young receivers in football — a big, fast target who could take the top off a defense.

Then his second season took a big step back. In 2025, he finished with 707 yards and just two touchdowns in 14 games. According to RotoWire, he was one of the biggest fantasy busts of the year.

And 2026 has been even worse. Through six games, according to RotoWire, Thomas has nine catches for 105 yards and one touchdown on 20 targets. He has zero catches in his last two games. He’s fallen to the third receiver on Jacksonville’s depth chart, behind Parker Washington and Jakobi Meyers. And on Sunday in London, in a 34-31 loss to the Texans, it came to a head. Thomas was targeted three times in the first half, dropped a pass, and didn’t catch anything. According to ESPN Research, he played 15 snaps in the first half — and zero in the second half. There was no injury. He was benched.

And when you look at his game log this season, according to RotoWire, the decline is clear: three catches for 40 yards against Denver in Week 2, one catch for eight yards in Week 3, two catches for 17 yards and a touchdown against Cincinnati in Week 4, and then zero catches against the Eagles and zero against the Texans. Meanwhile, Travis Hunter has started to take on a bigger offensive role — he caught four passes and a touchdown against Houston — and rookie Josh Cameron has taken snaps from Thomas, according to Heavy.

After the game, Jaguars head coach Liam Coen — who, by the way, was Sean McVay’s offensive coordinator with the Rams in 2022 — made his message clear. He said: “Just week to week. Week to week. Guys have to step up and make plays.”

And that Coen connection is worth thinking about. Coen worked under McVay and knows exactly what the Rams’ offense asks of its receivers. If anyone knows whether Brian Thomas Jr. could fit in a McVay system, it’s the Jaguars’ head coach. That could cut both ways. If Coen thinks Thomas could thrive in that offense, he might not want to hand him to a team that could rejuvenate him. Or, if the relationship is strong, it could make a deal easier to discuss. Either way, these two coaching staffs know each other well.

So is Jacksonville ready to move on? According to RotoWire, teams have reportedly turned their attention to Thomas, but the Jaguars are not in a rush to trade him. And back in March, Jaguars general manager James Gladstone called trade chatter about Thomas “fraudulent.” Benching a player for a half isn’t the same as shopping him. Jacksonville is a winning team this year, and they might still believe he can turn it around.

Now let’s talk about why Thomas could make sense for the Rams — because there are real reasons.

First, his skill set. Thomas is a vertical threat with undeniable speed. Puka wins with physicality, contested catches and yards after the catch. Adams wins with precise route running. Thomas would give Stafford something different: a deep threat who forces safeties to stay back. That could open space underneath for Puka and Kyren Williams.

Second, the Rams’ scoring problem. According to SB Nation, citing The Ringer’s Sheil Kapadia, no team has outgained its opponents by more yardage this year than the Rams. But they rank just 16th in touchdown rate. And according to Sports Illustrated, the Rams rank 10th in EPA per play and sixth in success rate — but only 19th in points scored. This offense moves the ball. It just doesn’t finish. Big plays are one of the fastest ways to score without needing to grind out long drives. Stafford said it himself after the Arizona game: “We are what our record is.” And Puka added: “I think there’s just a lack of execution, obviously on our offensive side of the ball.” Even so, Puka made sure to say the locker room is still together: “We’re still a connected group.” That’s the attitude this team needs right now.

And third — this is the part I didn’t expect — his contract could actually fit. According to Spotrac, Thomas is on a four-year rookie deal worth about 14.66 million dollars, fully guaranteed, running through 2027 with a fifth-year option available. His 2026 cap hit is about four million dollars. But here’s the key: his 2026 base salary is only about 1.075 million. When a player is traded, his original signing bonus stays on the old team’s books, and the new team takes on the remaining base salary. By the November 10th deadline, only a fraction of that 1.075 million would be left. For a Rams team with roughly one million dollars in cap space, that matters a lot. It’s one of the few young receivers who could realistically fit.

So there’s a real case. Now let me give you the case against it, because it’s just as real.

First, the drops and the confidence. According to the SB Nation piece, Thomas’ drops have been blamed on a “fear of contact.” That’s a serious concern for a receiver who’d be asked to make tough catches in a playoff race. And a player who has three catches in his last four games isn’t a plug-and-play replacement for a six-time Pro Bowler.

Second, the price. SB Nation pointed out that Marvin Harrison Jr. was traded from Arizona to Houston earlier this month for a 2027 second-round pick and a 2027 sixth-round pick. Thomas’ best season was better than Harrison’s best season. So if Jacksonville asks for something similar, that’s a big price. And the Rams have already traded their 2027 first-round pick, their 2027 third-rounder, their 2028 second-rounder and their 2029 third-rounder in the Garrett and McDuffie trades. Spending another high pick on a struggling receiver would be a huge gamble for a 2-4 team.

Third, the timing. Even if the Rams made a trade this week, Thomas would have to learn McVay’s system on the fly. That’s not easy. McVay’s offense depends on timing, motion and precise spacing. A player coming off a benching, learning a new playbook in the middle of the season, might not help much before the bye.

And fourth, the honest truth: a new receiver doesn’t fix what’s actually losing games. Against Arizona, the Rams lost because of a kickoff return, a fumble deep in Cardinals territory, a false start on fourth down, a failed two-point try, and a 15-yard penalty on the final drive. McVay said it himself after the game: “We won’t win a game unless we figure out how not to beat ourselves.”

Now, let’s compare Thomas to the other receiver names we’ve talked about on this channel, because this is starting to become a real conversation.

Courtland Sutton: not happening. He signed a four-year, 92 million dollar extension in Denver, the Broncos are 4-2, and the money doesn’t work.

Jerry Jeudy: a real trade candidate. His 2026 base salary is 1.5 million dollars, and an executive told Josina Anderson a fourth- or fifth-round pick could get it done — although Jordan Schultz reported the Browns want a third or a fourth. He’s 27, made the Pro Bowl in 2024, but has just 81 yards through five games this season.

Brian Thomas Jr.: younger, faster, with a higher ceiling — but also a bigger price, a recent benching and real concerns about drops.

Here’s how I see it, Rams House. Jeudy is the safer, cheaper short-term fix. Thomas is the higher-upside, higher-risk swing. And for a team with Super Bowl expectations and almost no draft capital left, the Rams have to be honest about which one they can afford.

And there’s an in-house option, too. Tutu Atwell had 105 catches for 1,535 yards and five touchdowns in his first five seasons with the Rams. He knows this offense, and he brings speed. McVay could also lean even harder into three-tight-end sets with Colby Parkinson, Tyler Higbee and Davis Allen, and get Kyren Williams more involved as a receiver. Kyren already has 27-plus catches this season. Sometimes the best answer at the deadline is the one already in the building. Jordan Whittington has already been handling kickoffs and has earned McVay’s trust as a tough, physical player. Konata Mumpfield has been part of the rotation. And rookie CJ Daniels will likely get his first real opportunity now. None of them is Davante Adams. But together, with Puka leading the way, they can at least make defenses respect more than one target.

So here’s my verdict on Brian Thomas Jr. I like the idea. I don’t like the price. If Jacksonville wants a second-round pick, the Rams should walk away. If the Jaguars would take a Day 3 pick for a player they’ve just benched, then it’s worth a call — because his salary fits, and his speed is exactly what this offense lacks. But I’d put Jeudy slightly ahead as the more realistic option, simply because the cost would likely be lower and he’s already shown he can adjust to a new team. And remember, the deadline isn’t the only window. If Jacksonville’s price stays high now, Thomas’ situation could look very different in the offseason. Teams that bench a young receiver in October sometimes become much more willing to move him in March. The Rams don’t have to force this today.

Now let’s look ahead to Sunday, because no trade is going to save the Rams this weekend. They have to do it with the players they have.

The Rams travel to Las Vegas to face the Raiders. According to Fox Sports, Las Vegas is 3-3 under head coach Klint Kubiak, with Kirk Cousins at quarterback. Cousins has thrown for 1,582 yards, 14 touchdowns and five interceptions. The Raiders are averaging 27 points per game, so this is not a pushover offense. But their defense has been vulnerable, allowing 27 points per game and more than 237 passing yards per game. The Rams are 5.5-point favorites, with an over/under of 47.5.

For the Rams, the numbers show what this game should look like. According to Fox Sports, the Rams are averaging 288.7 passing yards per game, and they’re allowing only 167.0 through the air. Stafford has 1,794 passing yards, 11 touchdowns and six interceptions. If the Rams can protect him and finish drives, this is a game they should win — even without Adams.

And let’s be honest about the standings. According to Fox Sports, the Rams are fourth in the NFC West right now. Fourth. A team that opened the season as the Super Bowl favorite is at the bottom of its own division after six weeks. That’s why Sunday matters so much. A win gets them to 3-4 with the Chargers coming to SoFi next. A loss drops them to 2-5 and puts real pressure on the front office heading into the deadline.

And it’s a little bittersweet, too. Adams spent parts of three seasons with the Raiders. This was supposed to be his trip back to Las Vegas. According to Raiders Wire, he’ll miss that reunion now.

So here are my three keys for Sunday.

Key number one: Puka has to be Puka. Without Adams, defenses will try to double him. The Rams have to scheme him open with motion, bunch formations and quick throws — and Stafford has to trust him in big moments. Remember, Puka caught 17 passes against Buffalo. When the Rams need a play, he’s the guy.

Key number two: the new kicker has to make his kicks. Whether it’s McManus or Prater, the Rams need a clean operation. After three straight weeks of kicking drama, a quiet day would be a beautiful thing.

Key number three: no self-inflicted wounds. No false starts on fourth down. No fumbles in the red zone. No 15-yard penalties on the final drive. McVay has said it again and again. Sunday is the week the Rams have to prove they can actually do it.

Brian Thomas Jr. is an interesting name, but he’s a project, not a savior. And the only thing that will truly save this season is cleaner football, starting Sunday in Las Vegas.
