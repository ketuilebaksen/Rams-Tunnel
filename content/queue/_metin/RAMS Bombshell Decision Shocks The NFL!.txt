How the personal conduct policy works

Since a lot of fans aren’t sure how this process works, let me explain it simply.

The NFL’s personal conduct policy covers what players do off the field. It’s separate from the legal system. That means the league can discipline a player even if there’s no criminal conviction, and even while a civil case is still going on. The league does its own review and makes its own decision.

When the league decides to discipline a player, the player and the NFL Players Association can challenge it. But sometimes, like in this case, the two sides negotiate a settlement instead. According to Tom Pelissero, that’s what happened here. The NFL and the NFLPA negotiated the three-game suspension, and it’s final.

Suspensions under the conduct policy are typically without pay, and that’s the case here.

So when you hear “the NFL acted before the civil case was over,” that’s normal under this policy. The league’s process and the court’s process are two separate things.

Part three: why he won’t miss extra games

Now let’s talk about the football side.

Normally, when a star player gets suspended, the big question is: how will the team survive without him?

But in this case, the Rams were already preparing to play without Puka. He suffered MCL and PCL sprains in his knee plus turf toe against Washington in Week 9. He was placed on injured reserve on November 12. And under IR rules, he has to miss at least four games. The bye week doesn’t count as one of them.

So he was already going to miss Arizona, Green Bay, Kansas City, and San Francisco.

According to the Associated Press, NFL rules allow him to serve the three-game suspension at the same time. That means the suspension covers games he was already going to miss because of his injury.

So the Rams’ plan on the field doesn’t change. They were already building an offense without Puka. They’ll keep doing that.

But remember, that’s eligibility, not a guarantee. He still has to recover from his knee and toe injuries. His health will decide when he’s ready, not the calendar.

Here’s where the suspension does have a real effect: his paycheck.

According to the Associated Press, Puka will not be paid during the suspension.

That’s important, because a player on injured reserve usually keeps getting paid. But a suspension is different. The league’s discipline overrides that. So Puka loses the game checks for those three weeks.

The original video also made a point about his contract. Puka is still on his rookie deal, so his salary right now is much smaller than what he could earn on a future extension. If this had happened after a big new contract, the money he lost would have been much bigger.

That doesn’t make the situation good. Losing pay is still a punishment. But it explains why some people are talking about the timing.

That’s a serious warning. It means this is not just a three-game issue. The league is saying the next step could be much worse.

So for Puka, the road forward is clear. Complete whatever the league requires. Stay out of trouble. Focus on recovering and playing football.

The IR designation math still matters

Here’s one more football detail that connects to Puka’s return.

According to SB Nation’s Turf Show Times, the Rams have only four injured reserve return designations left this regular season. They started with eight and already used them on an offensive tackle in October, Terrance Ferguson, Myles Garrett, and Ronnie Rivers.

Even after the suspension ends, Puka will still be on injured reserve. So to bring him back, the Rams will still need to use one of those four remaining designations.

The Turf Show Times writer predicted Davante Adams, Puka, and Kobie Turner would get three of the four. That would leave only one for players like Justin Dedich, Dylan McMahon, Dean Connors, and Jordan Whittington.

So the suspension doesn’t change the IR math. But it’s one more reason the Rams have to be careful with every roster decision from here on.

Part ten: now, the good football news, the “pony” formation

Okay, let’s shift to the football story that’s actually fun.

McVay found a new weapon in Arizona, and the original video called it “pony personnel.”

What is pony personnel? It’s another name for 20 personnel: two running backs, zero tight ends, and three wide receivers. For the Rams, that meant Kyren Williams and Blake Corum on the field at the same time.

And it worked. According to the original video, in the 17 plays the Rams ran from this formation, Stafford completed 12 of 16 passes for 202 yards and a touchdown. ESPN’s Sarah Barshop, citing NFL Next Gen Stats, had the total production from those 17 plays at 197 yards and a touchdown. The numbers are a little different depending on how you count them, but either way, it was almost 12 yards per play.

That’s incredible for a formation the Rams had never used before.

Part eleven: why pony personnel is so hard to defend

Here’s why this is such a big deal.

Defenses almost never see two running backs and three receivers on the field at the same time. They don’t practice against it much. So they’re not sure how to match up.

Think about it from the defense’s side. If you put in extra linebackers to stop the run, now you have linebackers trying to cover a running back split out wide, or a receiver in the slot. If you put in extra defensive backs to cover the pass, now you’re small against two running backs who can run inside.

Either way, you’re making a choice you’re not comfortable with. And that’s exactly what McVay wants.

McVay explained it after the game. He said it was “a way to be able to get two of our best players on the field at the same time, with Blake and Kyren.” And he said it was “something different that we hadn’t put on tape yet.”

And Corum loved it. According to Barshop, he joked that he wished he could have seen the faces of the Cardinals defenders when they saw two running backs line up together.

Part twelve: McVay’s history of breaking the rules

This isn’t the first time McVay has done something like this.

He’s already made the Rams one of the league’s most aggressive users of three-tight-end formations, 13 personnel. A lot of teams barely use it. McVay leaned into it.

And this season, before Arizona, the Rams used 11 personnel, three receivers and one tight end, on 53% of their plays. Against Arizona, they used it only three times. Instead, they went heavy with 13 personnel and introduced the pony look.

That’s a coach who isn’t afraid to throw out his normal plan when it doesn’t fit his players. And it’s a big reason the Rams won 34-6 without Puka and Adams.

Part thirteen: how pony personnel helps without Puka

Here’s how the two stories connect.

Puka is out. Davante Adams is out, with McVay saying “after Green Bay is a realistic timeline.” So the Rams need new ways to move the ball.

The pony look gives them one. Instead of forcing young receivers to carry the offense, McVay gets his two best healthy playmakers, Kyren and Corum, on the field together. That takes pressure off guys like Xavier Smith, Konata Mumpfield, Tutu Atwell, and CJ Daniels.

And the run game has been historic. Over the last four games, the Rams have outrushed opponents 653 yards to 175. According to ESPN Research, that 478-yard advantage is the franchise’s biggest four-game rushing differential since 1984, when Eric Dickerson ran for 2,105 yards.

So the suspension doesn’t change the plan, and the plan was already working.

And the defense helps too. In Arizona, the Rams held the Cardinals to just 123 total yards, with five sacks and two interceptions. When your defense plays like that, the offense doesn’t need a superstar receiver every week.

Xavier Smith: 92.6. First career touchdown on the screen before halftime. McVay gave the play-call credit to assistant coach Nate Scheelhaase.

Plus the tight ends. Colby Parkinson graded 83.5, Tyler Higbee 75.0, and Davis Allen 68.1. Higbee and Allen both scored in Arizona.

So nobody has to replace Puka by himself. The Rams need a group effort, and in Arizona, they got it.

How the pony look could work against Green Bay

Now let’s think about the Packers game. This is not a report. It’s my analysis.

The Packers have now seen the pony formation on film. Their coaches have studied all 17 of those plays. So McVay probably won’t just run the same calls again.

Here’s what I’d expect. The Rams will line up in the same two-back look, but run new plays out of it. Maybe Corum lines up wide as a receiver. Maybe Kyren runs a route out of the backfield while Corum stays in to block. Maybe they use it to set up a deep play-action shot.

That’s how McVay usually works. He shows you something one week, then uses it to set up something different the next.

McVay said the bye is a time to “creatively come up with new ideas based on who’s available and who’s not.” With Puka out and Adams likely out, the pony look is probably one of the biggest tools he has. I’d be surprised if we don’t see a new version of it on Wednesday night.
