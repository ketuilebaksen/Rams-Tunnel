Now let’s talk about who George Pickens is, because he’s a really talented player — and a complicated one.

Pickens was a second-round pick by the Pittsburgh Steelers in 2022. In Pittsburgh, he was known for spectacular catches — in 2023, he went over 1,100 receiving yards for the Steelers. But there were also questions about his focus and discipline at times, and the Steelers traded him to Dallas before the 2025 season. That move paid off for the Cowboys right away. According to Yahoo Sports, in 2025 he had career highs with 93 catches, 1,429 yards and nine touchdowns, and he was named an All-Pro and a Pro Bowler.

The Cowboys couldn’t agree on a long-term deal with him, so they used the franchise tag. According to ESPN and NFL.com, Pickens signed his franchise tag in the spring, and he’s playing this season on a one-year deal worth 27.3 million dollars.

This season hasn’t been as explosive. According to Yahoo Sports, through six games, Pickens had 33 catches for 428 yards and one touchdown. Add Monday night’s four catches for 57 yards and a touchdown, and he’s at 37 catches, 485 yards and two touchdowns through seven games — that’s my own math from the reported numbers. Good, but well below his 2025 pace.

And there’s been some tension in Dallas. According to The Spun, via Yahoo Sports, Cowboys head coach Brian Schottenheimer publicly criticized Pickens’ effort in the running game, saying he needs to block the way running back Javonte Williams does. Schottenheimer said: “He needs to understand that he impacts us in the running game as well.” According to the same report, Pickens was also dealing with a shoulder injury and was limited in practice. And Solak himself mentioned concerns about Pickens’ effort and focus in his column. Solak also pointed out how expensive it would be for Dallas to pay both Pickens and cornerback Joey Porter Jr. long term. That’s the real reason a trade could make sense for the Cowboys — not because Pickens isn’t good, but because the money might not work for them in the future.

So here’s the full picture: Pickens is a big, physical, explosive receiver who was an All-Pro last season. He’s also on a one-year franchise tag, in a contract standoff, and has drawn public criticism from his head coach. That’s a high-reward, high-risk player. The kind of player who can win you a game on his own — or become a headache if things go wrong.

Now let’s be fair to the idea, because there are real reasons Pickens would fit the Rams.

Reason number one: the receiver room. According to the AP, in the Raiders game, Konata Mumpfield’s 15-yard catch was the only reception by a Rams wide receiver other than Puka Nacua. That’s it — one catch. Davante Adams is out for weeks. According to Heavy, he isn’t expected back until around the bye. The Rams need help outside.

Reason number two: the deep ball. According to SB Nation, the Rams’ deep passing game still isn’t there. They’ve had very few completions of 20 yards or more. Pickens is exactly the kind of receiver who wins down the field. Rams Wire’s Cameron DaSilva wrote that Pickens could play the outside “X” role that Adams fills and be an immediate deep threat alongside Puka.

Reason number three: the quarterback. Stafford is 38, and he just became the Rams’ all-time leader in touchdown passes. A quarterback with a strong arm and a receiver who wins contested catches downfield is a natural match. The clip was right that the Rams’ window with Stafford won’t stay open forever.

So yes, on paper, Pickens next to Puka would be scary. I get why people are excited.

But now let’s talk about why it almost certainly won’t happen. And it comes down to two things: money and draft picks.

Here’s the money. According to ESPN, Pickens is playing on a 27.3 million dollar franchise tag. According to Pro Football Rumors, citing Over the Cap, the Rams have just under 990,000 dollars in cap space.

Let me show you why that’s a problem. When a team trades for a player in the middle of the season, it takes on the rest of his salary. Players are paid over the 18 weeks of the regular season. If a trade happened around the deadline, with about half the season left, the Rams would be taking on roughly half of that 27.3 million — somewhere in the range of 13 to 15 million dollars. That’s my own rough math, not an official figure. And the Rams have less than one million dollars in space.

So to make a Pickens trade work, the Rams would have to send something like 13 to 15 million dollars in salary back to Dallas, or restructure several contracts to free up room. That’s not a small adjustment. That’s reshaping the roster in the middle of a playoff push.

The clip said a player-for-player trade “closes the gap.” But think about which players that would have to be. Let me give you an example. According to Pro Football Rumors, tight end Colby Parkinson has a 5.5 million dollar base salary this season. Even if the Rams traded Parkinson — the guy who’s been carrying the passing game with Adams out — that would only cover a fraction of what they’d need. They’d have to include several more contracts on top of that. To match that kind of money, the Rams would probably have to give up a starter — and Dallas would have to want that starter. That’s a lot harder than it sounds.

And think about what Dallas would want back. The Cowboys just won in Philadelphia and are chasing a division title. A team in that position wouldn’t want draft picks two years from now. They’d want players who help them win this season. And the Rams can’t afford to give away starters in the middle of their own playoff push. So the two teams’ needs don’t really line up.

Now the draft picks. According to Pro Football Rumors, the Rams have already traded their 2027 first-round pick, their 2027 third-round pick, their 2028 second-round pick and their 2029 third-round pick in the Myles Garrett and Trent McDuffie deals. According to Yahoo Sports, the Chiefs — the other team Solak named — have all of their picks. And according to the same article, the Chiefs were 5-0. So if Dallas did decide to sell, Kansas City would have more draft capital and fewer cap problems than the Rams. Solak described Pickens as a possible downfield target for Patrick Mahomes, and the Chiefs have a top-five offense, according to Yahoo Sports. So even in Solak’s own column, the Chiefs look like the more realistic buyer — the opposite of what the fake Schefter clip claimed.

And there’s one more issue: what happens after the season? Pickens is on a one-year tag. If the Rams traded for him, they’d either have to sign him to a big long-term deal or watch him walk in free agency. With Garrett, McDuffie and Stafford already taking up a lot of money, and young players like Kobie Turner heading toward new contracts, that’s a tough position. And here’s another wrinkle. Under the NFL’s rules, if a team uses the franchise tag on the same player a second year in a row, the price goes up to 120 percent of his previous salary. So keeping Pickens on another tag in 2027 would cost well over 30 million dollars. That’s why Solak framed it as a trade for “the remainder of the season” — a rental. And a 27-million-dollar rental is a luxury the Rams simply can’t afford right now.

So when the clip says the Rams have “a maxed-out credit card,” that part is actually true. And it’s exactly why this trade doesn’t add up.

So what would a realistic receiver move look like for the Rams?

We’ve talked about this all week. Writers have floated cheaper names that fit the Rams’ cap. Josh Downs of the Colts is in the final year of his rookie deal, and according to Heavy, Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak estimated the Rams would only take on about 2 million dollars in salary. Jerry Jeudy of the Browns has a 2026 base salary of just 1.5 million dollars, according to Pro Football Rumors. Stefon Diggs of the Commanders has been floated with a proposed price of a 2028 third-round pick, according to Heavy. Again, those are ideas from writers, not reported talks. But they’re the kind of moves that actually fit a team with under a million dollars in cap space.

And remember what McVay said last week. When he was asked about adding an outside receiver, he said, according to the Rams’ official website: “That’s not something that we’ve talked about right now.” He added: “We’ve got some depth on our roster.” That doesn’t rule out a move before November 10th. But it tells you the Rams aren’t panicking — especially after Sunday’s win. And remember, the Rams have found help in creative ways before. In 2021, they added Odell Beckham Jr. in the middle of the season as a free agent after Cleveland released him, and he became a big part of their Super Bowl run. Sometimes the best addition isn’t the flashiest trade. It’s the move nobody sees coming that actually fits the cap and the locker room.

Speaking of that win, let’s talk about why it matters for the trade question.

Against the Raiders, the Rams found a new identity. According to the AP, they had thrown on about 64 percent of their plays coming into the game. On Sunday, they ran 38 times and threw just 29 passes, and rushed for 199 yards. Kyren Williams ran for 99 yards and Blake Corum ran for 93, and each scored a touchdown. The tight ends — Davis Allen, Colby Parkinson and Tyler Higbee — combined for 11 catches, 82 yards and two touchdowns.

After the game, McVay said: “If we’re able to do those types of things and have a little bit of run-pass balance, that’s the identity that we’re hunting.” And on Monday, according to the AP, he said about the running backs: “Both Blake and Kyren did a great job.”

So the Rams’ biggest need might not be a superstar receiver after all. If the run game and the tight ends can carry the offense, the Rams might only need a cheaper receiver who can stretch the field — or nobody at all if Terrance Ferguson comes back from injured reserve and Davante Adams returns around the bye. Ferguson was the Rams’ top pick in the 2025 draft, according to Heavy, and he’s been out since Week 3. SB Nation’s JB Scott suggested the Rams may not push the ball downfield effectively until he’s back. So in a way, Ferguson’s return could be the Rams’ best “trade” of the season.

And don’t forget Puka Nacua. He played through a calf injury against the Raiders, caught eight passes and scored the touchdown that broke Stafford’s record. After the game, according to HITC, via Yahoo Sports, he said his MRI showed nothing significant. A healthy Puka, plus the tight ends, plus the run game, is a lot of offense — even without a big trade. Another receiver would help Puka by taking some coverage away from him. But that receiver doesn’t need to cost 27 million dollars.

And there’s more help on the way. According to the LA Times, Myles Garrett and Ferguson are both eligible to come off injured reserve. On Monday, McVay said the team hadn’t decided yet whether to open their practice windows. According to Heavy, he said: “I’m not sure yet. Have not decided on those things.” Trent McDuffie could return soon, too, according to the LA Times.

If the Rams get Garrett and McDuffie back, that’s like adding two stars without trading anything. And that’s a much better use of the next two weeks than chasing a 27-million-dollar receiver they can’t afford. Remember, Garrett was the 2025 AP Defensive Player of the Year with a record 23 sacks, according to Pro Football Talk. According to Last Word on Sports, the Rams’ pass rush ranks 27th in the league with just 10 sacks. Getting Garrett back would fix the defense’s biggest weakness. Even podcaster Ryen Russillo said, according to Heavy, that “the Myles Garrett trade looked like a slam dunk, but has not aged well so far.” The best way for the Rams to change that story isn’t another big trade — it’s getting Garrett back on the field.

Now let’s look ahead to Sunday, because the Rams have a game to win.

According to the AP, the Rams host the 0-6 Chargers at SoFi Stadium, and the Chargers are coming off a bye. A winless team with an extra week to prepare can be dangerous, and the Chargers will treat this like their Super Bowl. The two teams share SoFi Stadium, so there will be plenty of fans from both sides in the building. For the Rams, this is a must-win if they want to keep climbing. For the Chargers, it’s a chance to save some pride. Never overlook a team with nothing to lose.

According to Pro Football Network, the Rams are now 3-4 and third in the NFC West after both the 49ers and Cardinals lost on Sunday. After the Chargers, the Rams play Washington and then the Cardinals. According to Last Word on Sports, all three of those teams have losing records. That’s a real chance to climb. And here’s a timing detail that matters for any trade. The Rams play Washington on November 8th, and the deadline is two days later, on Tuesday, November 10th, at 4 p.m. Eastern. So the Rams will get to see two more games before they have to decide anything. If they win both, they’ll be 5-4 and can afford to be picky. If they lose both, they might decide to protect what’s left of their future picks instead.

But McVay was honest about what still needs work. On Monday, according to the AP, he said the special teams were “not good enough,” and added: “We’ve talked about this a lot, and it’s just got to get fixed.” The Raiders converted a fake punt, and the Rams allowed kickoff returns of 42 and 36 yards, according to the AP. The Rams also had seven penalties for 90 yards, including two that wiped out Blake Corum touchdowns.

Here are my three keys for Sunday.

Key number one: keep running the ball. Thirty-plus carries for Kyren and Corum. That’s the identity McVay talked about.

Key number two: fix special teams. No fake punts, no long returns. A winless team will look for any spark it can find.

Key number three: get one deep shot. Whether it’s Puka, Mumpfield, Whittington or a tight end, the Rams need to show they can still hit a big play down the field. If they do, the pressure to trade for a receiver drops even more.

My early prediction: Rams 30, Chargers 17. And I’ll say this: if Kyren and Corum both go over 80 yards again and Brandon McManus stays perfect on his kicks, nobody in the Rams House will be talking about George Pickens next week. After the Raiders game, Corum said, according to the LA Times: “It feels great being able to have a partner back there like Kyren.” That’s the kind of chemistry that wins games in November. Trades get the headlines, but a backfield that works together, a defense that gets off the field on third down and a kicker who makes his kicks — that’s what actually wins football games.

So here’s my bottom line.

No, the Rams are not about to land George Pickens. The clip claiming Schefter confirmed it on First Take doesn’t hold up: I couldn’t find the quote, the timing doesn’t make sense, and it even gets Davante Adams’ injury wrong. What’s real is that ESPN’s Ben Solak named the Rams and the Chiefs as possible fits if the Cowboys kept losing. But the Cowboys just beat the Eagles 38-21 to get to 3-4 and second place in the NFC East.

And even if Dallas decided to sell, the math doesn’t work for the Rams. Pickens is on a 27.3 million dollar franchise tag. The Rams have under a million dollars in cap space and are missing several future draft picks. The Chiefs, with all their picks and a 5-0 start, would be a much more natural buyer than the Rams.

If the Rams add a receiver before November 10th, I’d expect it to be a cheaper option — or no trade at all, if Ferguson, Garrett and Adams come back healthy and the run game keeps working.
