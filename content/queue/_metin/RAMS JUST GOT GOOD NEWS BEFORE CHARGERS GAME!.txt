Let’s start with the good news, because there’s more of it than you might think.

Good news number one: Trent McDuffie is back on the practice field.

McDuffie came over from the Kansas City Chiefs in one of the two blockbuster trades the Rams made this offseason — the other one was for Myles Garrett. According to Pro Football Rumors, those two trades are a big reason the Rams don’t have their 2027 first-round pick, their 2027 third, their 2028 second or their 2029 third. So when McDuffie misses time, it’s not just a hole in the lineup. It’s an expensive hole.

McDuffie has missed the last two games with a groin injury. That’s a big loss, because he was brought in to be one of the best cornerbacks in football. But according to the Rams’ official website, McDuffie was a limited participant in practice on Wednesday, his first practice since the injury. And according to the Rams’ injury report, he was limited again on Thursday. According to Yahoo Sports, he’s “trending in the right direction.”

Now, I want to be careful here. Limited participation doesn’t guarantee he plays on Sunday. Sometimes a team puts a player out there to see how his body responds, and if there’s any soreness, he sits another week. Groin injuries can be tricky, and nobody wants a player to come back too early and miss even more time. So I’d call McDuffie a real possibility for Sunday, not a lock. But two straight days of practice is a lot better than zero.

And think about what it means if he does play. The Chargers have one of the most talented young receivers in the league in Ladd McConkey, who was a full participant on Wednesday after a concussion, according to the Chargers’ website. Having McDuffie back to match up on him would be a huge lift for this defense.

Good news number two: Nate Landman is cleared.

According to Yahoo Sports, Landman has cleared the concussion protocol and should be good to go on Sunday. According to the Rams’ injury report, he was a full participant on Wednesday and Thursday. Against the Raiders, Troy Dolac filled in for Landman and did a solid job, but getting your starting middle linebacker back — the guy who makes the defensive calls — is a big deal. Linebacker Desjuan Johnson was also a full participant both days, according to the Rams’ report.

Good news number three: Davante Adams.

When Adams ruptured his hamstring against Arizona, the timeline was six to 10 weeks. That’s a big range. But on Wednesday, according to Heavy, McVay said Adams’ surgery “went really well,” and that he’s “feeling good in the process of recovering and doing really well.” On the timeline, McVay said: “I think it’s probably closer to the shorter term.” And according to the Rams’ official website, McVay said: “Based on how everything went, on the shorter end is what is projected.”

That’s genuinely good news. Six weeks from his injury would put Adams back in late November, around the Rams’ bye. If that holds, the Rams would have their full receiving corps — Puka, Adams and hopefully Terrance Ferguson at tight end — for the final stretch of the season.

Good news number four: the other locker room has problems, too.

According to the Chargers’ official website, safety Derwin James Jr. didn’t practice on Wednesday with a hamstring injury, and Jim Harbaugh said James had a setback in his rehab and could potentially go on injured reserve. Guard Kayode Awosika didn’t practice with a fibula injury. And receiver Brenen Thompson didn’t practice with a quad and hamstring injury. If James can’t go, the Chargers lose their best defensive playmaker against a Rams offense that’s starting to find its rhythm.

Now the bad news. And there’s no sugarcoating this one.

Myles Garrett still isn’t back at practice.

According to the Rams’ official website, the Rams decided on Wednesday not to open Garrett’s 21-day practice window this week. Terrance Ferguson’s window won’t open this week either. McVay said: “I know it’s frustrating, but he’s making good progress, and we’re just on a week-to-week basis right now.” According to the AP, Garrett will miss his seventh straight game on Sunday.

Here’s the timeline. According to the Rams’ website, Garrett has been on injured reserve since September 17th, after knee surgery. On September 27th, according to Pro Football Talk, NFL Network’s Ian Rapoport reported that Garrett would need about six weeks to recover and that his most likely return would be the Rams’ Week 9 game against the Commanders on November 8th.

So the transcript going around is right about one thing: the original target was the Washington game. And that’s still possible on paper. But here’s the problem. If Garrett’s practice window doesn’t open until next week, he’d have only a few practices before the Washington game. And according to Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak, writing this week, Garrett might not be back before the Rams’ bye week. Grisak pointed out that reports said Garrett would return to practice this week, and that didn’t happen.

Let me quickly explain why that window matters so much. Once a team opens a player’s 21-day practice window, that clock can’t be paused. If the player isn’t activated within 21 days, he has to stay on injured reserve for the rest of the season. So teams only open the window when they’re confident the player will be ready in time. The Rams not opening it this week doesn’t mean anything has gone wrong. It just means they aren’t sure yet.

And remember why this matters so much. Garrett was the 2025 Defensive Player of the Year with a record 23 sacks, according to Pro Football Talk. According to Last Word on Sports, the Rams’ pass rush ranks 27th in the league with just 10 sacks. The Rams gave up major draft capital to get him, and they’re still waiting for him to make an impact on the field.

This week, McVay told reporters, according to the LA Times, that the Rams are counting on their key injured players to “return within a month.” According to the New York Post, he said: “Hopefully, some of those trades will be guys getting back from injury that haven’t played for us.” Garrett is the biggest of those “trades.” The question is when he actually arrives.

If you think Garrett will be back for the Washington game, drop “Week 9” in the comments. If you think it’ll be after the bye, drop “after the bye.”

Here’s how the schedule lines up, and why it matters. After the Chargers on Sunday, the Rams play Washington on November 8th. Then the trade deadline hits on Tuesday, November 10th. After that comes Arizona, and then the bye. According to Grisak, the Rams play in Washington next week and then face the Cardinals. So if Garrett isn’t back by the Washington game, the Rams will have to make their trade deadline decisions without knowing exactly when their best pass rusher will play. And if he’s not back until after the bye, he’d only have the second half of the season to make his mark. That’s why every Wednesday practice report right now feels like a big deal.

Now let’s get to the strangest story of the week: the Rams’ special teams shake-up.

On Wednesday, according to Heavy, the Rams announced six practice squad moves. They signed kicker Joshua Karty, long snapper Jake McQuaide and linebacker Jon Rhattigan. And they released offensive lineman Austin Blaske, cornerback Nyzier Fourqurean and linebacker Elias Neal.

By the way, the transcript calls these players “Jake Mud,” “Josh Cardi” and “John Ratigan.” Let’s get the names right: Jake McQuaide, Joshua Karty and Jon Rhattigan.

Let’s take them one at a time.

First, Jake McQuaide.

Some fans were confused by this one at first, and I understand why. This offseason, the Rams signed Joe Cardona, a two-time Super Bowl champion with the Patriots. According to Rams Wire’s Cameron DaSilva, contract details showed the Rams made Cardona the highest-paid long snapper in the NFL, with a fully guaranteed two-year deal.

Some background on Cardona, from my own knowledge: he played at Navy, was drafted by the Patriots in 2015 and spent years as one of the most reliable long snappers in the league in New England, winning two Super Bowls there. The Rams paid him like the best at his position because they wanted one part of their special teams they never had to worry about.

So why bring in another long snapper?

The answer is simple: Cardona is hurt. According to the Rams’ injury report, he didn’t practice on Wednesday or Thursday because of an ankle injury. McVay said the injury happened “early” in the Raiders game, according to the Rams’ website. So McQuaide isn’t a replacement because of performance. He’s an insurance policy.

And McQuaide is about as safe an insurance policy as you can find. According to Pro Football Talk, he was the Rams’ regular long snapper from 2011, back in St. Louis, through 2020. He’s a two-time Pro Bowler, according to Heavy. And he came back to the Rams last season for the final nine games, according to Pro Football Talk. He knows the building, he knows the operation, and he’s done this for a long time. If Cardona can’t go on Sunday, McQuaide is the right guy to have.

And let me explain why the Rams didn’t just take a chance with someone else snapping, because long snapping is one of those jobs nobody notices until it goes wrong. On every punt, field goal and extra point, the long snapper has to fire the ball backward between his legs — about seven or eight yards to the holder on kicks and roughly 15 yards to the punter — with perfect accuracy, and then block a defender charging at him. One high or low snap, and a field goal becomes a disaster. And this Rams team has already lost games because of special teams mistakes. That’s my own explanation of the job, not something from this week’s reporting, but it’s why teams treat a long snapper injury so seriously.

There’s also a roster detail worth knowing. McQuaide and Karty were signed to the practice squad, not the active roster. Under the NFL’s practice squad rules, a team can temporarily elevate a practice squad player to the game-day roster for a game and then send him back to the practice squad without exposing him to waivers — but only a limited number of times per season. That’s my own knowledge of the rules, not something the Rams have announced. But it means that if Cardona can’t play on Sunday, the Rams can elevate McQuaide for the game without having to cut anybody from the 53-man roster. That’s probably exactly why they did it this way.

And this isn’t the first time the Rams have turned to McQuaide in the middle of a season. According to Heavy, the Rams added him during last season’s mid-season special teams overhaul, replacing Alex Ward. So the Rams have been here before — and McQuaide steadied things last time.

Second, Joshua Karty.

This is the one that surprised a lot of people. According to Heavy, Karty was a sixth-round pick by the Rams in 2024. As a rookie, according to Pro Football Talk, he made 29 of 34 field goals and 32 of 36 extra points. But in 2025, he missed five field goals in the first eight games and three extra points, and the Rams waived him. According to Heavy, they cut him in November 2025, re-signed him to the practice squad in December, and then Arizona signed him to its active roster later that month before releasing him in June. According to Pro Football Talk, he didn’t appear in a game for the Cardinals.

The transcript also brings up a painful memory: the Eagles game last season. From my own memory of that game in September 2025, Philadelphia blocked a Karty field goal and returned it for a touchdown, and then blocked another kick late in the game that could have won it for the Rams. That’s my own recollection, not something from this week’s reporting — but I’m pretty sure most Rams fans remember it, too.

So why bring him back now? The Rams haven’t said. Right now, Brandon McManus is the Rams’ kicker. According to Pro Football Talk, the Rams signed McManus the week before to replace Harrison Mevis, and McManus made all five of his extra points against the Raiders, according to the Rams’ website. So Karty isn’t on the practice squad to take McManus’ job right now.

The transcript claims Karty is there because he can kick a hard-to-catch “knuckleball” on kickoffs. I couldn’t find any report that says that, so I’m not going to tell you it’s true. What I can tell you is that kickoffs have been a real problem. According to Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak, the Rams gave up kickoff returns of 36 and 42 yards against the Raiders, and after that, they started kicking the ball out of the back of the end zone.

And here’s why that matters. Under the NFL’s new kickoff rules, a touchback doesn’t put the ball at the 25-yard line anymore. It puts the ball at the 35. That’s my own knowledge of the rule, not something from this week’s reporting. So when the Rams kick the ball through the end zone, they’re giving the other team the ball at the 35-yard line every time. That’s a lot of free field position. If Karty or anyone else can place kickoffs in a way that keeps returners from breaking big plays without automatically giving up the 35, that would help. But whether that’s the plan, we don’t know.

My best guess is that Karty is simply insurance. The Rams have been through so many kickers that they want someone they know on the practice squad, ready to go. That’s not a report. That’s just me reading the situation.

Third, Jon Rhattigan.

This might be the most important of the three moves for this week. According to Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak, Rhattigan went undrafted out of Army in 2021, spent three seasons with the Seattle Seahawks and was with the Chicago Bears this summer before being released at roster cuts. He has 1,270 career special teams snaps, and in 2022, Pro Football Focus gave him a special teams grade of 90.1 with Seattle.

So the Rams are bringing in a specialist — a guy whose whole job is to run down on kickoffs and punts and make tackles. That’s exactly what a struggling coverage unit needs. The transcript describes him as 6-foot-3 and 236 pounds. I couldn’t confirm those numbers, so I’ll leave them out.

But I’ll be fair here, too. According to Grisak, Rhattigan had two special teams tackles and six missed tackles last season. So he’s not a guaranteed fix. He’s an experienced special teams player who’s been very good in the past, and the Rams are hoping he can bring that energy to a unit that badly needs it.

If you’re glad the Rams are finally doing something about special teams, hit that like button.

Now, here’s why all of these moves happened. McVay is frustrated. According to Grisak, after the Raiders game, McVay said: “It was not good enough. We’ve talked about this a lot and it has to get fixed.” And he added: “It’s one of those deals that’s driving me crazy.”

That’s not a coach who’s shrugging. That’s a coach who’s fed up.

And the numbers back him up. According to Rams Wire’s Cameron DaSilva, back in September, FTN Fantasy’s DVOA rankings put the Rams last in the NFL on special teams. According to Grisak, writing in mid-October, the Rams had one of the worst special teams starts in DVOA history through five games. Grisak listed a 45-yard kickoff return in Week 1, a missed 43-yard field goal against the Giants, a 41-yard punt return touchdown against the Eagles, and a missed 50-yard field goal and a blocked potential game-winner in overtime against the Bills. And against the Raiders, according to the AP, the Rams also gave up a fake punt.

Grisak also found two stats that show how long this has been going on. Since 2023, the Rams have signed six kickers. And over eight years, they’ve had five special teams coordinators.

Think about that for a second. Six kickers in about three seasons. Five coordinators in eight years. That kind of turnover makes it really hard to build any consistency. Kickers, holders and long snappers rely on timing and trust — they practice the same operation hundreds of times so that it becomes automatic. Every time one piece changes, that timing has to be rebuilt. So in a way, the Rams’ constant changes have been part of the problem. That’s my own read, but I think it’s an important point. The goal now shouldn’t be more change for the sake of change. It should be finding a group that works and sticking with it.

So here’s my honest take on the shake-up. Bringing in McQuaide, Karty and Rhattigan is a smart, low-cost move. These are practice squad signings — they don’t cost draft picks, and they don’t cost much money. But they’re also not a full solution. Special teams problems usually come from a lot of things: coaching, effort, the players on the coverage units and the return game. Adding a few veterans helps. It doesn’t fix everything overnight.

The real test is Sunday. If the Rams can get through the Chargers game without a big return, a blocked kick or a botched snap, that’s a step forward.

Now, there’s one more area worth talking about before we preview the game, and that’s the receiver room.

According to Grisak, writing this week, the Rams need a third receiver. He argued that Tutu Atwell and Konata Mumpfield haven’t been reliable enough. And the numbers from Las Vegas back that up. According to the AP, Mumpfield’s 15-yard catch was the only reception by a Rams wide receiver other than Puka Nacua against the Raiders.

Grisak thinks Colts receiver Josh Downs is the best fit, possibly for just a fourth- or fifth-round pick. According to ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler, as reported by Pro Football Talk, Downs has requested a trade. And Grisak also mentioned Jaguars receiver Brian Thomas Jr., though that would likely cost a second-round pick.

But remember what McVay said this week. When he was asked about trades, according to the LA Times, his first answer was “No.” He said the Rams’ biggest “trades” will hopefully be players coming back from injury. So if you’re hoping for a trade before November 10th, I’d keep expectations low. The good news is Adams might be back sooner than expected.

Now let’s preview Sunday.

According to Football Nation, the Rams host the Chargers on Sunday, November 1st, at 1:05 p.m. Pacific time at SoFi Stadium, on FOX. The Chargers are 0-6 and coming off a bye. According to Football Nation, the Chargers lost 22-6 at Kansas City before the bye, and the Rams are favored by about six and a half to seven points.

According to Football Nation, Justin Herbert has thrown five touchdowns and eight interceptions this season. And Khalil Mack sat out Wednesday’s practice for his usual veteran rest day, according to the Chargers’ website. So expect Mack to play — and expect him to be fired up, with his name all over the trade rumors.

On the Rams’ side, there are still some concerns. According to the Rams’ injury report, offensive lineman Coleman Shelton didn’t practice on Wednesday or Thursday with a hip injury. Tight end Colby Parkinson didn’t practice either, with a rib injury. And of course, Cardona’s ankle.

Here are my three keys.

Key number one: get McDuffie back on the field, if he’s ready. If he can go, he changes the Rams’ secondary. If he can’t, the Rams need their backup corners to keep McConkey in front of them.

Key number two: protect Stafford. If Shelton can’t play, Mack will be looking for a mismatch. Run the ball, use quick throws and let the tight ends help in protection. The Rams ran the ball 38 times against the Raiders, according to the AP. That formula works.

Key number three: a clean special teams game. No fake punts, no long returns, no blocked kicks and — if McQuaide has to play — no bad snaps. That’s the whole goal. Just be boring. Boring special teams would be a huge win for this team right now.

My prediction: Rams 27, Chargers 17. The Rams are getting healthier on defense, they’re coming off their best game of the season and they’re facing a winless team. But a team off a bye with Khalil Mack and Justin Herbert can make it close if the Rams make mistakes.

And here’s one small thing I’ll be watching that most people won’t: the Rams’ inactive list about 90 minutes before kickoff. If McDuffie is active, that’s the best news of the week. If McQuaide is elevated from the practice squad, that tells you Cardona couldn’t go. And if Rhattigan is elevated, it tells you the Rams want him on the coverage units right away. Those little roster details will tell you a lot about where this team stands.

So here’s my bottom line.

Yes, the Rams got real good news this week. McDuffie is back at practice. Landman is cleared. And Davante Adams is trending toward the shorter end of his recovery timeline. That’s three pieces of good news a team needed after a rough October.

But Garrett still isn’t practicing, Ferguson isn’t back yet, and the special teams have been bad enough that the Rams brought back two familiar faces and added a special teams veteran in the middle of the season. The Rams are clearly trying to fix things — but they still have a lot to prove.

And one more thing: don’t let a video convince you that this locker room is about to “boil over.” The transcript talks about “baffling coaching decisions” and a locker room that might lose its composure. I haven’t seen any report of that. What I’ve seen is a team that won its last game by two touchdowns, a coach who’s frustrated with special teams and a front office making low-cost moves to fix it. That’s not chaos. That’s a team in the middle of a long season trying to get better.
