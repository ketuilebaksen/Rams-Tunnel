Let’s start with what’s real and what isn’t.

Not real: a Khalil Mack trade. Not real: any agreement between the Rams and Chargers. Not real: the “scariest defense in the NFL” being on its way. That’s a headline, not a report.

Real: Blaine Grisak of Rams On SI wrote an article on October 29th titled “3 NFL Trade Deadline Deals the Rams Should Explore Right Now.” He proposed trades for Colts receiver Josh Downs, Buccaneers defensive lineman A’Shawn Robinson and Chargers pass rusher Khalil Mack. Those are a writer’s ideas — not reported talks.

Real: Grisak also wrote an article on October 30th about Les Snead’s trade deadline history, and it’s genuinely useful.

And one correction to the transcript going around. It says Grisak’s article “doesn’t provide a specific proposed trade package for Mack.” That’s not right. According to Rams On SI, Grisak proposed a 2028 late-round pick swap for Mack. So there was a proposed price. It’s just a writer’s proposal.

Let me take a second to explain the difference between a proposal and a report, because it’s the key to understanding almost every trade headline you’ll see over the next 10 days. A report comes from a reporter with sources inside teams — someone like Adam Schefter, Ian Rapoport or Jeremy Fowler — saying that talks are happening or that a deal is done. A proposal comes from a writer or analyst saying, “Here’s a trade I think would make sense.” Proposals can be smart. Grisak covers the Rams every day, and his ideas are well thought out. But a proposal tells you what a writer thinks the Rams should do, not what the Rams are actually doing.

So when a headline says “Rams bringing in Khalil Mack,” and the source is a proposal, the headline has turned an idea into a fact. That’s the trick. And once you know to look for it, you’ll see it everywhere.

Now, story number one: why haven’t the Rams made a trade yet?

It’s a fair question. On Tuesday, the Seahawks traded for All-Pro guard Quenton Nelson, according to the Seahawks’ official website. The same day, the 49ers brought back DeForest Buckner, according to Pro Football Rumors. And on Thursday, the Bears traded two first-round picks for Titans star Jeffery Simmons, according to Pro Football Rumors. Teams all around the league are making moves. The Rams have been quiet.

But according to Grisak, that silence fits a pattern. He looked at every trade Les Snead has made around the deadline, and here’s what he found. Under Snead, the Rams have made four trades on deadline day itself, two trades eight days before the deadline and one trade the day before the deadline.

Let’s put some names on that. According to Grisak, the Rams’ 2019 trades for offensive lineman Austin Corbett and cornerback Jalen Ramsey both happened outside the final 12-day window. From my own memory, the Ramsey deal cost the Rams two first-round picks and a fourth — the kind of blockbuster this front office used to make all the time.

In 2021, according to Grisak, the Rams traded for Von Miller the day before the deadline. From my own memory, that deal cost a second- and a third-round pick, and Miller became a huge part of the Rams’ Super Bowl run that season.

And last year, according to Grisak, the Rams traded for cornerback Roger McCreary eight days before the deadline. According to the AP, the Titans sent McCreary to the Rams for a conditional 2026 fifth-round pick. That’s the kind of small, smart move that doesn’t make headlines but fills a hole.

Grisak also compared Snead to other general managers. According to his research, only four active GMs have made more trades than Snead, and only two have made more deadline-day trades. He called Snead “one of the more active general managers” before the deadline.

So when people say the Rams are “doing nothing,” history says: be patient. Snead usually waits.

And let’s remember who we’re talking about. Les Snead is the general manager who built the 2021 Super Bowl team by trading picks for stars — Jalen Ramsey, Von Miller and, from my own memory, Matthew Stafford himself, who came over from Detroit in a trade before that season. Snead even famously wore a T-shirt at the Rams’ Super Bowl celebration poking fun at the idea of holding on to draft picks. That’s my own recollection, not something from this week’s reporting. But it tells you how this front office thinks. When Snead believes the Rams have a real shot, he isn’t afraid to spend.

The difference this year is that he already spent. The Garrett and McDuffie trades were this season’s version of that all-in mindset. So the question isn’t whether Snead is willing to be aggressive. It’s whether he has anything left to be aggressive with.

Now here’s the timing detail I found really interesting. According to Grisak, Monday is the most realistic day for a Rams trade in the next week. That’s when the Rams usually evaluate their roster after a game and start preparing for the next one. He pointed out that, outside of deadline day itself — which is usually a Tuesday — Snead hasn’t made a trade late in the week during that final 12-day window. So a Thursday trade like the Bears’ Simmons deal isn’t really Snead’s style.

And if nothing happens early this week, Grisak suggested the Rams might wait until the final days before the deadline. The deadline is Tuesday, November 10th, at 4 p.m. Eastern. And the Rams play Washington on Sunday, November 8th. So the Rams will see two more games before they have to decide.

Think about what that means for today’s game. If the Rams beat the Chargers and get to 4-4, Monday’s evaluation might be about fine-tuning — maybe a cheap receiver or a depth piece. If the Rams lose to a winless team at home, the conversation could change completely. Some people might even start asking whether the Rams should be buyers at all. So today’s result doesn’t just affect the standings. It could shape what Snead does on Monday.

Now let me add the other side of the story, because Snead’s history isn’t the only thing we have to go on. This week, McVay was asked whether there had been any new trade discussions, and according to the LA Times, his first answer was: “No.” According to the New York Post, he said: “Hopefully, some of those trades will be guys getting back from injury that haven’t played for us.” But he also said: “Like I’ve told you in years past, you always think about it.”

So you have a general manager whose history says he’ll make a move late, and a head coach who’s publicly leaning toward patience. Both can be true. The Rams might not make a big trade — but a small one, like the McCreary deal last year, would fit both Snead’s history and McVay’s message.

Now, story number two: Grisak’s three trade ideas. Let’s go through them one by one, with the real numbers.

Idea number one: Josh Downs.

According to Rams On SI, Grisak proposed a 2027 fourth-round pick for the Colts receiver. He wrote that Downs “wouldn’t cost as much as” Brian Thomas Jr. and would still fit the offense once Davante Adams returns. He also pointed out that the Rams don’t have a reliable third receiver for their three-receiver sets, and that Tutu Atwell and Konata Mumpfield have been inconsistent.

Here’s what makes Downs the most realistic name. According to ESPN, Downs has requested a trade, a report that came from Jeremy Fowler. He’s 25, he’s in the final year of his rookie contract, and the Colts are 2-5 and selling. According to Grisak, he’d cost the Rams about 2.4 million dollars for the rest of the season.

But he won’t be easy to get. According to ESPN’s trade candidate rankings, the teams listed as fits for Downs were the Chiefs and the Steelers. According to Heavy, NFL insider Jordan Schultz reported that the Steelers are interested. And The Athletic’s Nate Atkins, as cited by Heavy, pointed out that Downs is “a slot-only player, which makes him a harder fit with the three-tight end sets the Rams love.”

And here’s a detail that makes Downs more relevant this weekend. According to the Rams’ official website, receiver Jordan Whittington is questionable for today’s game after his back “locked up” on Friday, as McVay put it. If the Rams’ receiver room gets even thinner, Downs becomes more of a need than a luxury.

Idea number two: A’Shawn Robinson.

Grisak proposed a 2028 late-round pick for the Buccaneers’ defensive lineman. And Rams fans know Robinson well. According to SB Nation, via Yahoo Sports, Robinson won a Super Bowl with the Rams in 2021. He’s 31 now, and he spent the last two seasons with the Panthers under former Rams assistant Ejiro Evero before joining Tampa Bay.

He’s been playing well. According to SB Nation, Robinson has 20 tackles, a sack and two interceptions this season. Earlier this month, according to Heavy, Pro Football Focus gave him an 89.5 overall grade, which was the highest among defensive linemen with at least 90 snaps at the time, and an 88.8 run-defense grade. According to Heavy, he signed a one-year, 10-million-dollar deal with Tampa Bay this offseason. And according to SB Nation, the Bucs are 2-5.

And here’s the part that makes this idea more than just a writer’s wish. On ESPN’s list of the top 25 trade candidates, Robinson was ranked 21st with a 30 percent chance of being traded — and the Rams were one of the teams ESPN listed as a fit, along with the Ravens and Giants.

Why would the Rams want him? According to Grisak, Kobie Turner is on injured reserve and expected back “at some point,” and Poona Ford is out for the season. That’s two important players missing from the middle of the defensive line. And according to the Rams’ official website, Aaron Donald is being double-teamed at the highest rate of his career — 76 percent of the time — and still doesn’t have a sack this season. A run-stopping veteran like Robinson could take some pressure off Donald and help the defense against teams that want to run the ball.

And there’s a chemistry factor, too. Robinson was in the Rams’ defensive line room during the 2021 Super Bowl run, next to Aaron Donald. Now Donald is back from retirement, and Robinson could be back, too. A familiar face who already knows the culture, the coaches’ expectations and the man playing next to him doesn’t need much time to adjust. In the middle of a season, that matters. A player you trade for in November has to contribute right away, and Robinson would know where to stand on day one.

Idea number three: Khalil Mack.

This is the one the headline is built on. Grisak proposed a 2028 late-round pick swap. Let me quickly explain what a pick swap is, because it sounds more complicated than it is. In a pick swap, two teams trade picks in the same round, but one team moves up a little and the other moves down. So the Chargers might send the Rams their late-round pick, and the Rams send back a slightly better late-round pick in return. That’s my own explanation of the idea — Grisak didn’t spell out the exact picks. The point is that it would be a very small price for a player like Mack. He wrote that Mack “wants to play for a contender” and that the Rams have lacked consistent pressure off the edge without Myles Garrett. He suggested Mack could fit in the rotation with Josaiah Stewart and give the Rams insurance if Garrett’s recovery takes longer than expected.

Now let me give you the full picture on Mack.

According to Heavy, Mack is 35, in his 13th season, a nine-time Pro Bowler and the 2016 Defensive Player of the Year. According to Bettors Insider, he has three sacks and 19 tackles through six games this season, and 116 career sacks. According to ESPN’s Bill Barnwell, Mack is owed about 5.3 million dollars for the rest of the season and becomes a free agent after the year.

But here’s what I want you to notice. Grisak wrote that Mack wants to play for a contender. Mack himself said something different. According to Heavy, on October 15th, Mack said: “I’m focused on this team and what I’m doing right now.” And when he was asked about chasing a ring somewhere else, he said: “We’re still ring chasing here.” So Mack hasn’t asked out. That doesn’t mean he wouldn’t accept a trade — but he isn’t pushing for one.

And the Rams aren’t the team most linked to him. According to ESPN’s rankings, Mack was seventh with a 35 percent chance of being traded, and the fits ESPN listed were the Bears, Browns, Bills and Cowboys — not the Rams. According to Bettors Insider, insider Jordan Schultz said the 49ers and Bears are the two teams he’s watching most closely. And the Bears just traded two first-round picks for Jeffery Simmons, which shows they’re willing to spend big.

So is Mack to the Rams possible? Technically, yes — Grisak’s proposed price is cheap. But the Rams would be competing with teams that have more picks, more cap space and, in at least one case, more reporting tying them to Mack.

Now let’s talk about the problem that ties all three of these ideas together: money.

According to Athlon Sports, citing Over The Cap, the Rams have 206,406 dollars in cap space. That’s it.

Downs would cost about 2.4 million for the rest of the season, according to Grisak. Mack is owed about 5.3 million, according to Barnwell. And Robinson is on a one-year, 10-million-dollar deal, according to Heavy. I couldn’t find exactly how much of that is left to be paid, because it depends on how the contract is structured. But it’s clearly more than 206,000 dollars.

So any one of these moves requires the Rams to create cap space. According to Grisak, the most realistic way is restructuring Kyren Williams’ contract, which could free about 3.5 million dollars. That would cover Downs. It might not be enough for Mack without the Chargers paying part of his salary. And doing all three? That would take a lot more than one restructure.

And then there are the picks. According to Pro Football Rumors, the Rams are already missing their 2027 first-round pick, their 2027 third, their 2028 second and their 2029 third after the Garrett and McDuffie trades. Grisak’s proposals use a 2027 fourth and late-round 2028 picks, which the Rams do have. But every pick they trade now is one less cheap young player in the future, and this roster is going to need those.

So here’s my honest read: the Rams can realistically make one of these moves, maybe two if they get creative. Not all three.

Let me walk through what a realistic version could look like. And again, this is not a report — it’s just me showing you how the pieces could fit.

Step one: the Rams restructure a contract, like Kyren Williams’, to free up about 3.5 million dollars, the number Grisak estimated.

Step two: they use that space on the most affordable piece that fills the biggest need. Based on the numbers, that’s Downs at about 2.4 million.

Step three: if they also want a defensive lineman or a pass rusher, they’d need the other team to keep part of the salary. That’s not unusual. According to Pro Football Rumors, the Colts kept part of DeForest Buckner’s salary when they traded him to the 49ers this week. So a team like Tampa Bay or the Chargers could do something similar — but they’d probably want a slightly better pick in return for paying part of the salary.

That’s how a team with almost no cap space can still make moves. It’s just harder, and it usually means the moves are smaller.

Now let’s check the headline’s other claim: the “scariest defense in the NFL.” Let’s be honest about where the Rams’ defense actually is.

The good: according to the AP, the Rams held the Raiders to 3-of-14 on third down last week and made three fourth-down stops. According to the Rams’ official website, opponents have failed on fourth down seven times this season. And McVay said this week that the defense went about seven quarters without allowing a touchdown over the last two games.

The not-so-good: according to the Rams’ official website, the Rams have just 10 sacks and six takeaways, both near the bottom of the NFL. According to Last Word on Sports, the pass rush ranks 27th. And according to FOX Sports, Byron Young leads the team with four sacks.

Now, imagine a healthy Rams defense in December: Myles Garrett, Aaron Donald, Kobie Turner, Byron Young, Trent McDuffie — and maybe a veteran addition. That could be one of the best defenses in the league. But most of that improvement comes from getting their own players back, not from a trade. Garrett is still on injured reserve. According to the Rams’ official website, the team didn’t open his practice window this week, and McVay said: “We’re just on a week-to-week basis right now.” In September, according to Pro Football Talk, NFL Network’s Ian Rapoport reported that Garrett’s most likely return was the Rams’ Week 9 game against Washington. We’ll find out this week whether that’s still the plan.

So “scariest defense in the NFL” isn’t something the Rams can buy at the deadline. It’s something they’ll have to get healthy into.

Now let me give you my ranking of Grisak’s three ideas, and remember — this is not a report. This is just my opinion.

Number one: Josh Downs. He fills the most urgent need, he’s young, he asked for a trade and the price fits. The concern is the competition from the Steelers and Chiefs, and the fit with McVay’s three-tight-end looks.

Number two: A’Shawn Robinson. He’s a familiar face, ESPN actually named the Rams as a fit, and the Rams have a real hole in the middle of their defensive line. The question is how much of his salary the Rams would have to absorb.

Number three: Khalil Mack. He’s the most exciting name, and Grisak’s price is cheap. But he hasn’t asked out, he’s not linked to the Rams by any insider, and Garrett should be back eventually. If the Rams are going to spend their limited cap space on one move, I’d rather it go to a receiver or a defensive tackle.

Now let’s talk about today, because the Rams and Chargers play this afternoon.

According to Yahoo Sports, the game kicks off at 4:05 p.m. Eastern on FOX at SoFi Stadium, with Adam Amin, Drew Brees and Kristina Pink on the call. The Rams are 3-4. The Chargers are 0-6. According to the AP, the Rams are favored by six and a half.

And here’s the funny part: the player in today’s headline will be on the field — for the Chargers. According to the Chargers’ website, Mack was a full participant on Thursday and Friday and is off the injury report. So instead of joining the Rams’ defense, Mack’s job today is to sack Matthew Stafford.

The Rams’ final injury report, according to the Rams’ official website: long snapper Joe Cardona is doubtful, which means Jake McQuaide would be elevated to snap, according to McVay. Coleman Shelton, Colby Parkinson, Trent McDuffie and Jordan Whittington are all questionable. If Shelton can’t go, McVay said Dylan McMahon “would be the guy that would step up” at center.

For the Chargers, according to their website, safety Derwin James, guard Kayode Awosika, defensive back Deane Leonard and receiver Brenen Thompson are all out.

Here are my three quick keys.

Key number one: block Mack. Especially if McMahon is starting at center, the Rams need clean protection calls. Run the ball, throw it quickly and chip Mack with tight ends and backs.

Key number two: win the turnover battle. According to the Rams’ official website, the Chargers have the most giveaways in the NFL with 15, and Justin Herbert has thrown an interception in every game. McVay said this week that turnover margin is one of the stats that matters most. This is the week to win it.

Key number three: get a receiver besides Puka involved. If Atwell or Mumpfield make a few plays against a Chargers secondary missing Derwin James, it might change how urgently the Rams feel they need someone like Downs.

My prediction: Rams 27, Chargers 17. The Rams are the healthier, more confident team, and the turnover matchup favors them. But don’t be shocked if Mack makes at least one big play — he’d love nothing more than to remind every contender watching, including the team across the building, exactly what he can still do.

So here’s my bottom line.

No, the Rams are not bringing in Khalil Mack. The video behind that headline admits it’s a hypothetical. What’s real is that Blaine Grisak proposed three trades — Downs for a 2027 fourth, A’Shawn Robinson for a 2028 late-round pick and Mack for a 2028 late-round pick swap — and that Les Snead’s history suggests the Rams could make a late move, possibly on a Monday or on deadline day itself.

But with about 206,000 dollars in cap space, missing future picks and a head coach saying the Rams’ biggest “trades” will be players returning from injury, I’d expect one smart, affordable move at most. And if I had to bet on one name, it would be Downs, not Mack.
