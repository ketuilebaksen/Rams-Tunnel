Part one: the clip’s playbook

This one follows the exact template we’ve seen eleven times before. Let me walk you through it so you can spot it yourself next time.

The fake quote. It opens with a line it attributes to Schefter: “The same Cleveland seller who said Garrett wasn’t for sale is talking again.” Then it claims Schefter said, “I can confirm the Rams are making this call. A conditional fourth-round pick. This trade is going to happen.”

The fake date. It says this happened on “live ESPN First Take, Tuesday, November 3rd.”

The running joke. This time it’s a scavenger hunt: the Rams are a team captain in a visor reading clues, and the “item nobody can find” is the first-round pick they already spent.

The family story. This time the narrator was jogging behind his nephew, who was learning to ride a bike, when Schefter’s voice came out of his phone.

The Stephen A. Smith bit. As always, Stephen A. supposedly “took the whole thing into the stratosphere.”

The fake skeptic. This time it’s a retired gym teacher from his sister’s building named “Fainina,” who brings three objections, which the narrator then knocks down.

The hedge. And just like the last few clips, the narrator keeps saying it’s “expected, not official,” and that “nobody has reported that Cleveland has agreed to anything.”

Here’s the tell, and it shows up in every one of these. If Adam Schefter said “I can confirm” and “this trade is going to happen” on live national TV, you wouldn’t need a guy in a parking lot to tell you about it. It would be on Schefter’s X account, ESPN’s homepage, and every Rams and Browns beat writer’s feed within minutes. I checked all of those. It isn’t there.

Part two: what’s real in the clip

As usual, the fake clip is wrapped around real facts. Let’s give them credit.

The Bleacher Report article is real. On October 29th, Bleacher Report’s Kristopher Knox, with a K, not “Christopher,” published a piece on ideal landing spots for skill players at the deadline. For Jerry Jeudy, he picked the Rams. His reasoning: Davante Adams is hurt, and Jeudy’s route running and breakaway speed would fit McVay’s spacing-based offense. And yes, Knox wrote, “A strong offer would likely mean at least a conditional fourth-round pick.”

So the clip got this part right, almost word for word. But notice what it is: a columnist’s opinion about where a player would fit best. The clip even admits that, calling it “a columnist’s pick.” Then it puts the same idea in Schefter’s mouth as a confirmed call. That’s the trick.

There’s also something the clip left out of Knox’s article. Knox also mentioned Jeudy’s drop issues as a concern. You won’t hear that in the clip.

Jeudy’s 2024 season is real. He had 90 catches for 1,229 yards and made the Pro Bowl. The clip gets that right. Later it says “1,029 yards,” which is wrong, so the clip contradicts itself. But 1,229 is the correct number.

Jeudy did publicly admit frustration. On October 21st, he told Dan Labbe of Cleveland.com, “It’s kind of frustrating not being able to perform or be involved as much as you like.” He also said Deshaun Watson told him, “I’m going to need you some of these games, man,” and that he’s going to “continue to grind and continue to stay focused.” So that part is real. But listen to the whole quote. It’s a player saying he wants more work and he’s staying locked in. It’s not a trade request.

The Rams’ picks are real. The Rams sent their 2027 first-round pick to Cleveland in the Myles Garrett trade, and their 2027 third to Kansas City for Trent McDuffie. So the clip is right that the Rams’ remaining draft capital is mostly Day 3 picks.

Graziano’s report is real, sort of. Back on October 22nd, ESPN’s Dan Graziano reported that the Rams would explore a receiver trade after the Adams injury. I couldn’t find the exact quote the clip uses, “they don’t have much to trade,” so I’m marking that wording as unverified. But the general point, that the Rams want a receiver and don’t have a lot of premium picks, is accurate.

The Browns did once say Garrett wasn’t for sale. Cleveland was famously firm that it wasn’t trading Myles Garrett, until the deal finally happened in June. So the “seller who said not for sale” framing points at something real. The clip just uses it to suggest a new Jeudy deal is now in motion, with nothing behind it.

Part three: what’s wrong in the clip

Now for the parts that don’t hold up.

The Schefter quote. No record of it anywhere. Not on First Take, not on X, not on ESPN.com.

The Stephen A. Smith reaction. No record of that either.

The date. The clip says Tuesday, November 3rd. But on Thursday, November 5th, two days after that supposed “confirmation,” Tom Pelissero of The Ringer reported that the Browns are expected to keep Jeudy through the deadline, unless they get what he called a “truly crazy offer.” If Schefter had confirmed a Rams trade on Tuesday, Pelissero wouldn’t be reporting two days later that Jeudy is staying put.

The Rams’ receiver room. The clip says the room is Puka Nacua, Jordan Whittington, and Konata Mumpfield. It leaves out Tutu Atwell, who caught that 40-yard pass against the Chargers that set up a touchdown. It’s a small thing, but small mistakes like that tell you nobody checked.

Part four: the real Jeudy reporting

Let’s put the real reporting in one place, so you can see what the professionals are saying.

Tom Pelissero, The Ringer, November 5th: The Browns are expected to keep Jeudy through next week’s deadline, barring a “truly crazy offer.”

Mary Kay Cabot, Cleveland.com: The Browns would only trade Jeudy for premium draft capital.

Albert Breer, Sports Illustrated: Cleveland is focused on adding, looking at edge rushers and tight ends.

Jordan Schultz: Earlier, he reported the asking price was a third- or fourth-round pick.

Jeremy Fowler, ESPN: He doubted a fourth-rounder would be enough.

Jonathan Jones, The Athletic: Last month, he reported Jeudy looked like a trade candidate.

So the trend is clear. In October, Jeudy looked like he might be available. By November, the Browns had won enough games that they stopped thinking like sellers. Today, the most recent reporting says he’s staying unless somebody blows Cleveland away. That’s the opposite of “this trade is going to happen.”

Part five: why the Browns aren’t selling

To understand why, look at the standings. The Cleveland Browns are 5-2 and tied for first place in the AFC North. They’ve beaten the Steelers and the Ravens. That’s not a team holding a fire sale like the Colts. That’s a team trying to win its division.

And Jeudy has been part of those wins. According to Pro Football Talk, over the last few weeks he’s been more involved, including key late catches in the wins over Pittsburgh and Baltimore. His role is smaller than it was during his Pro Bowl year, because rookies KC Concepcion and Denzel Boston moved ahead of him on the depth chart and second-year tight end Harold Fannin leads the team in receiving. But a contender doesn’t usually trade a veteran receiver who’s catching big passes late in close wins.

Now here’s something I find really interesting, and to be clear, this is my own analysis, not a report. Remember the Garrett trade? Cleveland owns the Rams’ 2027 first-round pick. That means the Browns benefit if the Rams lose games this season, because a worse Rams record means a higher pick for Cleveland.

So think about it from the Browns’ side. Why would Cleveland send the Rams a receiver to help them win more games, for a conditional fourth, when every Rams win pushes down the value of the first-rounder the Browns already own? It’s not impossible. Teams make trades that help someone else all the time, if the price is right. But it’s one more reason a cheap Jeudy deal to the Rams makes less sense than the clip wants you to think.

The clip’s scavenger hunt joke says the item nobody can find is the first-round pick the Rams already spent. Here’s the punchline the clip missed: the team holding that missing item is the same team it says is selling Jeudy. Cleveland doesn’t just know where the Rams’ first-round pick is. It owns it.

Part six: Jerry Jeudy, the player in 2026

Let’s say the Browns did change their minds. Would Jeudy actually help the Rams? Here’s the full picture.

His 2024 season was excellent: 90 catches, 1,229 yards, Pro Bowl. He’s put up close to 4,900 career receiving yards, and at his best he’s a sharp route runner with breakaway speed after the catch.

But this season has been very different. Through seven games, per Athlon, Jeudy has 16 catches for 171 yards and no touchdowns. His PFF receiving grade is 62.2, which ranks 60th out of 76 qualifying receivers. Through the first three weeks, he had just two catches on five targets.

Then there’s the contract. Per Spotrac, via Athlon, his 2026 base salary is $1.5 million. In 2027, he’s due a $21.487 million option bonus, and it isn’t guaranteed. That’s the part that makes a trade workable for any team: you could get him for the rest of this year, then decide in the spring whether to pay that big bonus. Bleacher Report’s Knox wrote that trading him would save Cleveland $3.9 million in 2026 and $7 million in 2027. Those two sources don’t line up perfectly with each other, so I’d treat the exact 2026 cap number as unsettled.

Here’s what is settled. The Rams have $206,406 in cap space, according to Athlon. Whether Jeudy’s remaining 2026 cost is under a million or closer to four million, it’s more than the Rams have. So, like every receiver on this list, Jeudy would need the Rams to create space first, probably through a restructure like Kyren Williams’ deal, which could free around $3.5 million.

On fit. This is not a report, just my take. Jeudy is a better deep threat than Keenan Allen, which matters, because the Rams’ real problem is explosive plays. Since Adams got hurt, the Rams have had one completion over 20 yards. Their explosive play rate ranks 27th in the league. A receiver with real speed who can run the full route tree would help Stafford push the ball. The concern is that Jeudy’s 2026 numbers are some of the worst of his career, and Knox flagged his drops. You’d be betting on the 2024 version showing up, not the 2026 version.

Part seven: answering “Fainina”

The clip’s fake skeptic raised three objections, and I’ll be honest, they’re good ones. So let me give you my real answers.

Objection one: “A receiver who publicly admits frustration is a risk in any locker room.” I don’t think that’s fair to Jeudy, based on what he actually said. He said it was frustrating to be less involved, then said he’s going to grind and stay focused, and he’s made big catches in Cleveland’s wins since. That sounds like a competitor, not a problem. Wanting the ball is pretty normal for a receiver.

Objection two: “A seller who swears he’s not selling and then starts talking is either lying now or was lying then.” Here’s the thing. In Jeudy’s case, the seller isn’t even talking. The latest reporting, from Pelissero on Thursday, is that the Browns are expected to keep him. The clip’s whole premise, that Cleveland is “talking again,” isn’t supported by anything.

Objection three: “A 4-4 team shouldn’t hand out picks to fix a problem that heals in six to ten weeks.” This is the best of the three, and it’s the real debate around the Rams’ deadline. Adams’ timeline, according to ESPN’s injury tracker, is six to ten weeks after surgery that went well. McVay said he’s trending toward the shorter end. If that holds, Adams could be back around the end of November, and any receiver you trade for becomes your third option in December. So yes, I’d only pay a real price for someone you’d want beyond this season. Jeudy’s non-guaranteed 2027 bonus gives you that option, which is why his name keeps coming up. But the Browns aren’t selling.

Matthew Stafford’s elbow. After the scare on Sunday, Stafford practiced fully on Wednesday and Thursday. He said, “It feels good,” and, “I haven’t missed a rep at practice. The offensive line is elite. One stat I loved this week, from the game preview on Yahoo: the Rams’ offensive line has allowed the fewest pressures in the NFL. Against the Chargers, the line got an A-plus grade, allowing just one pressure. That’s a big reason Stafford’s elbow being sore didn’t turn into a disaster.

Part ten: Washington preview

Rams at Commanders, Sunday at 1 p.m. Eastern on FOX, at Northwest Stadium. The Rams are 4-4, tied with Seattle for second in the NFC West behind San Francisco at 6-1. Washington is 2-4-1, coming off a 27-10 win over the Eagles. The Rams are 5.5-point favorites, and the over/under is 47.5.

Washington’s injuries. Defensive tackles Daron Payne and Javon Kinlaw were placed on injured reserve this week, along with running back Jeremy McNichols. Washington is hoping Johnny Newton returns, but his status is uncertain. That’s a lot of missing interior beef.

The Rams’ run game. Kyren Williams and Blake Corum have been rolling. They combined for 192 rushing yards against the Raiders and 129 against the Chargers. Against a thin Washington interior, I’d expect McVay to lean on the ground game.

Turnovers. This is the number that worries me. The Rams have 12 giveaways in eight games, and Stafford has eight interceptions. Washington is coming off its best defensive game of the year, holding the Eagles to 10 points. If the Rams protect the ball, they should win. If they give Daniels short fields, it gets interesting.

Penalties. The Rams have 61 accepted penalties, 24th in the league. Washington has 55. On the road, in a noisy stadium, cutting down pre-snap mistakes matters.

The secondary. No Watson. McDuffie and Curl uncertain. Josh Wallace starting. Forbes against his old team. Jayden Daniels can extend plays, and the pass rush, with or without Garrett, has to finish.

My prediction. I’m sticking with Rams 24, Commanders 19. The run game takes over in the second half, the offensive line keeps Stafford clean, Puka gets his 9 or 10 catches, and the defense gets one late stop. If McDuffie and Curl are both out, it could be closer.

Part eleven: your fake-clip checklist, updated

Three fake clips this week, and four days left until the deadline. Here’s the updated checklist.

One: check the date against the latest reporting. Today’s clip claimed a Tuesday confirmation. Thursday’s real reporting said the Browns are keeping Jeudy. When the “report” is older than real news that contradicts it, it isn’t real.

Two: find the original source. Today’s clip took Kristopher Knox’s Bleacher Report column almost word for word, including “at least a conditional fourth-round pick.” Then it said Schefter said it. When the price in a “Schefter report” matches a columnist’s article exactly, the columnist is the real source.

Three: look for the hedge. Every one of these clips says “expected, not official” and “nobody has reported that the team agreed.” A real insider doesn’t hedge like that after saying “I can confirm.”

Four: check what’s missing. This clip skipped Jeudy’s drop issues, his weak 2026 numbers, Tutu Atwell, the Browns’ 5-2 record, and the fact that Cleveland owns the Rams’ first-round pick. A real report wouldn’t miss the most important context.
