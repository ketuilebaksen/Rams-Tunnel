First, let’s set the scene, because this is how the Rams got here.

This season started with huge expectations. According to FOX Sports, the Rams entered the year as the heavy Super Bowl favorite. In the offseason, they traded for Myles Garrett, the 2025 Defensive Player of the Year. And in late August, Aaron Donald came out of retirement. On paper, a defensive front of Garrett, Donald, Kobie Turner and Poona Ford was supposed to be one of the scariest in football.

It hasn’t worked out that way — at least not yet. Garrett played through knee discomfort in Week 1, then had a procedure and went on injured reserve. Donald has dealt with a back injury — according to Yahoo Sports, he missed the Week 4 game in Philadelphia because of it. According to the Washington Post, Donald said before the Buffalo game that it was just a short setback in his comeback, and he’s been back on the field since. He had a non-injury rest day on Friday and has no injury designation for the Raiders game. Turner went on injured reserve this week with a groin injury and will miss at least four games. And Ford is out for the season with a ruptured triceps. So the dream defensive line has barely been on the field together.

And the offense has been hit just as hard. Davante Adams ruptured his hamstring against Arizona and is expected to miss six to 10 weeks. Before the injury, he had 30 catches for 480 yards and four touchdowns. And then on Thursday, Puka Nacua left practice early with a calf injury. McVay called it “a little minor thing in there,” but Puka was listed as questionable for the Raiders game, and McVay said it would be a pregame decision. According to the AP, Puka has 37 catches for 417 yards and a touchdown in four games this season.

Behind those two, according to the Rams’ official website, the receivers on the 53-man roster are Konata Mumpfield, Jordan Whittington, Tutu Atwell, Alex Bachman and rookie C.J. Daniels Jr. They’re all capable players. But according to Newsweek, apart from Puka and Adams, the Rams’ wide receivers had just 13 combined catches through six games. That’s why trade talk is everywhere.

And don’t forget what the Rams already have outside the receiver room. According to Rams Newswire, Kyren Williams has 30 catches for 217 yards and a receiving touchdown this season — he’s one of the most productive pass-catching running backs in football. Tight ends Colby Parkinson, Tyler Higbee and Davis Allen can carry more of the load, too. Parkinson caught the go-ahead touchdown against Arizona and was a full participant in practice all week. So the Rams’ passing game isn’t empty. It’s just missing the two receivers who made it special.

And the numbers that FOX Sports shared this week show how much is riding on the next few weeks. Since the playoffs expanded in 2020, only seven of 36 teams that started 2-4 have made the postseason. That’s about one in five. The Rams aren’t dead. But they don’t have much room for error. And the betting markets have noticed. According to FOX Sports, the Rams are now 12-1 to win the Super Bowl. They entered the season as the favorite. That’s how far the expectations have fallen in six weeks.

Now let’s talk about the reporting, because it’s important to know exactly what’s real.

On October 20th, Rams Wire’s Cameron DaSilva published a list of eight receivers the Rams could trade for. These were his ideas, not reported talks. His list: Josh Downs, Brian Thomas Jr., Jordan Addison, Jerry Jeudy, George Pickens, Jauan Jennings, Stefon Diggs and Chris Godwin. Pro Football Rumors’ Sam Robinson also mentioned Josh Palmer, Keenan Allen and Calvin Ridley as possible options.

Then on Wednesday, ESPN’s Graziano wrote: “My understanding is that they will at least explore the possibility of trading for a wide receiver in the wake of the Davante Adams injury.”

And that same day, McVay was asked about it, and according to the Rams’ official website, he said: “That’s not something that we’ve talked about right now.” He added: “We’ve got some depth on our roster. Maybe we talked about the practice squad, but not anything external.”

So the honest picture is this: the Rams are expected to look at the market before the November 10th deadline. But nothing is close, and the head coach is focused on the players he has.

And there’s one more big obstacle: money. According to Pro Football Rumors, citing Over the Cap, the Rams have just under 990,000 dollars in cap space. And they’ve already traded their 2027 first-round pick, their 2027 third-round pick, their 2028 second-round pick and their 2029 third-round pick in the Garrett and Trent McDuffie deals. So whoever the Rams target has to be cheap — both in salary and in draft picks.

Now let’s get to the first big name: Josh Downs.

On October 23rd, Anthony Palacios of Last Word on Sports wrote that Downs could be an interesting option for the Rams. Here’s what we know.

Downs was a third-round pick by the Colts in 2023. According to Last Word on Sports, last season he had 58 catches for 566 yards and four touchdowns. This season has been tougher. According to Heavy, he has 24 catches for 289 yards, and according to Last Word on Sports, he hasn’t caught a touchdown yet. Palacios called his season “a complete disappointment.”

But there are reasons to think he could bounce back in a better situation. According to Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak, in 2025, Downs had a 75.3 percent success rate against man coverage and an 82.4 percent success rate against press. He ranked fourth in the NFL in first downs per route on third and fourth down. That’s exactly what the Rams have been missing — a receiver who wins on the money downs. And DaSilva at Rams Wire pointed out something else: Downs has experience returning punts, which has been a weak spot for the Rams this season. So he could help in two phases, not just one. He’s a shifty, fast slot receiver who can also line up outside, according to DaSilva — though DaSilva also said he “wouldn’t be a one-for-one replacement” for Adams.

And the price might be reasonable. According to Last Word on Sports, if the Colts fall to 2-6, a fourth-round pick is the likely ask. Palacios wrote that “Downs is not valued as a first-rounder.” According to Heavy, Grisak estimated the Rams would only take on about 2 million dollars in salary. That fits the Rams’ tight cap much better than most of the names on this list.

But there’s a complication. According to Last Word on Sports, the Colts’ top receiver, Alec Pierce, is sidelined and “won’t be back for a few more weeks.” Trading Downs would leave Indianapolis even thinner at receiver. So the Colts would need a good reason to sell. And the Colts aren’t the only team that could call. According to Last Word on Sports, the Dallas Cowboys, Carolina Panthers and Kansas City Chiefs have also been mentioned as possible fits. Those are just ideas, too — but competition could push the price up.

There’s one more interesting detail about Downs. According to Heavy, his uncle is Dre’ Bly, who won a Super Bowl ring with the St. Louis Rams after the 1999 season. And his brother, Caleb Downs, is a first-round rookie with the Cowboys.

My take on Downs: he’s not a superstar. He won’t replace Adams by himself. But he’s young, he’s cheap, he’s on an expiring contract, and he’s good at exactly the things the Rams need on third down. If the Colts would take a fourth- or fifth-round pick, I’d make that call.

Now let’s get to the second big name — the one we haven’t really covered yet: Stefon Diggs.

On October 23rd, Heavy’s Eduardo Razo wrote about a trade idea from FanSided’s Christopher Kline that would send Diggs from Washington to the Rams. Again, this is a writer’s proposal, not a report.

Here’s Diggs’ situation. He signed with Washington as a free agent this offseason. According to Heavy, the Commanders are 1-4-1 this season. That’s a team that could become a seller before November 10th.

And here’s what Diggs has done this year. According to Heavy, through five appearances, he has 24 catches for 214 yards and four touchdowns, with a 70.8 overall grade from PFF. He’s 32 years old. He’s not the 1,400-yard receiver he was in Buffalo, but he’s still productive — four touchdowns in five games is a lot more than most of the names on this list.

And remember the career he’s had. In Buffalo, Diggs was one of the most productive receivers in football — in 2020, he led the NFL with 127 catches and 1,535 yards, and he put together four straight 1,000-yard seasons with the Bills. Then came a tough stretch: he tore his ACL with Houston in 2024, moved on to New England in 2025, and signed with Washington this year. So he’s been through a serious knee injury and three teams in three years. But four touchdowns through five games this season tells you he can still find the end zone. According to DaSilva, he also made a one-handed catch on Monday Night Football this season and scored a 1-yard touchdown against the 49ers. When the ball is near the goal line, he’s still a weapon — and red zone touchdowns are exactly where the Rams have struggled.

Kline’s proposal would send a 2028 third-round pick to Washington for Diggs. That’s interesting, because a 2028 third is one of the picks the Rams actually still have. And Kline made a strong case: “In terms of guaranteed production, the Rams won’t do much better than Diggs.” DaSilva at Rams Wire also said Diggs could be a strong second receiver who would complement Puka’s game.

But there are real problems here, too.

Problem number one: Washington might not sell. According to DaSilva, Washington lost Terry McLaurin to a long-term injury. Trading Diggs would leave the Commanders without their top two receivers. DaSilva said that makes a Diggs trade less likely right now — unless Washington keeps losing.

Problem number two: the money. I couldn’t find Diggs’ 2026 contract numbers in any of the reporting I checked, so I’m not going to guess. But here’s the key point: with under a million dollars in cap space, the Rams can only take on a small salary. If Diggs is making a big salary this season, the Rams would need to send money back or restructure a contract to make it work. That’s something to watch if this idea gains steam.

Problem number three: age and fit. Diggs is 32. According to Heavy, he’s averaging 8.9 yards per catch this season, which tells you he’s being used more as a short and intermediate target than a deep threat. That’s not a bad thing — Stafford would love a reliable target on third down. But he’s not the vertical outside receiver who replaces everything Adams brings.

And there’s one more interesting angle. The Rams play Washington on November 8th — just two days before the trade deadline. So the Rams will see Diggs up close right before they have to make any final decision. And Washington will be deciding whether to buy or sell at the exact same time.

My take on Diggs: for a 2028 third-round pick and a salary the Rams can actually fit, I’d think about it. He’s productive, he’s experienced, and he’d help right away. But if it costs a big restructure or a better pick, I’d pass. A 32-year-old receiver for half a season isn’t worth mortgaging even more of the future.

Now let’s quickly go through some of the other names on DaSilva’s list, because a couple of them are interesting.

George Pickens of the Cowboys. According to DaSilva, Pickens has 428 yards and a touchdown so far this season — well below his 2025 pace of 84 yards per game and nine touchdowns. DaSilva noted there’s no clear path to a long-term deal in Dallas. Pickens would be the closest thing to a true X receiver who could replace Adams on the outside. But Pickens would be expensive in both money and picks, and the Cowboys would have to decide to sell. For the Rams’ cap situation, I just don’t see how it works. DaSilva’s argument was that trading Pickens could be better for Dallas than using the franchise tag on him again in 2027. That makes sense for the Cowboys — but it would still take a big package of picks and a lot of salary room to get him, and the Rams have neither right now.

Jordan Addison of the Vikings. According to DaSilva, Addison has two years left on his deal, so he’s not a rental. He had five catches for 90 yards against Tampa Bay, but only 73 yards on 13 targets in his other three games. He’s a deep threat on paper, but he hasn’t played like one this season.

Jauan Jennings, also with the Vikings now. According to DaSilva, Jennings signed with Minnesota as a free agent this year, but he has just two catches for nine yards despite playing 74 percent of the snaps. He does know the NFC West from his time with the 49ers. But the production just isn’t there.

And then there are the names we’ve covered before. Brian Thomas Jr. — according to DaSilva, he has 812 yards over his last 20 games after his 1,282-yard rookie season, and he plays for former Rams offensive coordinator Liam Coen in Jacksonville. But the Jaguars have “shown no interest” in trading him, according to Tom Pelissero, as reported by Bleacher Report. Jerry Jeudy — according to DaSilva, he has 12 catches for 190 yards, and the Browns are 4-2 and may not sell. And Chris Godwin — he has 23 catches for 216 yards, but according to Athlon, his cap hit is 33.7 million dollars this season, which makes him almost impossible for the Rams to fit.

So let me rank the options for the Rams, considering talent, price and cap space.

Number one: Josh Downs. Cheap salary, a reasonable pick, and a skill set that fits what McVay needs on third down. The only question is whether the Colts will sell with Alec Pierce hurt.

Number two: Stefon Diggs. The most proven producer on the list this season, and a proposed price — a 2028 third — that the Rams could actually pay. But Washington may not sell, and his salary is a question mark.

Number three: Jerry Jeudy. His 1.5 million dollar base salary fits, and the price has been reported in the Day 3 range. But Cleveland is winning.

Number four: Brian Thomas Jr. The most talent, but the highest price, and Jacksonville doesn’t seem interested.

And at the bottom: Pickens and Godwin. Great players at their best, but the money just doesn’t work for this Rams team right now.

Now, here’s the bigger question: should the Rams make any trade at all?

I think it depends on the next three games. Today, the Rams play the Raiders. Next week, they host the Chargers. Then they play Washington on November 8th. The deadline is two days after that. If the Rams win two of those three games, they’re 4-5 or better and back in the wild-card race. That’s when a small move makes sense. If they lose two of those three, they’re 3-6 or worse, and the smart move might be to protect what’s left of their future.

And there’s one more thing that could change everything: Myles Garrett. According to Pro Football Talk, NFL Network’s Ian Rapoport reported Friday that Garrett is “likely to return to practice next week.” McVay called it “a possibility.” A healthy Garrett could do more for this team than any receiver trade. And Adams himself could be back around the end of November if he heals on the fast end of his six-to-10-week timeline. Think about the timing. Adams got hurt on October 18th. Six weeks from then is November 29th — right around the Rams’ first game after their Week 11 bye, against the Packers. Ten weeks would be late December, around the Christmas Day game in Seattle. So any receiver the Rams trade for would really be a bridge for about four to eight games. After that, he’d become the third option behind Puka and Adams. That’s another reason the price has to stay low. You don’t pay a premium for a bridge. And Puka’s calf adds another layer. If his injury turns out to be truly minor, the Rams have their number one target back, and a trade becomes a luxury. If it lingers for a few weeks, the Rams could be without both of their top two receivers at the same time — and then a trade becomes a necessity. That’s why today’s inactive list matters so much for the trade deadline, too.

So I agree with the approach the transcript going around took: don’t panic, don’t sacrifice valuable draft picks, but don’t ignore an obvious weakness either. Make a smart, cheap move if one is there. And let the young receivers keep proving themselves in the meantime. The worst thing the Rams could do is panic after one bad week and give up a valuable pick for a receiver who plays six games and then becomes the third option. The best thing they could do is find a player like Downs or Diggs at a price that doesn’t hurt the future, and let him help Stafford get through the next month.

Now let’s talk about today’s game, because the results over the next few weeks will decide all of this.

According to FOX Sports, the Rams opened the week as 8.5-point favorites, and the line had dropped to 4.5 midweek. According to Heavy, it moved again to 3.5 after the Puka news. The series between these two teams is tied 8-8, and the Rams won the last meeting, 20-15, in October 2024.

According to FOX Sports, Matthew Stafford leads the NFL with 1,794 passing yards and 164 completions, but he’s also thrown six interceptions. Kirk Cousins is tied for the league lead with 14 touchdown passes. And Raiders tight end Brock Bowers is averaging 95.5 receiving yards per game — a pace that would break Kellen Winslow’s tight end record of 94.7 from 1984. That’s the matchup the Rams’ defense has to win, especially with linebacker Nate Landman out with a concussion. Veteran Eric Kendricks, a former first-team All-Pro, is being promoted from the practice squad to replace him. McVay said Friday: “Coaching against him for all those years, there was a lot of respect.” Kendricks has seen just about every kind of tight end in this league, and today he’ll need all of that experience against Bowers.

And the Raiders know they’ve been letting games slip. According to FOX Sports, they lost 45-17 at New England, and then lost by three points to both Kansas City and Buffalo. Head coach Klint Kubiak said hoping to keep games close in the fourth quarter is “not a winning mindset.” Cousins said that when he watches the Rams on tape, he sees “a really, really good football team.” According to the AP, the Raiders have scored touchdowns on only 12 of their 23 trips inside the red zone, and just four of 10 during their three-game losing streak. And according to the Rams’ official website, Raiders tight end Carter Runyon and receiver Dont’e Thornton Jr. are out today, and rookie running back Mike Washington Jr. is questionable. So the Raiders have their own injuries to deal with — it’s not just the Rams.

Stafford said this week: “That’s not the mindset at all around here.” And McVay said: “Our full focus and concentration is on the Raiders.”

Here are my three keys.

Key number one: protect the football. Stafford leads the league in yards, but six interceptions is too many. With a thin receiver group, the Rams can’t give the Raiders short fields.

Key number two: stop Brock Bowers. He’s on a record pace. Kendricks, the safeties and the slot corners have to make him work for every catch.

Key number three: make the receivers by committee work. Whether Puka plays or not, McVay said it’ll be “by committee with all of your eligibles, tight ends, your backs.” If Mumpfield, Whittington and the tight ends produce today, the trade talk gets a lot quieter. And if Puka sits, according to the AP, rookie C.J. Daniels could make his NFL debut. Mumpfield already showed what he can do when Puka missed Weeks 2 and 3 — four catches for 93 yards and a go-ahead touchdown in Denver. McVay said this week: “He’s one of those guys that when he’s been counted on, he’s delivered.” Today is another chance to prove it.

My prediction stays the same: if Puka plays, Rams 27, Raiders 20. If he doesn’t, Rams 24, Raiders 20.

So here’s my bottom line.

There is no blockbuster trade. The Rams have not brought in a star receiver to replace Davante Adams. What’s real is that ESPN reported the Rams will explore the receiver market, and writers have floated names like Josh Downs and Stefon Diggs. Downs is the cleanest fit — cheap, young and good on third down. Diggs is the most productive — four touchdowns this season — and the proposed price of a 2028 third-round pick is one the Rams could pay, if the money works. But nothing is close, and McVay is focused on the players he has.
