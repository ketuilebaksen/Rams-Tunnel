Part one: Terrance Ferguson's season is over

Let's start with the tough news.

According to a November 23 Fox Sports report, Terrance Ferguson needs surgery to repair the deltoid ligament in his ankle. That ends his season.

Here's how we got here.

Ferguson first hurt the ankle in the Week 3 loss to the Denver Broncos. He went on injured reserve. Then, at the beginning of November, the Rams opened his practice window, and he returned to practice. That was a great sign.

But then came another setback. During a Friday workout before the Week 9 win over Washington, Ferguson hurt his hamstring. He had been on track to play in that game. Instead, the hamstring rehab complicated his ankle recovery, and the ankle never got where it needed to be.

According to the report, McVay explained that Ferguson couldn't properly plant his foot. And for a tight end, that's a huge problem. Tight ends have to push off, change direction, and explode out of their breaks. If you can't plant, you can't play.

So surgery became the next step. It's not the outcome anyone wanted, but it's the one that gives him the best chance to be fully healthy next year.

Part two: what the Rams lose with Ferguson

Let's look at what Ferguson brought.

In three games this season, he caught seven passes for 63 yards and a touchdown. Those aren't huge numbers. But it was only three games, and he's a second-year tight end out of Oregon. The Rams were hoping he'd become a much bigger part of the offense.

And with Puka out and Adams hurt, Ferguson was one of the weapons the Rams were counting on getting back. He could have given Stafford another way to attack defenses, especially down the middle of the field.

Now that's gone.

There's one more painful detail. The Rams already used one of their IR return designations on Ferguson when they opened his practice window. So that designation is basically spent, and he never got back on the field.

The good news is that McVay already said they have "a little bit of a luxury" with designations, since Puka had surgery and won't need one. So the Rams aren't in a crisis there. But it still hurts.

Part three: the tight end room without Ferguson

So who's left at tight end?

Tyler Higbee. The veteran. Stafford's trusted target in the red zone. He scored in Arizona, his second touchdown of the season.

Colby Parkinson. He made that great play-action catch down to the 1-yard line in Arizona. PFF graded him 83.5, according to Blaine Grisak of Rams On SI.

Davis Allen. He scored a 19-yard touchdown in Arizona, his third of the year. McVay called him "tough as hell" for playing through a back injury.

Max Klare. The rookie. McVay said earlier this month he's "really progressing."

So the Rams still have four tight ends. And they've been a huge part of the offense. In Arizona, the Rams used a lot of 13 personnel, with three tight ends on the field. McVay said before that game it "was going to be a big part of the game plan."

But McVay also said after that game that the tight ends are "still so banged up." So depth matters. Losing Ferguson means less room for error if another tight end gets hurt.

What Ferguson's injury means for next year

Let's look at the bright side for a second. This is not a report. It's my take.

Ferguson is young. He's in only his second NFL season. Getting surgery now, in late November, gives him a full offseason to heal.

That's a lot better than trying to play through an ankle he can't plant on. If he had tried to come back, he might have hurt it even worse. And for a tight end, the ankle is everything. Blocking, running routes, catching in traffic. It all starts with your feet.

So if the surgery goes well, Ferguson should be ready for training camp next year, with a chance to finally have a full, healthy season. Remember, the Rams believed in him enough to make him their top receiving tight end option this year.

So this hurts now. But it could be the right move for his future.

How losing Ferguson changes the game plan

Here's how I think this affects McVay's plans. This is not a report. It's my analysis.

The Rams have been leaning on 13 personnel, with three tight ends on the field. That's a lot of tight end snaps every week. And with Ferguson out, those snaps will be spread across Higbee, Parkinson, Allen, and Klare.

That's fine for one game. But over a full month, it's a lot of wear and tear on a group McVay already called "so banged up."

So I think we'll see McVay use the pony formation even more. With two running backs on the field instead of a third tight end, the Rams can give their tight ends a break without losing their physical style. And in Arizona, that two-back look was the most productive thing the offense did.

So in a weird way, losing Ferguson might push McVay even further into the new identity he found in Arizona.

Part four: the offense keeps losing weapons

Let's step back and look at the bigger picture. The Rams' offense has lost a lot.

Puka Nacua: out for the season after MCL surgery. McVay said he'll be out "a couple months," and that he's "pretty much" done for the year.

Davante Adams: still on IR with his hamstring. McVay said there's "a possibility for the game after the Packers."

Terrance Ferguson: now out for the season with ankle surgery.

Jordan Whittington: on IR with a back injury.

The offensive line: Coleman Shelton out, with McVay saying they'll reevaluate him "in a couple weeks." Dylan McMahon on IR. Steve Avila dealing with a hamstring injury.

That's a lot of missing pieces. And it's why people are suddenly asking about Brandon Aiyuk.

Part five: who is Brandon Aiyuk?

Let's talk about the name in the headline.

Brandon Aiyuk was one of the best receivers in the NFL at his peak. In 2023, with the 49ers, he had 1,342 receiving yards. After that season, he signed a four-year contract worth $120 million.

But then everything changed.

In 2024, he played seven games and caught 25 passes for 374 yards before a serious knee injury. Then he missed the entire 2025 season. And according to the SB Nation article, he hasn't practiced in about two years.

The report says Aiyuk recently hired agent Drew Rosenhaus to help resolve his situation with the 49ers. And now, the 49ers have released him. So he's free to sign anywhere.

The Washington Commanders have long been seen as his preferred destination, according to the article. The Rams are just a hypothetical option in the writer's opinion.

Part six: why some people want the Rams to try

So why is Kenneth Arthur suggesting the Rams?

It's simple. The Rams need receivers. Puka is out for the season. Adams isn't back yet. And the young group of Konata Mumpfield, Xavier Smith, Tutu Atwell, and CJ Daniels has played well, but they're unproven.

And at his best, Aiyuk was a star. Great route runner, great after the catch, and he knows how to beat defenses in a Kyle Shanahan-style offense. McVay's system has a lot in common with what Aiyuk ran in San Francisco.

There's also something fun about the idea. Aiyuk leaving the 49ers and joining their division rival. You know Rams fans would love that.

So I understand why the idea is out there.

Part seven: why it probably wouldn't help soon

But here's the catch, and it's a big one.

Aiyuk almost certainly wouldn't help the Rams against the Packers on Wednesday. And probably not for weeks after that.

Kenneth Arthur himself said a November return is highly unlikely. He even admitted that Aiyuk might not play at all this season. He floated a possible return around Week 16 against the Seattle Seahawks, after about a month to get ready. But that's just the writer's guess, not a medical clearance or a plan.

Think about it. A player who hasn't practiced in about two years. He'd have to get back into football shape. Learn McVay's playbook. Build timing with Stafford. And prove his knee is healthy enough to play.

That's a lot to ask in the middle of a playoff race.

How a midseason free agent signing works

Since Aiyuk was released, let me explain how this would work if a team wanted him.

Because he was released, not traded, Aiyuk is a free agent. That means any team can sign him at any time. There's no trade deadline for free agents. And a team doesn't have to give up any draft picks.

That's why this is different from the Ladd McConkey situation at the deadline. With McConkey, the Rams would have had to trade for him and give up picks. With Aiyuk, a team just needs a contract offer, a roster spot, and enough salary cap room.

And the price could be much lower than you'd think. A player who hasn't played since 2024 isn't going to get anything close to $120 million on the open market. Depending on how his old contract was structured, his new team might only need to offer a short, cheap deal to see if he can still play.

So the real cost for the Rams wouldn't be money or picks. It would be a roster spot and time, while the team waits to see if he can get healthy.

Part eight: the risk

Let me lay out the risk clearly. This is not a report. It's my analysis.

If it works: Aiyuk gets healthy, learns the offense fast, and becomes a weapon for the playoffs. That would be a huge boost.

If it doesn't work: The Rams spend a roster spot, some salary, and a lot of time on a player who never gets on the field. Meanwhile, the young receivers lose snaps and the coaching staff spends energy on a long shot.

And there's the money. Before the deadline, the Rams were tight against the salary cap. Over The Cap had them about $600,000 over before restructures for Kyren Williams and Kam Curl created about $3 million in space. Any deal for Aiyuk would have to fit inside that.

So the Rams would have to evaluate his health very carefully before taking a chance.

And there's one more thing to remember. Every roster spot matters right now. The Rams are juggling injuries on the offensive line, at receiver, and at tight end. Using a spot on a player who can't play for a month means one less healthy player available on game day.

Part nine: my take on Aiyuk

So would I sign him? This is not a report. Here's my honest opinion.

I'd want the Rams to at least check on him. Bring him in, look at his knee, and see where he's at physically. That's just doing your homework.

But I wouldn't sign him unless the doctors say he's close to ready, and the price is cheap. A low-cost, low-risk deal? Sure, why not. A big contract or a major roster commitment for a player who might not play this season? No.

And honestly, I think Washington makes more sense for him, since that's been seen as his preferred destination for a while.

So my guess is that Aiyuk does not end up a Ram. But if he does, it'll be a smart, careful move, not a "blockbuster."

What about other free agents?

Some fans will ask: if not Aiyuk, then who?

Earlier this season, we talked about Tyreek Hill being a free agent. But there's still no report of Rams contact, no offer, and no negotiations. So I'm keeping that in the "fan idea" category.

And that's the problem with free agents in late November. The players still available usually have a reason they're available. Injuries. Age. Contract issues. If they were healthy and ready to help, a team would have signed them already.

So the Rams' best "new receiver" might actually be Davante Adams coming back from IR. McVay said there's "a possibility for the game after the Packers." That would be like adding a star, without spending a dime.

Part ten: what the Rams already have

While everyone talks about Aiyuk, let's not forget the receivers the Rams actually have.

Konata Mumpfield. In Arizona, three catches for 81 yards, with big gains of 27 and 44 yards. PFF graded him 80.6.

Tutu Atwell. He brings speed and knows the system. His 31-yard catch on fourth-and-6 in Arizona woke up the offense.

Xavier Smith. First career touchdown in Arizona. PFF graded him 92.6.

CJ Daniels. Caught his first NFL pass in Arizona. McVay says he "plays big at the catch point."

Brennan Presley and Tyler Scott on the practice squad.

And of course, Davante Adams, once he's back. McVay said he's been "attacking his rehab like crazy."

Nobody expects Mumpfield or Atwell to replace Puka by themselves. But together, with the tight ends and backs, they gave Stafford enough to win 34-6 in Arizona. Stafford threw to nine different receivers in that game.

McVay said after the Puka news: "I'm confident. I thought they did a really nice job last week."

What Stafford needs from this group

Let's talk about what Matthew Stafford needs from the receivers he has. This is not a report. It's my take.

He doesn't need a superstar. He needs three things.

First, be where you're supposed to be. Stafford throws with anticipation. He lets the ball go before the receiver breaks. If the receiver is in the wrong spot, it's an incompletion or an interception.

Second, win your matchup a few times a game. Not every play. Just enough to keep the defense honest. In Arizona, Mumpfield, Smith, Atwell, and Daniels each had a catch longer than 20 yards. That's enough.

Third, hold on to the ball. Young receivers sometimes drop easy passes when the pressure is on. Against the Packers, every possession matters.

McVay said before the Arizona game that Stafford keeps "everybody nice and calm" and that he can "give him information, and he's able to echo and articulate it to the other 10." That's going to matter even more now, with Puka and Ferguson both gone for the year.

Part eleven: McVay's formula without his weapons

Here's how McVay has been making it work. And I expect more of the same.

The run game. Over the last four games, the Rams have outrushed opponents 653 to 175. According to ESPN Research, that's their biggest four-game rushing differential since 1984, when Eric Dickerson ran for 2,105 yards.

The pony formation. Two running backs, Kyren Williams and Blake Corum, on the field together. In Arizona, 17 plays from that look gained 197 yards and a touchdown, according to ESPN's Sarah Barshop and NFL Next Gen Stats.

Three tight ends. Even without Ferguson, the Rams still have Higbee, Parkinson, Allen, and Klare.

Spreading the ball. Nine different pass catchers in Arizona.

When McVay was asked if he'd keep using the two-back look, he said: "We're in the midst of that right now, so we'll figure it out." He said it depends on "your players" first and then "the opponent's personnel and their scheme."

So McVay will keep adjusting. That's what he does.

Part twelve: the Packers game

Now, Wednesday night.

Rams vs. Packers, Wednesday, November 25, 8 p.m. Eastern, on Netflix. Both teams are 6-4.

This is a huge game for the NFC playoff race. With three wild card spots available, head-to-head results could become a tiebreaker.

Here's what the Rams will have:

On offense: No Puka, no Ferguson, probably no Adams. A patched-together offensive line, with Beaux Limmer likely at center and Justin Dedich coming back off IR. A run game led by Kyren, Corum, and Ronnie Rivers. And the young receivers.

On defense: Myles Garrett, Aaron Donald, and possibly Kobie Turner, who McVay said "hopefully will be ready to go this week." Plus Trent McDuffie, Kam Curl, and undrafted rookie Wesley Bailey, who graded 93.0 in Arizona.

So the Rams will probably try to win this game with defense and the run game. And whatever new ideas McVay came up with during the bye.

Part thirteen: the bigger question for the front office

The original video asked a good question: should the Rams trust the receivers they have, or look outside the building for help?

Here's my answer. This is not a report.

For the Packers game, they have no choice. They'll trust what they have. Aiyuk couldn't be ready even if they signed him today.

After that, it depends on two things. How the young receivers play against the Packers. And whether Adams comes back healthy for the Chiefs.

If the young guys play well and Adams returns, the Rams probably don't need to take a risk on anyone. If the passing game struggles and Adams has a setback, then the front office might start looking harder at outside options.

But remember, outside options are limited. The trade deadline is gone. So it would have to be a free agent, like Aiyuk, who may or may not be healthy.

Part fourteen: the 49ers side of this

One more interesting angle. The 49ers releasing Aiyuk tells you something about the 49ers too.

They signed him to a $120 million deal after his big 2023 season. Then injuries and his long absence changed everything. Now they've moved on.

And remember, at the trade deadline, the 49ers traded a 2027 sixth-round pick for Keenan Allen. But Allen is facing a pending suspension of at least three games.

The Rams play the 49ers on December 13. So the 49ers' receiver situation matters for the Rams too. It'll be interesting to see who San Francisco has available by then.

Five keys against the Packers

Since the game is almost here, here are my five keys for the Rams on Wednesday night.

One: protect Stafford. With Limmer at center and a shuffled interior line, the Rams have to keep the pocket clean. Quick throws and extra blockers will help.

Two: run the ball. Kyren, Corum, and Rivers have carried this offense for a month. They need to do it again.

Three: let the tight ends work. Even without Ferguson, Higbee, Parkinson, and Allen can make plays, especially off play-action.

Four: get to the quarterback. If Turner plays alongside Donald and Garrett, the Rams' pass rush could take over the game.

Five: win the turnover battle. The Rams forced three turnovers in Arizona. One or two more on Wednesday could decide it.

My Packers prediction

Rams 23, Packers 20.

I think it'll be close. The Packers are a much better team than Arizona, and the Rams are missing a lot. But I think the Rams' run game and pass rush will make the difference late. A big sack on a late Packers drive, then the Rams run out the clock.

And a win would put the Rams at 7-4 with five straight wins and a head-to-head tiebreaker over Green Bay.
