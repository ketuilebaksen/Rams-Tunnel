Where things stand

Quick reminder before we dive in.

The Rams beat Arizona 34-6. That’s four straight wins, and they’re 6-4. They did it without Puka Nacua, Davante Adams, Terrance Ferguson, and Steve Avila. And they lost two centers during the game.

Blaine Grisak’s position grades told the story. The offensive line got an A-plus. ESPN’s Sarah Barshop gave the running backs an A. The receivers and secondary got a B-plus. Stafford, the tight ends, the defensive line, and the linebackers got B’s. Special teams got a B-minus.

Now, the Rams, the Packers, and the Seahawks are all 6-4. ESPN’s Football Power Index gives the Rams a 47.1% chance to make the playoffs. The Packers game is Wednesday night, November 25, at 8 p.m. Eastern on Netflix.

So every decision this week matters.

Decision one: bring Presley back or not?

Let’s start with the move behind the headline.

Here’s who Brennan Presley is. He was a two-time All-Big 12 honoree at Oklahoma State. He went undrafted in 2025. He landed on the Rams’ practice squad after final cuts as a rookie, and the same thing happened this season.

Then, with Puka and Adams out, the Rams promoted him to the active roster for the Arizona game. To make room, they put Puka on injured reserve. McVay explained it at the time, saying, “We needed the roster spot.” And he praised Presley: “He’s just one of those guys that makes the plays when his number’s called.”

McVay also said Presley “can contribute on special teams, and he’s got some return ability.”

So why waive him a day later? The reports don’t say, and I’m not going to guess. But here’s how it usually works. When a team wants to move a player from the active roster back to the practice squad, it often has to waive him first. If no other team claims him, he clears waivers, and the team can sign him back to the practice squad.

So the decision for the Rams is simple: if Presley clears, do they bring him back?

This is not a report, it’s my opinion: I think they should. He knows the offense, he can return kicks, and McVay clearly likes him. And with Puka out until at least December 20, the Rams need receiver depth.

Decision two: who plays center?

This is the most important decision of the week.

During the Arizona game, starting center Coleman Shelton hurt his hamstring. Now he’s reportedly out four to six weeks. His backup, Dylan McMahon, hurt his knee and was placed on injured reserve.

So the Rams are down to their third center. Beaux Limmer slid over from left guard during the game and played center the rest of the way.

And look at the grades. PFF gave Limmer a 75.6 for the game. That’s a solid number, especially for a guy who changed positions in the middle of a road game. McVay said after the game, “Can’t say enough about Beaux Limmer. He starts the game at guard, slides into center.”

So the likely decision is that Limmer starts at center against the Packers. But that creates another question.

Decision three: who plays left guard?

If Limmer moves to center, someone has to play left guard. And Steve Avila, the usual starter, is still dealing with a hamstring injury.

In Arizona, Bill Murray came in at left guard. PFF gave him a 67.0. That’s okay, but not great.

The Rams also have David Quessenberry, an experienced veteran. McVay used him as an extra tight end for some snaps in Arizona.

So McVay has to decide: does he go with Murray, try Quessenberry, or hope Avila is ready?

And there’s another option. The Rams could sign another interior lineman. With McMahon on IR, they have room to add depth. Nothing has been reported yet, but it’s something to watch.

Here’s the good news. The rest of the line graded well. Warren McClendon was the highest-graded Rams lineman at 78.8. Alaric Jackson graded 73.4. Kevin Dotson graded 70.9. So the tackles and the right guard are solid. The question is just the middle.

Decision four: how to split the running back carries

This one’s a fun problem to have.

Here are the PFF grades for the running backs from Arizona:

Ronnie Rivers: 84.7.

Blake Corum: 73.7.

Kyren Williams: 46.5.

Now, Corum had the big box score: 100 rushing yards and a touchdown. That touchdown extended his streak to six straight games with a rushing score. Rivers had 15 carries for 81 yards and closed out the game. And Kyren was on the field a lot as part of the new two-back look.

So how should McVay split the work?

Here’s the thing. McVay has already shown us his answer. He doesn’t want to pick one. He wants all of them. The 20 personnel look, with Kyren and Corum on the field together, was built so the Rams could use “two of our best players on the field at the same time,” as McVay said.

And it’s working. The Rams have outrushed their opponents by 478 yards over the last four games. That’s their biggest four-game rushing differential since 1984, when Eric Dickerson ran for 2,105 yards.

So my guess is that the decision is “all three.” Corum gets the most carries. Kyren stays involved, especially in the two-back sets. And Rivers gets more work late in games, because he’s proven he can close.

Who would you give the most carries to against the Packers? Kyren, Corum, or Rivers?

Decision five: what to do with Shaun Dolac

Here’s a decision that might sneak up on people.

Shaun Dolac had an 88.0 PFF grade in Arizona. He led the team in tackles. And he forced a fumble with a “peanut punch” that helped end an Arizona drive.

Meanwhile, Nate Landman graded 36.2. And Omar Speights graded 76.8.

So should Dolac get more snaps?

Grisak was careful about this. He said Dolac’s sample size is still small, so one big game doesn’t mean he should take over as a starter. And I agree. One game is one game.

But if Dolac keeps playing like this, the coaches will have to find a way to get him on the field more. That’s how young players earn bigger roles. McVay calls it “confidence earned through evidence.”

So for now, I expect Dolac’s role to grow a little, not explode. But keep an eye on him.

Decision six: managing Garrett and Donald

This one might surprise you.

Aaron Donald got his first sack of the year in Arizona. But PFF graded him at 53.9 overall. And Myles Garrett graded 46.9.

Meanwhile, undrafted rookie Wesley Bailey graded 93.0, the highest on the defensive line. Tyler Davis and Braden Fisk both graded 79.9. Byron Young graded 73.4.

So does that mean Garrett and Donald should play less? No. Not at all.

Here’s what I think is going on. Garrett is still working back from his knee issue. McVay was careful with him all week, listing him as questionable and resting him on Friday. Against Washington, Garrett had three sacks, but he said he was feeling “a little bit slow.” So it makes sense if he had a quieter game.

And when you have Garrett and Donald on the field, offenses send extra blockers their way. That opens things up for guys like Bailey, Davis, and Fisk. So part of Bailey’s big grade might be thanks to the attention Garrett and Donald draw.

The real decision for McVay is snap management. How many snaps does Garrett play? How do you keep Donald fresh? And how do you keep giving Bailey chances to make plays?

And Bailey deserves a moment here. According to Grisak, he’s the first undrafted rookie since at least 2000 to have an interception, multiple sacks, and multiple forced fumbles in one season. That’s incredible.

If you’re a Wesley Bailey fan, drop a “Bailey” in the comments.

Decision seven: fix the kickoffs

This decision is the simplest, but it matters.

Special teams got a B-minus because two kickoffs missed their intended landing zone or went out of the field of play.

McVay noticed it too. After the game, he said: “We want to be able to keep those kicks where we’re actually keeping them in play. That wasn’t a positive for us today.”

That’s an easy problem to understand and a hard one to ignore. Every bad kickoff gives the other team better field position. In a 34-6 blowout, it didn’t matter. In a close game against the Packers, it could.

So the decision is just: fix it. And I’d expect the special teams unit to spend a lot of time on it this week.

Decision eight: how much 20 personnel now that it’s on tape

This might be the most interesting coaching decision of the week.

Here’s what happened in Arizona, according to ESPN’s Sarah Barshop and NFL Next Gen Stats. Coming into that game, the Rams had used 11 personnel, one back, one tight end, three receivers, on 53% of their plays this season. Against Arizona, they used it on only three plays.

Instead, they ran 17 plays out of 20 personnel, with two running backs and three receivers, for 197 yards and a touchdown. They also leaned heavily on 13 personnel, with three tight ends.

McVay said the two-back look was “something different that we hadn’t put on tape yet.” Well, now it’s on tape. The Packers’ coaches have watched every snap of it.

So what does McVay do?

Option one: keep using it. It worked. Almost 12 yards per play. Why stop?

Option two: use it as a decoy. Line up in the same look, but run totally different plays out of it. That’s what McVay usually does. He shows you something one week, then uses it to set up something else the next.

Option three: go back to his base offense. But without Puka and Adams, that’s harder.

This is not a report, it’s my guess: McVay goes with option two. The Rams will line up in two-back looks again, but with new plays the Packers haven’t seen. That’s what the bye is for. McVay said it’s a time to “creatively come up with new ideas based on who’s available and who’s not.”

Decision nine: the young receiver roles

Next, the receivers.

Here are the grades from Arizona. Xavier Smith: 92.6. Konata Mumpfield: 80.6. Tutu Atwell: 59.3. CJ Daniels: 55.8.

Smith scored his first career touchdown on the screen that McVay credited to assistant coach Nate Scheelhaase. Mumpfield caught three passes for 81 yards, including catches of 27 and 44 yards. Atwell made the 31-yard catch on fourth-and-6 that woke up the offense. And Daniels, who was inactive for the first six games of the season, caught his first NFL pass.

So how should McVay divide the snaps?

I think Smith and Mumpfield earned bigger roles. Those grades are hard to ignore. Atwell stays important because he has the most experience with Stafford among the healthy receivers, and he can stretch the field. And Daniels keeps growing. McVay said he “plays big at the catch point” and has “a mature presence.”

Here’s the key point. Nobody has to be Puka. The Rams just need each of these guys to make one or two big plays a game. In Arizona, all four of them had a catch longer than 20 yards. If that happens again, the offense will be fine.

Decision ten: the tight ends and Terrance Ferguson

The tight ends are a big part of this offense right now, so let’s talk about them.

The grades from Arizona: Colby Parkinson 83.5, Tyler Higbee 75.0, Davis Allen 68.1. Higbee and Allen both scored touchdowns. Parkinson made the great play-action catch down to the 1-yard line.

But McVay said after the game that the tight ends are “still so banged up.” That’s part of why he used the two-back look, to give them some rest.

And then there’s Terrance Ferguson. He’s on injured reserve with hamstring and ankle issues. The Rams opened his 21-day practice window, which means they have to activate him within 21 days or he’s done for the year. That window runs out around the Packers game.

So the Rams have a real decision to make: is Ferguson ready to come back, or not?

If he’s ready, that’s a huge boost. A healthy young tight end gives Stafford another weapon and lets the other tight ends rest.

If he’s not ready, the Rams could lose him for the season. That’s a tough call, and it’s one McVay will have to make very soon.

Decision eleven: the IR math

This one is less exciting but really important.

NFL teams only get eight return designations per season. That’s how many times a team can bring a player back from injured reserve. And the Rams are running low.

Look at who’s on IR and could come back: Puka Nacua, Davante Adams, Terrance Ferguson, Kobie Turner, Jordan Whittington, and now Dylan McMahon.

That’s a lot of players competing for a limited number of return spots. So the Rams have to think carefully about every IR move from now on.

That’s also probably why Coleman Shelton isn’t on IR yet, even with a four-to-six-week injury. Keeping him on the active roster might save a return designation for later. That’s my read, not something the team has said.

So the decision here is: who gets those last return spots? And can the Rams avoid putting anyone else on IR?

Bonus: the secondary

I want to quickly mention the secondary, because there’s a decision hiding here too.

Trent McDuffie graded 85.3 in his return from a back injury. Kamren Kinchens graded 84.5, with that huge interception before halftime that set up the Xavier Smith touchdown. Kam Curl graded 72.3 in his return from a toe injury. Emmanuel Forbes graded 70.7.

But Josh Wallace graded 49.6. And Quentin Lake graded 42.3 after allowing a big gain in coverage.

So the decision is about matchups. Against the Packers, who covers who? With McDuffie and Curl back, the Rams have more options. And McVay said the defense is “really starting to hit their stride.”

What’s NOT a decision right now

Let me also be clear about what’s not on the table this week.

Puka Nacua is on injured reserve. He has to miss at least four games, and the bye doesn’t count. So he’s out against the Packers, Chiefs, and 49ers. His earliest possible return is December 20 against Dallas, according to one report. But eligible doesn’t mean cleared. There’s no confirmed return date.

Davante Adams is on IR too. McVay said, “After Green Bay is a realistic timeline.” So he’s probably out for the Packers game.

Trades aren’t possible. The deadline passed on November 10.

So when you see a headline about a “bombshell decision,” remember: the big-name decisions are already set for now. The real decisions are the ones we just talked about.

How the Packers will test these decisions

Let me think about this from Green Bay’s side for a minute. I’m not going to make up any Packers injury news or specific game plans. But here’s how any good opponent would look at the Rams right now.

They’ll test the middle of the line. Two centers down, a backup at left guard. That’s the first place a defensive coordinator would look.

They’ll study the two-back look. Now that it’s on tape, they’ll have a plan for it.

They’ll make the young receivers beat them. Without Puka and Adams, Green Bay might load up against the run and dare Smith, Mumpfield, Atwell, and Daniels to win one-on-one.

They’ll attack the weak spots in coverage. Wallace and Lake had low grades in Arizona. A good quarterback will look for those matchups.

So McVay’s decisions this week aren’t just about the Rams. They’re about staying one step ahead of a team that’s had extra time to study them too.

And here’s the good news. McVay has been great at that after byes. Over the previous three seasons, his teams went 23-7 after their bye weeks.

Why the grades matter for Wednesday

Let me tie this all together.

The grades from Arizona tell us how the Rams are winning. It’s not one superstar. It’s the offensive line, the run game, the receiver depth, and timely plays on defense.

That’s the formula for Wednesday night.

On offense, the Rams need the new interior line to hold up, the run game to keep rolling, and the young receivers to keep making plays. Remember, Konata Mumpfield, Xavier Smith, Tutu Atwell, and CJ Daniels each caught a pass longer than 20 yards in Arizona. Smith graded 92.6 and Mumpfield 80.6.

On defense, the Rams need McDuffie and Curl to keep playing well, the pass rush to get home, and players like Bailey and Kinchens to keep creating turnovers.

On special teams, they need to fix the kickoffs.

If they do those things, they can beat the Packers. If the middle of the line struggles, it’s going to be a tough night.

Stafford’s grade, and why it matters

One more quick note on Stafford, because his grade is interesting.

Grisak gave him a B. His PFF grade was 81.6. And his expected points added per pass was 0.63, which ranked second in the league that week, behind only Brock Purdy.

So why only a B? Because of the interception. Grisak said turnovers still need work.

But here’s the thing. If Stafford is that efficient again against the Packers, the Rams will be in great shape. He spread the ball to nine different receivers in Arizona. He ran a brand-new two-back package. And he did it all behind a line that was down to its third center.

The decision for Stafford this week is simple: keep doing what you’re doing, and protect the football.

What McVay’s past byes tell us

Let’s look a little closer at those post-bye numbers, because they tell us something about how McVay makes decisions.

In 2023, the Rams were 3-6 at the bye. A lot of people had written them off. After the bye, they went 7-1 and made the playoffs.

In 2024, the Rams started 1-4, with injuries all over the roster, including to their top receivers. After the bye, they went 9-3 and won the division.

What did McVay do in those seasons? He didn’t panic. He looked at what was working, leaned into it, and built the offense around the players he actually had. That’s exactly what he did in Arizona with the two-back look and the three-tight-end sets.

So when McVay talks about using the bye to “creatively come up with new ideas,” he’s not just saying words. He has a track record of doing it.

The bigger picture

Let’s zoom out one more time.

ESPN Analytics says the Rams have the toughest second-half schedule in the NFL. After the Packers, it’s the Chiefs, then the 49ers.

But McVay’s teams have been great after the bye. Over the previous three seasons, they went 23-7 after their bye weeks. In 2023, the Rams started 3-6 and went 7-1 after the bye. In 2024, they started 1-4 and went 9-3 after the bye.

So history is on the Rams’ side. And the decisions they make this week could set up the whole second half.

McVay said after the Arizona game: “All I’m really worried about is the next game.” That’s the right mindset. One decision at a time, one game at a time.
