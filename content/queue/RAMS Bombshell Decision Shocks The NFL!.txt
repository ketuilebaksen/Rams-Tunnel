Let’s start with the claim itself, and let’s go through it piece by piece.

Claim number one: Jeremy Fowler reported that the Denver Broncos have received multiple calls on Courtland Sutton.

That’s real. Fowler did report that. But here’s the thing — he reported it on April 20th, 2024. At the time, Sutton was skipping voluntary workouts because he wanted a new contract. Fowler reported that the Broncos had received multiple trade inquiries, but he also reported the other half of the story, the half that always seems to get left out: the Broncos did not plan to trade him. Here’s how that report put it: “The Broncos do not plan to trade Sutton, who is skipping voluntary workouts due to his contract.” And Denver didn’t trade him.

Claim number two: Jarrett Bailey of USA Today added context on how serious it had gotten.

Also real — and also from 2024. On April 27th, 2024, Bailey reported that the Rams and the Pittsburgh Steelers were potential landing spots for Sutton. At that time, Sutton had two years left on a four-year, 60.8 million dollar contract. And in that same back-and-forth, 9News’ Mike Klis reported there were no plans to trade Sutton and that he remained in good standing with general manager George Paton and head coach Sean Payton. Again, no trade happened.

And here’s what happened next, which is the part of the story these recycled videos never tell you. Sutton came back to the Broncos. According to CBS Sports, he went on to have a career year in 2024, catching 81 passes for 1,081 yards and eight touchdowns with rookie quarterback Bo Nix. He even earned all one and a half million dollars of his performance incentives that season. And then, in July of 2025, instead of trading him, Denver rewarded him with a new four-year contract. Sean Payton was clearly happy to have him around. So the 2024 trade rumors didn’t just fail to happen — they went in the exact opposite direction. Sutton became a bigger part of Denver’s long-term plans, not a smaller one.

Claim number three: the story mentions Jerry Jeudy from the Cleveland Browns at a bargain salary.

That one really gives it away. Back in the spring of 2024, Jeudy had just signed a three-year, 52.5 million dollar deal with Cleveland, and reporters were using that contract as a comparison point for what Sutton might want. That was a 2024 conversation. It has nothing to do with the Rams’ trade plans in October of 2026.

Claim number four — and this is the one that should make every Rams fan stop and think: the story describes Sutton joining a receiver room where “Cooper Kupp runs underneath” and Puka creates after the catch.

Cooper Kupp is not on the Rams. The Rams moved on from Kupp after the 2024 season, and he’s been playing for the Seattle Seahawks. So any story in October of 2026 that describes Kupp as part of the Rams’ receiver room is describing a roster that hasn’t existed for nearly two years. And think about what Kupp means to Rams fans. He was the Super Bowl LVI MVP. He won the receiving triple crown in 2021, leading the league in catches, yards and touchdowns. He’s one of the most beloved players in franchise history. Any Rams fan who’s been paying attention knows he’s not in the building anymore. A story that still has him running routes for McVay in 2026 wasn’t written about the 2026 Rams.

Claim number five: the 8.4 million dollar cap hit that supposedly makes Sutton “the best value in the wide receiver market.”

This is where the math completely falls apart. In July of 2025, the Broncos signed Sutton to a four-year, 92 million dollar extension that runs through the 2029 season. According to CBS Sports, that averages about 23 million dollars per year. Whatever an 8.4 million dollar figure once referred to, it doesn’t describe the contract Sutton is playing on right now. And the Rams, as we’ve covered on this channel, have roughly one million dollars in salary cap space. Even if Denver wanted to trade him, the Rams would have to send a massive amount of salary back to make it work.

And notice something else about the supposed deal structure: “draft picks going to Denver along with a receiver heading back to the Broncos.” Which receiver? Which picks? What round? Real trade reports have names and numbers. When the Texans traded for Marvin Harrison Jr. last week, the AP reported exactly what went where: Harrison and a 2027 seventh-round pick to Houston, a 2027 second-round pick and a 2027 sixth-round pick to Arizona. When the Rams traded for McDuffie, we knew every pick involved. A trade story without names or numbers isn’t a report. It’s a sketch.

Now, I want to be fair, because there is one real 2026 connection between Sutton and the Rams. On September 29th, ESPN’s Ben Solak wrote that the Rams — whom he still considered Super Bowl favorites — could use another pass catcher. He suggested Sutton as an option and said the Rams need “a steady producer who can eat volume if needed.” But that was an analyst’s idea, not a report. Even Heavy, which wrote up Solak’s suggestion, called it “a pretty cold take.” There’s a big difference between an analyst saying “this might make sense” and an insider saying “this is happening.”

And to be fair to Solak, his reasoning wasn’t crazy. Think back to the start of this season. When Puka Nacua missed two games with groin soreness, the Rams’ passing game leaned almost entirely on Davante Adams. Davante was great — he caught seven passes for 137 yards in Denver — but defenses could roll coverage his way, and Stafford was forcing throws into tight windows. That’s part of why Stafford threw six interceptions in the first four games. So the idea of adding another reliable receiver as insurance makes sense in theory. The problem isn’t the logic. The problem is the price, the contract and the fact that Denver isn’t selling.

And since Puka came back, the problem has mostly solved itself. In his first two games back, Puka caught 26 passes for 263 yards. Stafford threw zero interceptions against Buffalo. And with Tutu Atwell back in the building, the Rams have more depth than they did in September.

So let’s take Solak’s idea seriously for a minute. Would trading for Courtland Sutton actually make sense for the Rams in 2026?

Let’s start with the case for it, because Sutton is a good player. The Broncos drafted him in the second round in 2018 out of SMU, he made the Pro Bowl in his second season, and he’s been one of Denver’s most consistent players for most of the last decade. He had back-to-back 1,000-yard seasons in 2024 and 2025. In 2024, he set career highs with 81 catches and 1,081 yards, along with eight touchdowns. He’s a big, physical receiver who wins contested catches, and he scored Denver’s only touchdown in last season’s AFC Championship Game loss to New England. On paper, the idea of a big-bodied receiver who can win jump balls in the red zone sounds appealing.

But here’s the thing: the Rams already have one of the best contested-catch receivers in football. Last season, Puka Nacua set a Pro Football Focus record with 27 contested catches. The recycled story describes Puka as a yards-after-the-catch guy, and he is — but he’s also the player who wins the 50-50 balls in this offense. The Rams don’t need to trade for a jump-ball specialist. They already have the one who set the record.

And if you want to know what this receiver room actually needs, it’s not another star — it’s health and balance. Puka was targeted 24 times against Buffalo. That’s a huge workload for a player who was dealing with a groin injury just a few weeks ago. The smartest thing the Rams can do is spread the ball around a little more, use Tutu Atwell and Alex Bachman to give Puka and Davante a breather on certain snaps, and keep getting Kyren involved as a receiver. With tight end Terrance Ferguson on injured reserve, the tight end group of Higbee, Parkinson and Davis Allen is also a big part of the passing game. That’s a deep, balanced group when everyone’s healthy.

Now back to Sutton — and to 2026, because his season has been very different. Through five games, according to The Fantasy Footballers, Sutton had just 11 catches for 104 yards and one touchdown on 25 targets. Denver’s offense has turned into a run-first operation, and Bo Nix has been struggling to push the ball downfield. Nix threw for 123 yards against the Chargers and just 107 yards against Seattle. He said himself: “I just think we’re really, for some reason, struggling to find those explosive plays.” And the Broncos also have Jaylen Waddle in their receiver room now, which means Sutton isn’t the only option anymore.

So Sutton is a 1,000-yard receiver in the right offense, but he’s off to a slow start, and he’s carrying a contract worth about 23 million dollars a year through 2029. That’s a lot of money for a team that already has its cap almost completely spent.

Now here’s the biggest reason this trade almost certainly isn’t happening: the Broncos aren’t sellers.

Denver is 4-2. They just beat the Seattle Seahawks 17-14 in overtime, with a defense that, according to the AP, hit Sam Darnold 17 times. They beat the Chargers. And remember — they beat the Rams 30-26 in Week 3. That was the night Jaylen Watson dislocated his shoulder, and the night the Rams’ kicking and special teams problems started to really show. Denver earned that win. Why would they now hand a key piece of their offense to the team they just beat? This is a contending team in the AFC. Contending teams don’t trade their veteran receivers in October to a team they could see in the Super Bowl. If anything, a 4-2 Broncos team is more likely to be buying before the November 10th deadline than selling.

So let’s add it up. The Broncos are contending. Sutton has a big new contract. The Rams have about one million dollars in cap space. And the Rams already spent major draft capital on Myles Garrett and Trent McDuffie. Every single piece of this trade is working against it.

And let me explain the money in a little more detail, because this is where most fake trade stories fall apart. When a team trades for a player in the middle of the season, it takes on the rest of that player’s base salary for the year. For a receiver making about 23 million dollars a year on average, even the remaining half of a season would be far more than one million dollars. That means the Rams would have to send one or more big contracts back to Denver to make the numbers work. And who would that be? Puka? Davante? A starter on the offensive line? Every big contract on this roster belongs to a player the Rams need to win this season. There’s no easy salary to dump. That’s why the Rams’ most realistic deadline targets are players on cheap contracts — like rookie-deal players or veterans on minimum deals.

Now, let’s ask the more important question: do the Rams even need a receiver like Sutton right now?

Look at what this passing game has done. Matthew Stafford leads the NFL in passing yards. The Rams are averaging over 300 passing yards per game. Puka Nacua just caught 17 passes against Buffalo — the most ever in a Monday Night Football game. Davante Adams caught two touchdowns in that same game and has been a reliable red zone weapon. Kyren Williams has turned into a legitimate receiving threat out of the backfield, with 27 catches in five games.

And the Rams already made a receiver move this year. On August 27th, they traded running back Jarquez Hunter to Miami to bring back Tutu Atwell, who spent his first five seasons with the Rams. McVay said: “There’s a lot of love for Tutu Atwell in this locker room and a lot of familiarity.” They also have Alex Bachman, who’s become a big part of the return game, and Jordan Whittington. And remember, the Rams love to use three-tight-end sets — Tyler Higbee, Colby Parkinson and Davis Allen are all part of the passing game.

And here’s something else: the “big contested-catch receiver” role is something Davante Adams already handles near the goal line. All five Rams touchdowns against Buffalo came from five yards or closer, and Davante caught two of them. The Rams’ problem in that game wasn’t finishing drives. It was special teams and run defense.

That’s why I keep saying this: the Rams’ real needs at the deadline aren’t at wide receiver. They’re on defense and special teams.

And look at where the Rams stand in the division. The 49ers are 5-0 and have already beaten every other team in the NFC West. Seattle, the defending Super Bowl champion, just lost to the Broncos in overtime. The Rams are 2-3, chasing a division they were expected to win. To climb back into that race, they need to stop losing close games. And the close games they’ve lost this season — Denver and Buffalo — were decided by special teams mistakes and defensive breakdowns, not by a lack of receivers. The Rams just gave up 234 rushing yards to Buffalo. Their special teams rank dead last in DVOA, according to NFL.com. Their pass rush has only seven sacks, which ranked 28th in the league entering this week, according to Fox Sports. If the Rams make a move before November 10th, I’d expect it to be a cheap linebacker or a special teams piece — something like the Cedric Gray idea we talked about earlier this week — not a 23-million-dollar receiver.

Now, let me give you something useful you can take with you, because this won’t be the last trade rumor you see this month. With the deadline coming on November 10th, the internet is about to fill up with “bombshell” trade videos. Here’s my five-step checklist for spotting the fake ones.

Step one: check the date of the quotes. If a video says “Jeremy Fowler reported” or “USA Today reported,” search for the actual report. In this case, both reports were from April of 2024. A real report from two years ago is still a two-year-old report.

Step two: if Adam Schefter actually reports a trade, it will be everywhere. ESPN will post it. Every major outlet will write it up. The team’s beat reporters will be talking about it. If you can only find it in one video, that’s a red flag.

Step three: check the roster details. If a story describes a player who isn’t even on the team anymore — like Cooper Kupp on the 2026 Rams — the story isn’t current.

Step four: check the contract. Trade rumors often use old salary numbers to make a deal sound cheaper than it is. Sutton’s current deal is four years and 92 million dollars. That changes everything.

And step five: be careful with the logic. One of the arguments in this story was that because nobody on the TV set pushed back, that silence was “basically its own confirmation.” That’s not how reporting works. Silence isn’t confirmation. A source, a report and a name attached to it — that’s confirmation.
