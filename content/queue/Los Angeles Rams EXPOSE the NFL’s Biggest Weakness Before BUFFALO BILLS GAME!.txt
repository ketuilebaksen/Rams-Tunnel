Let’s start in Seattle, because that game set the stage for everything tonight. The 49ers and Seahawks played a game that felt like a playoff game in October. According to FOX Sports, the two teams combined for seven turnovers — the most in any game this season — and 38 points off turnovers, the fourth-most in a single game since 2000. That’s chaos. And somehow, the 49ers came out of it unbeaten.

Here’s how it ended. The Seahawks tied the game in the fourth quarter on Jason Myers’ fifth field goal of the day. Then the 49ers answered with Eddy Piñeiro hitting a 44-yard field goal with 1:56 left — after he’d missed a 52-yarder earlier in the game. Seattle got the ball back with a chance to tie or win, and it fell apart. Jaxon Smith-Njigba, last year’s Offensive Player of the Year, dropped a catchable deep pass that could have set up a field goal. And on fourth-and-10, safety Malik Mustapha pushed Cooper Kupp out of bounds about a yard short of the first down. Replay couldn’t overturn it. Game over.

The star of the day for San Francisco was Mike Evans — yes, the longtime Buccaneers receiver, now in red and gold. Seven catches, 133 yards and a touchdown. On the 49ers’ 74-yard touchdown drive in the fourth quarter, Evans caught four passes for 75 yards, and then he caught the two-point conversion that gave San Francisco a 29-26 lead. And there was even drama on that drive: Brock Purdy threw a touchdown to Brandin Cooks, but Seattle had called timeout, so it didn’t count — and Purdy hit Cooks again for a seven-yard score on the very next snap.

The 49ers’ defense won it with takeaways. Renardo Green intercepted Sam Darnold twice. Fred Warner forced a fumble. Gracen Halton forced a fumble and recovered it in the end zone for a touchdown. Four takeaways — the 49ers’ most in a game since 2023. Seattle’s run game was a disaster: backup running back George Holani lost two fumbles, and Emanuel Wilson lost another one that led to a touchdown.

And here’s what should worry every team in the NFC: San Francisco did this while banged up. Nick Bosa has been out since Week 2. George Kittle was dealing with the flu. They were missing Kyle Juszczyk and several of their top receivers, and they lost more receivers to injury during the game. They even lost Dre Greenlaw to an ejection after a late hit on Darnold and a head-butt on Ernest Jones that drew blood. And they still won, on the road, against the champs. That’s a team that knows how to win ugly.

And don’t overlook what this means for Seattle. The Seahawks are the defending Super Bowl champions. They beat the Patriots in Super Bowl LX at Levi’s Stadium in February, a few weeks after ending the Rams’ season with a 31-27 win in the NFC Championship. Now they’re 3-2, two games behind San Francisco, and their running game is coughing up the football. Defending a title is the hardest thing in sports — every opponent circles your game on the calendar, and every mistake gets magnified. Seattle is still dangerous. Sam Darnold, Jaxon Smith-Njigba and that defense aren’t going anywhere. But they’re not invincible, and Sunday proved it. For the Rams, that’s an opportunity: the champs are vulnerable, and the door to second place in the division is wide open — if the Rams take care of their own business.

So what does it mean for the Rams? Let’s do the NFC West math, because it’s simple and it’s brutal.

The 49ers are 5-0. The Seahawks are 3-2. The Rams are 2-2. The Cardinals are trailing behind. If the Rams beat Buffalo tonight, they move to 3-2 — tied with Seattle, two games behind San Francisco, with twelve games left. That’s a race. If the Rams lose, they fall to 2-3 — three games behind San Francisco, a game behind Seattle, and remember, the 49ers already own the head-to-head win from Melbourne. Three games back with twelve to play isn’t over. But it’s a hole you’d rather not dig.

And look at the schedule ahead, because it tells you why every game matters. The Rams play at San Francisco on December 13th. They play Seattle on Christmas Day, on the road, and then again at SoFi to close the regular season. Those three division games late in the year could decide the NFC West — but only if the Rams are close enough to make them matter. That’s why tonight is so important. You don’t want to walk into December needing miracles.

And remember how the season started. The Rams’ only meeting with the 49ers so far was the 27-7 loss in Melbourne in Week 1 — a game where the defense was passive, the offense never found a rhythm, and Myles Garrett played his last snaps before knee surgery. That was a different Rams team than the one that just held Philadelphia to zero-for-twelve on third down. When these two teams meet again on December 13th, the Rams should have Garrett, Donald, a healthy Puka and a defense that’s completely changed its identity. But for that rematch to matter, the Rams have to stay close. Tonight is step one.

There’s also a lesson inside that 49ers-Seahawks game for the Rams. Look at how it was decided: turnovers. Seattle gave the ball away four times and lost by three points. The Rams have turned the ball over twice in every game this season — eight giveaways in four weeks — and they’re 2-2. The 49ers won in Seattle because their defense took the ball away and their offense made enough plays. That’s the formula. Protect the ball, take the ball, win close games. It’s not complicated, but it’s everything.

Now — tonight. Let’s go through the injury picture, because the Rams are as healthy as they’ve been all season.

The final injury report came out on Saturday. Aaron Donald, who missed the Eagles game with back soreness, has no designation — he’s expected to play. Jaylen Watson, who dislocated his shoulder in Denver, has no designation and will play with a harness. Colby Parkinson, back at tight end, no designation. Linebackers Nate Landman and Grant Stuard, and edge rusher Josaiah Stewart — all of whom missed practice earlier in the week — have no designation. The only Rams player listed as questionable is safety Quentin Lake with a hip injury, and McVay said the staff feels “like he’s trending in the right direction,” adding that Lake “feels good, so that’s a positive for us.” Watch the inactive list about 90 minutes before kickoff for the final word on Lake.

And keep an eye on one more name: Eric Kendricks. The Rams signed the former first-team All-Pro linebacker to their practice squad last Tuesday. He started both of San Francisco’s playoff games last January — including a playoff win in Philadelphia. With Landman, Stuard and Stewart all cleared, the Rams may not need to elevate him tonight. But if there’s any setback before kickoff, a veteran who has seen just about everything is sitting right there. That’s the kind of depth contenders build in October so they don’t panic in December.

Buffalo’s report is a different story. Defensive tackle Ed Oliver is out with a knee injury. Receiver Joshua Palmer is out with a shoulder injury he picked up in practice. DJ Moore is questionable with a shoulder. Nickel cornerback Dee Alford is questionable with an ankle. Defensive lineman Landon Jackson is questionable with a knee. The one piece of good news for the Bills: top cornerback Christian Benford, who missed the Patriots game, has no designation and is expected to start.

Think about what that means. Ed Oliver is one of Buffalo’s most disruptive interior pass rushers. Without him, the Bills’ defensive line loses a big piece of its push up the middle — which matters a lot for a Rams offense that wants Matthew Stafford comfortable in the pocket. And on the other side, the Rams are getting Donald back in the middle of their own defense. One team’s interior just got stronger. The other team’s interior just got weaker.

Now let’s talk about the betting market, because it tells an interesting story. As of Sunday afternoon, according to a betting snapshot from Cleatz, the Rams were favored by three to three and a half points depending on the sportsbook, with a total of about 54 and a half. And here’s the fascinating part: the public is split. Only about 37 percent of the bets on the spread were on the Rams — but those bets accounted for about 74 percent of the money. In other words, more casual bettors are taking Buffalo and the points, but the bigger bets — often from more experienced bettors — are on the Rams. And almost 80 percent of the bets are on the over. The market expects points tonight, and it expects the Rams to have the edge.

That fits with the national picks we heard this week too. One of the most prominent national hosts picked the Rams to win and cover at minus three and a half, saying he doesn’t like the matchup for Buffalo because the Bills’ defense has been giving up big chunk plays — and the Rams can be a chunk-play offense.

So let’s break down why. Start with Buffalo’s defense. The Bills changed defensive coordinators this offseason, with Jim Leonhard taking over. Through four games, Buffalo ranks 30th in the NFL in opponent EPA per play and 28th in opponent success rate. They’re allowing almost 27 points per game, 25th in the league. Their top cornerback just came back from injury, their nickel is questionable, and now their best interior pass rusher is out. And they’re coming off a home loss to the Patriots as six-and-a-half-point favorites, sealed by a Josh Allen interception on a throw his own head coach, Joe Brady, said he trusted him to throw away.

Now look at what the Rams can attack them with. Puka Nacua came back from his groin injury in Philadelphia and caught nine passes for 125 yards. Davante Adams had seven catches for 137 yards in Denver when he was the focal point. Kyren Williams is averaging 5.4 yards per carry with the highest PFF grade on the offense, and he caught ten passes in Philly. Colby Parkinson is back to give Stafford a reliable target in the middle. The Rams have topped 400 total yards in each of their last three games, and they have 1,668 total yards on the season — the second-most in the NFL. Against a defense this banged up and this inconsistent, the yardage should be there again.

But here’s the catch, and it’s the same catch we’ve talked about all week: yards haven’t turned into points. The Rams rank just 19th in scoring, at about 21 points per game. Why? Turnovers, penalties in the red zone, and special teams mistakes. This week, the Rams’ offensive staff admitted the team has been “hurting ourselves more than what we are accustomed to,” pointing out that around this time last year, the Rams were one of the least penalized teams in the league. Penalties in the “fringe area” — right around the red zone — have stalled drives and changed how McVay can call plays.

And then there’s the quarterback. Matthew Stafford has six interceptions in four games, with at least one in every game this season — the longest streak like that to open a season in his career, according to ESPN Research. But listen to how he responded this week: “Crawling in a hole — not an option.” He said he trusts his preparation and lives with the results. And McVay backed him all the way: “He was at his best in the most important moments... to go 98 and to go 78. That is everything you need to know about Matthew... There’s nobody that I’d rather have leading us.” Those were the two fourth-quarter touchdown drives that won the game in Philadelphia. Tonight, Stafford doesn’t need to be perfect. He needs to be patient — take the checkdowns, protect the ball, and let the big plays come.

And there’s a storyline here that makes tonight feel even bigger. Josh Allen won the MVP in 2024. Matthew Stafford won it in 2025. The last two MVPs, under the lights at SoFi. Stafford said this week that he has “a ton of respect for Josh, what he brings to their team,” and called the game “a big-time challenge for us as a team, as a unit.” There’s also a fun detail: Stafford will be facing Bills defensive coordinator Jim Leonhard for the first time as a coach. They overlapped as players — Leonhard was a safety in the league from 2005 to 2014. Now he’s in his first year running Buffalo’s defense, and it’s been a rough start. A veteran quarterback in his eighteenth season against a first-year coordinator whose unit ranks near the bottom of the league? If Stafford stays patient, that’s a matchup he should win.

Now the other side of the ball — the Rams’ defense against Josh Allen and the highest-scoring offense in the league. The Bills are averaging nearly 32 points per game, first in the NFL, with 17 pass plays of 20 yards or more — tied for second-most in football. The last time Allen came to SoFi, in December of 2024, he threw for 342 yards and three touchdowns and ran for three more — and the Rams still won, 44-42.

But this Rams defense is a different animal. Through four games, it’s allowed just 990 total yards — second-fewest in the league — and only five pass plays of 20-plus yards, tied for the fewest. Since the 49ers put up 379 yards on them in Week 1, no opponent has reached 260 yards. After the Melbourne loss, Chris Shula transformed the defense, going from a blitz rate of about 14 percent in Week 1 to over 50 percent in Philadelphia, according to Rams On SI’s film review — and the Eagles went zero-for-twelve on third down, the first time that’s ever happened in their franchise history.

And here’s the chess match. According to that same film review, Josh Allen ranks ninth in the league against the blitz but third when he’s not blitzed — so pressure helps. But he’s sixth against man coverage and much less efficient against zone. So the answer isn’t just “blitz more.” It’s disguise. Zone pressures. Four-man rushes when Aaron Donald and Kobie Turner can win on their own. And then the blitz as a surprise on key downs. Byron Young has been a monster — seventh in the NFL in pressures over the last three weeks, with three and a half sacks in that span, plus the strip-sack that sealed Philadelphia. Add Donald back in the middle, and Buffalo’s offensive line has a lot to handle.

Let me also mention the secondary, because this is where Quentin Lake’s status matters. According to PFF, Lake and Trent McDuffie have the two highest grades on the Rams’ defense this season — Lake at 90.0 and McDuffie at 89.1. McDuffie has allowed only five catches on 18 targets. If Lake can’t go, Josh Wallace — who earned the defense’s top grade in Philadelphia while filling in for Watson — gives Shula a versatile option who can play corner, nickel or safety. And with Watson back, the Rams have more trusted cover players than they’ve had all season.

And Buffalo’s receiving corps is thinner than usual tonight. Joshua Palmer is out. DJ Moore is questionable with a shoulder injury. That means Allen may lean even more on his remaining receivers and on his own legs. For a Rams secondary that’s allowed the fewest explosive pass plays in the league, a banged-up receiver room is good news — but it also means Allen will be looking to extend plays and create something out of nothing. The Rams’ coverage has to hold for an extra second or two when he scrambles. That’s where discipline in the secondary — and communication from Lake, if he plays — becomes everything.

So let’s put it all together. Here are my five keys to a Rams win tonight.

Key number one: zero turnovers. The Rams have given the ball away twice in every game this season. The 49ers just showed us, in Seattle, what turnovers do to a game. If Stafford and the offense play a clean game, I don’t believe Buffalo’s defense can keep up for four quarters.

Key number two: turn yards into touchdowns. The Rams will move the ball — they’ve topped 400 yards three straight weeks. But field goals won’t beat Josh Allen. Clean up the penalties in the fringe area, stay on schedule in the red zone, and finish drives in the end zone. Kyren Williams near the goal line is the best weapon they have for that.

And this is where the two-back system matters. Kyren Williams and Blake Corum have shared the load all season, which keeps both of them fresh for the fourth quarter. In Philadelphia, Kyren was the closer — two fourth-quarter touchdowns, including the seven-yard run where he broke three tackles and later said his motto is “by any means.” Against a Bills defense that just lost its best interior lineman, the run game should be there all night. A steady diet of Kyren and Corum keeps Allen on the sideline, keeps the Rams’ defense fresh, and sets up the play-action shots to Puka and Davante. Control the clock, control the game.

Key number three: win the interior. With Ed Oliver out for Buffalo and Aaron Donald back for the Rams, this is the biggest mismatch on the field. Stafford should have a cleaner pocket than he’s had in weeks. And on defense, Donald and Kobie Turner have to make Allen uncomfortable without the Rams needing to blitz on every snap.

Key number four: disguise against Allen. Don’t become predictable. Mix the blitz with zone pressures and four-man rushes. Keep Allen in the pocket, rush in disciplined lanes, and tackle him when he runs. One broken contain against Allen can turn into a 40-yard scramble. Make him earn every yard.

And one more thing about the start of this game. Primetime games at SoFi have a different energy, and the Rams have already had one this season — the 28-6 win over the Giants on Monday Night Football in Week 2, the night Aaron Donald returned and the defense held Jameis Winston to a 37.7 passer rating. The Rams fed off that crowd. Tonight, they’ll need that same energy early. McVay’s opening script — the first 15 or so plays he plans before the game — has to set the tone. If the Rams score on their first drive and make Buffalo chase, the pressure shifts to Allen and a Bills team that’s already searching for answers after last week. If the Rams start slow, Stafford gets pushed back into forcing throws — and that’s exactly what Buffalo wants.

Key number five: clean special teams. No kickoffs out of bounds. No big returns. No missed kicks in big moments. Harrison Mevis is five of seven on field goals this season, and the coverage units have leaked points in Denver and Philadelphia. In a game where the total is 54 and a half and the spread is a field goal, special teams could literally decide the outcome.

Now, what does a win mean? If the Rams beat Buffalo, they go over .500 for the first time this season, at 3-2. They beat the team they shared the top of the Super Bowl odds board with just a week ago. They stay two games behind the 49ers and pull even with the Seahawks. And with Myles Garrett expected back from his knee surgery around Week 9, according to reporting from Ian Rapoport, the Rams would be heading into the middle of their schedule — Arizona at home, then the Raiders, the Chargers and Washington — with real momentum. They’d also reinforce their status at the top of the Super Bowl odds board, where they moved after the comeback in Philadelphia. And remember: Super Bowl LXI is at SoFi Stadium in February. The Rams’ own building.

And what does a loss mean? 2-3. Three games behind San Francisco. The national conversation would snap right back to “overrated superteam.” Questions about Stafford’s turnovers would get louder. And the trade deadline on November 10th would come with a lot more pressure to “do something,” even though, as we broke down this week, the Rams have only about a million dollars in cap space and no 2027 first-round pick. That’s how much one Monday night can swing the narrative.

But I don’t think it goes that way. Here’s my prediction — the same one I’ve had on the record all week: Rams 31, Bills 27. Before the Eagles game, I called 24-20 Rams, and that’s exactly how it ended. Tonight, I think Stafford plays a clean game, Puka goes over 100 yards again, Kyren finds the end zone, and the defense makes one huge play on Josh Allen in the second half. Donald back in the middle, Ed Oliver out on the other side — that interior matchup is the hidden edge in this game.
