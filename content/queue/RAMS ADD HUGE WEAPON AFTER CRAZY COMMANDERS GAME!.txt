Part two: what’s real here

As always, the fake is built around real facts. Let’s separate them out.

Downs asked to be traded. Real. ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler reported it, and Downs told ESPN he’s “open to whatever.”

The Colts are 2-6 and selling. Real. They fired general manager Chris Ballard on October 19th and have already traded Quenton Nelson to Seattle and DeForest Buckner to San Francisco.

The Colts wanted a third. Real. Adam Schefter reported earlier that the Colts’ asking price for Downs is a third-round pick, though a fourth might get it done. That’s the actual Schefter reporting on Downs. Notice what it is: a price, not a destination.

The Colts were waiting. Also real. Jordan Schultz reported the Colts planned to wait until after Week 9 to make more trades, hoping offers would rise near the deadline. So Monday and Tuesday are when things should heat up.

Downs is on a cheap rookie deal. Real. He’s in the final year of the contract he signed as a third-round pick in 2023.

The Rams have almost no cap space. Real. That part of the clip is accurate, even if the exact number is off. More on that in a second.

The Rams have called. Kind of real. Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak wrote that the Rams have called about Downs and Brian Thomas Jr., though he didn’t say where that came from. Calling about a player is something teams do all the time before the deadline. It’s not the same as making an offer.

Davante Adams is out. Real. And the clip says, “Nobody gave me” a timeline. That’s not true, and it’s the same mistake we caught in another clip last week. McVay said six to ten weeks after the October 18th injury, and later said Adams’ surgery “went really well” and he’s trending toward the shorter end.

Part three: what I can’t confirm, and what’s wrong

Now the parts that don’t hold up.

“Schefter and Rapoport reported this morning that an offer including a fourth has come in for Downs.” I looked for this, and I couldn’t find it. Not on Schefter’s feed, not on Rapoport’s, not on ESPN or NFL.com. The real Schefter reporting I’ve seen on Downs is about the asking price: a third, maybe a fourth. If a real report of an actual offer does come out, I’ll tell you. But I’m not going to treat it as fact just because a clip says it.

And even the clip admits that “offer” doesn’t name a team. So even if it were real, it wouldn’t connect Downs to the Rams.

“Pro Football Rumors confirmed that the Rams are probably looking at the market.” I couldn’t verify that wording either. And even taken at face value, “probably looking at the market” is about the weakest thing you can say about a team. Every contender with a hole is “probably looking at the market” the day before the deadline.

The 49ers. The clip says Downs is “also linked to the San Francisco 49ers in this same set of reports.” The real reporting I’ve seen names the Steelers and Chiefs as the main suitors. Notice the pattern: the last Downs clip said he was “also linked to the Raiders,” and this one says the 49ers. These channels make versions of the same clip for different fan bases. That’s content, not news.

The 2028 fourth-round pick. The clip says the price is a 2028 fourth, because the Rams “have few 2027 picks.” That’s an invented detail. Nobody has reported a pick, a year, or a round for the Rams.

The cap math. The clip says Downs’ remaining salary is “under $1 million,” which “fits directly within the Rams’ approximately $1 million of cap space.” Here’s the problem. The most recent number I’ve seen, from Athlon Sports citing Over The Cap, has the Rams at only a few thousand dollars in cap space, not a million. So even if Downs’ remaining salary is under a million, it doesn’t fit without the Rams creating room first, probably through a restructure.

And the contradiction, again. The clip says Downs fits “without moving another contract.” Then in the very next breath, it says Emmanuel Forbes Jr. “goes along to balance the salary exchange.” Those can’t both be true. The narrator even admits it: “I am not going to reconcile them for you.” That’s because the script was stitched together from different articles. Fowler and Athlon floated Forbes as a trade candidate, and somebody glued that idea onto a fake Schefter quote.

Part four: why Sunday’s game matters more than this clip

Let’s leave the clip behind for a minute, because what happened on Sunday is the real story.

The Rams beat Washington 20-13. Three straight wins. They’re now 5-4.

Myles Garrett had three sacks, three tackles for loss, and a strip-sack right before halftime that led to points, in his first game back from knee surgery.

Puka Nacua caught 14 passes for 150 yards and the game-winning touchdown. On 21 targets.

And Matthew Stafford threw 43 passes for 219 yards. That’s about 5.1 yards per attempt.

Put those numbers together, and you can see the problem. Nearly half of Stafford’s throws went to one player. ESPN’s postgame crew said it directly: “It seems like it’s the Puka show.” One analyst said that if the Rams had picks, he could see them looking for “another pass catcher.”

That’s the real case for a Downs trade. Not a fake Schefter quote. Twenty-one targets to one receiver, and a passing game that can’t create big plays.

Coming into Sunday, Grisak had the Rams’ explosive pass play rate at 4.1 percent, compared with 17.5 percent at this point last season. Stafford’s average depth of target over the previous four weeks ranked 25th of 27 quarterbacks. Sunday didn’t fix that.

So yes, the Rams need help at receiver. The question is whether Downs is the right help, and whether they can afford him.

Part five: would Downs actually fix the problem?

Let’s look at Downs honestly.

What he does well. He’s a slot receiver who wins with quickness. He separates fast, beats man coverage, and gives the quarterback a quick, easy throw. The clip is right that McVay’s offense uses that kind of receiver. Downs caught 68 passes as a rookie, a Colts rookie record, and had 72 catches for 803 yards two years ago. For his career, he has 228 catches for 2,470 yards and 12 touchdowns.

What worries me. This season, he has 30 catches for 330 yards and one touchdown through eight games, and his catch rate entering Week 8 was a career-low 56.5 percent. And there’s the blocking question. ESPN’s Ben Solak wrote that his “biggest hang-up in trading Downs to anyone is that he has to be protected from blocking responsibilities.” McVay asks a lot of his receivers in the run game, and the run game has carried the Rams for three straight weeks.

The bigger issue. Here’s my honest take, and this is analysis, not a report. Downs would take targets off Puka. That’s real value. On a third-and-five, he gives Stafford a second option who can get open fast. But Downs is a short-area receiver. The Rams’ biggest problem is that they can’t create explosive plays. Adding another underneath target helps you move the chains. It doesn’t scare a safety.

So Downs would help. He just wouldn’t solve the whole problem.

Part six: the money, one more time

Here’s the Rams’ financial picture, as plainly as I can put it.

Cap space: a few thousand dollars, per Athlon’s most recent number. The clip says about a million. Either way, it’s the least room in the league, or close to it.

Picks: No 2027 first, which Cleveland owns from the Garrett trade, and no 2027 third, which Kansas City owns from the McDuffie trade. The Rams still have a 2027 second, fourth, fifth, and sixth, plus a 2028 third.

Ways to make room: A Kyren Williams restructure has been estimated to free up around $3.5 million. Or they could trade a player with salary. Forbes has a $2.85 million cap hit, and Athlon calculated that trading him would push the Rams’ cap space to about $2.86 million.

So if the Rams want Downs, the path would probably look like this. This is my thought experiment, not a report. Restructure a contract or move a player to create room, then send a mid-round pick to Indianapolis. That’s doable. It’s just not “cheap and simple,” and it’s definitely not “no other move needed,” like the clip claims.

Part seven: the Forbes problem, again

This is the third clip in a row to put Emmanuel Forbes in a fake trade, so let’s be clear about why that makes less sense now than it did a week ago.

ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler originally floated Forbes as a trade candidate because Forbes had “lost his starting job due to the additions of Trent McDuffie and Jaylen Watson.” That was the logic: Forbes was a backup, so trading him wouldn’t hurt much.

Then Watson went down for the season with a shoulder injury. McDuffie missed every practice last week with a back injury. Josh Wallace moved into the starting lineup, and Forbes moved up to the number three corner, against Washington, the team that drafted him in the first round in 2023.

And the Rams’ secondary held Washington to 13 points on Sunday, with that depleted group.

So trading Forbes now would mean taking away one of the few healthy cornerbacks on a roster that just lost its best outside corner for the year. Unless the Rams bring in another corner at the same time, that’s solving one problem by creating another.

Part eight: answering Quirin the neighbor

The clip’s fake skeptic had three good points, so let me give my honest answers.

“The Rams can’t afford anybody.” Mostly true. With almost no cap space, any trade needs a matching move. But a restructure could create enough room for a rookie contract like Downs’. So “can’t afford anybody” is too strong. “Can’t afford anybody without doing something first” is more accurate.

“‘Probably looking at the market’ isn’t a phone call.” Completely true. And I’d add: even “the Rams have called,” which is the closest real thing we have, isn’t a negotiation. Every team calls about every available player at the deadline.

“The offer could belong to somebody else.” True, and even the clip admits it. And I couldn’t confirm that the offer report exists in the first place. The real reporting has the Steelers and Chiefs as the main suitors for Downs.

And the clip’s narrator ended his argument with Quirin by saying that if all of that were enough, the Rams would already have done it. Quirin’s right. If the Rams wanted Downs badly enough at a fourth-round price, and the Colts were happy with a fourth, this would probably already be done. It isn’t.

The NFC West pressure

One thing that does make a Rams move more likely is what’s happening around them in the division.

The 49ers already added DeForest Buckner from the Colts. Seattle added Quenton Nelson, also from the Colts. The Athletic’s Nate Atkins put it simply after those deals: “The NFC West arms race is on.”

The Rams are the only team in that conversation that hasn’t made a midseason move. And they’re the team with the least cap space and the fewest premium picks to do it. So the pressure is real. But pressure isn’t the same as a plan. If the price for a receiver doesn’t make sense, Les Snead has shown in the past that he’s willing to wait, and sometimes, waiting is the right call.

Here’s the other side of the arms race. The Rams just added the best “trade” of all without giving up anything new: Myles Garrett coming off injured reserve. Three sacks in his first game back is the kind of addition no team can match at the deadline. And Adams is on the way back too. In a sense, the Rams are adding two Pro Bowl-level players in the next month without spending a pick.

What Downs would actually look like in this offense

Let’s imagine for a minute that the Rams do land Downs, so you know what to expect. This is my thought experiment, not a report.

Week one, Arizona. He’d have about four practices to learn the offense. That’s not much. Realistically, he’d get a small package of plays: quick slants, option routes, and maybe some third-down work. Think three to five targets, not ten.

Before the bye. The bye week would be huge for him. That’s when a new receiver can really learn the playbook, the terminology, and build timing with Stafford.

After the bye. This is where he’d matter. With a full week of prep, Downs could become the slot option on third down, with Puka moving around the formation more. That could take some of the 21-target pressure off Puka.

When Adams returns. Around the end of November or into December, if the short end of his timeline holds, Adams would come back as the number two, Downs would slide to number three, and suddenly the Rams would have real depth for the playoff run.

Next year. Downs is in the final year of his rookie deal. If the Rams like him, they’d need to pay him in the spring. Adams is 33. So Downs could be a long-term piece next to Puka.

That’s the best-case version. The worst case is that the Rams spend a mid-round pick on a receiver who catches a handful of passes, gets moved down the depth chart when Adams returns, and leaves in free agency. That’s why the price matters so much.

Part nine: the real Rams options for tomorrow

With less than a day to go, here’s my honest board of real options. These are based on actual reporting and analysis, not fake clips.

Josh Downs, Colts. Wants out. Colts asked for a third, maybe a fourth. Steelers and Chiefs are the main reported suitors. Cheap rookie deal, but still needs cap room. Helps underneath, not deep.

Brian Thomas Jr., Jaguars. The deep threat. ESPN’s Bill Barnwell called him the “exciting” option for the Rams. Struggled and was benched this season. Likely the most expensive in picks.

Dyami Brown, Commanders. ESPN’s Aaron Schatz named him as a possible Rams target. Cheap: about a $1.22 million base salary and roughly $250,000 in dead money for Washington. Has speed, and returns kicks. Missed Sunday’s game with a calf injury.

Standing pat. McVay’s public stance, per ESPN’s Sarah Barshop, is that the Rams would rather count on players coming back from injury. As he put it: “Hopefully, some of those trades will be guys getting back from injury.” On Sunday, Garrett showed exactly what that means.

My prediction, and this is not a report: if the Rams do something, it’ll be small, it’ll come with a restructure, and it’ll happen tomorrow afternoon. Les Snead has made four deadline-day trades over the years. And if the price for Downs climbs above a fourth, I think the Rams walk away and wait for Adams.

Part ten: what’s next on the field

Whatever happens tomorrow, the Rams have one more game before the bye: the Arizona Cardinals.

That’s the team that beat them 18-16 back on October 18th, the game where Adams got hurt. Arizona also beat Dallas 33-30 in overtime a couple of weeks ago, so they’re dangerous.

Here’s what I’ll be watching.

Does someone besides Puka get involved? If a receiver arrives by trade, he’d have a few practices to learn the offense before Sunday. If not, it’s on Tutu Atwell, Konata Mumpfield, Jordan Whittington, and the tight ends to take some of the load.

Can Garrett do it again? Three sacks in his first game back. If he does it two weeks in a row, the Rams have the scariest pass rush in the NFC, and the trade deadline stops mattering as much.

Can the run game stay hot? Three straight weeks over 120 rushing yards. That’s been the foundation of this winning streak.

And then the bye. A 6-4 record heading into the bye, with Adams, Kobie Turner, and maybe McDuffie getting healthier, would put the Rams right in the middle of the NFC playoff race.
