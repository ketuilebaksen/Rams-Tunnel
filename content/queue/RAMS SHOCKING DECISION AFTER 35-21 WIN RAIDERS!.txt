Let’s start with how this game unfolded, because the first half was the best half of football the Rams have played all season.

According to the Rams’ official website, the Rams opened the scoring in the first quarter with a 3-yard touchdown pass from Stafford to tight end Davis Allen. It could have been even better — a 2-yard Blake Corum touchdown run earlier was wiped out by a holding penalty. The Raiders answered with a 30-yard field goal from Matt Gay, and it was 7-3.

Then the Rams took over. Early in the second quarter, Stafford hit Allen again, this time from 5 yards out — 14-3. With 4:15 left in the half, Stafford found Puka Nacua for an 11-yard touchdown — 21-3. And with 1:19 left in the half, Kyren Williams ran it in from 17 yards — 28-3. That was four straight scoring drives. The Raiders kicked a field goal as time expired, and the Rams went into halftime up 28-6.

Sean McVay summed up that first half after the game: “I thought our guys came out ready to go. I thought we had the right energy. I thought we had the right mindset. I thought we played off of one another, particularly in the first half.” He added that outside of the first three-and-out, “I thought Matthew and the offense did a great job, where we were able to balance run and pass.”

And here’s a detail I love: the Rams’ fourth touchdown drive started because of the defense. According to the Rams’ official website, cornerback Emmanuel Forbes Jr. broke up a fourth-down pass, giving the Rams the ball at their own 48. A few plays later, Kyren was in the end zone. That’s complementary football.

Now let’s talk about the history, because Matthew Stafford just did something no player has ever done.

According to the Rams’ official website and the LA Times, Stafford’s three touchdown passes gave him 155 with the Rams, passing Roman Gabriel’s franchise record of 154. Gabriel played 11 seasons for the Rams, from 1962 to 1972, and his record had lasted more than five decades. Gabriel was the NFL’s MVP in 1969, one of the great quarterbacks of his era. And now Stafford has passed him in just his sixth season with the Rams. The record-breaker was the touchdown pass to Puka Nacua.

And it gets even better. According to the LA Times’ Gary Klein, Stafford also has 282 touchdown passes with the Detroit Lions, which makes him the first player in NFL history to hold the career touchdown pass record for two different franchises. Think about that. The Lions’ all-time leader and the Rams’ all-time leader is the same man.

Stafford’s line on Sunday, according to the Rams’ official website: 23 of 29 for 195 yards, three touchdowns and one interception. That’s not a huge yardage day. But it’s efficient, and it’s exactly the kind of game a quarterback can play when his running game is working. As Stafford put it after the game, according to the LA Times: “It’s nice feeling a little bit more comfortable in the fourth quarter.” After three straight weeks of needing a last-minute touchdown drive, I bet it was. According to the LA Times, Stafford now has 14 touchdown passes and seven interceptions this season. And McVay’s verdict on the whole day, according to the LA Times, was simple: “A good step in the right direction.” He also said after the game: “We got to be able to build on it.” That’s the right message. One win doesn’t fix a season.

According to Pro Football Network, Stafford’s QB Impact Grade for the game was 82.2, sixth among all quarterbacks that week.

Now let’s get to the real decision of the week: the run game.

According to SB Nation’s Steven Ridings, via Yahoo Sports, the Rams passed the ball about 65 percent of the time over the first six weeks. Against the Raiders, Kyren Williams and Blake Corum combined for 33 carries and 192 rushing yards — 5.8 yards per carry, according to the Rams’ official website. According to Rams Wire, the team ran for 199 yards in all.

Here’s how it broke down, according to the LA Times. Kyren: 16 carries for 99 yards and a touchdown. Corum: 17 carries for 93 yards and a touchdown. And Corum could have had a lot more — he broke a 43-yard touchdown run in the third quarter that was called back because of a penalty.

Remember the story from last week? After the Arizona loss, Kyren was asked why the offense’s energy rose and fell, and he said: “Probably because I’m not out there.” He had just 10 carries in that game, and the team ran only 20 times. Fans online said McVay had lost the locker room. McVay said Kyren came to him on his own to clear the air, and that Kyren just wanted to “be that spark.”

Well, one week later, Kyren was the spark. Sixteen carries, 99 yards and a touchdown. And the split with Corum still worked — Corum had 17 carries of his own. After the game, Corum said, according to the LA Times: “It feels great being able to have a partner back there like Kyren.” That’s not a locker room breaking. That’s a backfield working together.

And when things got tough, both backs answered. Kyren lost a fumble in the second half. But McVay said afterward: “I love the way that both” of his running backs “responded to be critical factors in us closing the game out.” Corum finished the game with the clinching touchdown, and Kyren kept running hard. That’s the kind of response you want from your captain.

McVay also gave a lot of credit to the big guys up front. He said: “I thought our offensive line was excellent. They had a great demeanor.” He noted the Raiders “got some great players on their front that can be very disruptive,” and said his tackles were “outstanding on the edges.” When you run for nearly 200 yards against a defense like that, the offensive line deserves its flowers.

McVay explained why the balance mattered so much. He said that when you run the ball well, “you’re really in 50/50 downs. And so that’s a key factor for us.” He added: “Any good offense that we’ve had here that I’ve been a part of, you got to be able to do both. You have to be able to have balance, or you at least have to have the threat of — if they overcommit to something, then you got to be able to make them pay.”

And the experts noticed. On FOX, Rob Gronkowski said, according to Pro Football Network: “This is the Rams that we expected to see from the beginning of the year.” And on ESPN, the analysts talked about the same thing: a team that had been relying on Stafford’s arm finally played with balance.

So if you’re asking me what McVay’s big decision was, here it is: he stopped asking Stafford to carry everything. And it worked. McVay also mentioned the details that made it work — changing Stafford’s launch point, using play action, and taking checkdowns. He said “the backs and the tight ends did a great job of getting vertical” after the catch. That’s how you take pressure off a quarterback without a deep threat on the outside.

Now let’s talk about the tight ends, because they were the answer to the Davante Adams question — at least for one week.

According to SB Nation, the Rams’ three tight ends combined for 11 catches, 82 yards and two touchdowns. Colby Parkinson led the group with five catches for 48 yards, and SB Nation’s JB Scott wrote that Parkinson handled a lot of Adams’ role between the 20s. Davis Allen scored both touchdowns — and according to Rams Wire, he came into the game with zero touchdowns this season. SB Nation noted he nearly had a third. And think about Allen’s last two weeks. Against Arizona, according to Rams On SI, the Rams handed him the ball on a fullback dive on fourth-and-1 — a player who had never taken a handoff in his NFL career — and it didn’t work. Fans were furious. One week later, he caught two touchdown passes in the first half. That’s a great bounce-back for a player who’s always been one of the most reliable blockers on this team.

And then there was Tyler Higbee’s big moment. According to Rams Wire, after the Raiders cut the lead to 10 in the fourth quarter, Higbee made a catch on third-and-2 that kept the game-sealing drive alive. That drive went 70 yards in 12 plays and burned 6 minutes and 38 seconds off the clock, and it ended with Corum’s 4-yard touchdown run. That’s how you close a game: run the ball, convert third downs and take the clock with you.

McVay talked about where this all started. When he was asked how the team avoided the same mistakes in the first half, he pointed to the week of practice: “the right kind of mindset and mentality, putting the work in throughout the course of the week, and being able to kind of revisit and reestablish some of the things that we want to be about.” After the Arizona loss, McVay said he couldn’t describe the team’s identity yet. On Sunday, we saw what it could be: physical, balanced and patient.

And yes, Puka played. He was questionable all week with a calf injury, and McVay said it would be a pregame decision. According to the LA Times, Puka finished with eight catches for 68 yards and that record-setting 11-yard touchdown. Rams Wire named him one of the three stars of the game. For a guy who left practice early on Thursday, that’s a huge relief. And notice something else: Puka wasn’t overloaded. Against Buffalo, he was targeted 24 times. On Sunday, with the run game and the tight ends carrying a big share of the load, he caught eight passes and still made the biggest play of the day. That’s how you keep your best receiver healthy for December — especially with a calf that was sore three days earlier.

Now let’s talk about the defense, because this might have been the most impressive part of the day.

According to SB Nation, the Rams played without five defensive starters: Myles Garrett, Trent McDuffie, Poona Ford, Kobie Turner and Nate Landman. And they still dominated for most of the game.

Against the run, the Raiders managed fewer than 50 rushing yards — SB Nation’s Steven Ridings put it at 48 yards on 17 carries, just 2.8 per carry. Ridings credited Aaron Donald, Ty Hamilton and Braden Fiske. And SB Nation’s JB Scott singled out Tyler Davis, calling him “a starting quality player who is getting his first opportunity.” Remember, the Rams’ defensive staff told us on Friday that Davis was “definitely ready for it.” He proved it.

On third down, according to Rams Wire, the Raiders converted just 3 of 14. And on fourth down, the Rams stopped them three times. One of those stops was Forbes’ pass breakup that set up a touchdown. Another came on a fourth-and-2 at the Rams’ 13. And another came after the Rams won a replay challenge on a Kirk Cousins pass to Jalen Nailor, according to the Rams’ official website.

At linebacker, with Landman out, second-year player Shaun Dolac stepped in. According to SB Nation, Dolac “performed admirably,” was strong in coverage and held up against the run. JB Scott even suggested he could earn more snaps going forward. And Rams Wire named cornerback Jaylen Watson one of the game’s three stars.

McVay loved what he saw. He said he loved the way the defense “continued to just make him snap it one more time.” And on the second-half turnover, he said: “Defense got a great sudden change when we ended up having the one fumble.” That’s exactly what Chris Shula talked about on Friday — the defense answering whatever the offense gives them. And Shula admitted on Friday that the defense hadn’t created as many sacks and turnovers as last season. On Sunday, with three fourth-down stops and a 3-of-14 day on third down, the defense found a different way to make game-changing plays: getting off the field when it mattered most. Do that every week, and the turnovers will come too.

And let’s not forget special teams, because after the last two weeks, a quiet day was a beautiful thing.

Brandon McManus made his Rams debut, and McVay said: “McManus did a great job being able to knock in all of his extra points, and then when we wanted to cover or kick touchbacks, he did a good job.” Five touchdowns, five extra points, no drama. That’s exactly what this team needed. McVay also praised punter Ethan Evans for hitting the ball well on his three punts and said Jordan Whittington was “getting down there” in punt coverage. After two weeks of special teams chaos, the third phase finally helped the Rams win.

Now, I have to be honest with you. This game wasn’t perfect, and the second half should worry us a little.

After building a 28-6 halftime lead, the Rams didn’t score in the third quarter. Early in the fourth, Cousins hit Tre Tucker for a 37-yard touchdown — 28-12. Then, according to the Rams’ official website, a Stafford pass was tipped by Colby Parkinson and intercepted by Raiders linebacker Nakobe Dean, and Cousins threw a 15-yard touchdown to Ashton Jeanty. Suddenly, it was 28-18. And Kyren Williams also lost a fumble at the Rams’ 30. Luckily, the defense held on that drive. The Raiders also did a few things to keep themselves alive. According to the Rams’ official website, they converted a fake punt with a pass, and they went for two after both of their fourth-quarter touchdowns — and failed both times. Those two failed conversions mattered a lot. Instead of a one-score game, the Rams kept a two-score cushion. And after the Raiders’ final field goal, a 36-yarder from Matt Gay with 4:11 left, Las Vegas tried an onside kick. The Rams recovered, and their clock management sealed the win. According to CBS Sports’ live tracker, Gay had also missed a 51-yard field goal wide left in the first half. So the Raiders left points on the field, too.

McVay didn’t hide from it. He said: “I love the fact that we had to weather a little bit of adversity in the second half. You know, some self-inflicted wounds.” That phrase again — “self-inflicted wounds.” It’s been the theme of this season, and it showed up again on Sunday. Two turnovers and two touchdowns called back by penalties. Against a better team, that could have cost the Rams the game.

There’s another concern, too. According to SB Nation’s JB Scott, the deep passing game still isn’t there. The Rams have had very few completions of 20 yards or more, and Scott wrote that receivers Jordan Whittington and Konata Mumpfield were “very disappointing” in this game. Without Davante Adams, the Rams found answers at tight end and running back, but they still don’t have a receiver who can stretch the field opposite Puka. That’s the question for the trade deadline.

So the takeaway is this: the formula works. Run the ball, use the tight ends, play great defense and stay clean on special teams. But the Rams have to stop giving teams second chances. If they can play four clean quarters, this team is dangerous.

Now, here’s the news that might matter even more than the win itself.

According to the LA Times, Myles Garrett and tight end Terrance Ferguson are now eligible to come off injured reserve. On Friday, McVay said it was “a possibility” that Garrett returns to practice this week, and NFL Network’s Ian Rapoport reported that Garrett is “likely to return to practice next week,” according to Pro Football Talk. Garrett was the 2025 Defensive Player of the Year with a record 23 sacks. Imagine adding him to a defense that just held the Raiders under 50 rushing yards.

And Ferguson matters, too. According to SB Nation, Ferguson has been on injured reserve since Week 3, and JB Scott suggested the Rams may not push the ball downfield effectively until he’s back. Adding him to Parkinson, Higbee and Allen would give McVay even more options.

According to the LA Times, Trent McDuffie, who missed the last two games with a groin injury, could return soon. Kobie Turner is out at least three more games, and could return for the Packers game the night before Thanksgiving. And Davante Adams will return at some point, though no timeline has been set.

If the Rams can get Garrett, McDuffie and Ferguson back over the next few weeks, this roster starts to look a lot more like the one everyone expected in August.

So where does this leave the Rams in the standings?

According to Pro Football Network, the Rams are now 3-4, and they moved up to third in the NFC West after both the 49ers and Cardinals lost on Sunday. Michael Strahan said on FOX, according to Pro Football Network: “This was a do-or-die win. They lose this game today, I think they’re completely out of it.” And he added that “the Rams are feeling pretty good about their chances to stay in there.” Strahan also described what the Rams had been hoping for before kickoff: “If you’re the Rams, you’re just hoping that you just keep it close, hang in there.” Instead, they won by two touchdowns. And remember the number FOX shared before the game: since the playoffs expanded in 2020, only seven of 36 teams that started 2-4 have made the postseason. The Rams just took the first step toward joining that group. And the Raiders? They’ve now lost four straight after starting the season 3-0. With the win, the Rams also took the lead in the all-time series. According to FOX Sports, the series was tied 8-8 before Sunday. Now it’s 9-8, Rams.

The road ahead looks favorable, at least for the next few weeks. According to Pro Football Network, the Rams host the winless Chargers next Sunday, November 1st, at SoFi Stadium, at 4:05 p.m. Eastern. The Chargers are 0-6. And don’t forget, this is a home game for both teams in a way — the Rams and Chargers share SoFi Stadium. A winless team in a rivalry game can be dangerous, because they’ll treat it like their Super Bowl. The Rams can’t overlook anybody after the start they’ve had. But this is exactly the kind of game a team trying to climb back into the race has to win. Then comes Washington on November 8th, two days before the trade deadline. According to SB Nation, the Rams likely need to win their next three games — the Chargers, the Commanders and the Cardinals — to put themselves in a strong position before a tough late-season stretch.

And this win could change the trade conversation. Remember, ESPN’s Dan Graziano reported that the Rams would “explore the possibility” of trading for a receiver, and McVay said that adding someone from outside isn’t “something that we’ve talked about right now.” After Sunday, I think the Rams’ priorities might shift. The run game and the tight ends showed they can carry the offense without Adams. What’s missing is a deep threat. So if the Rams make a move, I’d expect it to be a cheap, specific one — a receiver who can stretch the field — rather than a blockbuster. And if Garrett and Ferguson come back healthy, the Rams might not need to make any move at all. Names like Josh Downs of the Colts and Stefon Diggs of the Commanders have been floated by writers this week — but those are still just ideas, not reported talks. And remember, the Rams play Washington on November 8th, two days before the deadline. If the Rams keep winning, they can be patient. If the offense stalls again, the phone calls get more serious.

Here’s what I want to see against the Chargers.

Number one: keep the balance. Thirty-plus carries for the running backs should be the plan every week, not a one-time thing. McVay said it himself — “you got to be able to do both.”

Number two: clean up the second half. No tipped interceptions, no fumbles in your own territory, no penalties wiping out touchdowns. Four clean quarters.

Number three: find one deep shot. Whether it’s Puka, Mumpfield, Whittington or a tight end, the Rams need to hit at least one big play down the field to keep defenses honest.

My early prediction: Rams 30, Chargers 17. I’ll break it down fully later in the week.

So here’s my bottom line.

There was no shocking announcement after the game. The real decision came before it: McVay leaned into the run game, and it worked. Kyren and Corum combined for 192 yards on the ground, the tight ends scored twice, Puka played through his calf injury and scored the record-breaking touchdown, and Matthew Stafford became the Rams’ all-time leader in touchdown passes — and the first player ever to lead two franchises in that category. The defense, without five starters, held the Raiders to 3-of-14 on third down and stopped them three times on fourth down.

The Rams are 3-4. They’re not fixed yet. The second-half turnovers are a warning. But with Garrett and Ferguson eligible to return and a winless Chargers team coming to SoFi next week, this win could be the start of something.
