Good news number one: Puka Nacua’s calf.

This was the biggest worry of the week. On Thursday, Puka left practice early with a calf injury. He didn’t practice on Friday, and he was listed as questionable for the Raiders game. McVay said it would be a pregame decision.

Then came the first piece of good news. According to Rams On SI, an MRI on Puka’s calf came back better than the Rams had hoped. And ESPN’s Adam Schefter reported before the game that the Rams were optimistic, quoting a source who said: “as long as there aren’t any setbacks, we feel good about him being able to play.”

He played — and he played well. According to the LA Times, Puka finished with eight catches for 68 yards and an 11-yard touchdown. And that touchdown was the one that made Matthew Stafford the Rams’ all-time leader in touchdown passes, with 155, passing Roman Gabriel.

After the game, Puka explained what happened. According to HITC, via Yahoo Sports, he said: “Just trying to run, and deceleration after, guys, it kind of tapped off on the play. I just planted, and just felt something in my lower calf.” And he said the MRI showed nothing significant.

He was also asked about the fear of getting hurt again. His answer, according to Heavy: “I try to mentally prepare and say that I’m full focus.” According to Heavy, he also praised the training staff, McVay and Stafford for how they communicated with him all week.

Now, here’s the caution. According to Heavy, this calf issue comes on top of a groin injury that cost Puka two games earlier this season, and a hip injury he dealt with in training camp and the preseason. So the Rams have to be smart. Heavy noted the Rams might limit his practice reps to protect him over the next few weeks. That’s exactly what I’d do. With Davante Adams out, Puka is the one player this offense can’t afford to lose.

But for now, the news is good: Puka played, he scored, and the MRI didn’t show anything serious. And his numbers are still excellent. Adding Sunday’s game to the AP’s earlier totals, Puka now has 45 catches for 485 yards and two touchdowns in five games this season — that’s my own math from the reported numbers. Over a full season, that’s still on pace for well over 1,000 yards, even after missing two games. Remember, this is the same player who set NFL rookie records with 105 catches and 1,486 yards in 2023. When he’s healthy, he’s one of the best receivers in football.

Good news number two: Myles Garrett.

This could be the biggest news of the rest of the season. According to Pro Football Rumors, via Yardbarker, NFL Network’s Ian Rapoport reported that Garrett is likely to return to practice this week. Rapoport said on The Pat McAfee Show, according to Rams On SI: “I was told on Myles Garrett that it is likely that he returns to practice next week.” And on Friday, when McVay was asked about it, he said: “That’s a possibility.”

According to Rams On SI, Rapoport noted that this would be about seven weeks after Garrett’s injury. Garrett played through knee discomfort in the Week 1 loss to the 49ers in Australia, then had a procedure and went on injured reserve in mid-September, according to Pro Football Rumors. According to Pro Football Talk, he played 62 percent of the Rams’ defensive snaps in that opener but didn’t record a stat. That’s how much the knee was bothering him. Garrett is 30, and the Rams traded significant draft capital to get him from Cleveland in the offseason. So it makes sense they’d want to be careful — they need him healthy not just for this season, but for the years ahead.

Here’s how the process works. Once Garrett returns to practice, the Rams open his 21-day practice window. During those 21 days, they can activate him to the 53-man roster at any time. According to Rams On SI, the Rams are expected to stay cautious. So the realistic timeline is somewhere between the Chargers game this Sunday and the Washington game on November 8th — or a little later, if the Rams want to be extra careful.

And think about what Garrett adds. According to Pro Football Talk, he was the 2025 AP Defensive Player of the Year with a single-season record 23 sacks. Now imagine him joining a defense that just held the Raiders to 3-of-14 on third down and stopped them three times on fourth down — without him, without Trent McDuffie, without Poona Ford, without Kobie Turner and without Nate Landman.

That’s the part that excites me most. This defense has been good while badly short-handed. Add the reigning Defensive Player of the Year, and it could become one of the best in football again.

And remember what Aaron Donald said when he came out of retirement in August, according to theScore: “Hopefully, we do some special things together.” We’ve barely seen Garrett and Donald on the field together. That could change very soon.

Good news number three: more help is on the way.

According to the LA Times, tight end Terrance Ferguson is also eligible to come off injured reserve. According to SB Nation, Ferguson has been on IR since Week 3. And SB Nation’s JB Scott suggested the Rams may not push the ball downfield effectively until he’s back. That’s a big deal, because the deep passing game is the one part of this offense that still isn’t working.

And think about the tight end room once Ferguson returns. Against the Raiders, according to SB Nation, Colby Parkinson, Davis Allen and Tyler Higbee combined for 11 catches, 82 yards and two touchdowns. Allen caught both touchdowns. Add Ferguson to that group, and McVay has four tight ends he trusts — and a lot more ways to attack defenses without Davante Adams.

At cornerback, according to the LA Times, Trent McDuffie could return soon after missing the last two games with a groin injury. According to the AP, before he got hurt, McDuffie had 16 tackles, two tackles for loss, seven passes defended and an interception in five games. The AP called him arguably the Rams’ best defender. Getting him back for the Chargers would be huge. And the Rams’ backups held up well without him. According to Pro Football Rumors, Josh Wallace stepped in for McDuffie against Arizona and allowed just 20 yards on eight targets, according to PFF. Add McDuffie back to that secondary, and the pass defense — already one of the best in football — gets even better.

And what about Davante Adams? According to Heavy, Adams isn’t expected back until about the bye week. Adams ruptured his hamstring on October 18th and was given a six-to-10-week timeline. The bye is in Week 11, and the Rams play the Packers after it. So if Adams is back around then, the Rams could have Puka, Adams, Garrett and McDuffie all together for the stretch run.

The only longer wait is Kobie Turner. According to the LA Times, Turner is out at least three more games and could return for the Packers game, the night before Thanksgiving.

So here’s the big picture. Over the next month, the Rams could get back Garrett, McDuffie, Ferguson, Landman, Turner and eventually Adams. That’s a lot of talent returning to a team that just won without most of them.

Good news number four: the standings.

According to Pro Football Network, the Rams are now 3-4 and moved up to third in the NFC West after both the 49ers and Cardinals lost on Sunday. A week ago, the Rams were in last place. Now they’re climbing.

On FOX, Michael Strahan said, according to Pro Football Network: “This was a do-or-die win. They lose this game today, I think they’re completely out of it.” And he added that “the Rams are feeling pretty good about their chances to stay in there.” Rob Gronkowski said: “This is the Rams that we expected to see from the beginning of the year.”

And the schedule helps. According to Pro Football Network, the Rams host the winless Chargers this Sunday, November 1st, at SoFi Stadium. The Chargers are 0-6. Then comes Washington on November 8th, and then the Cardinals. According to SB Nation, those three games could decide whether the Rams are in a strong position heading into a tough late-season stretch. Win all three, and the Rams are 6-4 going into the bye — right in the middle of the playoff race. Even two out of three would get them to 5-5 heading into the bye, with Garrett, McDuffie and possibly Adams on the way back. That’s a very different conversation than the one we were having a week ago. Before the Raiders game, according to FOX Sports, the Rams were 12-1 to win the Super Bowl — a long way from where they started the season as the favorite. Keep winning, and those odds will start moving back in the other direction.

Before Sunday’s game, according to FOX Sports, only seven of 36 teams that started 2-4 had made the playoffs since the field expanded in 2020. The Rams just took the first step toward being one of the exceptions. Matthew Stafford said it best after the game, according to the LA Times: “It’s nice feeling a little bit more comfortable in the fourth quarter.” And McVay called the win “a good step in the right direction.” Not a finish line. A step.

Good news number five: the depth showed up.

Here’s something we didn’t know before Sunday: the backups on this roster can play. According to SB Nation, second-year linebacker Shaun Dolac replaced Nate Landman and “performed admirably,” holding up in coverage and against the run. SB Nation’s JB Scott even suggested Dolac could earn more snaps going forward.

On the defensive line, with Ford and Turner out, Tyler Davis stepped up. JB Scott called him “a starting quality player who is getting his first opportunity.” And SB Nation’s Steven Ridings credited Aaron Donald, Ty Hamilton and Braden Fiske for holding the Raiders to just 2.8 yards per carry.

In the secondary, Emmanuel Forbes Jr. broke up a fourth-down pass that set up a Rams touchdown, according to the Rams’ official website. And Rams Wire named cornerback Jaylen Watson one of the three stars of the game.

On offense, Davis Allen scored his first two touchdowns of the season. And Blake Corum ran for 93 yards and the game-sealing touchdown. After the game, Corum said, according to the LA Times: “It feels great being able to have a partner back there like Kyren.”

This matters for the rest of the season. When the starters come back, the Rams won’t just be healthier — they’ll be deeper. Players like Dolac, Davis and Allen have now proven they can be trusted in big moments.

Now, let me balance all this good news with a little caution, because I always want to give you the full picture. The Rams still have to clean up their second halves. Against the Raiders, according to the Rams’ official website, they didn’t score in the third quarter. A Stafford pass was tipped by Colby Parkinson and intercepted, and Kyren Williams lost a fumble at the Rams’ 30. Two touchdowns were also wiped out by penalties — including a 43-yard Corum run. McVay himself called them “self-inflicted wounds.” Against a better team, those mistakes could cost a game. So yes, the news is good. But the work isn’t done.

And the injury list is still long. Puka has now dealt with hip, groin and calf issues this season. Adams is still weeks away. Turner is out until at least the Packers game. Ford is done for the year. Good teams survive injuries, but the Rams still need a little luck to stay healthy over the next month.

Good news number six: the kicker.

After two weeks of kicking chaos, Brandon McManus made his Rams debut and did exactly what the Rams needed. After the game, McVay said: “McManus did a great job being able to knock in all of his extra points, and then when we wanted to cover or kick touchbacks, he did a good job.” Five touchdowns, five extra points. No drama. That’s the best review a kicker can get. McVay also credited punter Ethan Evans for hitting the ball well on his three punts, and Jordan Whittington for getting downfield in coverage. After two weeks where special teams cost the Rams games, they finally helped win one.

And good news number six-and-a-half — because this one deserves its own moment: Matthew Stafford’s record. According to the Rams’ official website and the LA Times, Stafford’s three touchdown passes on Sunday gave him 155 with the Rams, passing Roman Gabriel’s record of 154, which had stood since Gabriel’s career ended in 1972. And according to the LA Times’ Gary Klein, Stafford also has 282 touchdown passes with the Detroit Lions — making him the first player in NFL history to lead two different franchises in career touchdown passes. Whatever happens the rest of this season, that’s a piece of history every Rams fan should be proud of.

And good news number seven: the identity.

After the Arizona loss, McVay was asked about the team’s identity and said: “We’re figuring it out. I can’t answer that right now.” On Sunday, the answer looked a lot clearer. According to SB Nation, the Rams had passed about 65 percent of the time over the first six weeks. Against the Raiders, Kyren Williams and Blake Corum combined for 33 carries and 192 rushing yards. McVay explained it after the game: “Any good offense that we’ve had here that I’ve been a part of, you got to be able to do both.”

And the best part? Kyren, who said after the Arizona loss that the offense lacked energy “probably because I’m not out there,” ran for 99 yards and a touchdown on 16 carries. The Rams found an identity, and their captain got his spark back. And Corum, according to the LA Times, added 17 carries for 93 yards and a touchdown of his own. As a team, according to Rams Wire, the Rams ran for 199 yards. If McVay keeps that balance going, it takes so much pressure off Stafford — and off Puka’s calf.

Now, let’s go back to that Vita Vea clip, because I want to explain why it doesn’t hold up — especially now that the game has been played.

The clip claimed Schefter said on First Take on October 23rd that the Rams “already made the call” to Tampa Bay, and that “this trade is going to happen.” I couldn’t find any record of that. It’s the same format we’ve seen in several fake clips this month — same show, same refrain repeated over and over.

Here’s what is real. Vea did request a trade in July, and according to the New York Post, via Yahoo Sports, his reported wish list included the 49ers, Chargers, Rams and Raiders. McVay called him “a stud” in July, but said: “I think we feel good about our group right now.” In August, Vea signed an extension with Tampa Bay. And ESPN’s Dan Graziano did name him as a possible trade candidate this week. But no trusted reporter has said the Rams called, made an offer, or are close to a deal. The clip also said the Rams were “the favorites per SportsGrid” in July — I couldn’t verify that either. And notice something else: the clip is dated October 23rd, two days before the game, so even its own “urgent” logic about needing a nose tackle for Sunday is now out of date.

And the clip’s main argument just fell apart on the field. It said the Rams needed Vea because the Raiders were “a run-heavy team” and the Rams needed someone to “stop the run” on Sunday. Well, without Vea, the Rams held the Raiders to fewer than 50 rushing yards. SB Nation put it at 48 yards on 17 carries. Tyler Davis, Ty Hamilton, Braden Fiske and Aaron Donald did that job just fine.

There’s also the money. According to Yardbarker, Vea is guaranteed 23 million dollars in 2027. And according to Pro Football Rumors, the Rams have just under 990,000 dollars in cap space. Even if the Rams wanted him, that 2027 guarantee is a huge commitment for a defensive tackle who, according to Athlon, had zero sacks through six starts this season.

The clip also mentioned Stephen A. Smith laying out a “survival plan” with George Pickens on offense, Vea on defense, and Maxx Crosby if the Raiders sell. I couldn’t find any real report connecting any of those players to the Rams. And after Sunday, the Raiders are 3-4 — not exactly in fire-sale mode yet.

So my take is simple: the best “trade” the Rams can make right now is getting Garrett, McDuffie and Ferguson back healthy. Think about it: Garrett is a former Defensive Player of the Year, McDuffie was the Rams’ best cover corner, and Ferguson gives the offense another target who can work the middle of the field. Getting all three back would be like winning the trade deadline without making a single call. That’s real help, and it doesn’t cost a single draft pick.

Now let’s talk about the one area where the Rams still might need help: wide receiver.

ESPN’s Graziano reported last week that the Rams “will at least explore the possibility of trading for a wide receiver.” And according to SB Nation’s JB Scott, the deep passing game still isn’t there. Scott wrote that Jordan Whittington and Konata Mumpfield were “very disappointing” against the Raiders. The Rams have had very few completions of 20 yards or more this season.

So if the Rams make a move before the November 10th deadline, I’d expect it to be a receiver who can stretch the field — not a defensive tackle. Names like Josh Downs of the Colts and Stefon Diggs of the Commanders have been floated by writers. Those are ideas, not reported talks. But with Ferguson coming back and Adams expected around the bye, the Rams might decide they can wait. A strong showing against the Chargers would make that decision even easier. Before the Raiders game, according to Pro Football Rumors, Mumpfield had eight catches on 16 targets for 127 yards this season. Whittington had three catches for 12 yards, and Atwell had three for 31. That’s where the need is. The Rams don’t need a star. They need one more receiver who can make defenses respect the deep ball.

One more note before we wrap up, and it’s a sad one. According to Rams On SI, the Rams announced the passing of former running back Lawrence McCutcheon this weekend. When McCutcheon left the Rams after the 1979 season, he was the franchise’s all-time leading rusher. He was a big part of some great Rams teams in the 1970s, and on behalf of the whole Rams House, our thoughts are with his family and friends.

And here’s one fun note to end on. According to Rams On SI, NFL Commissioner Roger Goodell said Tokyo is “clearly on our list” for a future international game, and the Rams hold the league’s marketing rights in Japan. So if the NFL ever plays a game in Tokyo, the Rams are the most likely team to go. After the Melbourne trip this season, the Rams are becoming the NFL’s global team.

Here’s what I’ll be watching this week.

Number one: Garrett at practice. If he’s on the field Wednesday, his 21-day window opens, and the countdown to his return begins.

Number two: Puka’s practice reps. If the Rams limit him early in the week, that’s smart, not a red flag. What matters is that he’s ready for Sunday.

Number three: McDuffie and Ferguson. If either one practices fully, the Rams get a big boost for the Chargers game. And keep an eye on Nate Landman, too. McVay said Friday that Landman was “making good progress” in the concussion protocol. If he clears the protocol, the Rams get their leading tackler and defensive signal-caller back — and Shaun Dolac becomes valuable depth after a strong game.

Here’s my early prediction for Sunday: Rams 30, Chargers 17. Here’s the thing about this matchup: the Rams and Chargers share SoFi Stadium, so this is a home game for the Rams that feels like a rivalry game. A winless team has nothing to lose, and they’ll throw everything at the Rams. But if the Rams bring the same balance, the same physical run game and the same defense they showed in Las Vegas, they should handle it. The Rams just found their formula. Now they have to prove it wasn’t a one-week thing — and the best way to do that is to win ugly or win pretty, but win.

So here’s my bottom line.

Yes, the Rams got real good news after the Raiders game. Puka’s MRI showed nothing significant, and he played through the calf injury. Garrett is likely to return to practice this week. Ferguson is eligible to come off IR, McDuffie could be back soon, and Adams is expected around the bye. The Rams moved up to third in the NFC West, and the winless Chargers are next. And the depth players — Dolac, Davis, Allen and Corum — showed they can be trusted.

What’s not real is the Vita Vea trade. No trusted reporter has said the Rams called Tampa Bay, and the clip spreading online gets basic facts wrong. And the Rams just proved they could stop the run without him.
