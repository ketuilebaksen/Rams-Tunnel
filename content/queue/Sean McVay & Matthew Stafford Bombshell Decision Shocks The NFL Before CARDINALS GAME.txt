The headline says “Sean McVay and Matthew Stafford bombshell decision shocks the NFL before Cardinals game.” Let me tell you what’s actually behind it, because this time there is a real decision, and it’s one I have to correct myself on.

This is McVay’s real Wednesday press conference before Sunday’s game in Arizona. And the biggest news is this: the Rams are not putting Puka Nacua on injured reserve. And there’s a second big piece of news that a lot of Rams fans have been waiting for: McVay gave a real timeline for Davante Adams. He said, “I would think after Green Bay is a realistic timeline for him.”

Is any of that a “bombshell”? Not really. But it’s real, it’s important, and it tells us a lot about how the next month is going to look.

Part one: the Wednesday injury report

McVay started by going through who wouldn’t practice on Wednesday. Here’s the list.

Steve Avila, guard, hamstring. He left the Washington game with a hamstring strain, and McVay said Monday it’ll be “a little bit of time.”

Puka Nacua, receiver, knee and toe. That’s the MCL and PCL sprain and the turf toe from the final offensive play against Washington.

Trent McDuffie, cornerback, back.

Kam Curl, safety, toe. On last week’s injury report, Curl was listed with a foot injury, so this is the same general area.

Terrance Ferguson, tight end, hamstring.

That’s five important players who didn’t practice. Two starters on offense, two key pieces of the secondary, and a pass-catching tight end. That’s a lot to work around for a road game.

Part two: why Puka isn’t going on IR

Let’s talk about the “decision,” because I think it’s the most misunderstood part of this press conference.

On Monday, McVay said Puka would be “out for a good amount of time,” that he’d “probably be a guy that we’ll put on IR,” and that “we’re thinking it’s going to be at least a month.” I reported that, and so did everyone else.

On Wednesday, McVay changed the plan: “We’re not going to put him on IR just because of some semantics with the roster and things like that. But he still is going to be out for an extended period of time. It wasn’t like any sort of new information.”

So let me be clear. This is not good news about Puka’s knee. It’s not a sign he’s healing faster than expected. McVay specifically said it “wasn’t like any sort of new information.” It’s a roster decision.

Here’s why I think they did it. This is my analysis, not something McVay said directly. On Monday, McVay told reporters the Rams are running low on IR return designations. He said “that number is getting so high that we’re going to run out of those eight to be able to return.” Myles Garrett used one when he came back. Ronnie Rivers used one. Davante Adams and Kobie Turner will each need one. Terrance Ferguson’s situation is tied to one too.

If the Rams put Puka on IR, they’d have to use another return slot to bring him back. By keeping him on the active roster, they don’t burn a slot. The trade-off is that he takes up a spot on the 53-man roster while he can’t play. With so many injuries, that’s a real cost. But the Rams apparently decided keeping the return slots was more important.

So the timeline is still the same. Puka is out “for an extended period of time.” Monday’s “at least a month” is still the best estimate we have. He’s just not on IR.

And I want to own my part of this. In my last few videos, I said Puka was “likely headed to IR.” That’s what McVay said on Monday. It changed on Wednesday. That’s how injury news works, and I’ll always tell you when it changes.

Part three: the trade deadline answer

A reporter asked McVay about the deadline, since the only move the Rams made was bringing back Xavier Smith.

McVay said, “We look at all that kind of stuff.” Then he gave the most honest explanation I’ve heard of why the Rams didn’t trade: “It kind of takes two, and got to be able to see what you’re willing to give, what’s out there, what does it look like in terms of envisioning things.”

That’s a real answer. A trade needs a partner, a price, and a player who fits. McVay is basically saying the Rams looked, and the right combination wasn’t there.

Later in the press conference, another reporter asked whether the Rams weren’t active because of the playbook’s complexity, since a new player would need time to learn it. McVay said it was “not as much that as much as just getting guys back, and then, okay, who are those players that you would acquire, and what does that really look like?”

And he said something really interesting about the roster: “This is as much as I can remember, spending time with Les in the early parts of the week, of just the roster mechanics.” In other words, he and general manager Les Snead have spent more time this week on roster management than he can remember in any other year.

He explained why: “It’s different than previous years, where maybe you’ve had a high volume in certain positions. We’ve just had a lot of different things across the board at a number of spots.”

And then the key line: “The main thing is you’re hopeful that you’re getting some of these guys back, and sooner than later, rather than saying, okay, these guys are out for the year and we’re going to try to fill that void for the year.”

That’s the whole philosophy. The Rams didn’t trade because they think their injured players are coming back. If those players were done for the season, McVay suggested the approach would’ve been different.

Part four: Xavier Smith and Jordan Whittington

On the moves the Rams did make, McVay sounded genuinely happy to have Xavier Smith back. He called him “X-Man” and said, “It’s good to be able to get X-Man back, familiarity with our system.”

That familiarity matters. With only a few practices before Arizona, the Rams needed someone who already knows the offense and special teams.

McVay also talked about Jordan Whittington going on injured reserve. He said, “It’s a bummer we had to put Jordan Whittington on injured reserve. He’s been pushing through with his back.” He added that the goal is to “get some clarity for him and figure out how he starts feeling better and being able to do the things that he’s able to do at a high level.”

That’s a kind way to describe it. Whittington had been playing through the back injury, and the Rams decided to shut him down to let him heal. Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak suggested earlier this week that Whittington’s season may be over because of the IR math, but McVay didn’t say that. He talked about getting him healthy.

Part five: the Davante Adams timeline

This is the part of the press conference I’ve been waiting for.

On Monday, McVay wouldn’t give a date for Adams. He said he was “definitely concerned” and was trying to live “one day at a time.” On Wednesday, he was much more positive.

First, he said: “What we are optimistic is Davante’s return is sooner than later, which is a big deal to us.”

Then a reporter asked how far off Adams is, and McVay said: “I would think after Green Bay is a realistic timeline for him. But he looks great. I talked to him the other day, and he’s feeling good, and excited whenever that time is to get him back.”

So here’s the schedule. The Rams play Arizona this Sunday. Then they have their bye. Then they play Green Bay. So “after Green Bay” means Adams could be back for the first game in December, or around then.

That lines up with the original timeline. Adams got hurt on October 18th, and McVay said six to ten weeks. “After Green Bay” puts him in that window, closer to the shorter end.

And McVay talked about something interesting: return to play versus return to performance. A reporter asked whether 80 percent of Davante Adams could still help, given how thin the receiver room is. McVay said the main question is always whether a player could “further risk injury,” and that the answer to that is always no. But if a player is just not feeling 100 percent, and “there’s no risk for further injury,” they’ll “explore those things.”

He also said veteran players like Adams understand their bodies better: “He’s got great awareness, great understanding of how he physically feels. He’s a guy that’s played at such a high level for such a long period of time, but there’s been some games that he probably didn’t feel great that he was still able to figure it out and perform.”

So here’s how I read that. The Rams aren’t going to rush Adams back. But once he’s medically cleared, they might play him before he’s at full speed, because even a less-than-100-percent Adams would help this offense a lot.

Part six: the Terrance Ferguson situation

This one’s a little technical, but it matters.

Ferguson, the young tight end, hurt his hamstring in practice last Friday after he’d just been designated to return from injured reserve. McVay called it “unpredictable,” and explained why it hurts: “Losing a big-time guy for us that is really an extension of that passing game, whether he’s a tight end or a receiver. It’s very similar for us.”

When asked about his timeline, McVay said, “It probably is a couple weeks at least.”

And then he explained the roster problem. Ferguson’s 21-day practice window has already started. “Our goal is, by the time his 21-day clock expires, to hopefully be able to have a spot to keep him on the active roster. Because if you don’t activate him by the time that 21-day clock expires, then he would go on IR for the rest of the season. And that is not what our hope is.”

McVay said he believes the clock runs out around the Green Bay game. And he said even if Ferguson can’t play against Green Bay, they’re “hopeful to save a roster spot for him.”

So that’s another roster puzzle. The Rams need to keep a spot open for Ferguson, keep Puka on the active roster while he heals, and still field a team. That’s why McVay said this week has been more about “roster mechanics” than any he can remember.

Part seven: the McDuffie update

Some good news here. McVay said about Trent McDuffie: “I think he’s feeling good. I think we’re optimistic that this week he will be able to go.”

He explained why a back injury is tricky for a cornerback. “You got these muscles around it in the back with just some of the movement, especially at his position.” And he talked about the specific things McDuffie has to be able to do: “the functional movements and some of the unpredictability of when you’re mirroring, redirecting, your lateral quickness, your short space.”

McVay also gave McDuffie some love: “He’s so damn tough.” And: “He’s a physical player. We play him outside and inside.”

He finished with, “Hopefully this is the week.”

That’s huge for the Rams. With Jaylen Watson out for the season, McDuffie is the most important player in the secondary. If he’s back, the Rams can move Josh Wallace and Emmanuel Forbes back into more comfortable roles. If not, they’ll be thin again against Arizona.

Part eight: the Stafford collaboration

Here’s where the “Stafford decision” part of the headline comes from. A reporter asked what the collaboration between McVay and Matthew Stafford looks like as they get ready to play without Puka.

McVay said, “Matthew’s been great.” He explained that it’s really about “what is the best way to maximize the group that we have. What are the personnel groupings? Where are the different players that we can get involved? How do we want to run it, throw it? What are some of the plans?”

He walked through how they install a game plan during the week: “We introduce the game plan in incremental steps. Today is a big emphasis on normal down and distance. So you’re talking about first and second down, and the different personnel groupings that you’d activate, both in the run and the pass.”

And then: “Here’s who we envision in these spots, and it’ll be a great opportunity for some new guys to get chances that they haven’t gotten yet.”

So there’s no “bombshell decision” from Stafford. It’s a quarterback and a head coach figuring out how to run an offense without their top two receivers. And that last line is important. “New guys” getting “chances that they haven’t gotten yet” means we might see players on Sunday we haven’t seen much of this season. CJ Daniels, Xavier Smith, maybe more tight end snaps.

Part nine: the two-back idea, and Bill Parcells

One reporter asked a fun question: with the receiver room this thin, would McVay consider a two-back offense, or even a T formation?

McVay didn’t rule anything out. “I think you explore everything.” He said, “It’s really our job to figure out how do you maximize the pieces and the players that you do have.”

And then he said something I loved: “I always respect the people the most from afar that make the most out of whatever circumstances they are. They’re putting players in positions to be successful, to allow their skill set to shine, but not pigeonhole them into, you know, they can only do this.”

Later, when asked how much the Rams need to lean on the run game with Kyren Williams and Blake Corum, McVay said it’s “really valuable” to have both backs. And he quoted Bill Parcells: “There’s a way to win all of them.” He said, “What that looks like doesn’t have to be the same week in and week out. And I think that’s especially applicable to us when you do have the amount of moving parts.”

That’s the key to the Rams right now. There’s no one formula. Against the Chargers, it was defense and Puka. Against Washington, it was Garrett, the run game, and Puka. Against Arizona, without Puka, it’ll have to be something else.

And the run game has been carrying them. Kyren and Corum combined for 192 rushing yards against the Raiders, 129 against the Chargers, and 124 against Washington. Expect two-back sets, more tight ends, and a lot of handoffs.

Part ten: the Robert Woods joke

This was the funniest moment of the press conference.

A reporter asked whether McVay had talked to Robert Woods about coming back. Woods is a former Rams receiver, and apparently he’s been around the facility.

McVay said, “I have. Yes. I’ve looked at him, and the way that he looks out there, I’ve definitely been tempted.” Then he laughed: “I think that’d be a violation of the CBA, or he’d have to, I don’t know what he’d have to do. But he looks damn good, and he still is smooth out there. So, very tempting.”

To be clear, that’s a joke. Nobody is reporting that Robert Woods is coming back. But it tells you how thin the receiver room is when the head coach is joking about un-retiring a former player he sees around the building.

Part eleven: Aaron Donald is having fun

One more great moment. A reporter mentioned that McVay had said Aaron Donald looked like he was having a lot of fun on the field, and asked for examples.

McVay said, “I thought he was having a lot of fun, particularly in the game the other day.” He pointed to “something subtle,” like Donald’s reaction after Myles Garrett got a sack: “The smile, you know. When you know somebody and you pay close enough attention, you know what it looks like when their energy is in alignment with enjoying themselves.”

And then: “He’s not so different than me. He’s not so hard to read. You can tell when he’s having fun and when he’s not.” And he finished with, “That’s always enjoyable as a coach, to be able to see people you love and care about enjoy themselves. And he’s playing great.”

That connects to what Donald said last week, when he admitted the season “ain’t what I expected, but I’m enjoying it.” With Garrett back, Donald finally has the partner he came out of retirement to play with. Garrett had three sacks against Washington, and McVay said Monday that Donald “had a couple awesome things that allowed him to be able to get some one-on-ones.”

Part twelve: can the defense lead the way?

The final question, which got cut off in the clip, was whether the Rams, a franchise known for explosive offense under McVay, might lean on the defense to get through this stretch.

Here’s my answer, and this is opinion, not McVay’s. Yes, absolutely. The defense has held the Chargers to 9 points and Washington to 13. Garrett is back, Donald is playing great, Byron Young and Josaiah Stewart have been strong on the edge, and McDuffie might return. This is the best the Rams’ defense has looked all season.

And the offense is the most short-handed it’s been all season. No Puka, no Adams, no Whittington, no Ferguson, and maybe no Avila. So for the next few weeks, the defense is going to have to be the leader. That’s not a weakness. It’s just this team’s reality right now.

What I liked, and what worried me

Here’s my honest read on the whole press conference.

What I liked.

The Adams timeline. “After Green Bay” is the first real date McVay has given us since the surgery, and he said Adams “looks great.” That’s the single best piece of news for this offense.

The McDuffie update. “Optimistic that this week he will be able to go” is about as positive as McVay gets before a Friday injury report.

The honesty about the deadline. “It kind of takes two” isn’t an excuse. It’s the truth about how trades work. The Rams looked. The right deal wasn’t there.

The Parcells mindset. “There’s a way to win all of them” is exactly the right approach for a team this banged up. Don’t force the old offense. Find a new way each week.

And Donald having fun. When a future Hall of Famer comes out of retirement and his coach says “you can tell when he’s having fun,” and he is, that matters for the whole locker room.

What worried me.

The number of DNPs. Avila, Puka, McDuffie, Curl, and Ferguson all sat out Wednesday. That’s a lot of talent on the sideline.

The roster crunch. When a head coach says he’s spent more time on “roster mechanics” this week than he can remember, that tells you how stretched this team is. Keeping Puka on the 53 while he can’t play, saving a spot for Ferguson, and managing IR return slots all at once is a lot.

The receiver room for Sunday. Mumpfield, Atwell, Daniels, and Smith have to carry the wide receiver load. That’s a group with very little production this season.

And Ferguson’s clock. If he isn’t ready by the time his 21-day window closes, McVay said he’d go on IR for the rest of the season. That would be a real loss for a passing game that’s already thin.

The road map for the next four weeks

Let’s put all of McVay’s timelines together, because for the first time, we can actually map out the next month.

This Sunday: at Arizona. No Puka. No Adams. No Whittington. No Ferguson. Probably no Avila. McDuffie hopefully back.

The bye week. Rest and recovery for everybody. Avila, Curl, and McDuffie all benefit.

Green Bay. Ferguson’s 21-day clock runs out around then, per McVay. Adams likely still out, since McVay said “after Green Bay.” Puka still out.

The first game after Green Bay. That’s McVay’s “realistic timeline” for Adams. If that holds, Stafford gets his veteran receiver back.

Mid-December. If Monday’s “at least a month” estimate holds, that’s when Puka could start getting close. And remember, the Rams play the 49ers in San Francisco on December 13th.

So the next two games, Arizona and Green Bay, are the hardest stretch for this offense. Get through those two, and the Rams should start getting their weapons back for the run to the playoffs.

That’s why Sunday matters so much. A win in Arizona means 6-4 at the bye, with the offense about to get healthier. A loss means 5-5, with a tough Green Bay game coming. Big difference.

Part thirteen: Arizona preview

The Rams play at Arizona on Sunday, then get their bye.

Arizona beat the Rams 18-16 on October 18th, the game where Adams got hurt. Now the Rams go back with an even thinner offense.

What I expect from the Rams:

A heavy run game with Kyren and Corum, probably with more two-back sets.

More tight end targets: Tyler Higbee, Davis Allen, and Colby Parkinson if healthy.

New faces getting chances, like McVay said. CJ Daniels and Xavier Smith, plus Konata Mumpfield and Tutu Atwell.

Garrett with a bigger snap count, and a defense that tries to win the game.

My prediction: Rams 20, Cardinals 16. Close, physical, and decided by the defense. And the Rams go into the bye at 6-4 with four straight wins.
