Let's start with who Eric Kendricks is, because some younger fans might only know the name. Kendricks was a second-round pick by the Minnesota Vikings back in 2015, and he spent his first eight seasons in Minnesota. In 2019, he was named a first-team All-Pro and made the Pro Bowl. First-team All-Pro means that in that season, he was recognized among the very best at his position in the entire NFL. Not a good linebacker. An elite one. He was the brain of the Vikings' defense for nearly a decade — the guy who got everyone lined up, made the calls, and was always around the football.

And at his peak, Kendricks wasn't just a tackling machine. In that All-Pro season, he was known as one of the best coverage linebackers in football — a guy who could carry tight ends down the seam, break up passes, and erase the middle of the field. That's a rare skill set for a linebacker, and it's why Minnesota built its defense around him for so long. Age has taken some of that away, as we'll talk about in a minute. But the instincts, the eyes, the understanding of where the ball is going — those things don't disappear at 34.

After Minnesota, the journey started. He played for the Chargers in 2023 — right here in Los Angeles, at SoFi Stadium. Then the Cowboys in 2024, and that's the season people forget about. In Dallas, Kendricks put up 138 combined tackles — the second-most of his entire career — and a career-high three forced fumbles. At 32 years old. That's not a guy hanging on. That's a guy who could still run a defense. Over his career, he's piled up roughly 1,180 combined tackles, 21 and a half sacks, eight forced fumbles and seven fumble recoveries. Those are the numbers of a player who has been on the field, every week, for more than a decade.

And think about that Chargers year for a second, because it matters more than you'd expect. Kendricks has already played a full season of home games at SoFi Stadium. He knows the building. He knows the Los Angeles routine — the traffic, the travel, the West Coast schedule. When a veteran joins a new team midseason, the little things can slow him down. For Kendricks, at least some of those little things are already familiar.

And then came last season — the part of the story that makes this signing so interesting. In late November of 2025, the 49ers were in trouble at linebacker. Fred Warner, the heart of their defense, was out for the season. His replacement, Tatum Bethune, was dealing with a high ankle sprain. San Francisco needed someone who could step in immediately, and on November 26th, they signed Eric Kendricks to their practice squad. Sound familiar? He worked his way up to the active roster, played three regular-season games with one start, and then — when the playoffs came — he started both of San Francisco's postseason games. Nineteen total tackles. Three tackles for loss. And one of those starts was a playoff win over the Philadelphia Eagles — the same Eagles team the Rams just beat on Sunday.

Think about that timeline. Signed to a practice squad in late November. Starting a playoff game in January. That's roughly seven weeks from "insurance policy" to "starting middle linebacker in the postseason." The 49ers didn't sign him expecting him to start in the playoffs — they signed him because they needed a veteran who could be ready if things went wrong. Things went wrong, and he was ready. That's exactly the blueprint the Rams are copying. The best depth signings aren't the ones that make headlines in October. They're the ones that save your season in December and January.

So when I say the Rams signed an experienced linebacker, I don't mean a guy who's been sitting on a couch since 2019. I mean a guy who, just months ago, was starting playoff football in the NFC, in the NFC West, for the team the Rams are chasing in the division.

Now — the real question. Why now? Why would the Rams sign a 34-year-old linebacker to the practice squad six days before Monday Night Football?

Let's start with the obvious answer: depth. The Rams' linebacker room has been dealing with injuries — Nate Landman has been banged up, and every team in the league knows what October does to a roster. The bumps and bruises from September turn into real injuries. Players who were supposed to be depth suddenly have to play real snaps. And the Rams are not walking into an easy stretch. Buffalo on Monday night. A division race where the 49ers are 4-0. A season with Super Bowl expectations. In that situation, what kind of player do you want sitting on your practice squad, ready to go? Not a guy who's never seen NFL game speed. A guy who's started playoff games.

And here's what one Rams beat writer said about him, which I think is the most accurate description I've read: "Not the player he was, but a reliable tackler." That's exactly it. The Rams don't need 2019 Eric Kendricks. They need a veteran who can step in if injuries hit, line everyone up, and make the tackle in front of him. And in a game against Josh Allen — a quarterback who turns missed tackles into 30-yard runs — reliable tackling is worth its weight in gold.

Now let's talk about the part that's going to get people talking: he just came from the 49ers. Some fans' minds go straight to the conspiracy: "He's bringing the 49ers' playbook! He's giving away their secrets!" Let's slow down. Defensive players don't walk around with a copy of another team's playbook, and NFL teams change their calls and terminology all the time. That's not what this is. But there is real value in the experience. Kendricks spent a playoff run inside one of the best defensive cultures in the NFC. He knows how San Francisco prepares, how their linebackers communicate, how a contending team handles the grind of January. That's not a secret — it's perspective. And when the Rams see the 49ers again later this season, having a linebacker who has lived in that building isn't going to hurt.

And here's the other side of that coin: San Francisco didn't bring him back, and the Rams picked him up. In a division where every edge matters, the Rams are adding a player who knows what winning playoff football in the NFC West feels like. That's a quiet, subtle win.

And the division race makes every one of those small edges matter. The 49ers are 4-0. The Seahawks are 3-1. The Rams are 2-2, and on Sunday San Francisco goes to Seattle in a game that could reshape the NFC West before the Rams even take the field on Monday night. In a division this tight, the difference between first place and a wild card spot can come down to one game in December — and one game in December can come down to whether your backup linebacker is ready when the starter goes down. That's the level of detail contenders play at.

Now let me be fair, because I promised you honesty. What can't Eric Kendricks give the Rams? Speed is the first thing. At 34, he's not going to run sideline to sideline like a 25-year-old. And coverage has been the biggest weakness in his recent seasons. In 2024 with Dallas, he allowed a passer rating of about 110 when he was targeted in coverage — and against offenses that attack linebackers with tight ends and running backs in space, that's a real concern. So if you're picturing Kendricks covering Buffalo's pass catchers one-on-one on Monday night, that's not the role. His value is in the run game, in communication, in tackling, and possibly on special teams.

And the practice squad label matters. He's not on the 53-man roster. For him to play in a game, the Rams would have to elevate him for that game day — and teams can only do that a limited number of times for each player before they have to sign him to the active roster. So this isn't a promise of snaps. It's an option. An insurance policy the Rams can cash in the moment they need it.

And there's a reason the Rams went the practice squad route instead of putting him straight on the 53. Roster spots are tight on a contender. Signing him to the active roster would have meant cutting someone else — maybe a special teams contributor, maybe a young player the staff wants to keep developing. The practice squad lets the Rams have it both ways: they keep their current roster intact, and they get a veteran in the building who can learn the defense at his own pace. The risk, of course, is that another team could sign him off the practice squad to its active roster. But a 34-year-old California kid who just signed with a contender sitting at the top of the Super Bowl odds board probably isn't looking for a reason to leave.

But here's why I think that insurance policy matters more than people realize. Think about what a linebacker actually does before the snap. When the offense shifts, motions or changes formation, somebody has to see it, process it, and get everyone on the same page before the ball is snapped. Communication. Alignment. Recognizing the formation. Knowing the down and distance, what the offense likes to do in that situation, what's coming next. Young linebackers can have all the athleticism in the world, but the mental side takes years. A veteran who's done it thousands of times doesn't just know the answers. He knows the questions before they're asked.

And a lot of games turn on exactly those moments. A busted coverage. A missed assignment. A defender running to the wrong gap. Nobody puts "he got everybody lined up correctly" on a highlight reel. But the plays a veteran helps prevent can be more valuable than the plays he makes. Even from the practice squad, Kendricks is in the meeting rooms, on the practice field, showing younger players what preparation looks like at the highest level.

Now let's zoom out to the defense as a whole, because the context matters. Chris Shula's defense isn't broken — it's actually been one of the best in the league by the numbers. Through four weeks, the Rams rank third in the NFL in opponent EPA per play, fifth in opponent success rate, and top ten against the run. In Philadelphia, they shut out the Eagles in the second half and held them to zero-for-twelve on third down. But look at who they've been missing. Aaron Donald is questionable for Monday with his back. Myles Garrett is on injured reserve. Jaylen Watson is working back from his shoulder. This defense has been winning with its young players and its depth. That's exactly why depth moves matter. When you have a good defense playing short-handed, the worst thing that can happen is one more injury at the wrong spot exposing a player who isn't ready. Kendricks is a hedge against exactly that.

So what should Rams fans watch for this week? Three things. First, the injury report — especially the linebacker spots and Nate Landman's status. If the Rams' linebacker room gets thinner, the chances of Kendricks being elevated go way up. Second, Aaron Donald's availability. If Donald plays, the front gets stronger and the linebackers get cleaner lanes to the football — which reduces the need for emergency depth. Third, the game-day elevations, which usually get announced the day before the game. If Kendricks' name shows up there, you'll know the coaching staff believes he's already picked up enough of the defense to help. If it doesn't, that's fine too. Insurance is supposed to sit in the drawer until you need it.

And this fits a pattern with this front office. Les Snead and Sean McVay have never been the type to stand still. Remember 2021. In the middle of that season, the Rams traded for Von Miller and signed Odell Beckham Jr. — two veterans who became key parts of a Super Bowl run. Those were blockbuster moves. This one is much smaller. But the mindset is the same: when you have a championship-level roster, you don't wait for problems to become crises. You keep adding experience, keep adding options, keep preparing for January while everybody else is focused on October.

And I love the timing for another reason. The Rams just won a huge, dramatic game in Philadelphia. Plenty of teams would exhale after a comeback like that and say, "We're back, we're fine." Instead, the Rams went out two days later and looked for another way to get better. McVay made it clear after the Eagles game that there's still a lot to clean up.
