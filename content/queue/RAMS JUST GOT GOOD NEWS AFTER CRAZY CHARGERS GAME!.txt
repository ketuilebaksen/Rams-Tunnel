Rams House, for once I get to say it without any asterisks: the headline is true. The Rams did get good news. They beat the Chargers 24-9 at SoFi Stadium on Sunday, they’re now 4-4, and they’ve won two straight games for the first time since January, according to the AP. But “crazy” is the right word for how they got there. The Rams went five straight drives without a first down to start the game. They trailed 9-3 late in the second quarter. And then everything flipped — 21 unanswered points, two interceptions by the defense and a shutout in the second half. Now, quick heads-up: the video going around with this headline spends most of its time on a CBS studio show talking about the Bills and Ravens. I’m going to focus on the Rams — but I’ll also tell you why what happened to the Ravens actually matters for the Rams this week. So today, I’ll break down the slow start, the turnaround, the defense that carried this team, the injuries to watch, where the Rams stand in the NFC West, and what this win means for the trade deadline. Let’s start with the ugly part, because it was really ugly.

According to The Sporting Tribune, the Rams’ first five drives produced zero first downs. Their first 15 plays gained just 24 yards. And according to the same report, that was a first for a McVay offense in an opening stretch of a game. According to Pro Football Talk, the Rams had just 10 rushing yards at halftime.

And there were mistakes. According to the Rams’ official website, Matthew Stafford threw an interception to Chargers cornerback Tarheeb Still on a pass intended for Konata Mumpfield, and it came right after a Tyler Higbee false start. According to The Sporting Tribune, that was on the Rams’ second possession.

Meanwhile, the Chargers were moving the ball, but they kept stalling out. According to the Rams’ official website, Chargers kicker Cameron Dicker hit field goals from 34, 46 and 41 yards, and the Chargers led 9-3 in the second quarter.

The only reason the Rams had any points at all was the defense. According to the Rams’ official website, safety Quentin Lake intercepted Justin Herbert and returned it 46 yards to the Chargers’ 19-yard line. The AP had the return at 45 yards. Either way, it set up a 30-yard Brandon McManus field goal for the Rams’ first points.

McVay was honest about the first half. According to The Sporting Tribune, he said: “It’s really hard, you know, to even establish any sort of momentum or rhythm.” And according to the Rams’ official website, he said the offense never really had a chance to get into a rhythm early.

If you were stressed watching that first half, drop “five drives” in the comments. I know I was.

Now the turning point — and it came out of nowhere.

According to Pro Football Talk, the Rams didn’t get their first first down until 1:26 left in the first half. And it was a big one: a 40-yard completion from Stafford to Tutu Atwell up the left sideline. According to The Sporting Tribune, the Rams were in no-huddle on that drive. A few plays later, Kyren Williams scored on a 1-yard run with 19 seconds left in the half, and the Rams took a 10-9 lead into halftime.

Stafford said after the game, according to The Sporting Tribune: “That two-minute drive was a big boost for us.” And think about who made the big play: Tutu Atwell. A lot of people, including me, have been saying the Rams need more from their receivers not named Puka. Atwell answered with the biggest catch of the game.

And there’s a story behind that. According to the Rams’ official transactions page, the Rams traded running back Jarquez Hunter to the Dolphins this year to bring Atwell back. A lot of fans wondered why. On Sunday, with the offense completely stuck and the clock running down in the first half, Atwell made the play that flipped the game. Sometimes the move that gets the least attention ends up mattering the most.

And then the second half was a completely different game.

According to the Rams’ official website, Blake Corum scored on a 23-yard run to open the second half, making it 17-9. On that drive alone, Corum carried the ball five times for 55 yards. That’s the kind of drive that changes the feel of a game. The Rams came out of the locker room and just ran right at the Chargers.

Then the defense struck again. According to the Rams’ official website, Trent McDuffie intercepted a Herbert pass intended for tight end Charlie Kolar and returned it 26 yards to the Chargers’ 22-yard line. Shortly after — according to the Rams’ website, with 3:41 left in the third quarter — Stafford hit Puka Nacua on an 18-yard screen pass for a touchdown. 24-9.

According to Pro Football Talk, that was 21 straight points for the Rams. And the Rams went from 10 rushing yards in the first half to 129 in the second half.

The Chargers had one last chance. According to the Rams’ official website, they drove to the Rams’ 24-yard line with under seven minutes left, but the Rams stopped them on fourth down. Game over.

McVay summed it up perfectly. According to The Sporting Tribune, he said: “It’s not really how you start, it’s how you finish.”

Now, let me be honest about what that slow start tells us, because I don’t want to just celebrate and ignore it. Before the game, Stafford said the Chargers’ defense “runs around, flies around” and that they “mix their personnel really well.” He was right. The Chargers’ defense is better than its record. But five drives without a first down is still a problem, and it’s the kind of problem that loses games against better teams. Last week, McVay said the key to the Raiders win was being “efficient on early downs.” On Sunday, the Rams weren’t efficient on early downs in the first half, and the whole offense stalled. That’s my own read on it — and it’s the first thing I’d want fixed before Washington.

The good news is what happened when they went no-huddle. The two-minute drive gave the offense rhythm, and the Rams carried that into the second half. Don’t be surprised if McVay uses more tempo early in games going forward. When this offense gets going, it’s one of the best in the league — according to NFL.com, the Rams came into this game first in the NFL in yards per game.

Now let’s go through the individual numbers.

Matthew Stafford: according to Pro Football Talk, 17-of-25 for 157 yards, one touchdown and one interception. Not a huge day, but efficient after the slow start. And Stafford said there’s still work to do. According to the Rams’ official website, he said: “Still some stuff in the second half that we can clean up as always.”

Blake Corum: 11 carries, 73 yards and a touchdown, according to Pro Football Talk.

Kyren Williams: 16 carries, 56 yards and a touchdown.

So for the second straight week, both running backs scored. Last week against the Raiders, according to the AP, Kyren and Corum both ran for over 90 yards and a touchdown. This week, they did the damage when it mattered most. Remember what Stafford said about them this week: “I think you feel really comfortable with both those guys doing everything we ask of a halfback in this offense.”

And Corum deserves a special mention. Two weeks ago, a lot of fans saw him as just Kyren’s backup. Now he’s scored in back-to-back weeks, and his 23-yard touchdown run was the play that put the Rams ahead for good in the second half. After the Raiders game, according to the LA Times, Corum said: “It feels great being able to have a partner back there like Kyren.” That partnership is becoming one of the best things about this team. When one back gets tired, the other comes in fresh — and defenses don’t get a break.

Puka Nacua: 11 catches for 80 yards and a touchdown on 12 targets, according to Pro Football Talk. And he moved up the Rams’ all-time list. According to The Sporting Tribune, Puka now has 369 career receptions, passing Robert Woods for ninth in Rams history, and he has 4,756 receiving yards through just 50 games. That’s incredible production.

Puka also had the line of the day. According to ESPN, he said: “This is something that we’ve got to build upon, but it’s not something we can exhale from.” In other words: we won, but we can’t get comfortable.

On the Chargers’ side, according to Pro Football Talk, Herbert went 24-of-32 for 257 yards with two interceptions and no touchdowns. Ladd McConkey had four catches for 86 yards. According to the AP, the Chargers finished with 295 total yards but didn’t score a touchdown for the second straight game. They’re now 0-7, still the NFL’s only winless team.

Herbert was honest about what went wrong. According to ESPN, he said: “I think the turnovers are what’s stopping us.” And according to ESPN, Herbert has now thrown an interception in all seven games this season, and the Chargers haven’t scored a touchdown in their last nine quarters. Jim Harbaugh, according to the AP, said: “There’s no quit,” and “There’s no surrender.” You have to respect that. But turnovers are killing the Chargers right now, and the Rams took full advantage.

And a fun piece of history: according to ESPN, the Rams have now won two of the three games between the two Los Angeles teams since both moved back to L.A.

Now let’s talk about the real star of this game: the Rams’ defense.

Two interceptions. Zero touchdowns allowed. A fourth-down stop in the fourth quarter. And according to the Rams’ official website, the Chargers were held scoreless in the second half.

According to The Sporting Tribune, Herbert was under pressure on 17 of his 37 dropbacks. That’s a big number for a defense that came into the game ranked 27th in sacks, according to NFL.com, and that’s still playing without Myles Garrett, Poona Ford and Kobie Turner.

And remember what we talked about before the game. McVay said this week that turnover margin is one of the stats that matters most. According to the Rams’ official website, the Chargers came into the game with the most giveaways in the NFL, and Herbert had thrown an interception in every game. The Rams took advantage — two interceptions, and both turned into points.

McVay gave out two game balls after the game, according to Yahoo Sports — one to Quentin Lake and one to Trent McDuffie, for their interceptions. In his victory speech, according to Yahoo Sports, McVay said: “I cannot say enough about the way our defense started out that game.” And according to the Rams’ official website, he said: “They just kept getting stop after stop.”

Lake explained his interception like this, according to The Sporting Tribune: “Sometimes, you know, if you get a good key, just take your shot.”

And Lake is a great story. According to the Rams’ official transactions page, the Rams signed him to a three-year extension, and according to Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak, a later restructure of his deal freed up 3.4 million dollars in cap space. So Lake has been valuable to this team both on the field and on the salary cap. On Sunday, he was the player who kept the Rams alive when the offense couldn’t do anything.

And Stafford gave the defense full credit. According to the Rams’ official website, he said the defense kept the team in the game early and that the offense owed them, because both interceptions led to points.

Here’s a stat I love: according to the Rams’ official website, the defense has now gone two straight home games without allowing a touchdown. Last week McVay told reporters the defense had gone about seven quarters without giving up a touchdown. This defense is starting to build an identity.

Now, I have to mention the funniest moment of the game. According to the California Post, via Yahoo Sports, in the third quarter, linebacker Wesley Bailey sacked Herbert and forced a fumble that bounced right in front of Aaron Donald. Donald went for it — and tripped over his own feet, and the ball rolled out of bounds. According to the report, the FOX broadcast showed Donald lying on the ground, smiling. And his teammates pretended to revive him on the sideline.

McVay loved it. According to the California Post, he said: “Oh man, I’m going to give him such a hard time about that.” He said he could tell Donald was thinking, “I’m going to scoop and score.” And he added: “His face, he couldn’t even look at me and make eye contact after that.” And then: “But at least we can laugh about it right now.”

That’s what winning does. When you lose, a play like that becomes a talking point. When you win by 15, it becomes a locker room joke.

Now let’s talk about special teams, because we said before the game this was a big question.

According to the Rams’ official website, Joe Cardona was inactive with his ankle injury, and Jake McQuaide handled the long snapping. There were no snap disasters, which is exactly what you want. McManus handled the field goals and extra points.

But here’s an interesting detail. According to the Rams’ official website, Joshua Karty was elevated from the practice squad and handled kickoffs. And it was mixed. Two of his kickoffs fell short, which gave the Chargers the ball at their own 40-yard line. But the two that reached the landing zone weren’t returned past the 30. So the Rams are clearly experimenting with how to handle kickoffs, and it’s still a work in progress.

So no disasters, but not perfect either. And the Chargers’ three field goals in the first half came partly because they kept getting decent field position. That’s something to clean up before Washington.

Now the injuries, because this is the part of the “good news” story that could turn into bad news.

According to the Rams’ official website, cornerback Jaylen Watson left in the third quarter with a shoulder injury and was ruled out. And Trent McDuffie hurt his back late in the third quarter and was listed as questionable to return. According to the AP, McDuffie stayed on the sideline. According to a postgame injury roundup, McDuffie said after the game he was “feeling good.”

So the Rams lost both of their starting cornerbacks in the same game. And this matters because Watson has dealt with a shoulder problem before. According to the Rams’ official website, earlier this season he played through a dislocated shoulder and still recorded his first interception as a Ram. We’ll have to wait for McVay’s Monday press conference for an update.

McDuffie’s situation is especially worth watching. He had just returned from a groin injury that cost him two games. If his back is fine, as he said, that’s great. But if either cornerback misses time, the Rams’ secondary gets very thin very quickly — right before a trade deadline.

If you’re worried about the cornerback room, drop “CB depth” in the comments.

And just to put it in perspective: the Rams’ defense has been one of the few constants during a season full of injuries. Garrett hasn’t played. Ford is out for the year. Turner is on injured reserve. Landman missed time with a concussion. McDuffie missed two games. And yet, this defense has carried the team through two straight wins. If the cornerbacks are healthy, the Rams’ defense could get even better when Garrett and Turner come back. If they aren’t, the next two weeks will test the depth of this group like nothing before.

Now, one more fun story from after the game. According to Yahoo Sports, McVay was asked whether he considers the Chargers a rival. He said, according to Gary Klein of the LA Times: “Kevin Demoff does. I don’t.” Demoff, the Rams’ president, celebrated the win on X with a GIF of Will Ferrell’s Ron Burgundy saying: “That’s amazing.” So at least one person in the Rams’ building enjoyed beating the neighbors a little extra.

Now let’s look at the NFC West, because this win matters in the standings.

According to Yahoo Sports, the 49ers were on a bye in Week 8, so they’re still 6-1. The Seahawks were 4-3 heading into their Monday night game against the Bears. The Rams are now 4-4. And the Cardinals beat the Cowboys 33-30 in overtime on Sunday, according to CBS Texas, so they’re 3-5.

So the Rams are still third, but they’re climbing. Think about where this team was just two weeks ago: 2-4, coming off a loss, with Davante Adams freshly injured and people writing the season off. Two wins later, they’re at .500 with a manageable stretch ahead. After Washington, the Rams play Arizona, and then they have their bye before facing the Packers the night before Thanksgiving. Right now, they’re half a game behind Seattle heading into the Seahawks’ Monday night game against the Bears. San Francisco still has a big lead, with three fewer losses than the Rams, but there’s a lot of football left. And according to Last Word on Sports, their next two opponents after the Chargers — Washington and Arizona — both had losing records when the Rams started this stretch.

And here’s a side note from that Cardinals-Cowboys game. The Cowboys blew a two-touchdown lead in the fourth quarter, according to CBS Texas, and fell to 3-5. Remember the George Pickens trade rumors from a couple of weeks ago? ESPN’s Ben Solak wrote that if the Cowboys “keep losing games,” he’d call about Pickens. Dallas just lost again. That doesn’t mean Pickens is coming to the Rams — the money still doesn’t work, with the Rams at about 206,000 dollars in cap space, according to Athlon Sports. But it’s worth watching how Dallas approaches the deadline.

Now, about that Bills-Ravens game the original video spent so much time on. Here’s why it matters for the Rams.

According to a postgame NFL injury roundup, Ravens quarterback Lamar Jackson was hurt on Baltimore’s first offensive play, and he’ll be placed on injured reserve and have surgery on his right thumb. Ravens coach Jesse Minter was asked whether it could be season-ending, and he said: “Potentially. We’ll see where it goes.” Backup Tyler Huntley also left with a toe injury. The Bills won 36-10, and the Ravens are now 4-4 after three straight losses.

Why does that matter for the Rams? Two reasons.

First, the playoff race. The Ravens are an AFC team, so they don’t directly compete with the Rams for a playoff spot. But the more good teams that struggle, the more the league’s balance shifts. That’s a small point.

Second — and this is the bigger one — the trade market. On ESPN’s list of top trade candidates, the Ravens were listed as a fit for A’Shawn Robinson, the former Rams defensive lineman, along with the Rams and Giants. If Baltimore’s season goes sideways without Lamar, they may be less likely to be aggressive buyers. That could leave fewer teams competing for players the Rams might want. That’s my own read, not a report — but it’s the kind of ripple effect that happens every year at the deadline.

So what does this win mean for the trade deadline?

According to Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak, Les Snead has historically made deadline moves on Monday or on deadline day itself. Grisak wrote that Monday after the Chargers game was the most realistic day for a Rams trade this week. So today is a day to watch.

And the win changes the conversation. At 4-4, with two straight wins, the Rams look like buyers, not sellers. But the needs may have shifted. Before the game, the talk was about a receiver like Josh Downs, who requested a trade according to ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler, and a defensive tackle like A’Shawn Robinson. Now, if Watson or McDuffie misses time, cornerback could suddenly jump to the top of the list.

But remember what McVay said last week. According to the LA Times, when he was asked about trade discussions, his first answer was “No.” According to the New York Post, he said: “Hopefully, some of those trades will be guys getting back from injury that haven’t played for us.” And with about 206,000 dollars in cap space, any move would need a restructure first.

The deadline is Tuesday, November 10th, at 4 p.m. Eastern. The Rams play at Washington on Sunday, November 8th. So the Rams will get one more game before they have to decide.

And that game won’t be easy. According to RotoWire, the Commanders beat the Eagles 27-10 on Sunday to improve to 2-4-1. Jayden Daniels went 16-of-29 for 217 yards and a touchdown to Stefon Diggs. So Washington is coming off its best win of the season, and it’ll be at home. According to ESPN’s game page, Rams-Commanders is set for 1 p.m. Eastern on FOX next Sunday.

Here’s what I’ll say about that matchup early. Daniels is a dual-threat quarterback, and the Rams’ defense will have to contain him without Garrett, unless his window opens this week. If Watson or McDuffie can’t play, Washington’s receivers — including Diggs, a former Pro Bowler — will be a real test for the Rams’ backup cornerbacks. And the Rams can’t afford another five-drive start without a first down on the road. A strong start would go a long way.

Here’s what I’ll be watching this week. Number one: McVay’s injury update on Watson and McDuffie. Number two: whether the Rams finally open Myles Garrett’s practice window. Back in September, according to Pro Football Talk, NFL Network’s Ian Rapoport reported that the Washington game was Garrett’s most likely return date. And number three: any roster moves on Monday — especially a contract restructure, which would be a sign the Rams are clearing space for a trade.

So here’s my bottom line.

Yes, the Rams got good news. They beat the Chargers 24-9, they’re 4-4, and they’ve won two straight for the first time since January. The offense was awful for most of the first half — five drives without a first down — but a two-minute drive sparked by Tutu Atwell’s 40-yard catch changed everything. Corum, Kyren and Puka all scored. And the defense was the real hero: two interceptions, both turned into points, no touchdowns allowed and a shutout in the second half.

But there are things to watch. Both starting cornerbacks got hurt. The kickoff plan is still a work in progress. And the trade deadline is eight days away.

As McVay said, “It’s not really how you start, it’s how you finish.” Right now, the Rams are finishing games. And that’s how a 2-4 team becomes a playoff team.
