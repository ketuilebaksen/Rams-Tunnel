All three were limited in practice — which, for players coming off injuries, is exactly the step you want to see. So the headline is real: the Rams are trending toward getting a future Hall of Famer back in the middle of their defense and their starting cornerback back on the outside, just in time for Josh Allen and Monday Night Football. But here’s what most people are going to miss. The same Thursday injury report had a list of names that did NOT practice — and several of them are linebackers. And the player who might matter most in this whole story isn’t Donald or Watson at all. It’s a former undrafted free agent from Michigan who just earned the highest defensive grade on the team in Philadelphia. Let’s start in the middle of the defensive front, because that’s where everything starts. Aaron Donald missed the win in Philadelphia with back soreness. He didn’t practice at all last week. On Monday, McVay said the hope was that Donald would return for the Bills game, and that while he didn’t want to “pigeonhole” it, “that’s the direction that it looks like it’s trending in.” Then on Thursday came the next step: Donald was listed as limited in practice. And McVay said exactly what you’d want to hear: “Everything has been progressing in the right direction.” Asked whether the hope is that he plays Monday: “That’s what the hope is, yes.” And then the important part: “The fact that he’s limited is a good thing, and we’ll kind of just see how he responds.”

So let’s be disciplined about this. Donald is not cleared. He’s not confirmed. The final injury report comes out Saturday, and a back can flare up at any time. But the trend is clearly positive — a week ago he wasn’t practicing at all, and now he’s on the field in a limited role with the head coach saying the hope is he plays.

And let’s also be honest about where Donald is in his comeback. He’s played only a couple of games since coming out of retirement, and his box-score numbers have been modest. Nobody should expect the 2018 version of Aaron Donald to walk out of the tunnel on Monday night and record three sacks. That’s not what this is about. This is about what his presence forces an offense to do before the ball is even snapped.

Because here’s the thing about Aaron Donald: he changes how you have to block. When a guard or a center has to deal with him, the offensive line can’t play comfortably. They need help. And when you send help inside, you take it away from somewhere else — usually from the tackles who are trying to block Byron Young and Jared Verse. That’s how one player makes four players better. And against Josh Allen, that interior pressure is everything. Allen is at his most dangerous when he can step up into the pocket, reset his feet, and launch the ball downfield — or escape and improvise. If the middle of the pocket collapses, he can’t climb, and he can’t reset. You can sometimes run away from edge pressure. You can’t run away from a collapsing middle.

Now, the secondary. Jaylen Watson dislocated his shoulder in Denver and missed the Eagles game. On Monday, McVay said the goal and the optimism were that Watson would be able to practice, and that “if he feels good throughout the week in practice, the expectation is he’ll be ready to roll against the Bills.” On Thursday, the Rams put a shoulder harness on him for practice, and he was listed as limited. McVay confirmed the plan is to ramp him up as the week goes on.

Watson isn’t just another cornerback. The Rams invested in him this offseason, and he started the first three games of the season before the injury. That tells you where he sits in the plan. And against Buffalo, a healthy, physical outside corner matters. Josh Allen has one of the strongest arms in football. He’ll attack outside the numbers, push the ball down the field, and challenge your corners to win at the catch point. If you can trust your corners on the outside, Chris Shula can be aggressive with his pressure. If you can’t, you have to protect them with safety help — and then the offense finds a weakness somewhere else.

But here’s the twist — and this is my favorite part of the whole story. The Rams might not be coming back to the same secondary they had before. Because of Josh Wallace.

Who is Josh Wallace? He joined the Rams in 2024 as an undrafted free agent out of Michigan. No draft-day phone call. No signing bonus headlines. Just a chance. And according to his teammate Trent McDuffie, Wallace learned the safety spot, the cornerback spot and the nickel “star” position almost as soon as he arrived. Inside the building, they call him the team’s “Swiss-Army knife.” Last season, he made plays — McDuffie pointed to several interceptions and a near pick-six against Seattle. And in Denver this year, after a rough start to his day — a missed tackle and a pass interference flag on his first two snaps — he finished strong, including an interception.

Then came Philadelphia. With Watson out, Wallace stepped into the starting role. And according to Pro Football Focus, he graded out at 82.0 — the highest grade on the entire Rams defense. He was targeted three times and allowed one catch for two yards. Two yards. On the road, in the rain, in a game the Rams had to win. Chris Shula described him perfectly this week: “just unfazed.” Smart. A true professional the team trusts.

Now, let’s be careful, because I’m not here to invent news. Wallace hasn’t won a starting job. Nobody has announced a depth chart change. If Watson is healthy, the logical expectation is that he reclaims his role. But look at what Wallace’s performance does to the picture. First, it gives McVay and Shula a real option if Watson’s shoulder isn’t 100 percent — and with a harness on that shoulder, it might not be. Second, it opens the door for rotation. You can keep corners fresh, you can use specific packages, you can match up differently. Third, it gives Shula more ways to disguise coverages against a quarterback who reads defenses as well as anyone. That’s the kind of problem every defensive coordinator wants: not “who do we have?” but “who do we choose?”

And the cornerback room as a whole looks deeper than it did a week ago. Emmanuel Forbes Jr. was targeted twice in Philadelphia and didn’t allow a single catch, and McVay said earlier this week that Forbes’ knee contusion isn’t a concern. Trent McDuffie had a tougher day by the grades, but he’s a proven cover corner who knows how to bounce back. Add Watson returning and Wallace playing at a high level, and suddenly the Rams have four corners they can trust. Against a quarterback who loves to spread defenses out with three and four receivers, that depth is a weapon.

Now — the part of the injury report that worries me. Because the good news on Donald and Watson came with some bad news in the same report. On Thursday, the Rams had several players who didn’t practice at all. Linebacker Nate Landman, with a shoulder. Linebacker Grant Stuard, with a hamstring. Linebacker Josaiah Stewart, with a groin injury. And safety Quentin Lake, the team captain, with a hip. Davante Adams also sat out, but that was a veteran rest day, not an injury.

And note something about Josaiah Stewart. He’s listed with the linebackers, but he’s part of the Rams’ edge rush rotation — one of the young pass rushers who helps keep Byron Young and Jared Verse fresh. If he can’t go, that rotation gets thinner, too. That’s another reason Aaron Donald’s possible return matters: when one part of the pass rush loses a body, a player like Donald can make up for it by drawing attention away from everyone else.

Look at that list again. Three linebackers and the captain of the secondary. If even a couple of those players can’t go on Monday night, the Rams’ defense has a real problem in the middle of the field — exactly where you need discipline against a scrambling quarterback like Josh Allen.

And this is where everything we talked about earlier this week suddenly connects. Remember the Eric Kendricks signing? The former first-team All-Pro linebacker the Rams added to the practice squad on Tuesday? At the time, it looked like smart depth for later in the season. Now, with Landman, Stuard and Stewart all missing practice on Thursday, it looks like the front office might have seen this coming. I’m not saying Kendricks will be elevated for Monday night. Nobody has said that. But if the linebacker room is thin when the final injury report comes out, a veteran who just started two playoff games last January is sitting right there. That’s what good roster building looks like: the move makes more sense every day.

And Quentin Lake’s status is just as important. Lake is the captain, the communicator, the guy who gets the secondary lined up. In Philadelphia, he made five tackles and generated pressure as a blitzer — though he also gave up Darius Cooper’s touchdown in coverage. If Lake can’t play, the back end loses its voice. And that’s another reason Josh Wallace’s versatility matters so much. Remember, Wallace has played safety. A player who can line up at corner, nickel or safety becomes incredibly valuable when injuries hit multiple spots in the same week.

Now let’s connect this to the players who are already carrying this defense. Because the Rams didn’t beat Philadelphia on hope. Byron Young led the team with seven pressures and two sacks on the Eagles’ final drive — including the strip-sack. Kobie Turner added five pressures from the interior and was rated as an outstanding pass rusher by PFF. Emmanuel Forbes Jr. was targeted twice and allowed zero catches. That’s a defense that made plays without Aaron Donald, without Myles Garrett, and without Jaylen Watson.

And that brings me to the most important distinction in this whole video. This isn’t Aaron Donald coming back to rescue a broken defense. This is Aaron Donald potentially returning to a defense that just proved it can win without him. Through four weeks, the Rams rank third in the NFL in opponent EPA per play and fifth in opponent success rate. They held the Eagles to zero-for-twelve on third down and shut them out in the second half. There’s a huge difference between a defense that needs a savior and a defense that’s adding a weapon. The first is a team hoping. The second is a team building.

Picture the interior if Donald plays: Aaron Donald next to Kobie Turner. Turner just had five pressures on his own. Now offenses have to choose which one to double — and whichever one they choose, the other gets a one-on-one. Add Byron Young off the edge, coming off the best game of his season. That’s a pass rush that can make Josh Allen uncomfortable without blitzing — and when you can get pressure with four, you can drop seven into coverage. That’s the formula for slowing down a quarterback like Allen.

Because here’s the key with Allen: pressure and coverage have to work together. If the Rams rush four but can’t cover, Allen extends the play and eventually someone comes open. If they cover well but can’t pressure him, Allen sits back and waits for the route to develop. You need both. Pressure makes the coverage easier. Coverage gives the pressure time to arrive. And that’s exactly why this injury report matters so much. Donald affects the coverage. Watson and Wallace affect how aggressive the pressure can be. Lake and the linebackers affect whether the middle of the field holds up when Allen escapes. Everything is connected.

And on the other side, remember who Buffalo is bringing to SoFi. The Bills’ defense ranks 30th in opponent EPA per play. Their top cornerback, Christian Benford, is questionable with a foot injury. Their nickel corner, Dee Alford, is questionable. Ed Oliver and T.J. Sanders on the defensive line are questionable. DJ Moore on offense is dealing with a shoulder. Both teams are banged up. But the Rams’ injuries are trending toward returns, while Buffalo’s are piling up in the secondary — right where Puka Nacua and Davante Adams are about to attack.

And don’t overlook the third name on McVay’s list: Colby Parkinson. The tight end room has been a mess this season. Terrance Ferguson went on injured reserve last week with what McVay called a severe medial ankle sprain — the Rams expect him back before the end of the season, but not anytime soon. Parkinson has been dealing with knee and shoulder issues and missed the Eagles game. On Thursday he was limited, and McVay said he’s “progressing really in a very positive direction,” while adding that the team will “be smart with the kind of reps that he takes.” Why does that matter so much? Because the tight end injuries are a big part of why Matthew Stafford has been forcing throws this season. When your tight ends are hurt and Puka is out, the quarterback runs out of safe answers. A healthy Parkinson gives Stafford a big, reliable target in the middle of the field — and fewer reasons to throw into coverage against a Buffalo defense that’s desperate for turnovers.

Now let’s talk about the decisions McVay and Shula have to make before Monday night, because this is where coaching matters. Decision one: Donald’s workload. If he plays, how many snaps? You want him effective, but you also want him healthy in December and January. A hurt Donald in Week 5 matters. A hurt Donald in Week 15 matters more. Decision two: Watson versus Wallace. If Watson plays with a harness, do you start him and rotate Wallace in? Do you give Wallace a specific package? Decision three: the middle of the defense. If the linebackers can’t go, who plays — and does Kendricks get elevated? Decision four: pressure packages. Do you trust a four-man rush with Donald and Turner inside, or do you have to manufacture pressure with blitzes?

And here’s a truth worth remembering: if the Rams hold someone back on Monday night, that’s not panic. That’s planning. This team has Super Bowl expectations, and the goal is to be at full strength in January, not just on October 12th. McVay has made that clear with Myles Garrett, who’s still working back from his knee on injured reserve: the Rams are not going to rush anyone back. If Donald or Watson isn’t ready, they’ll sit — and the defense has already shown it can win without them.

Here’s my bottom line, Rams House. A week ago, the Rams went into Philadelphia without Aaron Donald, without Jaylen Watson, without Myles Garrett — and won. Now Donald and Watson are trending toward a return, Josh Wallace has proven he can start in this league, and the Rams have a veteran linebacker waiting on the practice squad in case the injuries in the middle pile up. That’s not a team surviving anymore. That’s a team that’s starting to build depth at exactly the right time. The questions about Lake and the linebackers are real, and we’ll know a lot more on Saturday, when the Rams release their final injury report with official game designations. Watch for three things: whether Donald and Watson come off the report or carry a questionable tag, whether Lake and the linebackers get upgraded, and whether any practice squad elevations are announced before kickoff. But the overall direction is clear: this defense is getting stronger, not weaker.
