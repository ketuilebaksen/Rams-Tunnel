Now let’s set the scene, because the timing of all this matters.

On Sunday, the Rams beat the Raiders 35-21 in Las Vegas and improved to 3-4. Matthew Stafford threw three touchdowns and became the Rams’ all-time leader in touchdown passes, according to the LA Times. Kyren Williams and Blake Corum both ran for over 90 yards and a touchdown, and the Rams ran for 199 yards as a team, according to the AP. It was the most complete game the Rams have played this season, and McVay called the balance between run and pass “the identity that we’re hunting.”

But less than 48 hours later, the two teams the Rams are chasing in the NFC West went shopping.

According to ESPN, the Seahawks are 4-3 and two games behind the 49ers, who sit at 6-1 on top of the division. The Rams are 3-4, in third. And according to The Athletic’s Nate Atkins, the Rams still have three games left against those NFC West rivals. So every one of these moves could show up directly on the Rams’ schedule.

Let’s start with Seattle.

Quenton Nelson is one of the best guards of his generation. According to the Seahawks’ website, he was the sixth overall pick in 2018, a first-team All-Pro in each of his first three seasons and a second-team All-Pro three times, including the last two seasons. He’s made the Pro Bowl in all eight of his NFL seasons. According to the Seahawks, the only other offensive lineman to do that in his first eight seasons was Joe Thomas. That’s the kind of company we’re talking about.

And he’s still playing at a high level. According to the Spokesman-Review, Nelson has an 85.7 grade from Pro Football Focus, third among 74 qualified guards, and he hasn’t allowed a sack through seven games.

So why did Seattle do it? According to ESPN, the Seahawks’ run game has been ordinary — 3.9 yards per carry, 23rd in the league through seven games. Their offensive line hasn’t been as strong as they hoped. And according to the Spokesman-Review, quarterback Sam Darnold has been sacked 10 times in Seattle’s last two losses and has thrown a league-leading eight interceptions. A team that wants to protect its quarterback and run the ball better just added a future Hall of Fame candidate at guard.

The transcript going around also mentions a stat: that the Seahawks are 22-3 since the start of 2025 when Anthony Bradford starts at guard. That number comes from Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak. Here’s the funny thing, though: according to ESPN, Nelson is an upgrade over Bradford. So Seattle took a lineup that was already winning and still decided it wasn’t good enough. That tells you how serious they are about defending their title. And yes — I said defending. According to the Spokesman-Review, three of Seattle’s deadline additions from last season helped the Seahawks win a Super Bowl.

Now there is a cost. According to ESPN, Nelson’s 2026 base salary is 16 million dollars, and Seattle takes on the rest of that for this season. According to the Spokesman-Review, his contract expires after this season. So this is an all-in move for this year. But Seattle can afford it. According to ESPN, the Seahawks are projected to get five compensatory picks in 2027, so giving up a third and a fifth doesn’t hurt them much.

Now San Francisco.

DeForest Buckner is coming home. He started his career with the 49ers in 2016, and San Francisco traded him to the Colts in 2020. Now he’s back. According to Pro Football Rumors, Buckner is 32 years old, and through the trade he had 31 tackles, four for loss, eight quarterback hits, four sacks and three pass deflections. He was playing about 82 percent of the Colts’ defensive snaps. He’s in a contract year with a 13-million-dollar base salary, and the Colts are reportedly splitting what’s left of that salary with San Francisco.

Why did the 49ers need him? According to Pro Football Rumors, defensive tackle Alfred Collins tore his patellar tendon before Week 1, ending his season, and CJ West has been on injured reserve for over a month with a bone chip in his knee. So the 49ers had a real hole in the middle of their defensive line, and they filled it with a former Pro Bowler for a fourth-round pick.

And remember: the 49ers are 6-1. According to Pro Football Rumors, their only loss came in Week 7 against Atlanta. They’re the best team in the division, and they still went out and got better.

So when you put it together, the team in first got stronger, and the team in second got stronger. And the team in third — the Rams — hasn’t done anything yet.

According to Heavy, The Athletic’s Nate Atkins wrote on X: “The NFC West arms race is on.” And he added something every Rams fan should hear: “The cupboards aren’t as stocked in L.A. after trading so many picks out for Myles Garrett and Trent McDuffie. But this is an all-in year, so…”

That’s the tension right there. The Rams are all-in on this season. But they already spent a lot of their chips before it started.

Now let’s talk about whether the Rams really need to respond.

Blaine Grisak of Rams On SI wrote that “the pressure is on for the Rams to make a corresponding move.” And I agree with part of that. The Rams didn’t build this roster to finish third in the division. They traded for Myles Garrett, the 2025 Defensive Player of the Year. They traded for Trent McDuffie. They brought Aaron Donald back out of retirement. This roster was built to win a championship now.

But here’s the other side. Not every rival’s move requires an answer. Seattle added a guard. San Francisco added a defensive tackle. Neither of those changes what the Rams need. The Rams don’t need to “match” those trades. They need to fix their own weak spots.

And there’s a lesson from last season that the transcript brings up, and it’s a good one. In 2025, Seattle traded for Rashid Shaheed at the deadline. According to Rams On SI, it was Shaheed who sparked Seattle’s comeback in Week 16 with a punt return touchdown. That was the wild Thursday night game, which Seattle won 38-37 in overtime, according to NFL.com. And then the two teams met again in the NFC Championship Game at Lumen Field in January, and the Rams’ season ended one win short of the Super Bowl. The Seahawks went on to win it all.

So yes — one deadline addition can change a season. Shaheed wasn’t a superstar. He was a specialist who made one or two huge plays at the right time. That’s the point. Sometimes the move that matters isn’t the biggest name. It’s the one that fills a specific hole.

If you’re still not over that NFC Championship loss, hit that like button. I’m not either.

So what are the Rams’ specific holes? There are two.

The first is wide receiver. According to the Rams’ official website, Davante Adams is out with a ruptured hamstring, and his recovery timeline is six to 10 weeks. But there’s some good news on that. On Wednesday, McVay said Adams’ cleanout surgery went well, and he added: “Based on how everything went, on the shorter end is what is projected.” So Adams could be closer to six weeks than 10. That matters for the trade conversation. If Adams is back in December, the Rams might only need a receiver for a handful of games.

And we saw the problem against the Raiders. According to the AP, Konata Mumpfield’s 15-yard catch was the only reception by a Rams wide receiver other than Puka Nacua. The tight ends picked up the slack, and Puka played through a calf injury, but you can’t win in January with one healthy receiver.

The second hole is defensive tackle. According to Grisak, Poona Ford is out for the season and Kobie Turner is on injured reserve. Ford tore his triceps, according to The Athletic. That’s two important players out of the middle of the defensive line. The Rams have depth, but asking backups to play big snaps for weeks is risky — especially with games against Seattle’s new and improved offensive line still on the schedule.

Now here’s the injury update from Wednesday, because it matters for all of this.

According to the Rams’ official website, the team will not open Myles Garrett’s 21-day practice window this week. McVay said: “I know it’s frustrating, but he’s making good progress, and we’re just on a week-to-week basis right now.” According to the AP, Garrett will miss his seventh straight game. Tight end Terrance Ferguson’s window won’t open this week either.

But there’s good news, too. Trent McDuffie was a limited participant on Wednesday — his first practice since getting hurt in Week 6. And linebacker Nate Landman was a full participant after missing time with a concussion.

There’s also some new bad news. Tight end Colby Parkinson didn’t practice because of a rib injury — McVay said “somebody kind of rolled back on him” — and long snapper Joe Cardona has an ankle injury. The Rams signed veteran Jake McQuaide to the practice squad because of it.

So let’s be honest: the Rams were hoping to get Garrett and Ferguson back sooner. That hasn’t happened. And every week that passes without Garrett makes the trade deadline decision a little harder.

Before we get to the trade targets, let’s talk about money, because this is where every conversation about the Rams has to start.

According to Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak, citing Over The Cap, the Rams have only about 206,000 dollars in cap space right now. Other reports earlier this month had them just under a million. Either way, it’s almost nothing. And according to Grisak, the Rams don’t have their 2027 first- or third-round picks after the Garrett and McDuffie trades. What they do have in 2027 is a second, a fourth, a fifth and a sixth.

So how could they make room? According to Grisak, the most realistic path is restructuring Kyren Williams’ contract, which could free up about 3.5 million dollars. Alaric Jackson’s deal is another option. And the Rams have done this before. Grisak points out that Quentin Lake’s earlier restructure freed up 3.4 million.

Keep those numbers in mind. Because when we go through these five receivers, you’ll see very quickly which ones fit and which ones don’t.

Now, the five receivers.

These are the names CBS Sports listed as possible Rams trade targets after the Adams injury, according to a summary of that article. Again — these are ideas from a writer, not reported talks. CBS itself noted ESPN’s report that the Rams were expected to explore the market, and it also noted that McVay said the team hadn’t talked about adding an outside receiver.

Name number one: George Pickens of the Dallas Cowboys.

We covered Pickens in detail in our last video, so I’ll keep this short. Pickens is a big, explosive receiver who was an All-Pro last season. But according to the CBS summary, he’s playing on a 27.3-million-dollar franchise tag, and a midseason trade would leave the Rams covering about 18.2 million of it. The Rams have around 206,000 dollars. That’s not a gap — that’s a canyon.

And Dallas just beat the Eagles 38-21 on Monday night to move to 3-4, alone in second place in the NFC East, according to The Big Lead. A team chasing a division title usually doesn’t sell its best young receiver. So for the Rams, Pickens is a dream, not a plan.

Name number two: Brian Thomas Jr. of the Jacksonville Jaguars.

This one is fascinating. As a rookie in 2024, Thomas had 87 catches for 1,282 yards and 10 touchdowns. Those are star numbers. But this season, things have fallen apart. According to CBS Sports’ Tyler Sullivan, Thomas had just nine catches for 105 yards and one touchdown on 20 targets through the first six games. In Week 6 against Houston, he played 15 snaps in the first half and then didn’t play at all in the second half. He also went two straight games without a catch, and CBS described “a brutal drop on the opening possession” of that game.

When Jaguars coach Liam Coen was asked about his confidence in Thomas, he said: “Just week to week. Week to week. Guys have to step up and make plays.” Then on Monday, according to NBC Sports, Coen said Thomas was “gonna be able to help us make plays this week.” So Jacksonville hasn’t given up on him.

Here’s why Thomas is interesting for the Rams. He’s cheap. According to Grisak, he’d only cost about 600,000 dollars for the rest of this season. He’s on his rookie contract, so he wouldn’t be a rental. And there’s a connection: according to The Athletic, Jaguars general manager James Gladstone worked for the Rams from 2016 to 2024 and has relationships with both Les Snead and Sean McVay.

But here’s the problem. According to Grisak, the price would likely be a second-round pick — and that’s the Rams’ best remaining pick in 2027. Maybe a third or fourth if Jacksonville is desperate to move him, but the Rams don’t have a 2027 third. And in an earlier article, Grisak noted that Thomas ranks 85th out of 86 receivers in adjusted catch percentage. That’s a concern for a team that needs reliable hands now.

So Thomas is the high-upside option. If McVay could get him back to his rookie form, he could be a long-term piece next to Puka. But it’s a gamble, and the Rams don’t have many chips left to gamble with.

Name number three: Jauan Jennings of the Minnesota Vikings.

Rams fans know Jennings well. He spent years with the 49ers, where he was known as a tough, physical receiver who made big catches on third down — often against the Rams. According to his Wikipedia page, Jennings had 55 catches for 643 yards and nine touchdowns with San Francisco in 2025. And according to CBS Sports, he led the 49ers’ wide receivers in both 2024 and 2025.

Then he signed with Minnesota in May on a one-year deal worth up to 13 million dollars. And it hasn’t worked. According to CBS Sports, through the first five games, Jennings had just two catches for nine yards on five targets. He’d started only two games and missed another for personal reasons. CBS called him “an afterthought” in the passing game.

There’s been some frustration, too. CBS embedded a social media post claiming Jennings was upset he didn’t get the ball when he was wide open in the end zone. But I want to be careful here: there’s no confirmed trade request from Jennings in the reporting I found. CBS cited NFL Media and ESPN saying his trade market was expected to heat up before the deadline, and that’s it.

For the Rams, Jennings has real appeal. According to Grisak, he’d only cost a Day 3 pick, and he knows the NFC West inside and out. He’s not a speed guy. He’s a possession receiver who wins with toughness. And there’s something pretty fun about the idea of a former 49er helping the Rams beat the 49ers. But he’d cost more cap space than the Rams have right now, so they’d need to make room first. And CBS listed the 49ers themselves as a possible landing spot. Imagine if San Francisco brought back Buckner and Jennings in the same week. That’s a reunion tour.

Name number four: Calvin Ridley of the Tennessee Titans.

Ridley is 31, and he’s been a productive receiver for most of his career. But Tennessee is rebuilding, and Ridley has been pushed aside. According to Athlon Sports, via Yahoo, through the first three games, Ridley had just two catches for 35 yards on three targets. He took an 8.75-million-dollar pay cut in March to stay with the Titans, and his base salary this season is 5.49 million. According to the CBS summary, his injury history is a concern.

For the Rams, Ridley makes the least sense of the five. He’d cost more cap space than most of these names, he’s the oldest, and his production has dropped. If the Rams are going to stretch their budget, they’d want a younger player or a better fit.

And name number five: Josh Downs of the Indianapolis Colts.

The transcript going around cut off before getting to this name, but according to the summary of the CBS list, Downs was the fifth receiver. And on Wednesday, he became the most interesting name of all.

According to ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler, as reported by Pro Football Talk, Downs has requested a trade. When he was asked about it, he didn’t confirm it, but he didn’t deny it either. He said: “Whatever comes my way, comes my way, and we’ll go from there.” He also said: “My feet are here right now, so I’ve got to be right here. It could be different tomorrow, could be different the next day — I don’t know.”

Think about the timing. The Colts just traded Quenton Nelson and DeForest Buckner on the same day. According to ESPN, Indianapolis is 2-5, fired general manager Chris Ballard last week and doesn’t have a 2027 first-round pick. Ian Rapoport wrote on X that the trades “show that the Colts are open for business.” So a receiver on an expiring contract asking out right now makes a lot of sense.

According to Pro Football Talk, Downs has 26 catches for 307 yards through seven games this season. He’s not a big outside receiver like Pickens or Thomas. He’s a quick, shifty slot receiver. And here’s the big one: he’s affordable. According to Grisak, Downs would cost the Rams about 2.4 million dollars for the rest of the season, and the Rams might be able to get him for a fifth- or sixth-round pick in 2027. Grisak called a smaller move like this one the kind that makes sense because the Rams “can afford it without mortgaging their future.”

Now, the Rams wouldn’t be alone. According to Behind the Steel Curtain, the Steelers have shown interest in Downs. So if the Rams want him, they might have to move fast.

Of the five names, Downs is the one I’d watch the closest. Not because he’s the best player — Pickens and Thomas have more upside. But because he’s the one who fits the money, the picks and the timeline. A 2.4-million-dollar receiver who can help now, while Adams is out, is exactly the kind of move a team with no cap space can actually make.

Now, I said the Rams have two holes, so let’s talk about the defensive line for a moment, too.

According to The Athletic’s Nate Atkins, there are some defensive tackles who could make sense. Grover Stewart of the Colts could be available, but according to Atkins, he has up to 13 million dollars remaining on a deal that expires after the season. That’s too expensive for the Rams right now. Daron Payne of the Commanders has more than 16 million due this season, according to Atkins — even further out of reach. Roy Lopez of the Cardinals has one year left at 4.1 million dollars. And Atkins also mentioned A’Shawn Robinson of the Buccaneers and Dalvin Tomlinson of the Chargers.

Again, those are ideas, not reported talks. And honestly, the Rams’ defensive line problem might be better solved by getting healthy. Kobie Turner is expected back later in the season, and if Garrett finally returns, he changes the whole front. According to Last Word on Sports, the Rams’ pass rush ranks 27th in the league with just 10 sacks. A healthy Garrett would do more for this defense than any defensive tackle they could afford at the deadline.

So let me answer the question in the title one more time. Are the Rams signing two new star wide receivers? No. And realistically, they aren’t going to.

Here’s what I think is realistic.

Option one: one affordable receiver. Someone like Josh Downs, whose salary fits after a small restructure and who wouldn’t cost a high pick. He’d help now, and when Adams comes back, he’d give the Rams real depth for the playoff run.

Option two: no trade at all. That’s possible, too. According to Heavy, Underdog’s Sosa Kremenjas posted: “I think, ultimately, the Rams won’t trade for anybody.” McVay has already said the team hadn’t talked about adding an outside receiver and that “we’ve got some depth on our roster.” And if the run game keeps working like it did in Las Vegas, the need for a receiver gets smaller.

Option three: the big swing. Brian Thomas Jr. for a second-round pick. That’s the one that could change the conversation around this offense. But it’s also the one with the most risk, and with the Rams’ picks already thin, I’d be surprised.

What I don’t see is a Pickens-type blockbuster. The money just isn’t there.

And one more thing. Remember, this front office has surprised us before. In 2021, the Rams traded for Von Miller at the deadline and signed Odell Beckham Jr. after Cleveland released him, and both played huge roles in the Super Bowl run. So I’m not going to tell you Les Snead will definitely sit still. I’m just telling you that the realistic move is a smaller one.

Now let’s look at the next two weeks, because this decision won’t happen in a vacuum.

According to the AP, the Rams host the 0-6 Chargers at SoFi Stadium on Sunday, and the Chargers are coming off a bye. Then the Rams play Washington on November 8th. And the trade deadline is two days after that, on Tuesday, November 10th, at 4 p.m. Eastern.

So the Rams will get two more games of information before they have to decide. If they win both and get to 5-4, Snead can be choosy. If they lose either one, the pressure goes way up.

And Sunday isn’t a gimme. A winless team with an extra week to prepare can be dangerous. McVay said on Monday that the special teams were “not good enough,” and that “it’s just got to get fixed.” And now the Rams might be without their regular long snapper, according to the Rams’ official website. That’s something to watch in a game where every field goal and extra point matters.

The Rams could also be without Colby Parkinson, who’s been huge for this offense with Adams out. If Parkinson can’t go, Davis Allen and Tyler Higbee will have to carry even more of the load. Allen caught two touchdowns against the Raiders, so the Rams have options — but it’s another reason the receiver room is under the microscope.

Here are my three keys for Sunday.

Key number one: keep the run game going. Kyren and Corum combined for almost 200 yards against the Raiders. Do it again, and the offense doesn’t need a new receiver to win.

Key number two: clean up special teams. With a new long snapper possibly in the mix, the operation has to be smooth. No fake punts, no long returns.

Key number three: get the receivers involved. One catch from a non-Puka receiver isn’t going to cut it. Tutu Atwell, Konata Mumpfield and Jordan Whittington need to make plays. If they do, it changes how urgent this whole trade conversation feels.

My prediction: Rams 27, Chargers 17. And if McDuffie and Landman are both back on the field, this defense could look a lot better than it has in weeks.

So here’s my bottom line.

The Rams are not signing two new star wide receivers. That headline isn’t true, and the video behind it admits it. What is true is that Seattle and San Francisco both made real moves on Tuesday. The Seahawks added Quenton Nelson for a third- and a fifth-round pick. The 49ers brought back DeForest Buckner for a fourth-round pick. And the Rams, at 3-4, are now under real pressure to do something before November 10th.

But the Rams’ situation is different. They have almost no cap space, and they’re missing important future picks after the Garrett and McDuffie trades. So when you look at the five names on the list — Pickens, Brian Thomas Jr., Jauan Jennings, Calvin Ridley and Josh Downs — only a couple actually fit. And the one that fits best just asked for a trade.

My guess is that if the Rams do something, it’ll be one smart, affordable move — not two stars. And the biggest “addition” of all might still be Myles Garrett finally getting on the field.
