First, let’s hear how McVay summed up the win, because it tells you what he wants this team to be.

In his postgame press conference, McVay said the Raiders game and the Giants game were the two games this season where “you at least felt like you had control throughout from wire to wire.” Remember, the Rams beat the Giants 28-6 back in Week 2. That was the last time this team looked this comfortable for a full game. And then he added a line that tells you exactly how he’s thinking: “This league is so humbling, week in and week out. Humility is only a day away.” That’s a coach who has been through a 2-4 start and knows how fast things can turn — in either direction.

He also talked about the team’s energy on the road: “Celebrating each other’s success, having the right energy when you’re on the road and it’s just you versus the opponent and their fans, you got to make a choice to celebrate the play of one another. And thought our guys did that.” He said you could feel “the vibe was right” in pregame warmups. And then he admitted: “It wasn’t all perfect, but the mindset and the mentality was exactly what we’re looking for.”

On Monday, according to the AP’s Greg Beacham, McVay went further: “That was the first game where we were really able to do some of the things that have been in alignment with when we’ve been able to operate at a pretty decent clip offensively.”

So that’s the starting point. McVay believes the Rams finally looked like themselves. But he was careful not to overreact. In his postgame press conference, he called it “a positive day and it’s something that we got to build on, because this is a long journey.” That’s the balance McVay is trying to strike this week: confidence, without complacency. Now he has to keep it going. And that brings us to the decisions.

Decision number one: Myles Garrett’s practice window.

This is the biggest one. On Friday, McVay said it was “a possibility” that Garrett would return to practice this week. NFL Network’s Ian Rapoport reported that Garrett was “likely” to return to practice this week. But on Monday, McVay said the Rams hadn’t decided yet. According to Heavy, he said: “I’m not sure yet. Have not decided on those things.” According to Yahoo Sports, McVay said he hadn’t yet spoken with Reggie Scott, the team’s senior vice president of sports medicine.

Here’s why this decision is tricky. According to Yahoo Sports, Garrett has been on injured reserve since Week 2 after arthroscopic knee surgery, and he’s now missed six straight games. Once his practice window opens, the Rams have 21 days to activate him to the 53-man roster — or he has to stay on injured reserve for the rest of the season. So opening the window starts a clock. If Garrett’s knee isn’t ready, the Rams can’t stop that clock.

And there’s a split in the reporting about his timeline. According to Heavy, ESPN’s Dan Graziano wrote on October 21st that Garrett was “likely out another two or three weeks at least.” Rapoport said he could even play in Week 8 against the Chargers. And ESPN’s Adam Schefter suggested the Rams’ record could affect the timing: “But the more they lose, the more I think they’re going to be a little bit impatient to get them back there.”

Here’s my take: now that the Rams have won, they can afford to be patient. If Garrett needs one more week, give him one more week. The Rams play the 0-6 Chargers this Sunday. They don’t need to rush a reigning Defensive Player of the Year back for that game. What matters is having him healthy for the stretch run — the Packers, the 49ers and the playoff race in December.

But they also can’t wait too long. According to Last Word on Sports, the Rams’ pass rush ranks 27th in the league with just 10 sacks. That’s the one area of the defense that’s clearly missing something. And Garrett, who set the single-season sack record with 23 last year, is the answer. Right now, the Rams’ edge group is Byron Young, rookie Darryl Peterson III and young players like Josaiah Stewart, who McVay praised on Sunday. They’ve battled. But adding Garrett would change how every offense has to block the Rams.

There’s also some outside pressure building. According to Heavy, podcaster Ryen Russillo posted: “The Myles Garrett trade looked like a slam dunk, but has not aged well so far.” That’s a fair point after six missed games. But Garrett has the rest of the season to change that story — and the Rams gave up too much to rush him back and risk losing him for the year. The right decision is the one the doctors make, not the one the critics want.

Decision number two: Terrance Ferguson.

According to Heavy, Ferguson was the Rams’ top pick in the 2025 draft, and he’s been out with an ankle injury since the Week 3 game in Denver. McVay said the same thing about Ferguson as he did about Garrett: the Rams haven’t decided whether to open his practice window.

Here’s why it matters. According to SB Nation, the Rams’ deep passing game still isn’t working, and SB Nation’s JB Scott suggested the Rams may not push the ball downfield effectively until Ferguson is back. Meanwhile, the tight ends were the stars of the Raiders game. According to the AP, Davis Allen, Colby Parkinson and Tyler Higbee combined for 11 catches, 82 yards and two touchdowns.

So McVay has a good problem here. If Ferguson comes back, he’d have four tight ends he trusts. That could mean even more two- and three-tight-end sets — the kind of personnel that helped the Rams run for 199 yards on Sunday. And McVay noted in his postgame press conference that “the tight ends and the receivers also contributed to the blocking surface in a good way.” Ferguson adds another body to that blocking surface, plus a receiving threat in the middle of the field.

Decision number three: does the run game become the identity?

This might be the most important long-term decision McVay faces. According to the AP, before the Raiders game, the Rams had thrown on about 64 percent of their offensive snaps — the fourth-highest rate in the NFL. Against the Raiders, they ran 38 times and threw 29 passes. And according to Last Word on Sports, Stafford attempted only 10 passes in the second half, while the Rams ran 23 times after halftime.

The result? According to the AP, the Rams rushed for 199 yards — a season high, and more than their previous two games combined. Kyren Williams ran for 99 yards and Blake Corum ran for 93, and they each scored a touchdown. According to Last Word on Sports, both backs topped 100 total yards when you include their catches. According to the AP, they’re the first Rams duo with at least 90 rushing yards and a touchdown each in the same game since 2001.

McVay was asked what running the ball well does for the offense, and his answer was clear: “You’re really in 50-50 downs. And so that’s a key factor for us. Any good offense that we’ve had here that I’ve been a part of, you got to be able to do both.” He added: “Us being able to stay efficient on early downs changes everything.” And then he said the line that might define the rest of this season: “If we’re able to do those types of things and have a little bit of run-pass balance like you guys have kind of asked me about, that’s the identity that we’re hunting.” On Monday, according to the AP, McVay doubled down on the backs: “Both Blake and Kyren did a great job.” And on the heavy rushing workload, he said: “I was really pleased with that.” When a coach says that the day after a game, it’s a strong hint about the plan for the next one.

Think about that. After the Arizona loss, McVay said he couldn’t answer the question about the team’s identity. One week later, he’s saying out loud what it is: run-pass balance, staying ahead of the sticks, and making defenses respect both.

He also explained why the runs worked. He said Corum “saw the holes well” and “pressed the line of scrimmage,” and that both backs used “backside A-gap cuts on some downhill direct runs at the tight end surface.” He said it was “a bummer the one long one came back” — Corum’s 43-yard touchdown that was wiped out by a penalty — but “you see the explosiveness though.”

And McVay gave credit to the offensive line. He said, “I thought our offensive line was excellent. They had a great demeanor.” He singled out left tackle Alaric Jackson, who made a key kickout block on Kyren’s touchdown run. McVay said: “That left tackle position is so important. He’s been really productive in both phases. I thought he and Warren both were instrumental to the success that we had” — meaning right tackle Warren McClendon.

So the decision is this: does McVay keep this balance against the Chargers and beyond? My answer: yes. The Rams scored 28 points in the first half with this approach. Stafford didn’t have to force anything. And the defense got to rest. I’d want 30-plus running back carries again on Sunday.

Decision number four: managing Puka Nacua.

McVay told an interesting story in his postgame press conference about how the Puka decision really came together. He said: “When he leaves on Thursday practice, doesn’t participate in Friday, really he and Reggie did a great job of being able to get a bunch of treatment.” He explained that he just asked Puka how he was feeling, “because so much of the offense does run through him.” And he said: “When I spoke with him yesterday, he kind of had a good look in his eye, and you could see the mindset was, unless there was any setbacks, he was going to be ready to go.”

And McVay loved what he saw. He said Puka had “10 targets and eight catches on those 10,” and called him “a key factor for us.” He even joked that the team would “give him a hard time” about going down to the ground on a slant late in the game, saying “he could have had a little bit more yards after the catch on that one.” Then he summed it up: “He’s a stud.”

According to the LA Times, Puka finished with eight catches for 68 yards and the 11-yard touchdown that broke the franchise passing touchdown record for Matthew Stafford. After the game, according to HITC, Puka said his MRI showed nothing significant.

So here’s McVay’s decision: how much does he use Puka in practice this week? With Davante Adams still out, the Rams need Puka on Sundays more than they need him on Wednesdays. I’d expect a lighter practice load early in the week. That’s not a red flag. It’s smart management.

Decision number five: special teams.

This one surprised me. In his postgame press conference, McVay sounded happy with special teams. He said: “I thought we covered punts pretty well,” praised punter Ethan Evans for hitting the ball well on his three punts, and said “McManus did a great job being able to knock in all of his extra points.”

But by Monday, after watching the film, his tone changed. According to the AP, McVay said: “It was not good enough.” And he added: “We’ve talked about this a lot, and it’s just got to get fixed.”

Here’s why. According to the AP, the Raiders converted a fake punt for a first down. Kickoff coverage allowed returns of 42 and 36 yards. And Jordan Whittington misplayed a touchback, which cost the Rams 15 yards.

So McManus did his job — every extra point, every touchback when they wanted one. But the coverage units and the decision-making on returns still need work. That’s a decision for McVay and special teams coordinator Bubba Ventrone: do they change personnel on the coverage teams? Do they simplify the return plan? With the Chargers coming to town, special teams can’t be the reason the Rams give a winless team life.

Decision number six: linebacker.

Nate Landman missed the Raiders game in the concussion protocol, and second-year linebacker Shaun Dolac stepped in. According to SB Nation, Dolac “performed admirably,” was strong in coverage and held up against the run. SB Nation’s JB Scott even suggested Dolac could become a full-time starter or at least earn more snaps.

On Friday, McVay said Landman was “making good progress.” Landman leads the Rams in tackles, and he’s the defense’s main communicator. So if Landman clears the protocol this week, McVay has a choice: go right back to Landman, or find a way to keep Dolac involved. I’d expect Landman to start if he’s cleared — but Dolac has earned a bigger role.

And there’s a related decision in the secondary: Trent McDuffie. According to the AP, McDuffie missed his second straight game with a groin injury. According to the LA Times, he could return soon. Before he got hurt, according to the AP, McDuffie had 16 tackles, two tackles for loss, seven passes defended and an interception in five games. But the backups have held up. Jaylen Watson was named one of the game’s three stars by Rams Wire, and Josh Wallace has been solid in coverage when called on. So when McDuffie is ready, the Rams will have a deeper, more confident cornerback room than they had two weeks ago. McVay’s decision is simply when to bring him back — and with the Chargers coming off a bye, there’s no reason to rush him either.

And the defense as a whole earned a lot of praise. McVay was asked about the run defense, and he said: “They weren’t able to get much of a run game going.” He pointed out that with a couple of minutes left, the Raiders had “17 carries for less than 50 yards.” He credited defensive line coach Giff Smith and players like Tyler Davis, Ty Hamilton, Larrell Murchison, Braden Fiske and Aaron Donald. He said: “I thought everybody contributed to the collective.” And he praised the edge players, too, saying Josaiah Stewart “did a great job. You could feel his presence.”

He was also asked about the fourth-down stops, and he said they were “huge,” especially the one after a turnover. He said: “Part of the competitive stamina that we’re hunting is making them snap it one more time.”

And against Brock Bowers, who came in averaging 95.5 receiving yards a game, McVay credited safety Quentin Lake: “Q Lake was awesome.” A reporter pointed out that the Rams held Bowers under his average, and McVay called Lake “a great leader, great player for us.” He also gave the Raiders credit, saying “Coach Kubiak does a great job,” and that Kirk Cousins has “a great feel for being able to be a great point guard and distribute it to a bunch of playmakers.” That’s what made the defense’s performance so impressive. According to the AP, the Rams allowed just 128 yards in the first half and kept Las Vegas out of the end zone until the fourth quarter. McVay said Jaylen Watson also covered Bowers at times, and added: “The players executed the game plan.” According to the AP, Watson allowed just two short catches on 10 targets and broke up four passes.

Decision number seven: the receiver room, and the trade deadline.

Here’s the number that jumps out from Sunday’s game. According to the AP, Konata Mumpfield’s 15-yard catch was the only reception by a Rams wide receiver other than Puka Nacua. That’s it. One catch. The tight ends and running backs carried the passing game. According to Last Word on Sports, Davis Allen had four catches, including those two touchdowns. Parkinson had five catches for 48 yards. The Rams found a way. But defenses will start loading up against the tight ends if the wide receivers can’t win on the outside.

That worked against the Raiders. But it’s not sustainable for a whole season. Davante Adams is still weeks away — according to Heavy, he’s not expected back until around the bye week. And according to SB Nation, Jordan Whittington and Mumpfield were “very disappointing” in this game.

ESPN’s Graziano reported last week that the Rams “will at least explore the possibility of trading for a wide receiver.” McVay said on Wednesday that adding someone from the outside “is not something that we’ve talked about right now.” The trade deadline is November 10th. So McVay and general manager Les Snead have about two weeks to decide.

Here’s the challenge. According to Pro Football Rumors, the Rams have just under 990,000 dollars in cap space and are missing several future draft picks. So if they make a move, it has to be cheap. Writers have floated names like Josh Downs of the Colts and Stefon Diggs of the Commanders. Those are ideas, not reported talks. But a receiver who can stretch the field would help this offense a lot.

My take: if the run game keeps working against the Chargers, and Ferguson comes back, the Rams can afford to stand pat. If the offense stalls again, they should make a small move before the deadline.

Decision number eight: what about Stafford?

This isn’t a decision about whether Stafford plays — he’s the starter, and he just became the Rams’ all-time leader in touchdown passes with 155. It’s about how McVay uses him. Stafford threw only 29 passes on Sunday, and only 10 in the second half. He went 23 of 29 for 195 yards, three touchdowns and one interception, according to the Rams’ official website.

After the game, Stafford talked about the record, and he made it about his teammates. He said: “Just super appreciative of all the guys that have helped me do that. Catching touchdowns and throwing touchdowns isn’t a one-man job.” And according to Last Word on Sports, he also said about replacing Adams: “It’s a group effort. You’re not going to replace Davante with one person.”

That’s the right attitude. Stafford doesn’t have to throw 40 times a game for the Rams to win. If the run game and tight ends keep working, he can be efficient and smart, and save his big throws for the moments that matter. And there’s a long-term benefit, too. Stafford is 38. Every hit he avoids in October is a hit he doesn’t feel in December. A balanced offense isn’t just better football right now — it’s how the Rams keep their quarterback fresh for a playoff push.

Now let’s quickly look at the Chargers, because Sunday’s game is the first test of all these decisions.

According to the AP, the Rams host the 0-6 Chargers at SoFi Stadium on Sunday, and the Chargers are coming off a bye. That’s important. A winless team with an extra week to prepare can be dangerous. The Rams can’t overlook them. And remember, the Rams and Chargers share SoFi Stadium. It’s a home game for the Rams, but it’s also a game the Chargers will treat like their Super Bowl. If the Rams come out flat, a desperate team can steal it. If they come out with the same energy McVay saw in Las Vegas warmups, they should be in control.

And after that, according to Heavy, the Rams play Washington and then get a rematch with the Cardinals — the team that beat them 18-16 in Week 6. According to Last Word on Sports, all three of those opponents have losing records. That’s a real opportunity. Win all three, and the Rams go into the bye at 6-4 with Garrett, McDuffie, Ferguson and eventually Adams on the way back.

Here are my three keys against the Chargers.

Key number one: keep the balance. At least 30 carries for Kyren and Corum. Stay ahead of the sticks, like McVay said.

Key number two: clean up special teams. No fake punts, no long returns, no misplayed touchbacks. McVay said it “just got to get fixed.” Sunday is the time.

Key number three: limit penalties. According to the AP, the Rams had seven penalties for 90 yards against the Raiders, and two Corum touchdowns were wiped out. Against a better team, that’s the difference between winning and losing.

My early prediction: Rams 30, Chargers 17. I’ll update it once we know about Garrett, Ferguson and McDuffie.

So here’s my bottom line.

McVay didn’t announce a shocking decision after the Raiders game. But he does face real decisions this week. Whether to open Garrett’s and Ferguson’s practice windows — he said he hasn’t decided yet. Whether to make the run game the identity — and after Sunday, he basically said it is: “That’s the identity that we’re hunting.” How to manage Puka’s calf. How to fix special teams, which he called “not good enough.” Whether to go back to Landman or keep Dolac involved. And whether to add a receiver before the November 10th deadline.

The good news is that these are the decisions of a team that’s climbing, not falling. A week ago, the questions were about whether McVay had lost the locker room. Now, the questions are about how to get even better. That’s progress. And the standings back it up. According to Pro Football Network, the Rams moved up to third in the NFC West after both the 49ers and Cardinals lost on Sunday. One more win, and the conversation around this team changes completely.
