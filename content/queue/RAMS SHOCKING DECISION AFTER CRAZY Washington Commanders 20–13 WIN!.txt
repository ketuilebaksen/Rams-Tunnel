The final score and the big numbers

Let’s start with the numbers, because they tell the story.

Final score: Rams 20, Commanders 13.

Matthew Stafford threw the ball 43 times for 219 yards. That’s roughly 5.1 yards per attempt, which is low.

Puka Nacua was targeted 21 times. Twenty-one. He caught 14 passes for 150 yards, including the game-winning touchdown.

The Rams ran for 124 net yards, and Blake Corum found the end zone.

And Myles Garrett, in his first game since Week 1, had three sacks, three tackles for loss, and a strip-sack right before halftime that led to points.

So you’ve got a team that won with defense, the run game, and one receiver. Remember that, because it’s the theme of today’s video.

Myles Garrett is back, and he didn’t wait long

Let’s start with the best news of the day.

Coming into this game, there were a lot of questions about Garrett. He hadn’t played since the opener against the 49ers in Australia. He had arthroscopic knee surgery in mid-September and missed seven games. He practiced on a limited basis Wednesday and Thursday, then sat out Friday, which McVay said was “more of just being smart with him.” Rams On SI had projected him to play around half the defensive snaps as the Rams eased him back.

And for a while, it looked like he might need time. The ESPN crew said after the game that it wasn’t the convincing first half you’d want to see from Garrett. He didn’t show up on the stat sheet early.

Then right before halftime, he got home. Strip-sack. The Rams got the ball, and the offense turned it into points. That was the moment the game changed.

One of the ESPN analysts put it perfectly. He said he expected Garrett to need some time to get the truck rolling, but instead he “instantly left the residential area” and was “on the interstate driving 80-plus.” That’s a great way to describe it.

By the end of the day, Garrett had three sacks and three tackles for loss. In his first game back. On a knee that kept him out for two months. Think about that.

Here’s why it matters so much. Before this game, the Rams were tied for 26th in the league with just 12 sacks, per ESPN. Garrett added a quarter of that total in one afternoon. He’s the 2025 Defensive Player of the Year, the guy who set the NFL single-season sack record with 23. And this was the opening act.

Another ESPN analyst made a point I really liked. He said great players don’t just affect the game with their own play, they affect it through their teammates. When Garrett makes a play like that strip-sack, the whole defense feels it. “Okay, we got him. Here he is.”

And remember what Garrett said before the game about playing next to Aaron Donald: “I think offenses really have to pick their poison. Who’s going to be freed up this play?” On Sunday, Washington found out the answer was Garrett.

Now, I’ll be careful here. I don’t have his exact snap count yet, and we don’t know how his knee feels today. The Rams have been clear they want to build his workload slowly, with the goal of having him full-time after the bye. But if this is what Garrett looks like at less than full speed, the rest of the NFC should be nervous.

The Puka show

Now let’s talk about Puka Nacua, because he was unbelievable, and also because his workload tells you something important about this offense.

Fourteen catches. A hundred and fifty yards. The game-winning touchdown. On 21 targets.

Let me put that in perspective. Stafford threw 43 passes. Nearly half of them went to Puka. One ESPN analyst said it plainly: “It seems like it’s the Puka show. If we got to throw it to him 21 times to win the game, then that’s what we’re going to do.”

And this isn’t new. Last week against the Chargers, Puka caught 11 of his 12 targets, and the rest of the wide receivers combined for two catches on six targets. Since Davante Adams went down with the hamstring injury on October 18th, the Rams’ passing game has basically become: get the ball to Puka and see what happens.

The good news? It’s working. Puka is a superstar, and he’s proving he can carry an offense when he has to. Defenses know the ball is going to him and they still can’t stop him.

The bad news? You can’t do this forever. Twenty-one targets a game is a lot of hits for one receiver. And in the playoffs, a good defense is going to bracket him, double him, and dare somebody else to beat them. Right now, I’m not sure who that somebody else is.

That’s exactly why the deadline conversation matters so much. And we’ll come back to it.

The Stafford question

This is the part of the game that’s getting the most attention, so let’s be honest about it.

Stafford threw 43 times for 219 yards. That’s about 5.1 yards per attempt. For a quarterback who won MVP last season, that’s a tough number.

The ESPN crew didn’t hold back. One analyst said Stafford is “missing throws that he does not miss.” He pointed to an early play where Tyler Higbee was open on a seven route, a corner route, and Stafford sailed it just out of his reach. Another analyst said, “I haven’t seen Stafford” look like this.

And the numbers back up the concern. Coming into this game, Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak had the Rams’ explosive pass play rate at 4.1 percent, compared with 17.5 percent at this point last year. Stafford’s average depth of target over the previous four weeks ranked 25th out of 27 quarterbacks. Sunday’s 5.1 yards per attempt fits right into that trend.

Now, there’s context here, and I want to be fair.

First, the elbow. Stafford was on the injury report all week with right elbow soreness, though he practiced fully every day. He said, “It feels good,” and, “I haven’t missed a rep at practice. I haven’t missed a rep in a game, and I feel good.” McVay called the listing “more semantical than anything else” and said Stafford had been “spinning the ball.” I have no new information on the elbow after the game, so I’m not going to speculate. But when the throws keep landing short and a few are a little off, people are going to keep asking.

Second, the receivers. Without Adams, there’s no second receiver who scares a defense. When everything goes to Puka, defenses can sit on him, and the throws get harder.

Third, the scheme. One ESPN analyst made a really smart point: “Two things could be true.” Stafford may have taken a step back, but the offense’s identity may be changing too. The Rams added tight ends this offseason, and they run 12 and 13 personnel, two and three tight ends, more than almost anyone in the league. That’s a run-first, ball-control style. It produces fewer big plays by design.

So is it Stafford, the receivers, or the scheme? My honest answer: it’s probably some of all three. Stafford is 38, he’s dealing with some elbow soreness, he’s missing his number two receiver, and the offense is built around the run and the tight ends right now. Put those together, and you get 219 yards on 43 attempts.

But here’s the thing. He still made the throw that won the game. When the Rams needed it, he found Puka for the touchdown. That’s what veteran quarterbacks do.

The run game did its job

Let’s give the run game its credit, because it was a big reason the Rams won.

The Rams ran for 124 net yards, and Blake Corum scored a touchdown. That’s three straight weeks of the run game carrying the offense. Kyren Williams and Corum combined for 192 rushing yards against the Raiders and 129 against the Chargers. Now 124 against Washington.

Washington’s defensive interior was banged up coming in. They’d put Daron Payne and Javon Kinlaw on injured reserve, and Jer’Zhan Newton was questionable. The Rams saw that and went right at the middle.

One ESPN analyst asked the obvious question: is McVay leaning into the run game more to find wins? I think the answer is clearly yes, and I think it’s the right call for now. When your passing game is averaging five yards an attempt, running the ball keeps you on schedule, keeps the clock moving, and keeps your defense rested.

But the same analyst made the counterpoint: “You’re not winning games throwing it 40 times” in the NFC West the way the Rams did Sunday. If Stafford is throwing 43 times and getting 219 yards, the balance isn’t right. The run game has to set up the pass, not cover for it.

The defense closed it out

The other hero of this game was the defense as a whole.

Washington scored 13 points. And the Rams did it with a banged-up secondary. Jaylen Watson is out for the season, and Trent McDuffie and Kam Curl were both questionable going in after missing practice all week. Josh Wallace had stepped into the starting lineup, and Emmanuel Forbes was the number three corner against his old team.

The defense also made the stand it needed at the end to protect the lead. That’s the kind of thing a team needs if it wants to make a real playoff push.

And this is the third straight strong week. The Chargers didn’t score a touchdown. Washington scored 13. With Garrett back and Donald playing a lot of snaps, this front is starting to look like what the Rams imagined in the summer.

One more thing. McVay said before the game that the key against Jayden Daniels was “rushing four as one” and not giving him “vertical seams” to escape. And he said the defense had to “earn the right” to get into obvious passing situations by winning on first and second down. Three sacks from Garrett tells you the defense did earn those chances, and cashed them in.

How we got here: the three-game streak

Let’s zoom out for a second, because this win means more when you remember where the Rams were.

On October 18th, the Rams lost 18-16 to Arizona. That dropped them to 2-4. Davante Adams pulled up in the end zone on a noncontact play and was later ruled out for six to ten weeks with a ruptured hamstring. The next day, McVay announced Poona Ford was out for the season with a ruptured triceps and Kobie Turner was headed to injured reserve. A lot of people wrote this team off that week.

Then the run game took over against the Raiders, with Kyren Williams and Blake Corum combining for 192 rushing yards.

Then came the Chargers. The Rams had a slow start, five straight drives without a first down to open the game, but the defense never let the Chargers score a touchdown. Puka caught 11 of 12 targets, Corum had a 23-yard touchdown run, and the Rams won 24-9.

And now Washington, 20-13, with Garrett back and Puka carrying the passing game.

So in three weeks, the Rams went from 2-4 and in trouble to 5-4 and right in the middle of the NFC race. And they did it without their number two receiver, without two starting defensive tackles, and, until Sunday, without Garrett. That’s a resilient football team.

Garrett, Donald, and the big bet

Here’s the bigger picture behind Garrett’s day.

The Rams paid a huge price for Garrett on June 1st: Jared Verse, a 2027 first-round pick, a 2028 second, and a 2029 third, all to Cleveland. One of the ESPN analysts said it well: this is “why they went out and gave the investment” for Garrett, because of that “dominant presence.”

And then there’s Aaron Donald, who came out of retirement after two years to play with Garrett. Donald said this week that the season has been “not what I expected, but we here,” and that he’s “enjoying the process.” On Garrett’s return, he said: “It’s good to have the pieces back to the puzzle.”

McVay praised Donald on Friday too. He talked about Donald’s “growth mindset,” how “intentional” and “locked in” he is, and said, “Once he commits to something, he’s all in.” He also pointed out that Donald is adjusting to “the workload that you do as an inside defensive lineman” after two years away, and “he’s only getting better.”

When Garrett was out, a lot of people asked whether the whole plan had been a mistake. Sunday was the first real answer. It was only one game, but this is what the Rams had in mind when they built this defensive front.

My quick game grades

Let me give you my quick grades for each part of the team. These are my opinions, based on what we saw and the numbers we have.

Pass rush: A. Three sacks from Garrett, a strip-sack that flipped the game, and steady pressure once he got going.

Run defense: B-plus. Washington’s offense managed only 13 points, and ESPN’s crew pointed out that Washington couldn’t run the football.

Secondary: B. Banged up and missing Watson, but it held up. Considering the injuries, that’s a solid grade.

Run game: B-plus. A hundred and twenty-four yards and a Corum touchdown against a depleted interior. It did its job.

Puka Nacua: A-plus. Fourteen catches, 150 yards, game-winning touchdown. Nothing more to say.

Passing game overall: C-minus. Two hundred nineteen yards on 43 attempts, a few missed throws, and almost nothing going to anyone besides Puka. This is the area that has to improve.

Coaching: B. McVay found a way to win by leaning on the run and the defense. But the offense still needs answers before the playoffs.

The ESPN debate: are the Rams for real?

The best part of ESPN’s postgame coverage was the debate about whether this Rams team is actually good, so let’s go through both sides.

The case against. One analyst said, “I’m not sold on the Rams yet.” His reasons were clear: the passing game doesn’t look like last year’s, it’s too dependent on one receiver, and Washington isn’t a great team. He even said he had the Rams as his NFC Super Bowl favorite coming into the season, “but as it stands right now, it just doesn’t look like it used to look.” Another analyst said he wasn’t sure whether Washington played well or “the Rams just played that poor,” because the offense hasn’t looked good. He also said he’s worried about Arizona, the team that beat the Rams 18-16 back in October.

The case for. Another analyst said you have to feel differently about a team that wins three straight, because “it’s hard to win in the National Football League.” He pointed out that the Rams are finding ways to win at a high clip even without their full offense. And he said that if they beat Arizona before the bye, they’ll be in position for “a legit chance” at a significant playoff run.

So who’s right? Here’s my honest take. Both are.

The skeptics are right that this offense isn’t where it needs to be for a deep playoff run. A 5.1-yards-per-attempt passing game won’t beat the 49ers or Seahawks in January.

But the optimists are right too. Three weeks ago, this team was 2-4, Adams had just gone down, and people were writing the season off. Now the Rams are 5-4 with three straight wins, Garrett is back and dominating, Adams is trending toward the shorter end of his six-to-ten-week timeline, and the defense is playing its best football of the year. That’s real momentum.

The question is whether the passing game can catch up before the playoffs. And that’s where tomorrow comes in.

Three things I loved, three things that worry me

Let me boil the game down to three positives and three concerns.

What I loved.

One: Garrett’s response after a quiet start. A lot of players coming off two months on the shelf would’ve had a quiet game and called it a step forward. Garrett had a quiet first half and then took over. That tells you his knee and his confidence are in a good place.

Two: Puka’s toughness. Twenty-one targets means a lot of contact, a lot of routes, and a lot of attention from the defense. He still made the biggest catch of the day.

Three: the team found a way. Not a pretty way, but a way. Good teams win games when they aren’t at their best, and that’s what the Rams did on the road.

What worries me.

One: the passing game is one-dimensional. When nearly half the targets go to one guy, a good defense will take him away, and then what?

Two: Stafford’s accuracy. The missed throw to Higbee on the seven route is the kind of play Stafford usually makes in his sleep. If that keeps happening, it’s a real problem, whether it’s the elbow, the timing, or something else.

Three: the margin for error. A 20-13 win over a 2-5-1 team is a win, and it counts. But the Rams are winning close games without much room. Against the 49ers or Seahawks, that margin could disappear fast.

What this means for the deadline

The trade deadline is tomorrow at 4 p.m. Eastern. Here’s how Sunday’s game changes the conversation.

The case for a receiver just got stronger. Twenty-one targets to one player. Two hundred nineteen yards on 43 attempts. Even ESPN’s crew said that if the Rams had more picks, they’d be looking for “another pass catcher.” The real names connected to the Rams this week are Josh Downs of the Colts, who requested a trade, and Brian Thomas Jr. of the Jaguars. Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak wrote that the Rams have called about both, though he didn’t name a source. Dyami Brown of Washington came up too, though he was out Sunday with a calf injury.

But the case for standing pat is real too. McVay has said publicly that he’d rather count on players coming back from injury: “Hopefully, some of those trades will be guys getting back from injury.” Garrett just proved that point in a big way. And Adams is trending toward a return around the end of November.

And the money is still the problem. The Rams have almost no cap space and no 2027 first- or third-round pick. Any trade would need a restructure or a player going the other way. Athlon’s idea of trading Forbes makes less sense now, with the secondary this banged up.

So what would I do? This is my opinion, not a report. I’d make a small move for a receiver if the price is right, a late-round pick plus a restructure, not a big package. Somebody who can take a few targets off Puka and give defenses one more thing to worry about for the next three or four weeks, until Adams is back. If the price goes up, and the Colts said they’d wait for offers to rise, I’d stand pat and trust the returns.

We’ll know by tomorrow afternoon. If something real happens, I’ll be on it, with the source.

Looking ahead: Arizona, then the bye

Next up is the Arizona Cardinals, and the Rams can’t take this one lightly.

The Cardinals beat the Rams 18-16 back on October 18th. That was the game where Davante Adams got hurt. Arizona beat Dallas 33-30 in overtime a couple of weeks ago, so they’re capable of beating anybody on a good day.

Here’s what I’ll be watching.

Can the passing game spread it out? If the Rams throw to Puka 21 times again, Arizona will be ready. Somebody else has to step up. Tutu Atwell, Konata Mumpfield, Jordan Whittington if his back is okay, or the tight ends, especially if Terrance Ferguson gets healthy after his hamstring injury.

Can Garrett do it again? One great game is a sign. Two great games is a trend. And Garrett’s workload should keep going up.

Can the run game stay hot? Three straight weeks over 120 rushing yards. Keep that going, and the Rams can win games even when the passing game is quiet.

And the bye. After Arizona, the Rams finally get their bye week. A 6-4 record heading into the bye would put them right in the middle of the NFC playoff race, with Adams, Turner, and maybe McDuffie getting healthier. That’s a huge opportunity.

And there’s a funny bit from the ESPN segment. They do a “Sharpie season” thing where they cross teams off the playoff list. They crossed off Washington. One analyst joked, “They don’t give you games for grit.” The Rams, on the other hand, are very much still in it.

Wrapping it up

Let’s bring it home.

There was no “shocking decision” after the game. There was a 20-13 win, the Rams’ third straight, that puts them at 5-4.

Myles Garrett was the story of the day: three sacks, three tackles for loss, and a strip-sack before halftime that changed the game, all in his first game back from knee surgery. Puka Nacua was incredible, with 14 catches for 150 yards and the game-winning touchdown on 21 targets. The run game put up 124 yards, and Blake Corum scored. The defense held Washington to 13.

But the concerns are real. Stafford threw for 219 yards on 43 attempts, missed some throws he usually makes, and the passing game is leaning almost entirely on Puka. ESPN’s crew is split on whether this team is for real. I think the answer is: the defense is, and the offense needs help.

The trade deadline is tomorrow at 4 p.m. Eastern. Then it’s Arizona, then the bye.
