Now, let’s talk about what McVay actually said after the win, because that’s what the headline should have been about.

McVay’s message was about how the Rams responded. According to The Sporting Tribune, he said: “It’s not really how you start, it’s how you finish.” And that sums up the game perfectly. According to ESPN, the Rams went five straight drives without a first down to start the game — the first time that has happened in McVay’s career. They trailed 9-3 late in the first half. Then a two-minute drive, sparked by a 40-yard catch by Tutu Atwell, ended with a Kyren Williams touchdown with 19 seconds left. And in the second half, Blake Corum ran for a 23-yard touchdown and Puka Nacua caught an 18-yard screen for another. The Rams scored 21 straight points, according to Pro Football Talk.

According to the Rams’ official website, McVay said: “What I did love is the guys stayed steady.” And he gave a lot of credit to the defense: “They just kept getting stop after stop.” According to Yahoo Sports, McVay gave out two game balls, to Quentin Lake and Trent McDuffie, for their interceptions. And in his victory speech, he said: “I cannot say enough about the way our defense started out that game.”

And the defense earned that praise. According to The Sporting Tribune, Herbert was under pressure on 17 of his 37 dropbacks. According to the Rams’ official website, Lake’s interception set up the Rams’ first field goal, and McDuffie’s interception set up Puka’s touchdown. And in the fourth quarter, the Chargers drove to the Rams’ 24-yard line, but the defense stopped them on fourth down. According to the Rams’ website, the Chargers didn’t score in the second half, and the defense has now gone two straight home games without allowing a touchdown.

And there’s a nice piece of history, too. According to ESPN, the Rams have now won two of the three games between the two Los Angeles teams since both moved back to L.A. And according to the AP, this is the Rams’ first back-to-back wins since January.

McVay also had a little fun. According to Yahoo Sports, when he was asked whether he considers the Chargers a rival, he said: “Kevin Demoff does. I don’t.” That’s about as “shocking” as McVay got after this game.

So no — there was no shocking decision. There was a coach who was happy his team stayed calm, finished strong and moved to 4-4.

Now, here’s where the Donte Jackson rumor accidentally touches a real issue. The Rams might actually need a cornerback.

According to the Rams’ official website, cornerback Jaylen Watson left Sunday’s game in the third quarter with a shoulder injury and was ruled out. And Trent McDuffie hurt his back late in the third quarter and was listed as questionable to return. According to the AP, McDuffie stayed on the sideline. After the game, according to ClutchPoints, McVay said about McDuffie: “He looks like he’s feeling OK.” And according to a postgame NFL injury roundup, McDuffie said he was “feeling good.”

So McDuffie sounds OK. Watson is the bigger question. And this isn’t the first time Watson’s shoulder has been an issue. According to the Rams’ official website, earlier this season Watson played through a dislocated shoulder and still recorded his first interception as a Ram. If this is the same shoulder, the Rams will have to be careful.

Here’s more detail on that. According to the Rams’ official website, Watson dislocated his shoulder in the first half of the Week 3 game against the Broncos. He missed Week 4 against the Eagles and came back in Week 5, playing with his shoulder in a harness. And in that Week 5 Monday night game against the Bills, he picked off Josh Allen just before halftime. The Rams’ website also noted that Watson had dislocated the same shoulder back in 2021 at Washington State. McVay said about him at the time: “He’s tough, mentally and physically, and he’s a great teammate.” And Watson said: “It’s football, everybody’s gonna get banged up, get hurt, stuff’s gonna happen.”

So Watson is tough, and he’s played through this before. But a shoulder that has come out more than once is a real concern, and the Rams will have to decide whether to rest him or push through.

And who would play if Watson can’t? Back in September, when Watson missed time, the Rams planned to use McDuffie, Emmanuel Forbes and Josh Wallace at outside cornerback, according to Rams Wire, via Yahoo Sports. And according to Grisak, the Rams signed cornerback Damarion Williams a few weeks ago. So there are options. But none of them is a proven starter like Watson. That’s why this injury matters so much, especially with a road trip to Washington next.

We’ll know more after McVay’s Monday press conference. But if Watson has to miss time, the Rams’ cornerback depth suddenly becomes a real concern — right before the trade deadline on Tuesday, November 10th.

So could a veteran cornerback like Donte Jackson make sense? On the football side, maybe. ESPN’s scouting report, which I mentioned earlier, describes exactly what a team needs in a pinch: a zone cornerback with good instincts who can close on the ball. And 23 career interceptions tell you he still knows how to find it. A 30-year-old on an expiring deal is exactly the kind of short-term help a contender adds at the deadline — no long-term money, no long-term commitment, just a few months of experience in the secondary.

But — and this is important — there’s no report connecting the Rams to Jackson. ESPN didn’t list the Rams. No insider has said the Rams have called. So this is a “makes some sense on paper” idea, not a rumor with real sourcing behind it.

Now, let’s look at why a Jackson trade would be hard for the Rams even if they wanted him.

Problem number one: money. According to Athlon Sports, citing Over The Cap, the Rams have 206,406 dollars in cap space. Jackson’s base salary is 6 million dollars, according to ESPN. Players are paid over the 18 weeks of the regular season, so after eight weeks, roughly 10 weeks’ worth — around 3.3 million dollars — would still be owed. That’s my own rough math. Either way, it’s more than 15 times what the Rams have. The Rams would need a restructure — Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak has suggested Kyren Williams’ contract could free about 3.5 million — or the Chargers would have to pay part of the salary.

Interestingly, that idea has come up before. On October 22nd, ESPN’s Bill Barnwell pitched a trade sending Khalil Mack and Donte Jackson to the Lions for a 2027 fourth-round pick and a 2027 sixth-round pick, with the Chargers paying about half of the money owed to both players. So it’s possible for a team to make that kind of deal work. But the Rams would be competing with teams that have more picks and more space.

Problem number two: picks. According to Pro Football Rumors, the Rams are missing their 2027 first and third, their 2028 second and their 2029 third. According to Grisak, their 2027 picks are a second, a fourth, a fifth and a sixth. A fifth-round pick, which the clip mentions, is something the Rams have. But the Rams also have other needs — receiver and defensive tackle — and limited picks to spend on all of them.

Problem number three: the Chargers’ attitude. According to Breer, as of October 30th, the Chargers had shut down trade inquiries. Now, here’s the twist. According to Yardbarker’s summary of Breer’s report, Breer suggested a fire sale could come if the Chargers lost to the Rams. Well — they lost. They’re now 0-7, according to the AP. So this week, the Chargers’ stance could change. And the Chargers are banged up. According to ESPN, safety Derwin James was placed on injured reserve with a hamstring injury before the Rams game. Justin Herbert said after the loss, according to ESPN: “I think the turnovers are what’s stopping us.” And according to ESPN, the Chargers haven’t scored a touchdown in their last nine quarters. Harbaugh, according to the AP, said: “There’s no quit,” and “There’s no surrender.” That’s admirable. But a front office looking at 0-7 has to think about next year, too. The Chargers host Houston next Sunday, according to the AP. If they lose again, the pressure to sell veterans on expiring deals will only grow. If it does, Jackson, Mack and Quentin Johnston could all become available. That’s the real story to watch — not a fake Schefter confirmation.

Problem number four: McVay’s approach. Last week, according to the LA Times, McVay was asked whether there had been any new trade discussions, and his first answer was: “No.” According to the New York Post, he said: “Hopefully, some of those trades will be guys getting back from injury that haven’t played for us.” If Watson’s injury turns out to be minor, the Rams may not feel the need to add a cornerback at all.

Now let me address one more thing from the clip, because it’s a myth a lot of people believe. The clip says Jackson could just “walk the same hall and change locker rooms” because the Rams and Chargers share SoFi Stadium. That’s a fun image, but it’s not really how it works. The two teams share a game-day stadium, but they practice at separate facilities. Remember what Stafford joked about this week? According to the AP, he said “it’s a big town to cross” — talking about the distance between the two teams’ training facilities. And he said: “I don’t run into those guys too often, to be honest with you.” So a player traded from one team to the other would still be moving to a completely different building during the week.

Now, the clip also mentions some real trades from around the league, and those are worth knowing.

According to Pro Football Rumors, the Bears traded two first-round picks and a fourth-round pick to the Titans for defensive tackle Jeffery Simmons. According to the Seahawks’ official website, Seattle traded a 2027 third-round pick and a 2028 fifth-round pick to the Colts for All-Pro guard Quenton Nelson. And according to Pro Football Rumors, the 49ers traded a 2027 fourth-round pick to the Colts to bring back DeForest Buckner.

So two of the Rams’ NFC West rivals have already made moves. The Rams have been quiet. Now, to be fair, while their rivals were trading, the Rams were winning. They beat the Raiders the Sunday before those trades and the Chargers the Sunday after. Their rivals improved on paper. The Rams improved in the standings. And in November, the standings are what count. And according to Grisak, the Rams’ quiet approach fits Les Snead’s history. Snead has made four trades on deadline day itself, two eight days before the deadline and one the day before. Grisak suggested Monday after the Chargers game was the most realistic day for a Rams move. So today is a day to watch.

And here’s another real piece of the Chargers’ story. On ESPN’s list, Chargers receiver Quentin Johnston was also listed — ranked 20th, with a 25 percent chance of being traded, according to ESPN. The fits ESPN listed for Johnston were the 49ers, Raiders and Steelers. So the Chargers have three real trade candidates in Mack, Jackson and Johnston. If they decide to sell after starting 0-7, it could shake up the whole market.

And Johnston is interesting for Rams fans for a different reason. According to ESPN, he’s 6-foot-3 and 208 pounds with straight-line speed, he has 18 career touchdowns, and he went over 700 receiving yards in both 2024 and 2025. But he only has eight catches this season, and ESPN noted he has 11 career drops. The Chargers already exercised his fifth-year option. Now look at the fits ESPN listed: the 49ers, Raiders and Steelers. The 49ers are already 6-1 and just added DeForest Buckner. If they also add a big receiver like Johnston, that’s another move by a division rival. So even if the Rams stay quiet, what the Chargers do this week could still affect the NFC West.

Let me give you my honest take, and remember — this is not a report.

I don’t think the Rams are trading for Donte Jackson. There’s no reporting linking them, ESPN didn’t name them, and the Rams’ cap situation makes any veteran addition hard. But the Watson injury is real, and if he has to miss time, I think cornerback could jump onto the Rams’ list of needs alongside receiver and defensive tackle. If that happens, a veteran on an expiring deal — someone like Jackson — is exactly the type of player a team in the Rams’ position would look at.

And the bigger picture is this: with only about 206,000 dollars in cap space, the Rams will probably get one move at most, maybe two with some creativity. So if the Rams do something, it’ll tell you exactly what they think their biggest need is. Receiver, defensive tackle or cornerback — that choice will say a lot.

Let me lay out those three paths side by side, so you can see the trade-offs. And again, this is my analysis, not a report.

Path one: a receiver. The most realistic name is Josh Downs, who requested a trade according to ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler. According to Grisak, he’d cost about 2.4 million dollars for the rest of the season, and Grisak proposed a 2027 fourth-round pick. He’d help the offense right away while Davante Adams recovers.

Path two: a defensive tackle. The name ESPN actually connected to the Rams is A’Shawn Robinson of the Buccaneers, with a 30 percent trade chance. According to Heavy, The Athletic’s Daniel Popper projected a cap hit of about 2 million dollars for an acquiring team. He’d help a defensive line missing Poona Ford and Kobie Turner.

Path three: a cornerback. If Watson’s injury is serious, a veteran like Donte Jackson becomes the type of player that makes sense, though nobody has linked him to the Rams. By my rough math, he’d cost around 3.3 million for the rest of the season, unless the Chargers paid part of it.

You can see why the Rams can’t do all three. With about 206,000 dollars in cap space and one realistic restructure worth about 3.5 million, according to Grisak, they can probably afford one of these — maybe two if another team pays part of a salary. So the Watson update on Monday might be the most important piece of information for the Rams’ deadline plans.

Now let’s look ahead, because the Rams have a big week coming.

According to RotoWire, the Commanders beat the Eagles 27-10 on Sunday to improve to 2-4-1, and Jayden Daniels threw a touchdown to Stefon Diggs. The Rams play in Washington on Sunday, according to ESPN’s game page, at 1 p.m. Eastern on FOX. Two days later, the trade deadline hits.

And Washington will be a real test for the Rams’ secondary. Jayden Daniels can hurt you with his arm and his legs. According to RotoWire, he went 16-of-29 for 217 yards and a touchdown against the Eagles. And Stefon Diggs, a veteran receiver who knows how to beat cornerbacks, caught that touchdown. If Watson can’t play and the Rams are relying on backup cornerbacks, Washington will go right at them. That’s one more reason why the cornerback question matters this week.

And the NFC West race is tight. According to Yahoo Sports, the 49ers were on a bye and are still 6-1. The Seahawks were 4-3 heading into Monday night against the Bears. The Rams are 4-4. And the Cardinals are 3-5 after beating the Cowboys 33-30 in overtime, according to CBS Texas.

Here’s what I’ll be watching this week. Number one: McVay’s update on Watson’s shoulder and McDuffie’s back. Number two: whether the Rams open Myles Garrett’s practice window. Back in September, according to Pro Football Talk, NFL Network’s Ian Rapoport reported that the Washington game was Garrett’s most likely return date. Number three: any Rams contract restructure, which would be a sign they’re clearing space for a trade. And number four: whether the Chargers change their stance and start taking calls on Mack, Jackson and Johnston now that they’re 0-7.

So here’s my bottom line.

No, Sean McVay did not announce a shocking decision after the Chargers win. What he said was simple: “It’s not really how you start, it’s how you finish.” The Rams are 4-4, they’ve won two straight, and the defense is carrying them.

The Donte Jackson clip is another fake Schefter confirmation — one that was recorded before the game, mislabels Jackson as a quarterback, and admits that ESPN didn’t even list the Rams as a fit. What’s real is that Jackson is a trade candidate, that Albert Breer reported the Chargers had shut down trade inquiries before Sunday, and that the Chargers’ 0-7 record might change that this week.

And what’s real for the Rams is the Watson injury. If he misses time, cornerback becomes a need. But with very little cap space and limited picks, the Rams will have to choose their one move carefully.
