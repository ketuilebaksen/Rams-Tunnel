THE MYLES GARRETT DECISION

The Los Angeles Rams have officially changed course with Myles Garrett. The team has placed him on injured reserve after his limited role in the season opener against the San Francisco 49ers made it clear that the original plan simply was not working. And to understand why this is such a big deal, you have to remember how we got here.

The Rams acquired Garrett in a blockbuster trade back in June, and the excitement around that move was enormous. We're talking about one of the most dangerous and most feared defensive players in all of football — a player who changes the math for opposing offensive coordinators the moment he steps on the field. When you add a player of that caliber to a defensive front, you're not just adding sacks. You're adding double teams, chips from running backs, altered protection schemes — everything an offense does starts with the question of where that man is lined up.

But here's the part that got buried under all of that excitement: Garrett barely practiced during the summer because of the injury he was dealing with. The Rams believed they could manage the situation. The idea was to work around his limitations, protect him during the week, and still get enough from him on game day to make it all worth it. It's the kind of calculated risk teams take with elite veterans all the time.

Against San Francisco, though, that approach became much harder to justify. What the Rams saw on the field told them the plan wasn't sustainable. After the game, Sean McVay sat down with Garrett and talked through how he was actually feeling and how much he was truly able to contribute. And after seeing the situation firsthand, the Rams made the call: the plan had to change.

So Garrett underwent knee surgery, and he is now on injured reserve, which means he is expected to miss at least four games. McVay has not given a firm recovery timetable, and he deliberately avoided giving a specific description of the injury during his Monday availability. What he did say is that a window of several weeks is a realistic possibility for a return — but he does not want to put unnecessary expectations on the player before the team has all the information. So for now, the Rams are taking it day by day.

And listen — I know that's frustrating to hear as a fan. We all want a date circled on the calendar. But think about it from the team's perspective: the worst thing you can do with a player like Garrett is rush him back at eighty percent, watch him aggravate the injury, and lose him for the season. Four weeks now is a much better outcome than four months later.

THE NEXT MEN UP

Garrett going down puts immediate pressure on the rest of the outside linebacker group, and this is where things get really interesting.

Byron Young and Josaiah Stewart are expected to take on much larger roles, with Desjuan Johnson also being asked to contribute more. For Byron Young, this is a massive moment. He has flashed real pass-rush juice since entering the league, and now there's no rotation cushion — he's the guy. Every week, opposing offenses that were planning their protections around Garrett will now slide that attention elsewhere, and Young has to make them pay for it.

McVay has been particularly positive about Josaiah Stewart's development, and that's worth paying attention to, because McVay doesn't hand out that kind of praise casually. He believes Stewart can help against both the run and the pass, and now the coaching staff needs that versatility even more than they expected to. Stewart earning genuine trust from this staff this early is one of the quietly encouraging stories of this young season, and with Garrett out, that development curve just got steeper — in a good way. More snaps, more responsibility, more chances to prove that the belief is justified.

Desjuan Johnson rounds out that rotation, and while his name doesn't generate headlines, roles like his are exactly what separates deep defenses from thin ones over a seventeen-game season.

THE WESLEY BAILEY OPPORTUNITY

But there is another name that could become very, very interesting over the next few weeks: Wesley Bailey.

Bailey is an undrafted rookie who fought his way onto the initial 53-man roster after an impressive preseason — and when I say impressive, I mean genuinely eye-opening. He was active for the opener against San Francisco, but he did not play a single defensive snap, because Garrett was still available and the Rams were running their normal rotation. Now that Garrett is out, Bailey could have a real opportunity in front of him.

During the Coach McVay Show on Monday, JB Long asked McVay directly about Bailey, and the Rams head coach said the rookie is another player the team is going to be counting on. McVay explained that Bailey can contribute on special teams while continuing to learn his responsibilities within the different defensive groupings. But the important part — the part that should make Rams fans lean in — is that McVay clearly sees him as someone who needs to be ready to step up.

And there's a reason to be excited about that. Bailey was one of the most impressive players on the field during the preseason, especially in the game against the New Orleans Saints. In that game alone, he recorded ten pressures, five tackles, a sack, a forced fumble, and four quarterback hits. Ten pressures in a single game. That performance played a major role in him securing his place on the initial roster — undrafted rookies do not make NFL rosters by accident.

Now, we have to be realistic here, and I always want to keep it honest with you guys. Preseason production does not guarantee regular season success. The competition is different, the speed is different, the schemes are more disguised, and the pressure is much, much higher. Plenty of August heroes disappear in September.

But Bailey has the physical profile you want to see from an edge rusher. He stands 6-foot-4, he has 34-inch arms, and he ran a 4.69 forty-yard dash at 260 pounds. Those are legitimate NFL tools. Length, speed, and power in one frame — that's not a camp body, that's a developmental edge rusher with real upside.

The Rams will still rely heavily on Young, Stewart, and Johnson. That's the plan, and that's the right plan. But if Bailey continues to perform well in practice, there is a clear path for him to earn defensive snaps — especially in obvious passing situations where you just let him pin his ears back and go. And honestly, that could be one of the most fascinating developments to watch while Garrett is away. THE AARON DONALD QUESTION

Now — the Rams have another major defensive question, and this one involves a name that needs no introduction: Aaron Donald.

Donald did not travel for the season opener, and McVay has not yet decided whether he will be available for Monday night's game. And here's what's important to understand: McVay specifically said that Garrett's situation and Donald's situation are completely separate. One is not connected to the other. The Rams are not rushing Donald back because Garrett got hurt.

The Rams are evaluating Donald purely on his own readiness. They want to know whether he is feeling good enough to resume one-on-one work and play at the level the team expects. This is about doing it right, not doing it fast.

And when McVay was asked whether he expects Donald to perform at the same level he did before his retirement, his answer was very clear: he does. Let that sink in for a second. That is McVay's stated expectation and his level of confidence in Aaron Donald. He's not framing this as a nostalgia tour or a part-time package player. He's talking about Aaron Donald being Aaron Donald.

But until Donald is actually ready to return, the Rams have to prepare for the possibility that they continue without him. And that makes the depth on this defensive front even more important. Losing Garrett means the Rams cannot simply plug in one replacement and move on. The entire rotation has to shift, and everyone involved is going to have to carry more weight.

THE OFFENSE HAS ITS OWN HOMEWORK

At the same time, let's be honest with each other — the defense is not the only area that needs fixing.

Sean McVay identified the passing game as the biggest area of improvement after the loss to San Francisco. The Rams struggled to sustain drives, they struggled to convert in important situations, and that lack of efficiency prevented the offense from ever establishing a real rhythm. When you can't stay on the field, everything else falls apart — your defense gets tired, your play-calling gets predictable, and the game script slips away from you.

McVay also did something that matters more than people realize: he took responsibility for the problems himself, saying the improvement starts with him. That matters, because the Rams cannot afford to spend the week pointing fingers at individual players. The opener exposed problems across all three phases, and the team needs to correct them quickly and collectively.

There were real positives, though, and I don't want them to get lost. The running game showed life. McVay liked the space created by the blocking and the way the Rams were able to move the line of scrimmage. He also praised Blake Corum for what he did with his limited opportunities — efficient, decisive running that deserves more touches going forward.

On defense, Quentin Lake was another player who stood out. Lake recorded an interception, he communicated well on the back end, and he was active against the run. That's exactly the kind of steady, high-IQ presence a defense needs when it's absorbing this much change up front.

Those are important positives — but they don't erase the bigger issue. The opener was below the standard the Rams expect from themselves, and McVay does not want one loss to infect the next opportunity. His message was simple: learn from what happened, but do not allow one loss to turn into two. That response is now the biggest challenge facing this team.

THE INJURY REPORT BEYOND GARRETT

There are also several other injury situations worth monitoring this week, so let's run through them.

Safety Kam Curl dealt with ankle soreness but was able to return during the opener, and McVay said he is doing well. That's a big sigh of relief, because Curl's role in this secondary is enormous.

Inside linebacker Nate Landman is dealing with shoulder and thumb issues, but he is expected to be ready for the next game. Tight end Davis Allen also returned to action in the opener, which is another quiet positive for the offense.

And inside linebacker Omar Speights suffered a hip pointer and is dealing with swelling, but McVay said Speights is actually feeling better than expected and making progress. So while the Garrett news dominates the headlines, the rest of the injury report is trending in the right direction — and that matters when your depth is about to be tested.

THE BIG PICTURE

So there are plenty of moving pieces for the Rams right now. The defense has to figure out how to create pressure without Myles Garrett. The offense has to become more efficient through the air. The coaching staff has to manage a crowded injury report. And the younger players have to be ready the moment their opportunities arrive.

And that brings us right back to Wesley Bailey. This could be one of those classic NFL stories — where an unexpected player gets a chance because of an injury and suddenly becomes a permanent part of the team's plans. The Rams did not plan on losing Garrett this early. They did not plan on needing Bailey this soon. But that's the NFL. You build your roster for moments exactly like this one. Now it's all about execution.

Byron Young has to step up. Josaiah Stewart has to continue his development. Desjuan Johnson has to contribute. And Wesley Bailey has to show that what we saw from him during the preseason can translate when the games actually matter.

And while all of that is happening, Rams fans will be watching Aaron Donald as closely as they've ever watched anyone. If Donald returns and contributes at the level McVay expects, that obviously changes everything up front. But the Rams cannot sit around waiting for one player to solve every problem. This team has to improve collectively.

The loss to San Francisco exposed problems — but it also created a clear opportunity to see how this roster responds to adversity. That's what makes Monday night so important. Not because one game decides an entire season, but because the Rams need to show that the opener was a setback they can learn from, rather than the beginning of a bigger problem.

The passing game has to find its rhythm. The defense has to adjust without Garrett. The injuries have to be managed carefully. And players who were never expected to have major roles now have to be ready.

For me, that's the real story coming out of this week. Myles Garrett is out, and that's obviously a major blow — there's no way to spin it otherwise. But now we get to see what the Rams have behind him. We get to see what Byron Young can do with real responsibility on his shoulders. We get to see how Josaiah Stewart handles a bigger stage. We get to see whether Desjuan Johnson can make an impact. And maybe most importantly, we get to see whether Wesley Bailey can turn an eye-opening preseason into a real regular-season opportunity.

And on the other side of the ball, Sean McVay knows the passing game has to be better. The Rams cannot afford another game where they struggle to sustain drives and fail to convert when it matters. There is still plenty of football left to play — but the response starts now.
