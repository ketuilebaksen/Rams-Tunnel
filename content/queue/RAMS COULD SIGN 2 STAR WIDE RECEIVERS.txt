Where the Rams stand right now

Let’s set the scene. The Rams are 4-4 after two straight wins. Kyren Williams and Blake Corum carried the run game in the win over the Raiders. Then the defense took over against the Chargers in a 24-9 win, holding them without a touchdown, pressuring Justin Herbert on 17 of 37 dropbacks, and picking him off twice.

But there are real problems. Davante Adams is on injured reserve with a ruptured hamstring. Sean McVay said six to ten weeks, and later said he’s trending toward the shorter end. And the secondary has taken some serious hits. Jaylen Watson is out for the season with a shoulder injury and is headed to injured reserve. Trent McDuffie missed every practice this week with a back injury and was listed as questionable for Washington. Safety Kam Curl was questionable too, with a foot injury.

On the positive side, Myles Garrett is back. McVay said Friday he expects Garrett to play against Washington, barring setbacks, which would be his first game since Week 1 and potentially his first game alongside Aaron Donald. Tight end Terrance Ferguson was expected back too, but he hurt his hamstring in Friday’s practice and was ruled out.

So you’ve got a team that’s getting healthier up front, thinner in the secondary, and still missing its number two receiver. That’s the context for every trade idea we’re about to discuss.

What McVay has actually said

Before we look at any names, let’s remember what the head coach has said, because it matters more than any analyst’s list.

According to ESPN’s Sarah Barshop, McVay has indicated the Rams don’t plan to add outside players and would rather count on injured players coming back. That lines up with what he said earlier this week, when he was asked about trades: “Hopefully, some of those trades will be guys getting back from injury.”

And this week, that’s exactly what’s happening. Garrett is back. Ronnie Rivers was activated off injured reserve. Adams is trending well. Kobie Turner should be back “at some point,” in McVay’s words.

Now, coaches say things like this before the deadline all the time, and sometimes they make a trade anyway. Les Snead has made four deadline-day trades over the years. So I’m not saying the Rams will definitely stand pat. I’m saying the coach’s public stance is “we like what’s coming back,” and you should keep that in mind when you see headlines about signing two stars.

The money problem, in plain numbers

Here’s the most important number in this whole video.

According to Athlon Sports, Over The Cap lists the Rams with only a few thousand dollars in available cap space. Earlier this season, the number we kept citing was about $206,000. Even that was tiny. Now it’s even smaller after recent roster moves. Whether it’s a few thousand or a couple hundred thousand, the point is the same: the Rams can’t take on a single dollar of salary right now without making room first.

And there’s a second problem. The Browns own the Rams’ 2027 first-round pick from the Garrett trade, and the Chiefs own their 2027 third from the McDuffie trade. So the Rams don’t have their premium picks to trade either. What’s left is a 2027 second, fourth, fifth, and sixth, plus a 2028 third.

No cap space and no premium picks. That’s why every trade idea for the Rams this year comes with a “but.”

Keep that in mind, and let’s go through Athlon’s four possibilities, from least likely to most likely.

Option one: Jerry Jeudy, Browns, least likely

We’ve talked about Jerry Jeudy a lot on this channel, mostly because of the fake Schefter clips. Here’s the real picture.

Jeudy had a big 2024 season: 90 catches, 1,229 yards, and a Pro Bowl. That’s why his name keeps coming up. But this season has been rough. Per Athlon, he has 16 catches for 171 yards. His role in Cleveland has shrunk, with rookies KC Concepcion and Denzel Boston moving up the depth chart.

More importantly, the Browns aren’t shopping him. Cleveland was 5-3 when Athlon published its piece, and they’re in the AFC North race. On Thursday, Tom Pelissero reported the Browns are expected to keep Jeudy through the deadline unless they get what he called a “truly crazy offer.”

Then there’s the contract. Athlon lists Jeudy with a $10.37 million cap hit, and says Cleveland would take on $22.3 million in dead money if it traded him. For the Browns, that’s a huge reason not to move him. For the Rams, even taking on what’s left of his salary would be nearly impossible with their cap situation.

And remember the angle we talked about in an earlier video. Cleveland owns the Rams’ 2027 first-round pick, so every Rams win makes that pick less valuable to the Browns. Why would Cleveland help the Rams win more games?

My verdict: I agree with Athlon. This is the least likely of the four. Talented player, wrong situation.

Option two: Jauan Jennings, Vikings

The second name is Jauan Jennings, now with the Minnesota Vikings. Rams fans know him well from his years with the 49ers. He’s physical, he blocks, and he made a lot of tough catches against the Rams in NFC West games.

On paper, Jennings fits the McVay style. He’s willing to block in the run game, which is something McVay asks of all his receivers, and something smaller slot receivers like Josh Downs have been criticized for. Earlier this season, ESPN listed the Rams as a possible fit for him, and ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler has named him as a trade candidate.

But his Minnesota numbers have been quiet. Per Athlon, Jennings has six catches for 54 yards in six games. Athlon also reports that he turned down a chance to return to San Francisco, and that Minnesota has been working him back into the offense.

The contract is the real problem. Athlon lists Jennings with a $9.53 million cap hit and a $1.63 million base salary, and Minnesota would carry about $4.5 million in dead money if it traded him. The base salary is what the Rams would mostly take on, prorated for the rest of the season. That’s smaller than the cap hit, but it’s still much more than the Rams have right now.

My verdict: I like the physicality, and I like that he already knows how to beat NFC West defenses. But six catches in six games and a complicated contract isn’t worth the cap gymnastics. If the Rams are going to stretch their budget for a receiver, it needs to be someone who changes the offense, not a depth piece.

Option three: Dyami Brown, Commanders

Now we get to the name that makes the most financial sense, and there’s a twist with this one.

Dyami Brown of the Washington Commanders. He’s not a star, but he has the one thing the Rams’ passing game is missing: speed. Per Athlon, Brown has nine catches for 94 yards this season, including a 48-yard gain. He’s also returned three kickoffs for 82 yards. ESPN’s Aaron Schatz named him as a potential Rams target.

And the money works much better. Per Athlon, his base salary is $1.22 million, his cap hit is $1.72 million, and Washington would only carry about $250,000 in dead money if it traded him. The Rams would still need to clear some room, but this is a much smaller lift than Jeudy or Jennings.

Here’s why speed matters so much. According to Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak, the Rams’ explosive pass play rate through eight weeks was 4.1 percent, down from 17.5 percent at this point last year. Since Adams got hurt, they’d had exactly one completion longer than 20 yards, the 40-yarder to Tutu Atwell against the Chargers. Stafford’s average depth of target over the last four weeks ranked 25th of 27 quarterbacks. The Rams don’t need another short-area receiver. They need someone who makes a safety back up.

Now the twist. The Rams play Washington on Sunday, two days before the deadline. That’s a fun coincidence, but it doesn’t mean anything is being negotiated. And there’s one more thing the video didn’t mention: Dyami Brown was ruled out for Sunday’s game with a calf injury, per Washington’s final injury report. So if the Rams were looking at him, they wouldn’t get to see him play against them first, and any team trading for him right now would be getting a receiver with a calf issue.

My verdict, and this is not a report: of the three receivers, Brown makes the most sense on paper. He’s cheap, he has speed, and he can help on returns. But the calf injury makes it less appealing in the short term, and Washington, at 2-4-1, would have to want to move him. I’d call this possible, not likely.

Option four: trading Emmanuel Forbes, the most likely according to Athlon

The fourth possibility is different, because it’s a Rams player going out, not coming in. Athlon ranked it as the most likely move of all four.

Emmanuel Forbes Jr. is a cornerback, not a quarterback, as the original video said. He was Washington’s first-round pick in 2023. Washington moved on from him in 2024, and the Rams claimed him off waivers. In limited snaps this year, he’s played well. Per Athlon, he has an 80.8 PFF grade and has allowed a 77.6 passer rating on eight catches against him.

ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler named Forbes as a possible trade candidate, saying he had “lost his starting job due to the additions of Trent McDuffie and Jaylen Watson.” The Rams also declined his fifth-year option, so he isn’t part of their long-term plan.

Here’s why Athlon likes this move. Forbes has a $2.85 million cap hit. Per Athlon’s math, trading him for a late-round pick would increase the Rams’ cap space to about $2.86 million. That alone could open enough room to bring in a cheap receiver like Dyami Brown. So in Athlon’s view, this is the move that makes another move possible.

But there’s a problem, and the video spotted it too. Fowler’s idea was based on Forbes being a backup behind McDuffie and Watson. That’s not the situation anymore. Watson is out for the season. McDuffie missed every practice this week. Against Washington, Josh Wallace was set to start in Watson’s spot, with Forbes moving up to the number three cornerback. If McDuffie misses time, Forbes could be a starter.

So trading Forbes now means trading away one of your few healthy cornerbacks in the same week your secondary got thinner. That’s fixing the receiver room by opening a hole somewhere else.

My take: the cap logic is clever, and on a normal week I’d understand it. But not this week. Unless the Rams bring in another cornerback at the same time, trading Forbes creates a bigger problem than it solves.

The second story: Donte Jackson, Chargers

That brings us to the second big idea, and it goes right at the cornerback problem.

CBS Sports analyst JP Acosta, as relayed by Heavy, named Donte Jackson of the Chargers as a realistic trade target for the Rams. Quick correction to the original video: Jackson is a cornerback, not a quarterback. And you might remember his name from this channel. A fake Schefter clip claimed the Rams had traded for him a couple of weeks ago, and we broke it down. That trade never happened. This time, it’s a real analyst making a real suggestion.

The reasoning makes sense. Watson is out for the season, McDuffie is banged up, and the Rams need another experienced corner next to Forbes and Wallace.

Jackson has plenty of experience. He’s played in 116 NFL games and started 109. He has 23 career interceptions, 373 tackles, four forced fumbles, three fumble recoveries, and a sack. Carolina drafted him in the second round in 2018. The Athletic’s Daniel Popper also called him an interesting trade option because of his versatility and manageable cost. He’s in the last year of a two-year, $13 million deal.

But there are two big catches.

Catch one: he’s hurt. Jackson has missed the Chargers’ last three games with a hamstring injury. If you’re trading for a cornerback because you need help right now, a player who’s been sidelined for three straight games is a risky bet.

Catch two: the Chargers aren’t selling. The Chargers are 0-7, but they’ve been clear. On October 30th, Albert Breer reported they had “shut down trade inquiries” and were “unwilling to wave any sort of white flag.” Ian Rapoport said this week that GM Joe Hortiz “has shown no indication that he wants to sell at all.” Jim Harbaugh said he wants Khalil Mack to stay. That’s a team telling you it isn’t holding a fire sale.

And of course, the money. Jackson’s contract is bigger than anything the Rams can absorb with almost no cap space. To make it work, the Rams would need a restructure, or would need to send salary back, maybe even Forbes.

Now, here’s something interesting. If you combine the two stories, there’s a version where it all fits. This is purely my thought experiment, not a report. The Rams trade Forbes to create cap room, then use that room on a healthy veteran corner, and maybe restructure Kyren Williams’ deal to add a cheap receiver too. That’s three moves in four days, with a team that’s barely made any all year. Possible? Sure. Likely? I don’t think so.

The names missing from this list

There’s something interesting about Athlon’s four options: two of the receiver names that have come up most in real reporting this month aren’t on the list at all.

Josh Downs, Colts. Downs requested a trade, per ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler, and told ESPN he’s “open to whatever.” ESPN’s Ben Solak proposed a Rams deal: Downs plus a 2027 fifth-round pick for a 2028 third. Adam Schefter reported the Colts want a third-round pick, though a fourth might get it done. The Steelers and Chiefs are considered the main suitors, and Jordan Schultz reported the Colts plan to wait until after this weekend to make more trades, hoping offers go up. Downs is on a cheap rookie deal in its final year, so the money is much easier than Jeudy or Jennings. The big question about him is blocking. Solak wrote that his “biggest hang-up in trading Downs to anyone is that he has to be protected from blocking responsibilities.”

Brian Thomas Jr., Jaguars. The deep threat. ESPN’s Bill Barnwell called him the “exciting” trade option for the Rams. He’s struggled badly this season and was benched in Jacksonville, but he has the kind of speed the Rams are missing. The problem is price. He’d likely cost the most in draft capital of anyone on the board.

Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak also wrote that the Rams have called about both Downs and Thomas, without naming a source. Calling about a player isn’t the same as negotiating, but it’s the closest thing we have to a real Rams link this week.

So if you’re keeping a list at home, here’s my full receiver board, ranked by how realistic I think each one is. Again, this is my opinion, not a report.

One: Josh Downs. A selling team, a player who wants out, and a cheap contract.

Two: Dyami Brown. Cheap and fast, but hurt and on a team that just played the Rams.

Three: Brian Thomas Jr. The best fit for the deep-ball problem, but the most expensive.

Four: Jauan Jennings. Physical, but a tough contract and very little production.

Five: Jerry Jeudy. Not available, unless Cleveland gets a crazy offer.

The defense might decide the deadline

One more angle the video didn’t cover. The Rams’ deadline plan might depend less on the receivers and more on what the defense looks like with Garrett back.

Think about it. Before Sunday, the Rams were tied for 26th in the league with just 12 sacks, per ESPN. They got pressure on Herbert last week, but they haven’t been finishing. Now they add Garrett, who set the NFL single-season record with 23 sacks in 2025 and won Defensive Player of the Year. Rams On SI projected him at around half the defensive snaps in his first game back, building up from there. And Garrett said himself that with him and Aaron Donald on the field together, “offenses really have to pick their poison.”

If the pass rush becomes elite, it covers up a lot of problems in the secondary. Quarterbacks have less time to find the weak spot in coverage. That could make the front office more comfortable not trading for a cornerback, and maybe more comfortable keeping Forbes as depth.

So when you watch the Washington game, don’t just watch the receivers. Watch how quickly Jayden Daniels has to get rid of the ball. That might tell you more about Tuesday than anything else.

So what should the Rams do?

Let’s put this all together, and I’ll give you my honest opinion.

First, don’t panic over the receiver room. Adams is trending toward the short end of his six-to-ten-week window, which could put him back around the end of November. A trade for a receiver now is really a trade for about three or four weeks of help, plus depth for December and January. That’s worth a late-round pick, not a big package.

Second, cornerback might be the bigger need now. A week ago, I’d have said receiver was the top priority. After the Watson news and McDuffie’s back issue, I’m not sure anymore. If McDuffie misses multiple games, the Rams’ pass defense is in real trouble against teams like Seattle, San Francisco, and Green Bay down the stretch.

Third, the cheapest move is the one that’s already happening. Garrett’s return is the biggest “acquisition” of the season. If the pass rush gets home faster, the secondary has less to cover. That’s McVay’s whole bet.

Fourth, any trade starts with a restructure. With almost no cap space, the Rams can’t do anything until they create room. A Kyren Williams restructure has been estimated at around $3.5 million. Trading Forbes would add roughly $2.85 million. If you see a restructure reported on Monday, that’s your sign a deal might be coming.

So my ranking of what’s most likely to happen by Tuesday at 4 p.m.:

Most likely: nothing major. The Rams ride with Garrett coming back, Adams on the way, and Turner after that.

Second most likely: a small move, a late-round pick for a cheap depth receiver or a healthy cornerback, probably paired with a restructure.

Least likely: two “star” receivers. That’s not happening with this cap sheet.

How to read deadline headlines this week

We’ve seen a flood of fake clips and misleading titles this week. Here’s how to read them.

“Could sign” usually means an analyst suggested it. Today’s headline is a good example. The actual content is four ideas from Athlon and one from CBS. That’s not reporting.

Check the contract. If a headline says the Rams are adding a player, ask how they’d pay for him. With almost no cap space, every addition needs a matching move.

Check the injury report. Two of the players in today’s video, Dyami Brown and Donte Jackson, are dealing with injuries. A real trade report would mention that.

Check what McVay said. The head coach says the plan is to count on returning players. Until someone credible reports otherwise, that’s the plan.

The Washington game and what it means for Tuesday

The Rams play Washington today, Sunday at 1 p.m. Eastern on FOX. Whatever happens in Landover, this game will shape the deadline.

If the passing game finally hits a few explosive plays against a Washington team that CBS said needs cornerback help, the urgency for a receiver goes down. If it’s another day of short throws and screens, expect the receiver talk to get loud again Monday.

If McDuffie plays and the secondary holds up against Jayden Daniels, the Rams can probably stand pat at corner. If Washington picks on Wallace and Forbes, the cornerback conversation will be the main story heading into Tuesday.

And if Garrett looks like the 2025 Defensive Player of the Year, even for half the snaps, the Rams’ best deadline move might turn out to be the one they made back in June.
