Part one: the final score and the big picture

Let’s start with the numbers that matter.

Rams 34, Cardinals 6. The Rams are now 6-4. That’s four straight wins: the Raiders, the Chargers 24-9, Washington 20-13, and now Arizona 34-6.

The Cardinals fall to 3-7. And according to ESPN, that was Arizona’s 11th straight home loss. They haven’t won a home game in a very long time.

And think about who the Rams were missing. Puka Nacua, on IR with his knee injury. Davante Adams, on IR with his hamstring. Terrance Ferguson, still on IR. Steve Avila, out with a hamstring injury. That’s two star receivers, a young tight end, and a starting guard.

And they still put up 34 points and 436 total yards: 249 passing and 202 rushing. They had five sacks and two interceptions on defense.

That’s not a team surviving. That’s a team dominating.

Part two: the slow start

Let’s be fair here. The game didn’t start pretty.

Arizona got the ball first and drove for a 49-yard field goal from Chad Ryland. Cardinals 3, Rams 0.

The Rams answered with a short Brandon McManus field goal from 24 yards. Tied at 3.

Then Ryland hit another 49-yarder late in the first quarter. Cardinals 6, Rams 3.

And that’s where it stayed for a long time. The Rams offense was trying to figure out life without Puka. Stafford also threw an interception, picked off by Cody Simon.

One of the ESPN analysts made a great point about this. He said the Rams lean on Puka so much that “it’s almost like he’s a running back the way they use him.” He’s the guy they go to on short throws, screens, and third downs. Without him, McVay had to “feel themselves out a little bit.” And it took a while.

But once McVay figured out what Arizona was doing, everything changed.

Part three: four touchdowns in about ten minutes

This is where the game turned into a blowout.

According to ESPN, the Rams scored four touchdowns in just over ten minutes of game time between the second and third quarters. Let’s go through each one.

Touchdown one: Tyler Higbee. With about a minute left in the first half, Stafford hit Higbee for a 5-yard touchdown. Rams 10, Cardinals 6. The veteran tight end Stafford trusts most, scoring the first touchdown. Exactly what we talked about before the game.

Touchdown two: Xavier Smith. This was the play that broke Arizona. Kamren Kinchens intercepted Carson Beck and returned it 33 yards. Four plays later, with barely any time left in the half, the Rams looked like they might settle for a field goal. Instead, McVay called a quick screen to Xavier Smith. And Smith broke a tackle and took it 22 yards for a touchdown. Rams 17, Cardinals 6, at halftime.

That’s the first touchdown of Xavier Smith’s career. The guy the Rams signed off the 49ers practice squad. “X-Man,” as McVay calls him. What a moment.

One ESPN analyst explained why that play mattered so much. If the Rams kick a field goal there, Arizona is still in the game. Instead, the Cardinals went to the locker room “demoralized a little bit.”

Touchdown three: Blake Corum. The Rams got the ball to start the second half and finished the drive with a 1-yard Corum touchdown run. Rams 24, Cardinals 6.

Touchdown four: Davis Allen. Then Stafford found Davis Allen for a 19-yard touchdown. Rams 31, Cardinals 6.

Remember, Davis Allen played through a back injury against Washington. McVay called him “tough as hell.” And now he’s catching touchdown passes.

Then McManus added a 43-yard field goal early in the fourth quarter to make it 34-6. And that was it.

Three touchdown catches by three different players, all of them tight ends or backup receivers. Plus a Corum touchdown run. That’s what a team effort looks like.

The Xavier Smith story

I want to stop for a second on Xavier Smith, because his story is one of the best of the season.

Just a couple of weeks ago, Smith was on the 49ers practice squad. The Rams brought him back because they needed help with Puka out and Jordan Whittington on IR. McVay was happy to have him, calling him “X-Man” and listing him as one of the kick return options with Brennan Presley and Ronnie Rivers.

And in his first real chance on offense, he scores the biggest touchdown of the game. A quick screen, a broken tackle, 22 yards, and the end zone, right before halftime. The first touchdown of his NFL career.

Think about what that means for a player like Smith. Practice squad players live week to week. They don’t know if they’ll be on a roster next month. And now he has a career touchdown, on a play that basically decided a divisional game.

That’s also a credit to McVay. He didn’t just call a screen. He called a screen to a guy he trusted, at a moment when a lot of coaches would have kicked the field goal and gone to halftime.

Part four: Stafford’s day

Matthew Stafford finished 18 of 26 for 249 yards, three touchdowns, and one interception.

And here’s the stat I love most. Nine different players caught a pass from Stafford in this game.

Think about that. No Puka. No Adams. And Stafford still spread the ball to nine different guys. That’s a quarterback who isn’t forcing anything. He’s just taking what the defense gives him.

Some of the key throws:

Konata Mumpfield had catches of 27 and 44 yards. Those are big plays down the field, and they came off play-action, exactly the way the ESPN crew described.

Tutu Atwell had a 31-yard catch on a fourth-and-6. That’s a huge conversion. Fourth down, game on the line in the first half, and Atwell makes the play.

CJ Daniels caught the first pass of his NFL career. McVay said on Friday that Daniels “plays big at the catch point” and has a “mature presence.” Now he’s on the board.

Higbee, Smith, and Allen each caught a touchdown.

Remember what McVay said before the game? Stafford’s “steadiness is the key thing.” He said Stafford keeps “everybody nice and calm” in the huddle. Well, that’s exactly what happened. A huddle full of young receivers, and Stafford made them all look good.

Part five: Blake Corum is taking over

Now let’s talk about the player ESPN couldn’t stop talking about.

Blake Corum had 17 carries for 100 yards and a touchdown. He also caught three passes for 19 yards. That’s his second 100-yard game of the season.

One of the ESPN analysts said Corum has been the “one consistent thing for this offense.” He said Corum has six straight games with a rushing touchdown. And he said Corum is “starting to steal away some carries from Kyren Williams” and could become “maybe 1A as the season’s rolling on.”

That’s a big statement. Kyren Williams has been the Rams’ lead back for years. But Corum is playing so well that he’s forcing his way into a bigger role.

And it’s not just about Corum. The other analyst gave the Rams backfield a great nickname. She calls Corum and Kyren “the Twins.” But she said there’s actually “a triplet,” Ronnie Rivers.

Rivers had 15 carries for 81 yards and helped close out the game. ESPN even said he got more carries than Kyren in this one. Rivers was an undrafted free agent out of Fresno State, and he’s been with the Rams for a while without getting many chances. Now he’s getting them.

So the Rams have three running backs who can carry the ball. And that’s the key to surviving without Puka and Adams.

The ESPN crew said it perfectly. Even when the receivers were healthy, maybe the offense should have flowed more through the backs and the tight ends. Now, because of the injuries, it’s forced to. And it’s working.

When the run game works, the play-action game opens up. That’s how Mumpfield and Atwell got those big catches. And that’s how the tight ends get open on crossing routes.

McVay’s creative offense

Let’s talk about how McVay actually built this offense without Puka and Adams, because this is where the coaching really showed.

According to the game coverage, one key was a two-running-back look, with Kyren Williams and Blake Corum on the field at the same time. That’s not something you see every week in the NFL. But it makes sense here. If your best receivers are hurt, why not get your best playmakers on the field together, even if they’re both running backs?

When both backs are out there, the defense has a problem. Who gets the ball? Is it a run? Is it a screen? Is one of them going out for a pass? It creates confusion, and confusion creates space.

Then there were the tight ends. Higbee and Allen both scored. The ESPN crew said the run game “creates space for these tight ends on those crossing routes,” where they can catch the ball on the move.

And then the deep shots. Once Arizona had to respect the run, Stafford hit Mumpfield for 27 and 44 yards, and Atwell for 31 on fourth down. That’s play-action at its best.

So the plan was simple, even if the execution was creative. Run the ball. Use the tight ends. Throw deep off play-action. And get the ball to playmakers in space, like Smith on that screen.

McVay is going to use the bye week to come up with more ideas around the players he has. Based on this game, I can’t wait to see them.

Part six: the defense against Carson Beck

Now the defense.

Arizona started rookie quarterback Carson Beck in his first NFL game. And the Rams made it a very long day for him.

Beck finished 11 of 18 for just 108 yards and two interceptions. The Rams sacked Arizona five times.

The interceptions. Kamren Kinchens had one and returned it 33 yards, setting up the Xavier Smith touchdown. Wesley Bailey had the other, the first interception of his career, on a pass meant for Tyler Allgeier.

The sacks. Five sacks in total. Aaron Donald got his first sack of the season, and it forced a punt. Tyler Davis also got his first sack of the season.

The forced fumble. Shaun Dolac punched the ball out with a “peanut punch,” and that set up McManus’s 43-yard field goal.

The original video also says Trent McDuffie and Kam Curl were back in the secondary for this game. I couldn’t confirm their full stats yet, so I’ll leave it there. But if both of them were back, that’s huge for this defense going forward.

Now, let’s be fair to Beck. The ESPN crew made some good points. He was missing a lot of help. They said he had a third-string center, a backup left tackle, and he was missing receivers like Michael Wilson and other key weapons. One analyst said he didn’t really have anyone to throw to.

They also said his receivers didn’t help him. On one play, Beck had a receiver open for what would have been a big gain, maybe 55 yards, and the receiver fell down. And on one of the interceptions, they said the receiver should have caught the ball.

So this wasn’t all on Beck. One analyst said it was like he was “fighting with two arms behind your back.”

But here’s the thing. The Rams don’t care about any of that. Their job was to make a rookie quarterback uncomfortable, and they did. Two picks, five sacks, a forced fumble, and six points allowed.

Special teams finally helped

One more area that deserves credit: special teams.

Before this game, the Rams were so short on returners that Kyren Williams was returning punts against Washington. That’s why they promoted Brennan Presley and brought back Xavier Smith.

In this game, Ronnie Rivers had a 34-yard kick return. That’s the kind of play that gives the offense a short field. And McManus was solid with field goals of 24 and 43 yards.

Then there was the forced fumble from Shaun Dolac that set up the 43-yarder. That’s three points the Rams got because one player kept fighting for the ball.

Special teams won’t always make the headlines. But when your offense is missing its two best receivers, small edges like this matter a lot.

Part seven: ESPN’s debate about Beck’s body language

This was an interesting part of the ESPN segment, even though it’s about Arizona.

One analyst said Beck “looked a little disinterested” and that the team’s struggles seemed to be “weighing him down.” He said a quarterback has to be “the motivator” and “the energy guy on the sideline,” even as a rookie.

But another analyst, who has worked in front offices, disagreed. He said: “I don’t think he was disinterested. I think that’s just his demeanor.” He pointed out that Beck was the same way at Georgia. He called him “a cool, calm, collected person.” And he said a rookie third-round pick probably has to earn the right to be loud with veterans, and “the way you earn that is by playing better.”

I think that’s a fair point. Not every quarterback is a yeller. Stafford isn’t a yeller either. McVay praises Stafford for being calm. So I wouldn’t judge Beck too much on one game.

But for the Rams, it doesn’t matter. They did their job.

Part eight: the injuries we need to watch

Okay, now the part of the game that worries me a little.

The Rams already had Steve Avila out at guard. Then, in this game, they lost two more offensive linemen.

Coleman Shelton suffered a hamstring injury and was ruled out at the start of the second half.

Dylan McMahon suffered a knee injury and was also ruled out at the start of the second half.

So the Rams had to shuffle the line. Beaux Limmer moved from left guard to center, and Bill Murray came in at left guard.

And here’s what’s impressive. The Rams scored two of their touchdowns after those injuries, with a patched-together offensive line. That’s a credit to the coaches and to the backup linemen.

But going forward, this could be a real problem. The offensive line was already down Avila. Now there are two more question marks. We’ll need to hear from McVay about how serious these injuries are.

This is not a report, it’s my concern: if Shelton and McMahon miss time, the line becomes the biggest worry on this team. Protecting Stafford is the most important thing this offense does. We’ll find out more when McVay gives updates this week.

Part eleven: what ESPN got right

I want to give the ESPN crew credit, because they were balanced.

They didn’t crown the Rams. One analyst said: “I think we need to remember again, this was the Arizona Cardinals.” He pointed out that Arizona was starting a rookie and had “a lot of missing pieces.” And he said: “I’m not ready to crown the Los Angeles Rams quite yet.”

Another analyst said if the Rams play the way they did in the first half against Green Bay, Kansas City, and San Francisco, “they’re probably not going to win those games.” He said, “They got to be better than what they were today.”

And I agree with that. The first half was slow. The offense struggled until McVay found the right adjustments. Against the Packers, Chiefs, and 49ers, the Rams won’t get as many free gifts.

But ESPN also gave McVay a lot of credit. One analyst said: “I thought Sean McVay did a great job.” He said McVay “adjusted to what Arizona was doing.” And they all agreed the run game is “what’s going to carry the Rams moving forward.”

That’s the right way to look at this win. It’s a great result. It’s proof the Rams can win without Puka and Adams. But it’s not proof they can beat the best teams without them. That test comes next.

Part twelve: what comes next

Here’s the schedule.

The Rams now get an abbreviated bye. Then they host the Green Bay Packers on the Wednesday before Thanksgiving. That’s a big national game, and it comes on a short week after the bye.

After that, the Chiefs come to SoFi Stadium. Then the Rams go to San Francisco on December 13.

That’s three tough games in a row. Puka will miss all three. And Davante Adams, according to McVay, has a “realistic timeline” of returning after the Green Bay game. So Adams could be back for the Chiefs or the 49ers.

The bye also helps a lot of players get healthier. Terrance Ferguson gets more time for his hamstring. Avila gets more time. Garrett gets more time to keep building. And hopefully Shelton and McMahon get a chance to recover.

So this bye is coming at the perfect time. The Rams are 6-4, they’ve won four straight, and they’re getting healthier.
