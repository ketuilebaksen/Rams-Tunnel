Part two: the clip’s own contradiction

There’s one part of this clip I want to highlight, because the narrator actually caught a problem in his own story, and it shows you exactly how these fake clips are made.

The clip says the Rams’ package for Downs is a fourth-round pick plus Emmanuel Forbes. But it also says there’s a separate “Schefter read” where Forbes goes in a package for Calvin Ridley of the Titans. And then the narrator says, “The Rams cannot send Forbes twice.”

He’s right. And that’s the tell. These clips are built by taking pieces from different articles and gluing them together. One article floated Forbes as a trade chip. Another listed Downs as a target. Another listed Ridley. The clip puts all of them in Schefter’s mouth, and they don’t fit together, because no real insider said any of it.

There’s another giveaway. The clip calls Forbes a “young quarterback.” Forbes is a cornerback. He was Washington’s first-round pick at cornerback in 2023. If you can’t get a player’s position right, you’re not reading from a real report.

And the clip lists the Rams’ draft currency as “a fourth, a fifth, a sixth, and a cornerback.” It leaves out the Rams’ 2027 second-round pick. The Rams don’t have their 2027 first, which Cleveland owns from the Garrett trade, their 2027 third, which Kansas City owns from the McDuffie trade, or their 2028 second. But they do have a 2027 second. Small details like that tell you nobody checked.

The Chiefs were interested in receivers. Partly real. CBS reported the Chiefs showed interest in Brian Thomas Jr. The clip’s claim that Kansas City was ahead of everyone for Downs isn’t attributed to anyone, and even the clip admits that.

Part five: what actually happened on deadline day

Let’s leave the fake clip behind and look at what really happened. This is the part that matters.

The Rams made no trades. On Monday, McVay said, “I would never rule it out.” But when 4 p.m. Eastern came on Tuesday, the Rams hadn’t made a deal.

Puka is likely headed to IR. McVay said Puka suffered “an MCL, PCL sprain, also turf toe,” that he’ll “be out for a good amount of time,” and that “it’s a strong chance that we put him on IR. So we’re thinking it’s going to be at least a month.”

Jordan Whittington went to injured reserve. He’d been battling back soreness, and the Rams placed him on IR. Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak suggested his season may be over, because the Rams may not have enough IR return designations left to bring him back. That’s Grisak’s assessment, not an official ruling. McVay did say Monday they’re “getting to be where that number is getting so high that we’re going to run out of those eight to be able to return.”

Xavier Smith is back. The Rams signed him off the 49ers’ practice squad. He was with the Rams earlier this season, and Grisak expects him to handle kickoff returns, maybe punt returns too.

The 49ers traded for Keenan Allen. San Francisco sent the Colts a 2027 sixth-round pick for Allen, reported first by NFL Network and ESPN. Remember the fake clip last week that said Allen was going to the Rams for a sixth? The price was right. The team was wrong.

Josh Downs stayed put, as far as I can find. CBS’s deadline coverage said the Colts were open to moving him “at the right price,” and that Pittsburgh reportedly wouldn’t make a big move. I haven’t seen any report of a Downs trade.

Brian Thomas Jr. stayed put, as far as I can find. CBS reported the Chiefs showed interest, but Jacksonville was cautious about trading him to an AFC contender.

So the real deadline story for the Rams is: no trade, Puka likely on IR, Whittington on IR, and Xavier Smith back. That’s it.

Part six: was standing pat the right call?

Now that the deadline’s over, here’s my honest opinion, and this is opinion, not reporting.

The case for standing pat. The Rams have almost no cap space. They don’t have their 2027 first or third. Any receiver would have needed a restructure or a player going the other way. Puka is expected back in about a month, and Adams after that. A trade would’ve bought a few weeks of help at the cost of future flexibility. And the Rams’ big moves already happened: Garrett in June, McDuffie in March.

The case against. The Rams are now missing their top three receivers: Puka, Adams, and Whittington. Outside of Puka, the receivers combined for four catches against Washington. Per Rams Wire’s Cameron DaSilva, Konata Mumpfield has 162 receiving yards this season and Tutu Atwell has 99. And the 49ers got Keenan Allen for a sixth-round pick. The Rams own a 2027 sixth.

My verdict. I understand the decision, but I think the Rams left a little on the table. A cheap receiver, even just for the next month, would have given Stafford one more option while Puka and Adams heal. Now the Rams are betting on the run game, the tight ends, and the defense to carry them until December.

Part seven: who catches passes now?

Here’s what the Rams’ pass-catching group looks like heading into Arizona.

Receivers: Konata Mumpfield, Tutu Atwell, CJ Daniels, and Xavier Smith. Grisak named Mumpfield, Atwell, and Daniels as the receivers who should take on bigger roles.

Tight ends: Tyler Higbee. Davis Allen, who McVay called “tough as hell” after he played through a back injury against Washington. Colby Parkinson, if his ribs are okay. Terrance Ferguson is dealing with a hamstring injury.

So here’s what I expect. The Rams already use more two- and three-tight-end sets than almost anyone in the league. Without Puka, the tight ends will probably be Stafford’s main targets. Atwell is the one real deep threat, and he had the Rams’ longest pass play since Adams got hurt, a 40-yard catch against the Chargers. And Kyren Williams and Blake Corum will carry the offense on the ground, like they have for three straight weeks with 192, 129, and 124 rushing yards.

McVay said Monday, “There are other guys that are very capable of doing some good stuff for us, and we’ll look to see those guys step up.”

The Josh Downs saga, start to finish

Since Downs has been the subject of four fake clips, let’s walk through the real timeline, so you can see how the real reporting compares with the fake versions.

Late October. ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler reported that Downs requested a trade from the Colts. Downs told ESPN he’s “open to whatever.” The Colts had already started selling, and they fired general manager Chris Ballard on October 19th.

Early November. Adam Schefter reported the Colts’ asking price: a third-round pick, though a fourth might get it done. Notice what that is: a price, not a destination. ESPN’s Ben Solak proposed a Rams deal, Downs and a 2027 fifth for a 2028 third, but that was an analyst’s idea, not a report. ESPN listed the Steelers and Chiefs as the main suitors.

The week before the deadline. Jordan Schultz reported the Colts planned to wait until after Week 9 to make more trades, hoping the price would go up. Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak wrote that the Rams had called about Downs and Brian Thomas Jr., without naming a source.

Deadline day. CBS reported the Colts were open to moving Downs “at the right price,” and that Pittsburgh reportedly wasn’t going to make a big move. As far as I can find, no trade happened.

Now compare that with the four fake clips. The first one, a couple of weeks ago, claimed a done deal too. The second said a fourth-round pick plus Forbes. The third said an offer including a fourth had come in, and that the Rams would send a 2028 fourth plus Forbes. And this one said a 2027 fourth plus Forbes, “before tomorrow’s deadline.”

Every real report was about price and interest. Every fake clip was about a done deal. That difference is the whole lesson.

Emmanuel Forbes: the most-traded player who was never traded

Here’s a funny thing about this deadline. Emmanuel Forbes was “traded” in at least three fake clips this week, for Downs twice and for Ridley once. And he never actually went anywhere.

The real story behind that: ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler floated Forbes as a trade candidate because he had “lost his starting job due to the additions of Trent McDuffie and Jaylen Watson.” Athlon Sports then calculated that trading him, with his $2.85 million cap hit, would raise the Rams’ cap space to about $2.86 million.

But then Jaylen Watson went down for the season, and McDuffie missed last week’s game with a back injury. Forbes moved up to the number three corner and played against Washington, the team that drafted him. The Rams held Washington to 13 points.

So the Rams kept him, and I think that was right. With the secondary this thin, Forbes isn’t a trade chip anymore. He’s a key depth piece.

Part eight: lessons from fifteen fake clips

Since the deadline is over, I want to look back at all fifteen fake clips we covered, because there’s a lesson here for every fan.

The names: Jerry Jeudy twice. Vita Vea twice. George Pickens. Josh Downs four times. A’Shawn Robinson. Donte Jackson. Amik Robertson. Khalil Mack twice. Keenan Allen.

The result: Zero of those trades happened. Not one.

The pattern: Every clip used the same format. A fake date. A fake Schefter quote ending with “I can confirm this trade is going to happen.” A family or neighbor story to make it feel personal. A running joke. A fake skeptic. A Stephen A. Smith bit. And then, inside the clip itself, a hedge admitting it was “expected, not official.”

The trick: Every clip mixed real facts with fake ones. Real trade requests, real cap numbers, real analyst proposals, real injuries. Then it attached Schefter’s name and the Rams to the parts that weren’t true.

The funny part: Some of them got eerily close. The Keenan Allen clip said a sixth-round pick, and Allen really did get traded for a sixth, just to the 49ers. These channels pick believable prices from real analysis. They just pick the wrong team.

So here’s the rule for the future, including the offseason, when the fake clips will come back: if a trade is real, it shows up on Adam Schefter’s or Ian Rapoport’s actual X accounts first, in one or two short sentences. If you can only find it in a 15-minute YouTube video with a story about paint, it isn’t real.

Part nine: the Puka timeline and the IR crunch

One more real story worth understanding, because it affects the rest of the season.

McVay said Puka will be out “at least a month.” If he goes on IR, he must miss at least four games. The Rams play Arizona this Sunday, then have their bye, then play Green Bay. So Puka’s return would likely come in December.

But there’s a catch. McVay said the Rams are running low on IR return designations. He mentioned “those eight,” and said “that number is getting so high that we’re going to run out.”

Let’s count the players who need, or used, return slots. Myles Garrett used one when he came back last week. Running back Ronnie Rivers used one. Davante Adams will need one. Kobie Turner will need one. Puka will need one. And there may be others.

That’s why the Rams are trying to keep Steve Avila, who has a hamstring strain, off IR if they can. And it’s why Grisak thinks Whittington’s season may be over.

So the Rams have to be smart about who they bring back, and when. It’s one more reason the deadline approach was so careful. Every roster spot and every IR slot matters right now.

Keenan Allen and the December 13th game

One more real deadline story that affects the Rams directly.

The 49ers got Keenan Allen for a 2027 sixth-round pick. He has 40 catches for 351 yards and a touchdown this season, and 1,095 catches for 12,402 yards in his career. NFL.com wrote that he “isn’t as steady as he once was,” but “can be counted on for third downs.”

The Rams play the 49ers in San Francisco on December 13th. So Allen could be lined up against this Rams secondary in one of the biggest division games of the season.

But there’s a wild card. Back in September, Adam Schefter reported that Allen faces a minimum three-game NFL suspension stemming from his August arrest, and that the league is expected to wait for his legal case to finish before imposing it. If that suspension lands in December, the 49ers could lose him for that game. If it’s delayed, they get him for the stretch run.

Either way, it’s a reminder that the 49ers added a receiver at the deadline and the Rams didn’t.

Chicago sent two first-round picks to Tennessee for Jeffery Simmons.

Detroit added Andre Cisco from the Jets.

San Francisco added DeForest Buckner earlier and now Keenan Allen.

Seattle added Quenton Nelson from the Colts.

Philadelphia sold, sending Jordan Davis to Jacksonville for a 2027 first and a 2029 second.

Most of the NFC’s top teams added someone. The Rams did their adding in the offseason, and now they’re counting on getting healthy.

Three questions for Sunday

One: who leads the Rams in targets? Puka had 12 targets against the Chargers and 19 against Washington. Without him, does one receiver step up, or does it spread across the tight ends?

Two: does Xavier Smith help the return game? With Whittington out, kickoff returns matter. Whittington averaged 26.3 yards per return, per Grisak. Can Smith match that?

Three: how much does Garrett play? McVay said his snap count will increase “as he’s feeling better.” If Garrett plays most of the snaps and gets home again, the Rams can win a low-scoring game.

Part ten: Arizona preview

The Rams play at Arizona on Sunday. Then they get their bye.

Arizona beat the Rams 18-16 on October 18th, the game where Davante Adams got hurt. Now the Rams return without Adams, without Puka, without Whittington, and maybe without Avila.

The Rams’ strengths: The defense. Garrett had three sacks, three tackles for loss, and a strip-sack in his first game back. McVay said Garrett and Aaron Donald’s first game together was “unscouted,” and that Donald “had a couple awesome things that allowed him to be able to get some one-on-ones.” And McVay said Trent McDuffie is “making good progress” with his back and could be back this week. The Rams have held their last two opponents to 9 and 13 points.

The Rams’ weaknesses: The passing game. Stafford threw 43 times for 219 yards against Washington, and that was with Puka.

The plan: Run the ball. Use the tight ends. Take a shot or two to Atwell. Let Garrett and Donald win the game up front. Don’t turn the ball over.

My prediction: Rams 20, Cardinals 16. Ugly, close, and exactly the kind of game this team has learned to win. And a 6-4 record going into the bye.

What’s next for Josh Downs

Here’s the funny ending to this whole saga. If Downs really did stay in Indianapolis, he can’t be traded again until after the season. So the next time you hear his name with the Rams, it’ll be in free agency.

Downs is in the final year of his rookie contract. That means next spring, he could hit the open market, and any team, including the Rams, could sign him without giving up a single draft pick. If the Rams still need a slot receiver in March, and if their cap situation allows it, that would be a far cheaper way to get him than a deadline trade.

That’s speculation, not a report. But it’s a good reminder that the deadline isn’t the only chance to add a player. Sometimes the smart move is to wait.

And when the free agency fake clips start in March, and they will, you’ll know the drill.
