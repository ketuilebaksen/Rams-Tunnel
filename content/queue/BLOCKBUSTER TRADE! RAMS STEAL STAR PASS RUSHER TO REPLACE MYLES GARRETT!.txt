Alright, let's start with the game itself, because we can't talk about anything else until we deal with what happened on that field.

The Rams opened the 2026 season in Melbourne, Australia, against the San Francisco 49ers — the first regular season NFL game ever played on Australian soil. A historic occasion, a massive global audience, and a chance to make a statement against a division rival. And the result was absolutely brutal. Los Angeles lost 27 to 7, and honestly, the score was kind to them. Brock Purdy threw three touchdown passes, San Francisco controlled the game from the opening drive, and the Rams never once looked like they were capable of changing the momentum. There is no way around it — the Rams were outplayed in every phase.

Now, some context is fair here. Sean McVay had the team travel to Australia at the last possible moment, arriving only the day before the game, to limit the effects of the massive time zone change. On paper, that strategy made sense. But once the game kicked off, the team looked physically drained — flat legs, slow reactions, none of the aggression we expected from a roster this talented. Whether it was the travel or simply getting beaten by a better team that night, the result was the same: the 49ers dictated everything.

But here's the thing, Rams fans — the biggest concern coming out of Melbourne wasn't the score. It was Myles Garrett.

Let's rewind for a second, because the stakes here are enormous. The Rams made one of the biggest defensive moves in the entire NFL this offseason when they acquired Garrett from the Cleveland Browns on June 1st. And the price was staggering. Los Angeles sent Jared Verse — a young, ascending edge rusher — plus a 2027 first-round pick, a 2028 second-round pick, and a 2029 third-round pick to Cleveland. That is not a depth move. That is not a calculated gamble. That is the kind of trade you make when you believe you are exactly one superstar away from taking your defense to a championship level.

And look, the logic was easy to understand. Garrett was coming off a historic season. He recorded 23 sacks, breaking the NFL's official single-season sack record. Twenty-three. The Rams expected him to walk in and become the centerpiece of their defensive front. The vision of Garrett rushing off one edge, Byron Young off the other, and a returning Aaron Donald wrecking the interior, with Chris Shula coordinating the whole thing — that vision was one of the biggest reasons expectations in Los Angeles were sky-high coming into this season. If you watched our last video, you know how much this front seven means to everything the Rams want to do.

Unfortunately, Garrett's first game in a Rams uniform could not have gone much worse. He had been dealing with a painful knee throughout training camp — that part is important, and we'll come back to it — but he decided he was healthy enough to play against San Francisco. Garrett ended up playing 40 defensive snaps, and he spent most of the night locked up with Trent Williams, one of the best offensive tackles of this generation. The result was genuinely shocking. Zero tackles. Zero sacks. Zero quarterback pressures. For a player of Garrett's caliber — the reigning single-season sack record holder — that stat line is almost unheard of.

And I'll give Garrett credit for one thing: he didn't hide from it. He didn't make excuses. After the game, he openly admitted it was the worst version of himself he had ever put on a football field. He said he wasn't the player he wanted to be, and that he needed to find a way to get healthy. When a superstar says something like that publicly, you know the situation is serious — and the Rams clearly took it seriously too.

After the team returned to the United States, the decision came quickly. Garrett was scheduled for arthroscopic knee surgery and placed on injured reserve. That means he will miss at least the next four games. And look at that stretch of opponents: the New York Giants, the Denver Broncos, the Philadelphia Eagles, and the Buffalo Bills. Two of those are legitimate heavyweight contenders. So the Rams now have to find a way to generate pressure and survive a brutal early schedule without the player they gave up a young star and three premium draft picks to acquire.

Now — here's where the story gets really interesting. Because Myles Garrett wasn't actually the first elite pass rusher the Rams tried to acquire this offseason. And the new information from NFL insider Ian Rapoport puts this entire summer in a completely different light.

Before Garrett ever became available, Los Angeles was chasing Maxx Crosby of the Las Vegas Raiders. And according to Rapoport's reporting, the Rams pursued Crosby hard after his proposed trade to the Baltimore Ravens collapsed back in March. Remember that saga? Crosby genuinely appeared to be on his way out of Las Vegas. The Raiders and Ravens had an agreement in place built around premium first-round draft compensation, and Crosby actually traveled to Baltimore and underwent a physical. And that's where the whole thing died. The Ravens backed away because of medical concerns involving his knee.

And those concerns weren't paranoia. Crosby had undergone knee surgery in January after missing the end of the 2025 season with a torn meniscus. For a team about to invest franchise-altering draft capital, that medical report was enough to walk away.

But once the Baltimore trade collapsed, Crosby suddenly became available again — at least in theory. Other teams smelled opportunity, and the Rams were one of them. Los Angeles tried to revive trade discussions and bring Crosby to Southern California. But Crosby made his position crystal clear: he did not want to leave Las Vegas. According to the reporting, he told the Rams — and every other interested team — that he intended to remain a Raider. And that was that. The door closed, and Los Angeles had to move on.

When you line up the timeline like that, you can see exactly what the Rams were trying to accomplish this offseason. They wanted an elite, game-wrecking pass rusher who could completely transform the identity of this defense. They tried Crosby first. When Crosby said no, they pivoted to the biggest name imaginable and went all-in on Myles Garrett. And honestly, that tells us a lot about the mindset inside this organization. Los Angeles was willing to spend premium draft capital on a proven superstar because the front office believes — right now — that this roster is in position to compete for a championship. That's not a rebuild mentality. That's a "the window is open" mentality.

And let's be fair to the Rams' evaluation: Crosby would have been a massive addition too. He entered this season with 69 and a half career sacks and four separate seasons with double-digit sacks. Even while playing through that knee issue in 2025, he still produced 10 sacks and a career-high 28 tackles for loss. That's an elite, tone-setting player. Of course the Rams wanted him. But he wanted to stay in Las Vegas, and you can't trade for a man who refuses to leave.

Now comes the uncomfortable part — the part that stings a little as a Rams fan. Maxx Crosby is still with the Raiders, playing football. And Myles Garrett — the player the Rams actually got — is recovering from knee surgery and will miss at least four games. Think about the irony here for a second: the Ravens walked away from Crosby because of a knee. The Rams walked into Garrett... and got hit by a knee anyway.
