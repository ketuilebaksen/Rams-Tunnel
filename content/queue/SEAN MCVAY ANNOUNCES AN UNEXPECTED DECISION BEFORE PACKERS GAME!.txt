Part one: the practice report

McVay started with the injury report from practice.

Did not practice, but expected to play:

Myles Garrett. McVay said Garrett was a DNP, "but just rest, expect him to be ready to go."

Veteran rest days, no injury status: Tyler Higbee, Byron Young, and Aaron Donald. McVay said, "They're good."

Did not practice due to injury:

Steve Avila, with his hamstring.

Coleman Shelton. Remember, McVay said earlier that Shelton will be reevaluated "in a couple weeks."

Limited:

Kam Curl, with his toe.

So here's what that tells us. Garrett, Donald, Higbee, and Young were all resting, not hurt. That's normal on a short week. The Rams are playing on Wednesday, so McVay is protecting his veterans.

Avila and Shelton are the concerns. If both are out, the Rams will again be leaning on a patched-together offensive line, with Beaux Limmer likely at center and Justin Dedich back off IR for depth.

Why resting veterans matters on a short week

Let me explain why so many veterans sat out practice.

The Rams play on Wednesday night. That's a short week, even after a bye. And McVay called this an "accelerated bye" because players only had a few days away from the building.

So McVay is being careful with his older players. Aaron Donald, Tyler Higbee, and Myles Garrett have all been in the league a long time. Garrett is still building back from his knee issue. And Byron Young has played a lot of snaps this season.

Resting them in practice doesn't mean they're hurt. It means McVay wants them fresh for the game. That's smart. Especially against a Packers team that's going to be physical.

The bigger question is the offensive line. Avila and Shelton aren't resting. They're hurt. And that's where the Rams are thinnest right now.

Part two: Terrance Ferguson's season is over

Then came the tough news.

McVay said: "Unfortunately, as it relates to Terrance Ferguson, he's been working to try to be able to get his rehab." He explained that Ferguson was "pushing to get back off the ankle," but "the ankle isn't getting better."

And then: "He's going to be on IR for the rest of the year. Probably have to get it fixed."

A reporter asked to clarify if it's season-ending surgery. McVay said: "It is. Yes." He explained that Ferguson's 21-day practice window would now expire, and the Rams won't activate him.

McVay clearly felt for him. He said: "It's a bummer, because he's been working his ass off to try to be able to get back, and that's where you feel for him." And he said he'd just talked with head trainer Reggie Scott "a couple minutes ago," and that surgery is "going to be the right thing to do for him, because it's just not responding the way that you want."

McVay even said he'd only found out about it 30 minutes before the press conference.

Part three: what happened with Ferguson's ankle

McVay gave more detail about what went wrong.

He said Ferguson had "a really good week of practice" when he first came back. But "you could still see it was kind of getting comfortable." He said Ferguson "took some meds to be able to do that."

Then he hurt his hamstring on a Friday. And as he kept working on both injuries, the team took "another look at it." McVay said: "It was like something in his deltoid in his ankle that really is just not healing the way that you want."

He said it wasn't one specific setback. It was more that the ankle "is just not coming along."

And here's the key part. McVay said Ferguson "wouldn't be able to be himself and put his foot in the ground." He explained that Ferguson's game is "predicated on being that twitchy athlete," with "change of direction" and "moving him across the formation."

So if Ferguson can't plant and cut, he can't be the player the Rams need. That's why surgery made sense.

McVay finished: "His eyes will be being a great teammate for the rest of this season and looking towards the future."

Part four: Puka's surgery went well

Then a reporter asked about Puka's surgery.

"How did the surgery go?"

McVay said: "Good. Went good."

"Do you expect him to be ready for the start of next season?"

McVay: "I do. Yeah."

That's great news. Last week, McVay said Puka would be out "a couple months" after MCL surgery, and that he's "pretty much" done for this season. Now we know the surgery itself went well, and the Rams expect Puka back for the start of 2027.

McVay also repeated: "His season is over now with regards to him getting that surgery, but do expect him to return and be back in a couple months and ready to go."

So the short-term news hasn't changed. Puka is out for this season. But the long-term news is good.

Part five: McVay on Puka as a person

This was the most thoughtful part of the press conference.

A reporter asked how Puka has handled this season, with the injuries and the three-game suspension the NFL announced on November 19 for a violation of the personal conduct policy.

McVay said there are "a couple parts to this." And the first one was: "You love the human."

He said there are "a lot of different things that he's navigated." And he explained that some of what came out recently "goes back to New Year's last year," and that it's hard when old things "come out in the public."

Then McVay said this: "A lot of love. I think great competitor, great person, where I trust his heart."

And he compared Puka to himself: "Young guys, no different than I'm continuing to try to mature as a man. Figure out how do you navigate these responsibilities and all parts of your life being a professional. And I think he's growing."

He finished: "I trust his heart, and I think he's making a lot of steps that are in the right direction."

So McVay didn't excuse anything, and he didn't go into details. But he made it clear that he still believes in Puka as a person. And he said he's "bummed for him" about the injury, because Puka "was banged up and playing through stuff even more so than normal" this season.

Part six: will Puka be back with the Rams?

Here's where it got interesting.

A reporter asked if McVay could say with some certainty that Puka will be back with the Rams next season.

McVay was careful: "I can't say for certainty anything." He explained he didn't want anything "taken out of context," because his focus is on the Packers game.

But then he gave some important context. He said: "When you go through the amount of free agents that we have, Puka is a huge part of the conversation that you love. But there's also guys that are part of his draft class. There's guys on our offensive line. There's some guys on defense."

And: "There's a lot of guys that are up, that as of right now would not be under contract, but there are mechanics that we'd like to be able to try to figure out how to keep these really good players and people that we love. And certainly, Puka is at the forefront of our mind."

So here's what that means. Puka's contract situation is coming up. He's one of several important players whose deals will need to be figured out. And McVay said Puka is "at the forefront" of the Rams' minds.

This is not a report, it's my read: I think McVay is telling us the Rams want to keep Puka. But he's not going to promise anything in November, especially with a big game coming up. That's just smart.

Remember, ESPN's Dan Graziano had raised a hypothetical about the Rams possibly letting Puka test free agency or using the franchise tag. McVay's answer today suggests that keeping Puka is a priority, even if it's not guaranteed.

Part seven: the trade deadline question

This was a funny moment.

A reporter reminded McVay that at the trade deadline, he said the Rams didn't pursue a receiver because they expected their injured guys to come back.

McVay laughed and said: "And that was not information that I had at that point."

Everyone in the room laughed. Because at the deadline, McVay didn't know Puka would need surgery. He didn't know Ferguson would need surgery either.

But then he got serious. He said: "Based on last week, I'm excited about what those guys that did get opportunities did."

And he named them: "I don't want it to ever be misunderstood, man. I am confident in Tutu Atwell and Konata Mumpfield and Xavier Smith and CJ Daniels, and Presley getting a chance to get back there and return kicks."

So there's a hint. Brennan Presley is likely to be elevated from the practice squad to return kicks against the Packers.

McVay finished: "It's a bummer to lose those two significant pieces. But I think you have to adjust and adapt, and this is the hand that we're dealt."

Part eight: could the Rams sign a free agent?

Another reporter asked directly: any thought of pursuing a veteran free agent receiver?

McVay said: "We've talked about all those types of things. So, that's a possibility."

And: "I would never rule out opportunities to upgrade. But like I said, I think the guys have done a really good job."

So that's not a "yes." But it's not a "no" either. The Rams have discussed it.

And that connects to a story from earlier this week. The 49ers released Brandon Aiyuk, and SB Nation's Kenneth Arthur suggested the Rams should at least consider him. But there's no report the Rams are pursuing Aiyuk. And remember, Aiyuk hasn't played since 2024.

This is not a report, it's my take: if the Rams sign a veteran receiver, I'd expect it to be a smaller, low-risk move. Not a blockbuster. And it probably wouldn't happen until after the Packers game.

Part nine: Davante Adams

McVay also mentioned Davante Adams briefly. He said: "We'll look forward to hopefully getting Davante back at some point too."

Last week, he said there's "a possibility for the game after the Packers." So Adams is still on track to possibly return against the Chiefs. But nothing is guaranteed.

With Puka and Ferguson both done for the year, Adams becomes even more important. He'd be the most experienced receiver Stafford has for the rest of the season.

Part ten: McVay on the young players

A reporter asked what McVay sees in the young receivers.

He said: "A gratitude and excitement, a competitiveness." He said what he's seen in practice "has been awesome," and then pointed to the Arizona game.

"I think it's cool when you have 18 completions and nine different guys catch the football. I think we're at our best when a bunch of different people are getting involved."

And then: "You have three backs with 12-plus carries, and you got a bunch of different guys that are making their plays."

That's Kyren Williams, Blake Corum, and Ronnie Rivers. In Arizona, Corum had 100 yards and a touchdown, and Rivers had 81 yards.

McVay also talked about what makes this team special: "One of my favorite things about our team, and if you were to say what do I hope that you guys would see from afar, is enjoyment of celebrating each other's successes."

He said: "This is a blessing, never a burden."

And he gave shout-outs to players who've stepped up. He mentioned Xavier Smith, saying "X-Man" coming back and scoring a touchdown was "awesome." He mentioned the excitement for Ronnie Rivers. And he mentioned Wesley Bailey's first career interception.

He finished: "This is the greatest team sport that there is. And when you watch us, I think we're at our best when we're connected and we're enjoying it and celebrating each other's successes."

Part eleven: the young defensive line

A reporter asked about the young defensive players, and McVay was full of praise.

He credited the defensive line coaches and the leadership of Myles Garrett and Byron Young.

Then he talked about Wesley Bailey: "It's been a really cool story watching him truly earn a roster spot. How he played in preseason, how he played in practice." And: "Wes is getting better."

Remember, according to Blaine Grisak of Rams On SI, Bailey is the first undrafted rookie since at least 2000 to record an interception, multiple sacks, and multiple forced fumbles in one season. He graded 93.0 in Arizona.

McVay also had big praise for Josiah Stewart: "Josiah Stewart's a stud. Thought he had a really good rookie year, continued to mature and ascend. He is a complete player for us that can play on all three downs."

He said: "I've been really pleased with that group as a whole. They're a fun, young group of guys to be around, and they've done really well for us."

So the young pass rushers are a big reason the Rams' defense is rising. And tonight, they'll face a much better offense than Arizona's.

Part twelve: McVay and Matt LaFleur

This was my favorite part of the press conference.

A reporter asked what it's like to face the Packers on Thanksgiving week, with McVay's longtime friend Matt LaFleur on the other side.

McVay said: "It's a cool thing to have as many friends as we do from just us all growing up pushing one another."

Then he told a great story: "I do have a lot of love for Matt. Our friendship goes way back. Before I met my wife and before they even had their kids, I'd go hang out with BreAnne and Matt. I was like the third wheel of their relationship, but I was like the little brother."

And then the competitive side came out: "I love Matt, love BreAnne, love their family. It'll be cool, but we're trying to kick each other's ass. I know that much. He's going to try to do the same thing."

He finished: "This is about the Rams versus the Packers, and excited for a good competitive game, and hopefully we both stay healthy."

I love that. Two friends who came up together, now going head-to-head in a game that could decide a playoff tiebreaker.

Part thirteen: the Fearsome Foursome throwbacks

The last question was about the Rams wearing "Fearsome Foursome" throwback shirts.

For younger fans, the Fearsome Foursome was the Rams' legendary defensive line in the 1960s. It's one of the most famous defensive fronts in NFL history.

McVay said: "I'm a big fan of the history of the game." He said it's about "setting a standard with the way that the game is played, particularly on the defensive front, imposing your will."

He added: "This organization has a history of a lot of great football players, a lot of great coaches. And I think it's a cool way to be able to honor" them.

And it fits this team perfectly. Look at the Rams' defensive front right now. Myles Garrett, who set the single-season sack record with 23 sacks in 2025. Aaron Donald. Byron Young. Wesley Bailey. Josiah Stewart. And possibly Kobie Turner coming back.

That's a group that could make the Fearsome Foursome proud.

Reading between the lines

McVay never tells you everything. But here's what I picked up from this press conference. This is not a report. It's my analysis.

Presley is getting a role. McVay specifically mentioned "Presley getting a chance to get back there and return kicks." That sounds like Presley will be elevated from the practice squad tonight.

The Rams are at least looking at receivers. "We've talked about all those types of things" is not something McVay would say if the team hadn't discussed it. It doesn't mean a signing is coming. But they're keeping an eye on the market.

Puka's future is a real priority. McVay could have dodged the question. Instead, he said Puka is "at the forefront of our mind" and that the Rams want to figure out "mechanics" to keep their best players. That's as close to a commitment as you'll get in November.

McVay trusts the young guys. He named every young receiver by name. He said, "I don't want it to ever be misunderstood." That's a coach backing his players publicly before a big game.

The defense is the identity now. McVay spent a lot of time praising Bailey, Stewart, and the young defensive line. With the offense missing so many pieces, the defense might have to carry this team for a while.

The contract year McVay hinted at

One more thing from that free agency answer.

McVay said the Rams have "a lot of guys that are free agents" after this season. He said Puka is one of them. And he mentioned "guys that are part of his draft class," plus players "on our offensive line" and "some guys on defense."

So the Rams have a big offseason coming. A lot of contracts to figure out, and not a lot of room for mistakes.

That's another reason the Metcalf rumor from Stephen A. Smith felt like a long shot. Before the Rams add a big contract from another team, they have to figure out how to keep their own players.

And remember, the Rams don't have their 2027 first-round pick. So keeping their own good young players is even more important.

Out for the season: Puka Nacua and Terrance Ferguson.

Probably out: Davante Adams, who's targeting the game after the Packers. Coleman Shelton, who'll be reevaluated in a couple of weeks.

Likely elevated: Brennan Presley, to return kicks.

The Packers game preview

Rams vs. Packers, tonight, Wednesday, November 25, 8 p.m. Eastern, on Netflix.

Both teams are 6-4. The Seahawks are 6-4 too. And with three NFC wild card spots up for grabs, this head-to-head game could become a key tiebreaker.

Five keys for tonight

Here are my five keys for the Rams against the Packers.

One: protect Stafford. With Avila and Shelton hurt, the line has to hold up. Quick throws and extra blockers will help.

Two: run the ball. Three backs with 12-plus carries in Arizona. Do it again.

Three: win the field position battle. If Presley is back there returning kicks, a big return or two could change the game.

Four: let the pass rush eat. Garrett, Donald, Young, Bailey, and Stewart. Make the Packers' quarterback uncomfortable all night.

Five: create a turnover. The Rams forced three turnovers in Arizona. One or two tonight could decide it.
