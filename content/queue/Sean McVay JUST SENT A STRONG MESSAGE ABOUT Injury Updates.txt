Part one: the injury report, one by one

McVay opened the press conference by saying, “I’m sure you guys are all going to want to know about the injuries.” And he was right. Here’s everything he said.

Puka Nacua. The big one. McVay said Puka “suffered an MCL, PCL sprain, also turf toe.” He confirmed it’s his right knee. And he said, “He’s going to be out for a good amount of time. Will probably be a guy that we’ll put on IR.”

When he was asked for a timeline, McVay said, “I’m reluctant to put too much of a timeline” on it. But then he gave one anyway: “It’s a strong chance that we put him on IR. So we’re thinking it’s going to be at least a month.”

Steve Avila. The guard left the game with a hamstring strain. McVay said, “It’s going to be a little bit of time for him, unfortunately. But hopefully we’ll get him back at some point.”

Trent McDuffie. Some good news. McVay said McDuffie is “making good progress with his back,” and that they’ll “take it a day at a time” and “hopefully be able to get him back this week.” That’s huge, because the secondary is thin with Jaylen Watson out for the season.

Davis Allen. The tight end hurt his back during the game. McVay said, “It bothered him. He ended up going out temporarily and coming back during the game and finished.” And then: “He’s tough as hell and did a great job.” With Terrance Ferguson out with a hamstring injury, Allen’s toughness matters even more.

Jordan Whittington. McVay said Whittington “continues to battle with some just soreness in his back,” and that they’ll “see what that means for him moving forward.” That’s worrying for a receiver room that just lost Puka.

Everyone else. McVay said the rest was “your typical bumps and bruises from the game.”

So here’s the scorecard. The Rams lost their best player for at least a month, lost a starting guard for some time, and have two more pass catchers dealing with back issues. But McDuffie might be back this week. That’s the injury picture going into deadline day.

Part two: how McVay found out about Puka

One reporter asked McVay how he first learned about Puka’s injury, and his answer was really human.

He said he saw the hit on “the last jet sweep that we ran.” He described it: “The way that he took a shot, just from that guy inside out, it looked like there could have been something there.” He said Puka “got up and came off,” and that “he’s as tough as it gets.” Then McVay talked to Reggie, the team’s head athletic trainer, after his postgame press conference, and learned Puka “did get a little bit of a knee.”

And then McVay said something I really respect. “When these things happen, the first thing is you feel for the players. They put so much work in, and it’s so unfortunate. And then you question, all right, what could you have done to maybe avoid it.”

That’s an honest coach. A lot of fans, me included, asked whether a jet sweep on the last offensive play was the right call for a player carrying that much of the offense. McVay didn’t hide from it. He said he asks himself the same question.

But then he moved forward: “One of the things that I have been proud of with our team is, we’ve lost a lot of guys, and it’s just about, hey, let’s figure out how to be in the moment, take it one day at a time, adjust and adapt.”

Part three: the trade deadline answer

This was the most important answer of the whole press conference.

A reporter pointed out that McVay had said in recent weeks that he didn’t expect the Rams to be active at the deadline, and asked whether the Puka injury changes that.

McVay said: “I think you look at all avenues. You got to look at all avenues to try to be able to solve some of the issues or situations or scenarios that have come up.” And then the key line: “A lot of new things occur on a daily basis. And so I would never rule it out.”

He also said that as soon as he was done with the press conference, the team would get back to “putting our heads together,” and that’s what the whole morning had been about. He said they were working through “what is the 48, the 53? What does our practice squad look like? How does that shape the different areas of our football team, defensively, offensively, and in the kicking game?”

Now, let’s be clear about what that means and what it doesn’t.

It doesn’t mean the Rams are making a trade. McVay didn’t name a player, and nobody has reported a deal.

But it’s a real change in tone. A week ago, according to ESPN’s Sarah Barshop, McVay indicated the Rams didn’t plan to add outside players and would rather count on players returning from injury. Now he’s saying, “I would never rule it out.” That’s the difference between closing the door and opening it a crack.

And the “48 and 53” part is interesting too. That’s the game-day active roster and the full roster. McVay is talking about how to fill Puka’s spot, whether that’s from the practice squad, a promotion, or an outside move.

Here’s my take, and this is not a report. The Rams were never going to make a big trade with almost no cap space and no 2027 first or third. But if a cheap receiver is available, like ESPN analyst Aaron Schatz’s idea of Washington’s Dyami Brown for a late-round pick swap, the Puka injury makes that kind of deal a lot more likely than it was a week ago.

Part four: the IR problem nobody’s talking about

This next part is a little technical, but it might be the most important detail McVay mentioned.

When he was asked whether Avila could also go on injured reserve, McVay said: “We’re getting to be where that number is getting so high that we’re going to run out of those eight to be able to return.”

Here’s what that means. NFL teams can only bring back a limited number of players from injured reserve during the season. McVay said eight. Once you use them up, any player you put on IR is done for the year, no matter how fast he heals.

And the Rams have been burning through them. Garrett used one when he came back last week. Davante Adams will need one. Kobie Turner will need one. Now Puka will need one. And other players have already used return slots, like running back Ronnie Rivers, who was activated off IR last week.

So McVay said putting Avila on IR is “not something that we’re trying to do right now.” He added, “I certainly wouldn’t say it’s a zero chance, but we’re hoping to avoid that as of right now.”

That’s a real strategic decision. If Avila only misses two or three games, the Rams would rather keep him on the active roster and play short-handed than waste a return slot they might need for someone else in December.

So who replaces Avila? McVay mentioned a few names. He said a player who stepped in on Sunday “did a nice job stepping up” against “a variety of looks and fronts and movements.” He said “Dylan McMahon’s backed up at the center spot,” and that Bill Murray has “done a really nice job” as a guard who’s played “meaningful snaps.” And when asked about Justin Dedich returning to the active roster, McVay said, “It could be,” but he wasn’t “ready to fully answer that just based on all the moving parts.”

The Rams’ offensive line has been one of the best in football at protecting Stafford. Losing Avila is a real test of that depth.

Part five: close games and mental toughness

One reporter asked how all these close games help the team for late December and January. McVay’s answer was interesting because he compared Sunday’s win to the losses earlier in the season.

He said, “Three of our four losses were very similar to what yesterday” was, meaning close games that came down to the end. And he said the experience of those earlier games helped the team finish this time.

He added: “I thought defensively we handled a lot of the situations the way that we wanted to. Would have loved to have closed the game with the ball offensively.” That’s a small admission. The offense didn’t put the game away. The defense had to. And remember, the play that did close out the offense’s day was the jet sweep where Puka got hurt.

But McVay said the result still matters: “To be able to get that result that you’re hunting, I think, was positive for our group. And I think we’re continuing to build skills in the areas of physical and mental toughness, which really serve you well as the season goes on.”

Here’s why that matters. The Rams’ early-season losses included that 18-16 loss to Arizona. They’ve learned how to lose close games. Now they’re learning how to win them. Three straight wins, all of them in games where the defense had to hold up late.

Part six: the Davante Adams answer

This was a subtle answer, and I think it’s important.

ESPN’s Sarah Barshop asked McVay whether he has an expectation of when Davante Adams will be back. Here’s how McVay answered.

He said, “I don’t really,” and explained that Reggie, the head trainer, keeps him updated on Adams “making good progress.” But “with the amount of moving parts,” McVay said he’ll “be excited when that time does come.”

Then he said something honest: “I don’t want to say I’m not concerned. I’m definitely concerned about it. It would be great to get him back, but I’m trying to truly live just one day at a time because of the amount of different things that you’re naturally having to navigate.” And then: “Otherwise, you drive yourself crazy.”

Here’s how I read that. A couple of weeks ago, McVay said Adams’ surgery “went really well” and that his recovery was trending toward the shorter end of the six-to-ten-week timeline. That’s still the most recent timeline we have. But on Monday, McVay wouldn’t give a date, and he admitted he’s “definitely concerned.”

I don’t think that means something went wrong with Adams. I think it means McVay, with Puka now out too, isn’t going to promise anything he can’t guarantee. But it’s a reminder that “the shorter end” isn’t a date on the calendar. If you were counting on Adams being back right after the bye, McVay isn’t making that promise.

And that makes the deadline even more important. If the Rams can’t count on Adams by a specific date, and Puka is out at least a month, they need a receiver who can help right now.

Part seven: life without Puka

A reporter asked about the game plan for Arizona without Puka, since he’s had such a big target share. McVay was honest: “I haven’t started that yet, so it’s hard to answer.”

But he gave some hints. He said, “We’ll have some tendencies, that’s for sure,” and that they’ll “figure out what that looks like to be able to get the best different groupings and best people involved.” And he said, “Guys will be asked to step up.”

He also made a point about why Puka gets so many targets. He said it’s “based on his production” and “because of some of the unselfish stuff he does in the run game.” And he gave the number himself: “Fourteen of 19, but he was outstanding.”

So there’s the official count. Fourteen catches on 19 targets. Earlier this week, I said 21 targets, based on the ESPN broadcast discussion. McVay and ESPN’s game story both say 19, so 19 it is.

Then McVay said, “There are other guys that are very capable of doing some good stuff for us, and we’ll look to see those guys step up. It’ll be a great opportunity for them, in spite of it being a bummer for those guys that are out.”

So who are those guys? Here’s the honest picture. Against Washington, Konata Mumpfield had two catches for 20 yards, Tutu Atwell had two for 16, and Jordan Whittington didn’t have a catch. According to Rams Wire’s Cameron DaSilva, Mumpfield has 162 receiving yards this season, Atwell has 99, and Whittington has 12. And Whittington is now battling back soreness.

That’s why I think the tight ends will be the real answer on Sunday. Tyler Higbee, Davis Allen, who McVay called “tough as hell,” and Colby Parkinson if he’s healthy. The Rams already use more two- and three-tight-end sets than almost anyone. Without Puka, expect even more.

Part eight: Garrett and Donald, finally together

Now the fun part. McVay was asked what he saw on film from having Myles Garrett and Aaron Donald on the field together for the first time.

He started with something smart. He said their combination was “unscouted.” In other words, Washington had no film of how the Rams would use Garrett and Donald together, because it had never happened. Now every opponent has a tape. “It’s a little bit different now that you at least have some tape,” McVay said.

Then he praised both of them. “I thought they both did an excellent job yesterday, as did the rest of our guys that were rushing.” He said there were “outstanding illustrations of rush and coverage working as one.” That’s the same idea he talked about before the game, “rushing four as one.”

And then he gave some credit to Donald that I think a lot of people missed. He said, “Myles obviously did a great job, but I thought AD had a couple awesome things that allowed him to be able to get some one-on-ones and get freed up.” That’s exactly what Garrett predicted before the game, when he said offenses “have to pick their poison.” Donald drew attention, and Garrett got one-on-ones.

Garrett finished with three sacks, three tackles for loss, and a strip-sack right before halftime that led to points. And McVay said his role “may or may not be the same or different” going forward, “as you continue to increase his snap count as he’s feeling better and getting back to being able to play that workload that he’s been accustomed to throughout his whole career.”

Translation: Garrett is going to play even more. And opponents now have film, so the Rams will mix up how they use him and Donald.

Part nine: “No flinch”

The final question of the press conference was the best one. A reporter asked how McVay manages the mood in the building when key players keep getting hurt, even with the team on a three-game winning streak.

McVay said the players “feel for each other because they care about one another.” He said injuries take guys away from “being able to do what they want to do with their teammates.”

But he said the team is “building skills where they’re finding ways to improve,” that the players are “excited about the preparation,” and that “they’re truly just taking it a day at a time.” He said everyone is “in lockstep” on being “totally and completely present.”

And then: “It provides another opportunity for somebody else to be able to step up. And it’s our job to have the flexibility and fluidity to adjust and adapt.” He said there’s “a lot of belief” that if they keep improving in the areas they can control, they’ll be fine. “Let’s go roll the ball out there, because the game’s going to be played at whatever time we’re supposed to kick off in Arizona on Sunday.”

And he finished with this: “You feel for those guys, but no flinch.”

That’s the strong message. Not a trade. Not a shocking announcement. Just a head coach telling his team, and everyone watching, that the Rams aren’t going to panic.

And honestly, that message fits this team. They started 2-4. They lost Adams, Ford, Turner, Watson, and now Puka. They’ve won three straight anyway. “No flinch” isn’t just a slogan for this group. It’s been their whole season.

What I liked, and what worried me

Let me give you my honest read on the press conference as a whole.

What I liked.

First, McVay’s honesty about Puka. He didn’t pretend the jet sweep was a perfect call. He said that when a player gets hurt, “you question, all right, what could you have done to maybe avoid it.” Coaches don’t always say that out loud.

Second, the McDuffie update. If McDuffie is back for Arizona, the secondary goes from scary thin to manageable. Josh Wallace and Emmanuel Forbes held up against Washington, but getting McDuffie back changes everything.

Third, the tone on the deadline. “I would never rule it out” tells me the front office is actually working the phones today, not just waiting for players to heal.

Fourth, the Garrett and Donald film. McVay sounded genuinely excited about how they worked together, and he made a point of crediting Donald for helping free up Garrett. That’s a sign this pairing is going to get even better as Garrett’s snap count goes up.

What worried me.

First, the IR math. When a head coach says out loud that he’s worried about running out of return slots in November, that’s a sign of how many injuries this team has dealt with. If more players get hurt, the Rams may have to make tough choices about who gets to come back.

Second, the Adams answer. “I’m definitely concerned” isn’t what you want to hear. I don’t think it means a setback, but it means McVay isn’t counting on Adams by any specific date.

Third, the receiver depth. With Puka out and Whittington battling back soreness, the Rams’ healthy receivers have combined for very little production this season. Somebody has to step up on Sunday, or the offense will be even more one-dimensional than it already was.

Fourth, the offensive line. Avila’s injury comes at a bad time. The line has been a strength all year, and without him, the Rams are leaning on depth guys against an Arizona team that already beat them once.

So my overall grade on the week: the Rams are 5-4, on a three-game winning streak, with the best defensive front they’ve had all season. But the offense is badly short-handed, and today’s deadline might be the last chance to fix that before the stretch run.

Part ten: what it means for today

So what does all of this mean for deadline day?

One: McVay opened the door. “I would never rule it out” is a lot different from what he was saying a week ago. With Puka out for at least a month, I think the odds of a small receiver trade went up.

Two: the money is still the money. The Rams have almost no cap space, per the most recent Athlon Sports number. Any deal needs a restructure or salary going the other way. Putting Puka on IR doesn’t create cap room, because his salary still counts.

Three: the IR slots matter. McVay is already worried about running out of the eight return slots. That might push the Rams toward a trade instead of waiting on injured players, because they can’t bring everyone back.

Four: the realistic targets are cheap. Based on real analysis this week, the names that fit the Rams’ budget are players like Dyami Brown, where ESPN’s Aaron Schatz proposed a late-round pick swap. Bigger names like Brian Thomas Jr. or Josh Downs would cost more, and they have other suitors, like the Chiefs and Steelers.

Five: the deadline is 4 p.m. Eastern. Les Snead has made four deadline-day trades over the years. If something happens, it might come late. And when it does, it’ll show up on Adam Schefter’s or Ian Rapoport’s real X accounts first, not in a fake First Take clip. We’ve already covered fourteen of those.

Part eleven: Arizona on Sunday

After the deadline, the Rams go to Arizona on Sunday, then get their bye.

Arizona beat the Rams 18-16 on October 18th, the game where Adams got hurt. Now the Rams face them without Adams and without Puka, and likely without Avila.

Here’s my plan for how they win. This is opinion, not reporting.

Run the ball. Kyren Williams and Blake Corum have carried the offense for three weeks: 192 rushing yards against the Raiders, 129 against the Chargers, and 124 against Washington. That’s the identity.

Feature the tight ends. Higbee, Allen, and Parkinson if he’s healthy. Short, safe throws that keep Stafford on schedule.

Let the pass rush win. Garrett and Donald now have a game together on film, and McVay said they’ll keep building Garrett’s snap count. If they get after the Arizona quarterback, the Rams don’t need a big passing day.

Get to 6-4 and the bye. That’s the whole goal. Win, rest, get McDuffie, Turner, Adams, and eventually Puka back, and make the stretch run.
