Forty-seven seconds. That’s how close the Rams were. Matthew Stafford had just thrown a touchdown pass to Colby Parkinson. The Rams’ punter, Ethan Evans — a player who had never attempted a kick in an NFL game — stepped up and drilled the extra point, with backup quarterback Stetson Bennett holding. Rams 16, Cardinals 15. And then, 47 seconds later, Chad Ryland kicked his sixth field goal of the day as time expired, and the Arizona Cardinals walked out with an 18-16 win. The Rams are now 2-4. First, let’s walk through how this game unfolded, because the story is in the details.

It actually started well. On Arizona’s first possession, Quentin Lake intercepted Jacoby Brissett on a pass intended for Trey McBride. The Rams turned that into points: Blake Grupe, in his very first attempt as a Ram, drilled a 44-yard field goal. Rams 3-0. After the week this team had — releasing Harrison Mevis, signing Grupe, all the questions about the kicking game — that kick felt like a turning point.

It lasted about one play. On the ensuing kickoff, Grupe made a tackle on returner Devin Duvernay — a return that went all the way to the Cardinals’ 47 — and he hurt his knee. He was evaluated in the medical tent, went to the locker room, and was ruled doubtful to return. Early in the fourth quarter, the Rams downgraded him to out. According to Yahoo Sports, he was seen on crutches after the game.

So for nearly the entire game, the Rams had no real placekicker. And that changed everything about how McVay could coach.

And notice what caused the injury. Grupe got hurt making a tackle — because the coverage team let Duvernay break free on the kickoff. A kicker should almost never have to make a tackle. When he does, it usually means the other 10 players on the coverage unit didn’t do their jobs. McVay mentioned it right away in his press conference: the big return and the kickoff coverage were on his list of what went wrong.

Ryland tied it at three with a 34-yard field goal, then added a 30-yarder. Then Stafford hit Blake Corum on a screen for a 15-yard touchdown — but with no kicker, the Rams went for two, and Stafford was sacked and fumbled. Rams 9-6. Ryland hit from 22 yards with 46 seconds left in the half. Tied 9-9.

In the second half, Ryland added a 38-yarder and a 27-yarder. Cardinals 15-9. Then came the late drama — Stafford’s touchdown to Parkinson, Evans’ extra point, and Ryland’s 43-yard winner as time expired.

Six field goals. Zero touchdowns. And it was enough.

Let me give Chad Ryland his credit, because he was perfect. He made field goals of 34, 30, 22, 38, 27 and 43 yards. Six for six. The week before, Ryland had missed his only attempt against Detroit, a 53-yarder. On Sunday, he was automatic. After the game, he said: “That’s being a good situationally aware football player and football team.” And Mike LaFleur summed it up in three words: “Chad’s a stud.”

And think about how upside-down this matchup turned out. According to Pro Football Talk, Arizona entered the week ranked 26th in yards allowed and dead last in points allowed. The Rams entered the game leading the NFL in total offense, according to the AP. Arizona was a double-digit underdog. And the Cardinals’ defense held the Rams to 16 points.

Now let’s get to the decisions, because this game came down to a series of choices McVay had to make without a kicker.

Last-minute decision number one: the end of the first half.

After Ryland tied the game at 9-9 with 46 seconds left, the Rams had the ball with a chance to try for points before halftime. Instead, they ran out the clock. A reporter asked McVay whether he considered going for it, and his answer explains a lot about this entire game. He said: “Well, we’re playing for a touchdown. We didn’t have a field goal operation.” And then he added: “If we had our normal kicker, if we had a kicker healthy, then that would have changed the approach.”

Think about what that means. Without a kicker, every drive near midfield has only two outcomes: a touchdown or nothing. Forty-six seconds isn’t much time to go the length of the field for a touchdown, and a turnover could have handed Arizona a short field for a field goal. So McVay chose to protect the tie. I understand the logic. But it shows exactly how much the kicker injury limited this offense. Normally, the Rams would be trying to get into field goal range there. Without a kicker, there was no field goal range.

Last-minute decision number two: the two-point attempt in the second quarter.

After the Corum touchdown, a normal team kicks the extra point. The Rams didn’t have that option. They went for two. Stafford was sacked — Arizona’s defense credited Josh Sweat with the sack on the two-point try — and the ball came out. That one point might not seem like much in the second quarter. But in a game the Rams lost by two, every single point mattered.

Last-minute decision number three: the fourth downs.

In the third quarter, the Rams went for it on fourth-and-1 at the Cardinals’ 27. Normally, that’s a spot where a team might kick a 45-yard field goal. Without a kicker, the Rams went for it — and Arizona linebacker Jack Gibbens made the stop. No points.

Then in the fourth quarter, with the Rams trailing 15-9, came the moment McVay pointed to after the game. The Rams faced a fourth-and-3. A false start pushed it back to fourth-and-8. The pass fell incomplete, and Arizona took over on downs with 3:23 left. McVay mentioned it specifically: “we jump offsides on a fourth and three to put it to a fourth down and eight.” That’s a self-inflicted wound in the biggest moment of the game.

And just before that, with 7:47 left, tight end Tyler Higbee caught a pass deep in Arizona territory and had the ball knocked out by linebacker Cody Simon. Arizona recovered at its own 25. According to the AP, the Rams turned the ball over twice in Arizona territory in the fourth quarter. Two trips that could have been game-winning drives ended with nothing.

Last-minute decision number four: the final 47 seconds.

Let’s give credit where it’s due first. Down 15-9 with time running out, Stafford led an 86-yard touchdown drive in just over two minutes. Puka Nacua caught a 42-yard pass to get the Rams to the Cardinals’ 35 with 1:50 left. He added a 16-yard catch to get to the 13. Kyren Williams picked up seven. And Stafford found Parkinson for the touchdown. The play was initially ruled down at the 1, but video review gave Parkinson the score.

Now, with the score tied at 15, the Rams had to decide how to get the go-ahead point. And McVay sent out Ethan Evans to kick the extra point, with Stetson Bennett holding. Think about that. Evans had never kicked in an NFL game. According to Yahoo Sports, in his senior season at Wingate University, he made 10 of 18 field goal attempts, with a long of 41. And he stepped up in that moment and made it. McVay said it was “pretty cool to be able to see Ethan punch in the extra point and Stetson to be able to hold.” That’s a moment those two will never forget.

But then, as McVay put it, “on the very first play of the two-minute,” the Rams gave up a big play — and “they tack on the 15.” After a touchback, Brissett hit Jalen Brooks for 21 yards. Cornerback Josh Wallace was flagged for unnecessary roughness, adding 15 more. Tyler Allgeier ran for five. And Ryland lined up from 43 yards and won it as time expired.

Arizona needed only about 36 yards to get into field goal range, and the Rams handed them 15 of those yards with a penalty. That’s the game.

And let’s talk about the man on the other sideline, because this result will sting McVay personally. Mike LaFleur, McVay’s offensive coordinator for the last three seasons, beat his former boss in their very first meeting. According to the AP, McVay had won 16 of his 19 meetings against Arizona coming into this game, and LaFleur is the eighth of McVay’s former assistants to become an NFL head coach. All week, McVay talked about not “chasing a ghost.” But LaFleur’s team clearly had a plan. Arizona blitzed Stafford throughout the game, and according to the AP, the Cardinals repeatedly went after backup left tackle David Quessenberry, who was making his first start as a Ram with Alaric Jackson suspended.

After the game, LaFleur admitted he believed his team would find a way at the end: “Deep down, I thought we were going to win the game when we had 51 seconds.” It was Arizona’s first NFC West win since the 2024 season, and its second win at SoFi Stadium this year — the Cardinals also beat the Chargers there in Week 1. LaFleur said he still wants to review why his offense didn’t score a single touchdown on five red zone trips. But for one afternoon, the student beat the teacher.

Now, let’s hear what McVay said after the game, because it was one of the most blunt press conferences of his career.

He opened with this: “Congrats to the Cardinals. We won’t win a game unless we figure out how not to beat ourselves.” He said there were “so many unforced errors, so many things that are not in alignment with what we want to do specifically on the offensive side of the ball.” He listed the problems himself: the big return to start the game, losing the kicker, the kickoff coverage, the turnovers, the negative plays, the false start on fourth down.

Then he said: “There’s nothing positive that I can say about today.” He also called it “a good, humbling day,” and added: “This is the challenging part of sports.”

When he was asked why the self-inflicted mistakes keep showing up this season, he was brutally honest. He said a lot of different players “are taking their turn” making those mistakes, and then: “I don’t have those answers right now, otherwise it would have gotten fixed.” He said, “I have to do a better job,” and that “it’s my job” to fix it.

And when a reporter asked him what this team’s identity is right now, McVay said: “We’re figuring out. I can’t answer that right now.”

That’s a remarkable thing to hear from Sean McVay six games into a season that started with Super Bowl expectations. But here’s what I respect: he didn’t blame his players. He didn’t blame the injuries. He said, “it starts with me,” and that he has to “have some better answers so that I’m not up here saying the same things to you guys.”

He also made sure to credit his defense: “I thought our defense did enough to keep us in the game.” And he’s right. We’ll get to that in a minute.

There were two more moments from that press conference worth hearing. One reporter told McVay that the team seemed “a little disinterested, a little checked out.” McVay didn’t argue the point and didn’t make excuses. He went back to the same message: the Rams have to figure out who they are, and fast. And when he was asked about the word “humbling” and what the team would do with that feeling, he said he didn’t know yet — the team was “fresh off the field.” But he did point out that “the guys did put together a drive to go score and there were some gutsy individual efforts that I am appreciative of.” Then he said: “But collectively, even if we had come away with the result, it’s just not good enough.” That’s a coach who isn’t going to let a last-minute touchdown hide the problems from the first 59 minutes.

Now, Matthew Stafford. He finished 25 of 33 for 222 yards and two touchdowns with no interceptions. That’s another turnover-free day through the air. But the offense as a whole struggled badly. According to the AP, the Rams were held to 286 total yards after entering the game leading the NFL in total offense. According to Pro Football Talk, the Rams had fewer than 100 yards at halftime and three three-and-outs. Arizona controlled the ball for 34 minutes and 52 seconds, compared to just over 25 minutes for the Rams.

Stafford took responsibility, just like his coach. He said: “Football is a game of execution when you boil things down to it. Can you go out there and can you make the plays?” And he added: “That’s what it boils down to in this league and we’re just not doing that at a consistent enough basis right now.” When he was asked about the self-inflicted mistakes, he said he looks in the mirror first: “How can I be better? How can I help us move the ball forward on offense and score more points?”

And then he said something every Rams fan needs to hear: “We are what our record is.” No excuses. The Rams are a 2-4 team right now, and their quarterback isn’t pretending otherwise.

Stafford was also asked whether the outside expectations — the Super Bowl favorite talk from the preseason — had gotten into the locker room. He said: “No, I don’t. Not at all.” He said the media “has a job to do” and needs narratives to write about, but the players understand “how difficult it is to win games in this league.” And he added: “especially if you’re not playing up to your capabilities.” That’s the key phrase. Nobody in that locker room is saying the Rams lack talent. They’re saying they aren’t playing up to it.

He was also asked whether Alaric Jackson’s absence at left tackle played a role in the sacks. Stafford said he’d have to look at the tape. But as we mentioned, Arizona went after Quessenberry and blitzed often. Josh Sweat and Budda Baker each recorded a sack — and Baker played through the groin issue that made him questionable.

And Puka Nacua? After catching 17 passes against Buffalo, he caught six for 80 yards against Arizona — and 59 of those yards came on the final drive. When a reporter asked about the drop in targets, Stafford explained: “The amount of targets he had last week was indicative of how the game was going. And it just wasn’t this week.” Puka himself was honest: “I think there’s just a lack of execution, obviously on our offensive side of the ball.” But he also said something important about the locker room: “We’re still a connected group. There’s no pointing fingers, and there’s no arguing.”

Now, the defense. Because honestly, the Rams’ defense did its job.

Arizona reached the red zone five times. And five times, the Rams’ defense held them to a field goal. Zero touchdowns allowed. Quentin Lake had the interception. Aaron Donald had a tackle for loss and created pressure. And they did it without Trent McDuffie, who was inactive with his groin injury. Bam Knight, filling in for the injured Jeremiyah Love, ran for 80 yards on 15 carries, and McBride caught seven passes for 53 yards. Brissett went 24 of 35 for 218 yards with one interception.

That’s a defense that gave its team a chance to win. When your defense holds an opponent without a touchdown, you should win almost every time. And that’s what makes this loss so painful.

And this is now a pattern worth noticing. In the losses to Denver and Buffalo, the Rams gave up 30 and 37 points. Against Arizona, the defense was outstanding. So the defense has improved at exactly the moment the offense and special teams fell apart. Look at the Rams’ losses this season: 27-7 to San Francisco in Melbourne, 30-26 to Denver, 37-34 in overtime to Buffalo, and now 18-16 to Arizona. Three of those four losses were decided by one score. And in each of the last three, special teams mistakes were part of the story. That’s not bad luck anymore. That’s a trend.

The one big mistake on defense came at the worst possible time: the 21-yard completion and Wallace’s 15-yard penalty on the final drive. Wallace was starting in place of McDuffie, and he’s been a tough, reliable player this season. But that penalty was the difference between a long field goal attempt and a makeable one.

And now we have to talk about the injuries, because they might be the biggest story coming out of this game.

Blake Grupe hurt his knee on the first kickoff he covered. When McVay was asked for an update after the game, he said: “I don’t have an update on him. I just know he got his knee.” According to The Athletic’s Nate Atkins, the Rams will need to sign a new kicker. Think about that. Grupe was signed on Tuesday to fix the kicking game, made his first kick, and was injured minutes later. According to the AP, Grupe was the Rams’ fifth kicker over their past 57 games. And now they’ll need a sixth.

Then there’s Davante Adams. Late in the fourth quarter, after cutting on a route, Adams came up limping, grabbing at his left leg. It was a non-contact injury, and he didn’t return. As of now, there’s no official diagnosis, and McVay didn’t give an update after the game. Adams finished with three catches for 39 yards. On the season, he has 30 catches for 480 yards and four touchdowns, and he carried this passing game when Puka was out earlier this year. He’s 33 years old and in his 13th season. I’m not going to speculate about the severity. We’ll wait for the Rams’ official update. But if he misses time, the Rams would lean on a receiver group that includes Konata Mumpfield, Jordan Whittington, Alex Bachman, Tutu Atwell and rookie CJ Daniels.

So now let’s talk about the decisions the Rams have to make right now — the ones that will shape the rest of this season.

Decision number one: the kicker. The Rams need a new kicker, and they need one this week. This will be the third kicker on the roster in a little more than a week. Whoever they bring in will have just a few days to build timing with long snapper Joe Cardona and holder Ethan Evans before the Raiders game. After everything that’s happened, I’d expect the Rams to prioritize a veteran with real game experience and a reliable track record from 40 to 50 yards. And think about the holder situation, too: during the game, CBS Sports reported that receiver Jordan Whittington was working as a possible holder while Evans moved to kicker, and in the end it was Stetson Bennett who held for the go-ahead extra point. Whoever the new kicker is, the Rams need the whole operation — snap, hold and kick — locked in by Sunday.

Decision number two: Myles Garrett. On Friday, McVay said he’d have an answer on Monday about whether Garrett’s 21-day practice window opens this week. He said Garrett has “a good look in his eye” and is “making good progress.” After a game where the Rams needed more pressure on the quarterback in the biggest moments, getting Garrett back on the practice field would be the biggest boost this team could get.

Decision number three: the receiver room. If Adams is going to miss time, McVay will have to figure out who plays opposite Puka. That could mean more snaps for Tutu Atwell, more three-tight-end sets with Parkinson, Higbee and Davis Allen, and even more targets for Kyren Williams out of the backfield.

And decision number four, the biggest one of all: identity. McVay said himself he can’t answer what this team’s identity is right now. The trade deadline is November 10th. The Rams are 2-4, 0-2 in the division, and well behind San Francisco in the division race. The next three games — at Las Vegas, home against the Chargers, and at Washington — will decide whether this front office is adding pieces or bracing for a long season.

Now let me give you my grades for this game.

Defense: B-plus. Five red zone stops, zero touchdowns allowed, and a takeaway — without McDuffie. The final-drive penalty keeps it from an A.

Offense: D-plus. Fewer than 100 yards at halftime, three three-and-outs, two turnovers in Arizona territory in the fourth quarter, a fourth-down false start and a failed fourth-and-1. The final drive saves it from an F.

Special teams: incomplete, with an asterisk. Grupe’s 44-yarder was perfect, and Evans’ extra point was clutch. But a long kickoff return, a kicker injured on coverage and no field goal operation for the rest of the game — that’s another week where the third phase hurt this team.

Coaching: C. McVay’s decisions without a kicker were understandable. But a team this talented shouldn’t be making these same self-inflicted mistakes six weeks into the season, and McVay was the first to admit it.

I’ll also own my own miss: I predicted 34-16 Rams. The 16 was right, but it was on the wrong side of the scoreboard. I said Grupe would make a 40-plus field goal — he made a 44-yarder before getting hurt. And I said Puka would go over 100 yards — he finished with 80. I’ll keep putting my predictions on the record, right or wrong.

So here’s my bottom line. This wasn’t a talent problem. The defense held Arizona without a touchdown. Stafford didn’t throw an interception. Puka came alive when it mattered. What lost this game were mistakes: a kickoff return, a kicker injury that took away a whole part of the playbook, a fumble, a false start on fourth down and a 15-yard penalty on the final drive. McVay said it best: the Rams won’t win “unless we figure out how not to beat ourselves.” Las Vegas is next. The Rams have to fix this — fast.
