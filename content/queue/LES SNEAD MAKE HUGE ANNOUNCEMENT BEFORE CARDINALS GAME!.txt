Part one: the correction, and the real Downs trade

Let’s start with the Downs trade, since I got it wrong.

Here are the terms, per NFL.com. The Chiefs got wide receiver Josh Downs and a 2027 seventh-round pick. The Colts got the Chiefs’ 2027 fourth-round pick and 2027 fifth-round pick. The deal came on deadline day, Tuesday, November 10th.

And NFL.com explained the backstory. The Colts traded Michael Pittman to Pittsburgh and gave Alec Pierce the extension that Downs wanted, which left Downs in a contract year. Downs requested a trade after the Colts moved Quenton Nelson and DeForest Buckner. At first, Indianapolis didn’t honor the request. But after two more games, the team decided to get the best return it could, with Downs headed for free agency in early 2027.

Why did Kansas City do it? According to NFL.com, the Chiefs’ receiver room is led by Rashee Rice, who came back from injury in Week 8. But Xavier Worthy hurt his hamstring in the Chiefs’ Week 9 win over the Jets, which left them thin. So they paid a fourth and a fifth for a slot receiver who can help right away.

Now think back to all the fake “Schefter on First Take” clips we covered. Four of them said Downs was going to the Rams, for a fourth-round pick plus Emmanuel Forbes. The real deal was a fourth plus a fifth, and it went to Kansas City. Like the Keenan Allen clip, the fake clips got close to the price and got the team wrong.

And here’s the honest comparison. The Chiefs paid a 2027 fourth and fifth. The Rams own a 2027 fourth and fifth. So on picks alone, the Rams could have matched that offer. What they couldn’t match was the cap space. The Rams have almost nothing. The most recent number I’ve seen, from Athlon Sports citing Over The Cap, is just a few thousand dollars.

Part four: why Snead stayed quiet, in McVay’s words

The original video said, correctly, that nobody outside the building knows exactly what Snead was thinking. That’s true. But McVay gave us some real clues on Wednesday.

On the deadline in general: “We look at all that kind of stuff.” And: “It kind of takes two, and got to be able to see what you’re willing to give, what’s out there, what does it look like in terms of envisioning things.”

On whether the playbook was the problem: “It’s not as much that as much as just getting guys back, and then, okay, who are those players that you would acquire, and what does that really look like?”

On how much time he spent with Snead: “This is as much as I can remember, spending time with Les in the early parts of the week, of just the roster mechanics.”

And the key line: “The main thing is you’re hopeful that you’re getting some of these guys back, and sooner than later, rather than saying, okay, these guys are out for the year and we’re going to try to fill that void for the year.”

The original video quoted McVay from Monday, when he said, “You got to look at all avenues,” and “I would never rule it out.” The video asked whether there was a gap between what McVay wanted and what Snead did. I don’t think we can know that. But McVay’s Wednesday answers suggest the Rams really did look, and then decided their own players coming back were worth more than what was available.

Part five: the reasons that make sense

The original video listed a few possible reasons for the quiet deadline. Let me tell you which ones are backed up by facts.

Limited cap space. Confirmed. The Rams have only a few thousand dollars, per Athlon’s most recent number. Any trade would have needed a restructure, like Kyren Williams’ deal, estimated at about $3.5 million, or a player going the other way.

Picks already spent. Confirmed. The Rams don’t have their 2027 first, which Cleveland owns from the Garrett trade, their 2027 third, which Kansas City owns from the McDuffie trade, or their 2028 second. They also traded their 2027 seventh to Baltimore in the Tre’Davious White deal.

Counting on injured players. Confirmed by McVay’s own words. He said “after Green Bay is a realistic timeline” for Davante Adams, and that the Rams are “optimistic” Trent McDuffie plays this week.

The price was too high. Possible, but not confirmed. The Chiefs paid a fourth and a fifth for Downs. The 49ers paid a sixth for Allen. Those aren’t huge prices. So I’m not sure price was the main reason. Cap space looks like the bigger issue.

Nothing worth the cost. That’s a judgment call, and it’s the one Snead made.

And the original video made the most important point of all: “A legitimate reason does not guarantee a successful result.” That’s exactly right. Snead’s decision can be reasonable and still turn out to be wrong.

Part six: Les Snead’s track record

The video brought up Snead’s history, and it’s worth remembering.

This is the general manager who traded for Jalen Ramsey, Von Miller, and Matthew Stafford, and won a Super Bowl. He’s made four deadline-day trades over the years. And this offseason, he made two huge moves: Trent McDuffie in March, and Myles Garrett in June, sending Jared Verse and three picks, including a 2027 first, to Cleveland.

So this is not a cautious GM. When Snead thinks the moment is right, he swings. That’s why this deadline feels strange.

My take, and this is opinion: Snead already made his swings this year. Garrett and McDuffie were the deadline moves, just done months early. The cost of those moves is exactly why he didn’t have the cap space or picks to do more in November. That’s the trade-off of going all in during the offseason.

Part seven: the pressure on Stafford

The original video asked a great question: how much pressure can the Rams put on Matthew Stafford before it shows up on the scoreboard?

Here’s the situation he’s in.

Out: Puka Nacua, out “for an extended period of time” with an MCL and PCL sprain and turf toe. Davante Adams, on IR after hamstring surgery. Jordan Whittington, on IR. Terrance Ferguson, the tight end McVay called “really an extension of that passing game,” with a hamstring injury that’s “a couple weeks at least.” And guard Steve Avila, with a hamstring strain.

Available: Konata Mumpfield, Tutu Atwell, CJ Daniels, and Xavier Smith at receiver. Tyler Higbee, Davis Allen, and Colby Parkinson at tight end. Kyren Williams and Blake Corum in the backfield.

Here’s the hard part. Against Washington, outside of Puka, the receivers combined for four catches. Per Rams Wire’s Cameron DaSilva, Mumpfield has 162 receiving yards this season and Atwell has 99. And the passing game was already struggling with Puka. Stafford threw 43 times for 219 yards against Washington.

And the original video is right: “Stafford can be a master. He can be tough. He can be brilliant. But he cannot overcome every roster problem by himself.”

So what does Stafford need? A reliable third-down option. Someone who can win in the red zone. Someone who can stretch the field so defenses can’t sit on short routes. And protection, with Avila out.

That’s a lot to ask of a receiver group that hasn’t carried this kind of load before. McVay said on Wednesday it’ll be “a great opportunity for some new guys to get chances that they haven’t gotten yet.” Now we find out whether they can.

Part eight: Xavier Smith’s chance

The video gave Xavier Smith a fair look, and I want to do the same.

Smith knows the system. He’s been in the building. He was the Rams’ primary returner before they released him, and Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak expects him to take over Whittington’s kickoff return job, maybe punts too. That matters, because after the Rams released Alex Bachman, Kyren Williams returned punts against Washington. You don’t want your starting running back taking extra hits.

Whittington was averaging 26.3 yards per kickoff return, per Grisak. If Smith can come close to that, the Rams’ offense starts drives with better field position.

And yes, a lot of Rams fans have complicated feelings about Smith, going back to the muffed punt in last season’s NFC Championship Game loss in Seattle. That’s part of his story. But one play doesn’t define a career. If he grabs this opportunity and makes a big play this season, that would be a pretty good redemption story.

Grisak also noted that Smith’s size limits what he offers as a receiver. That’s a fair evaluation. Smith is a depth and special teams move, not a replacement for Puka. But in a stretch like this, a guy who already knows the playbook is worth a lot.

Part nine: the real reasons for hope

The video ends on a lot of worry, so let me balance it with what’s going right.

Davante Adams is coming. McVay said Wednesday, “I would think after Green Bay is a realistic timeline for him. But he looks great. I talked to him the other day, and he’s feeling good.” He also said, “What we are optimistic is Davante’s return is sooner than later, which is a big deal to us.”

Trent McDuffie might be back. McVay said, “I think we’re optimistic that this week he will be able to go.” He called McDuffie “so damn tough,” and said, “Hopefully this is the week.”

The defense is rolling. The Rams held the Chargers to 9 points and Washington to 13. Garrett had three sacks, three tackles for loss, and a strip-sack in his first game back. McVay said Aaron Donald “had a couple awesome things that allowed him to be able to get some one-on-ones,” and that Donald is having fun: “You can tell when he’s having fun and when he’s not.”

The run game works. Kyren and Corum combined for 192 rushing yards against the Raiders, 129 against the Chargers, and 124 against Washington. McVay said having both backs is “really valuable.”

The team is winning. Three straight wins. 5-4. And McVay’s message this week was simple: “There’s a way to win all of them,” quoting Bill Parcells.

So yes, the deadline was quiet. But this team has real strengths, and its best receivers are coming back.

What Downs in Kansas City tells us

Let’s think about the Downs trade from the Rams’ side for a second.

For weeks, Downs was the receiver name most connected to the Rams in real analysis. ESPN’s Ben Solak proposed a Rams deal: Downs plus a 2027 fifth for a 2028 third. Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak wrote that the Rams had called about Downs and Brian Thomas Jr., though he didn’t name a source. And Adam Schefter reported the Colts’ asking price was a third, maybe a fourth.

In the end, the Colts got a fourth and a fifth, and they threw in a seventh. That’s right in the range everyone expected. So the price wasn’t crazy. The Rams just didn’t get there.

Why? We don’t know for sure. But here’s my best guess, and this is analysis, not reporting. Downs was on a cheap rookie deal, but even a cheap salary is more than a few thousand dollars of cap space. The Rams would have needed a restructure to fit him. And with Davante Adams expected back “after Green Bay,” a slot receiver like Downs would have been the third option by December. Maybe Snead decided that wasn’t worth a fourth, a fifth, and a restructure.

The other thing worth noting: Downs is in a contract year. He’ll be a free agent in early 2027. So the Chiefs are basically renting him for the rest of the season. If the Rams still want a slot receiver in March, Downs could be available in free agency without costing any picks at all. That’s speculation, not a report. But it’s a reminder that the deadline isn’t the only door.

The Ferguson clock and the IR crunch

There’s one more roster detail that explains a lot about Snead’s week.

Terrance Ferguson, the young tight end, hurt his hamstring in practice last Friday, just after his 21-day practice window opened. McVay said Wednesday his recovery is “probably a couple weeks at least.” And he explained the problem: “If you don’t activate him by the time that 21-day clock expires, then he would go on IR for the rest of the season. And that is not what our hope is.” He believes the clock runs out around the Green Bay game.

So the Rams have to save a roster spot for Ferguson. They kept Puka off IR to save a return slot. And McVay said Monday they’re running low on return designations overall: “We’re getting to be where that number is getting so high that we’re going to run out of those eight.”

Put that together, and you can see why Snead spent so much time on “roster mechanics.” Adding a receiver by trade would have meant cutting someone else, and with this many injuries, every roster spot is already spoken for. That’s one more reason the Rams stayed quiet.

What the Rams have to do to make the gamble pay off

If Snead’s bet is going to work, a few things have to happen. Here’s my list.

One: the defense has to keep winning. Nine points allowed to the Chargers, 13 to Washington. If the defense keeps opponents under 20, the Rams can win with a limited offense.

Two: the run game has to stay hot. Three straight weeks with at least 124 rushing yards. If Kyren and Corum keep that up, Stafford doesn’t need to throw 43 times.

Three: somebody has to step up at receiver. Atwell, Mumpfield, Daniels, or Smith. One of them needs a big game in the next two weeks. Even one 80-yard day would change how defenses play the Rams.

Four: the tight ends have to catch everything. Higbee and Davis Allen are the most experienced pass catchers left. They’ll be targeted a lot.

Five: the injured players have to come back on time. McDuffie this week, hopefully. Adams after Green Bay. Ferguson before his clock runs out. Puka in December. If any of those timelines slip, the gamble gets a lot riskier.

That’s a lot of “ifs.” But it’s the path Snead chose.

Part ten: the next two games decide the grade

Here’s how I’d frame Snead’s deadline decision. It all comes down to the next two games.

Sunday: at Arizona. The Cardinals beat the Rams 18-16 on October 18th, the game where Adams got hurt. Now the Rams go back without Puka, Adams, Whittington, or Ferguson.

Then the bye. Recovery time for McDuffie, Avila, Kam Curl, and others.

Then Green Bay. Ferguson’s 21-day clock runs out around then, per McVay. Adams is still expected to be out for that one, since McVay said “after Green Bay.”

Then, hopefully, Adams. That’s when the offense should start to look normal again.

If the Rams win both games, Snead’s quiet deadline looks smart. They’d be 7-4 with Adams and eventually Puka coming back for the stretch run. If they split, it’s fine. If they lose both, everyone will ask why the Rams didn’t match the Chiefs’ fourth and fifth for Downs, or the 49ers’ sixth for Allen.

That’s the decision the video asked you to make, and here’s mine. This is opinion. I think Snead’s choice was defensible, but risky. I would have liked one cheap receiver. But I understand betting on the defense and the returns. Ask me again after Green Bay.

Part eleven: Arizona preview

Let’s get to Sunday.

The Rams’ plan. Run the ball with Kyren and Corum. Use the tight ends: Higbee, Allen, and Parkinson if healthy. Take a shot or two to Tutu Atwell, the one real deep threat. Let Xavier Smith handle returns. And let Garrett and Donald take over up front.

McVay’s approach. He said the game plan is installed “in incremental steps,” with Wednesday focused on “normal down and distance,” meaning first and second down. And he said “here’s who we envision in these spots,” which means new players will get real roles.

What I’m watching. Who leads the Rams in targets with Puka out. Whether McDuffie plays. And how much Garrett’s snap count goes up. McVay said Garrett’s role will grow “as you continue to increase his snap count as he’s feeling better.”

My prediction. Rams 20, Cardinals 16. A low-scoring, defensive game. The run game controls the clock, a tight end scores, and Garrett gets at least one sack. The Rams go into the bye at 6-4 with four straight wins.
