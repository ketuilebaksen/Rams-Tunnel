Part one: why the Rams are shopping at receiver

To understand why Josh Downs keeps coming up, you have to go back to October 18th, the 18-16 loss to the Cardinals. On the Rams’ second-to-last offensive snap, Davante Adams pulled up in the end zone on a noncontact play and hobbled to the sideline. The next day, Sean McVay said it was a significant hamstring injury that would keep him out six to ten weeks. Adams went on injured reserve, and later that week McVay said he’d need a “cleanup” procedure on the hamstring.

At the time, Adams had 30 catches for 480 yards and four touchdowns. He was the main reason defenses couldn’t put all their attention on Puka Nacua.

The good news is that the recovery seems to be going well. According to Heavy, McVay said last week that Adams’ surgery “went really well” and that his recovery is trending toward the shorter end of that timeline. Six weeks from October 19th lands around the end of November. Ten weeks pushes it toward late December. So there’s a real chance he’s back before the playoff push. But “a real chance” isn’t a guarantee, and the Rams have to win games right now.

Here’s how much they miss him. Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak put out some numbers this week that stopped me cold. Through eight weeks, the Rams’ explosive pass play rate is 4.1 percent. At this point last season, it was 17.5 percent. Since Adams got hurt, the Rams have had exactly one completion longer than 20 yards: the 40-yard catch by Tutu Atwell against the Chargers. Their overall explosive play rate ranks 27th in the league. Stafford has averaged under six intended air yards per attempt in four straight games, and his average depth of target over the last four weeks ranks 25th out of 27 quarterbacks.

Then there’s the target share. On Sunday against the Chargers, Puka caught 11 of 12 targets. Every other wide receiver on the roster combined for two catches on six targets, both by Atwell. Defenses know where the ball is going.

So the question isn’t whether the Rams need help at receiver. They do. The question is who, at what price, and whether they can fit him under the cap. That brings us to the first name.

Part two: Josh Downs, the real profile

Josh Downs was a third-round pick by the Colts in 2023. As a rookie, he caught 68 passes, which set a Colts record for a first-year player. His best season came two years ago, with 72 catches for 803 yards. For his career, he has 228 catches, 2,470 yards, and 12 touchdowns.

This season has been tougher. Through eight games, he has 30 catches for 330 yards and one touchdown, and his catch rate entering Week 8 was a career-low 56.5 percent. That’s not all on him. The Colts are 2-6 and in the middle of what a lot of people are calling a fire sale. They’ve already traded DeForest Buckner to the 49ers and Quenton Nelson to Seattle.

More importantly, Downs has requested a trade. ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler reported that last week, and Downs told ESPN he’s “open to whatever.” He’s 25 years old and in the last year of his rookie contract.

So when the video says Downs requested a trade and the Colts have shown they’re willing to move players, that’s accurate. Credit where it’s due.

Let’s talk about fit. Downs is a slot receiver who wins with quickness. He separates fast, he’s good against man coverage, and he gives a quarterback an easy throw when pressure is coming. He has also returned punts, which matters on a Rams team that’s been banged up on special teams.

Here’s the concern, and the video mentioned it too. McVay asks his receivers to block, a lot. Downs is a smaller receiver who isn’t known for blocking. ESPN’s Ben Solak, who proposed a Downs-to-the-Rams trade this week, wrote that his “biggest hang-up in trading Downs to anyone is that he has to be protected from blocking responsibilities.”

Solak’s answer is interesting. The Rams have been using a lot of 13 personnel lately, three tight ends on the field. On Sunday, Tyler Higbee played 45 snaps, Davis Allen 40, and Klare 25. In those packages, Downs could line up out wide or in a spot where he isn’t asked to seal the edge on run plays. That’s how you’d protect him.

Part three: what a Downs trade would actually cost

Now let’s talk price, and keep in mind that every number here is either a reported asking price or an analyst’s proposal. None of it is an agreed deal.

Solak’s proposal at ESPN: The Colts send Josh Downs and a 2027 fifth-round pick to the Rams for a 2028 third-round pick. That’s exactly what the video described, and it’s a real proposal from a real ESPN analyst. One thing worth noting: the Rams do own their 2028 third-rounder. They don’t have their 2027 first or third, their 2028 second, or their 2029 third, but that 2028 third is available. So pick-wise, the proposal is doable.

Adam Schefter’s reporting: Per ESPN’s Schefter, the Colts want a third-round pick for Downs, though a fourth might get it done.

Jordan Schultz’s reporting: This is new. Schultz reported this week that the Colts plan to wait until after Week 9 to make more trades, because they think offers will go up as the deadline gets closer. So if you’re expecting a Downs trade this weekend, don’t hold your breath. The Colts want a bidding war.

Who’s bidding? According to Grisak’s midweek report, the Steelers and the Chiefs look like the main teams interested in Downs. ESPN listed those two as fits as well. Grisak also writes that the Rams have called about Downs and Brian Thomas Jr. I want to be careful here. The article doesn’t say who that information came from, and calling about a player is something front offices do with dozens of names every deadline. It’s not the same as negotiating. But it’s the closest thing we have this week to an actual Rams-Downs link.

And this week, ESPN’s Bill Barnwell named Brian Thomas Jr. as the “exciting” trade option for the Rams. Barnwell’s an analyst, so that’s an idea, not a report.

Here’s my take, and this is not a report, just my opinion. The video said it wouldn’t want the Rams getting into an expensive bidding war, and I agree. Downs is in the last year of his deal. If the Rams give up a third-round pick for what amounts to an eight-game rental plus the playoffs, that’s a steep price, especially when Adams might be back by early December. But if the Rams think they can extend him, and he becomes the long-term number two next to Puka as Adams gets older, the math changes completely.

The Colts waiting until after Sunday tells me the price is going to go up, not down. If the Steelers and Chiefs both push, a third might become the floor. And I’m not sure the Rams should be the team paying that.

Part four: Christian Barmore, the real profile

Now the second name. This one came from Kyle Crabbs, a national NFL writer at A to Z Sports. On November 3rd, he wrote that Barmore would be a perfect deadline target for the Rams. A quick correction on the names in the video: it’s Kyle Crabbs, not “Krabs,” and it’s Christian Barmore, Poona Ford, Kobie Turner, and Braden Fiske.

Barmore is a big man, 6-foot-5 and 315 pounds, with real burst for his size. The Patriots took him in the second round in 2021. In 2024, they signed him to a four-year, $84 million extension. Shortly after he signed it, he was diagnosed with blood clots and missed almost all of the 2024 season. He came back healthy last year and had a strong season, with 51 pressures, per NBC Sports Boston.

This season, his role has shrunk. Per Pats Pulpit, he’s played 43.6 percent of the Patriots’ defensive snaps, down from 65.6 percent last year. Albert Breer said on NBC Sports Boston that Barmore “fell behind Milton Williams and Cory Durden in the rotation.” Breer also said, “This is a player that very clearly is available,” and that his understanding is the Patriots would want about a fourth-round pick. That’s where the “mid-round pick” in the video comes from, and it’s accurate.

On production, the numbers depend on who you ask. Pats Pulpit has him at 14 quarterback pressures and 3.5 sacks through eight weeks, second on the team in sacks. Next Gen Stats, via NBC Sports Boston, has him at 11 pressures with a 7.8 percent pressure rate, down from 12.8 percent last year. He’s also being double-teamed a lot less, 29.1 percent of the time compared with 41.6 percent last year. Either way, the picture is the same. He’s still a useful player, but he’s not the force he was a year ago.

Now, here’s what the video didn’t tell you. On Wednesday, Patriots head coach Mike Vrabel was asked about the Breer report, and he pushed back hard. He said, “I have not shared that information with anybody,” and, “This is business as normal here, so I don’t know where a lot of these things come from.” He also said national reports “have the tendency” to stir up trade talk this time of year.

On Thursday, Barmore himself was asked about it. His answer: “I’m focused on ball. Really, I don’t know what to tell you.” He also asked a reporter, “Is it coming from my team?” Then he said, “I focus on playing ball. That’s all I do.”

And keep in mind, the Patriots are 5-3. They’re not sellers in the usual sense. If they move Barmore, it’s because of his contract and his role, not because they’re giving up on the season.

Part five: the money problem, and it’s a big one

This is the part I think matters most, and the video skipped it.

The Rams have about $206,000 in cap space, according to Athlon Sports and Over The Cap. Not two million. Two hundred and six thousand.

Christian Barmore’s cap hit this season is $17.1 million, the third-largest on the Patriots. According to Pats Pulpit, trading him would clear nearly $8 million in cap space for New England this year. In other words, whoever trades for him has to take on roughly $8 million for the rest of 2026.

So for the Rams to trade for Barmore, they’d need to clear about $8 million in cap room in the next five days. A Kyren Williams restructure could free up roughly $3.5 million. That’s not even halfway there. To get to $8 million, the Rams would need a combination of restructures, or they’d have to send salary back to New England, or the Patriots would have to agree to keep part of his salary. Teams that are trying to get out from under a contract usually don’t want to keep paying it.

Now, to be fair, there is something attractive about Barmore’s contract. Per NBC Sports Boston, he has no guaranteed money left after this season. His base salaries are $18 million in 2027 and $19 million in 2028, but because none of that is guaranteed, the Rams could cut him after the season with no dead money from future salary. So it’s basically a rental, with an option to keep him.

But for a team with $206,000 in cap space, an $8 million rental is a huge lift. Let me say this clearly. This is not a report, and I’m not saying the Rams are trying to do this. But from everything I can see, a Barmore trade would be one of the hardest deals for the Rams to pull off on this entire deadline list.

Compare that with Downs. He’s on a third-round rookie contract, so his salary is a small fraction of Barmore’s. I couldn’t find an exact figure for what’s left on his 2026 salary, so I won’t make one up. It’s probably still more than $206,000, so even Downs would likely take a small restructure. But that’s a manageable move. Barmore isn’t.

Part six: the defensive line situation

The video’s case for Barmore is the defensive line, so let’s look at that.

On October 19th, the same day the Adams news broke, McVay announced that Poona Ford was out for the season with a ruptured triceps. Ford had played just under 40 percent of the defensive snaps as a run-stopping, space-eating tackle. Kobie Turner went on injured reserve with a groin injury, and McVay said he’d miss “a handful of games.”

That’s a real hole. The video is right about that.

But here’s some context it left out. First, Turner is on injured reserve, not out for the year. Players on short-term IR have to sit at least four games, and he went on IR around October 20th. The Rams have played two games since, against the Raiders and the Chargers, so he’s getting closer. Remember what McVay said last week: “Hopefully, some of those trades will be guys getting back from injury.” Turner is one of those guys.

Second, Aaron Donald is back. Yes, the Aaron Donald, back from retirement and on this defensive line. And per Pro Football Talk, Sunday against Washington could be the first time Donald and Myles Garrett play together. More on Garrett in a minute.

Third, the defense has been playing well without Ford and Turner. On Sunday, they pressured Justin Herbert on 17 of 37 dropbacks, picked him off twice, and held the Chargers without a touchdown. Braden Fiske has held up in the middle.

So would Barmore help? Sure. But would he be worth $8 million in cap room the Rams don’t have, when Turner might be back in a couple of weeks and Garrett is about to return? I’m not convinced.

Part seven: the injury that changes the priority list

Here’s the news that broke on Wednesday, and honestly, I think it matters more than either name in the video.

Jaylen Watson is out for the season. The cornerback needs shoulder surgery and is going on injured reserve. He dislocated the shoulder back in Week 3, missed a game, then re-aggravated it against the Chargers on Sunday. McVay’s reaction: “That’s a big blow. He’s been such a stud for us.”

And it’s not just Watson. Trent McDuffie, who just had one of the best games of his season with nine tackles and a game ball against the Chargers, didn’t practice Wednesday or Thursday because of a back issue. Safety Kam Curl also missed both days with a foot injury.

So now look at the Rams’ secondary. Their best outside corner signing, Watson, is gone for the year. McDuffie’s status for Sunday is uncertain. Curl’s status is uncertain. And here’s the twist: ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler had suggested this week that the Rams could consider trading cornerback Emmanuel Forbes, because Forbes “has lost his starting job due to the additions of Trent McDuffie and Jaylen Watson.” That idea doesn’t make much sense anymore. Forbes might be starting on Sunday.

Turf Show Times ran a piece this week looking at cornerback trade options after the Watson injury. I couldn’t open the full article, so I can’t tell you every name they listed. But the fact that Rams beat outlets are now talking about cornerbacks tells you where the conversation has moved.

So here’s how I’d rank the Rams’ deadline needs right now. This is not a report, it’s my opinion.

Number one: wide receiver. The numbers on explosive plays are too ugly to ignore, and Adams is still a few weeks away at best.

Number two: cornerback. That moved way up this week because of Watson. If McDuffie’s back turns into something that lingers, it could become number one.

Number three: interior defensive line. Turner is coming back, Donald is there, and Garrett’s return will help everybody up front.

The video said it would lean toward the defensive line. I respect the reasoning, losing Poona Ford hurts, but the injury report changed this week, and I think the priorities changed with it.

Part eight: Matthew Stafford’s elbow, the update

Earlier this week, the big worry was Stafford’s right elbow. He left the locker room Sunday with ice on his arm, and on Monday McVay said only, “He’s had some soreness there,” and told reporters to ask Stafford.

So the reporters asked, and here’s what Stafford said.

“It feels good.”

“Full practice, as I have been all year, and hope to continue to be that way.”

“I haven’t missed a rep at practice. I haven’t missed a rep in a game, and I feel good.”

On the ice: “I’m not too worried about it.”

And my favorite line of the week: “Listen, I’m 38 years old, trying to keep up with these young bucks.”

And this time the actions match the words. He was listed on the injury report but practiced fully on Wednesday and Thursday. Barring a surprise on Friday’s final report, he’s playing Sunday.

There’s one more thing I need to mention, carefully. According to Grisak’s midweek report, Doug McCain of 97.1 The Fan in Los Angeles reportedly said the elbow injury happened when Stafford tried to tackle Talanoa Hufanga on a pick-six against Denver, and that the team considered surgery before Stafford decided to play through it. I want to be very clear: that’s one radio host’s account, passed along second-hand. The Rams haven’t confirmed it, and Stafford didn’t say it. I’m telling you about it because it’s out there, and because it would help explain why his air yards dropped. But treat it as unconfirmed.

What I can say for sure is this: the drop in deep passing showed up right when Adams got hurt. Whether it’s the elbow, the missing receiver, or the game plan, and it’s probably some mix of all three, Sunday against a weak Washington secondary is a chance to see whether the Rams can push the ball down the field again.

Part nine: Myles Garrett is back on the field

The real “star player” the Rams are adding this month isn’t coming in a trade.

Myles Garrett returned to practice on Wednesday for the first time since Week 1. He was limited on Wednesday and limited again on Thursday. Some background: he had knee arthroscopy in mid-September after playing 40 snaps in the opener, was given a six-week prognosis, and has missed seven games. His 21-day practice window is now open.

McVay has been careful not to set any expectations. On Monday he said, “I want to be careful to even set any sort of expectations,” and that they’d take it “a day at a time.” Ian Rapoport said the Rams thought Garrett would be back last week, but he wasn’t comfortable on the knee yet, so they waited.

Limited both days means he’s not all the way there yet. Friday’s report will tell us a lot. If he gets a full practice in, or even a limited one with no game status, he could be active Sunday. If he’s listed as doubtful or out, they’re giving him another week.

But think about what’s coming. Donald inside. Garrett on the edge. Byron Young on the other side. Turner coming back. That’s the “scariest front in the NFC” the headline wanted, and the Rams don’t have to trade a single pick for it.

Part ten: Washington preview

Now to Sunday. Rams at Washington, 1 p.m. Eastern on FOX. The Rams are 4-4, tied with Seattle for second in the NFC West behind San Francisco at 6-1. Washington is 2-4-1, coming off a big 27-10 win over the Eagles. The Rams are 5.5-point favorites on the road, per DraftKings.

Washington has its own injury problems. They put running back Jeremy McNichols and defensive tackles Daron Payne and Javon Kinlaw on injured reserve, so each will miss at least four games. Losing Payne and Kinlaw is a big deal for their interior run defense, which should help Kyren Williams and Blake Corum. Stefon Diggs didn’t practice Wednesday, but it was listed as rest. Jayden Daniels, who’s been dealing with a left elbow issue, practiced fully.

Matchups I’m watching.

The Rams’ run game vs. a depleted Washington interior. On Sunday against the Chargers, the Rams ran for just 10 yards in the first half and 129 in the second. Corum had 73 yards and a 23-yard touchdown, and Kyren had 56 and a score. Without Payne and Kinlaw, Washington is thin in the middle, so the Rams should be able to run.

Stafford’s depth of target. CBS listed cornerback as Washington’s biggest deadline need. If the Rams are ever going to push the ball downfield, this is the week. If the average depth of target is still around five yards on Sunday, we’ll know it’s not about the opponent.

The Rams’ secondary vs. Daniels and Diggs. This is my biggest worry. No Watson, McDuffie and Curl uncertain, against a quarterback who can extend plays and a veteran receiver like Diggs. The pass rush has to get home, because the coverage behind it may be thinner than it’s been all season.

The start. The Rams went five straight drives without a first down to open the Chargers game, the first time that’s happened under McVay. They can’t do that on the road against a team with momentum.

My prediction. Rams 24, Commanders 19. The run game takes over in the second half, Puka gets his usual 9 or 10 catches, and the defense gets one big turnover. If McDuffie and Curl are both out, I’d knock a few points off the margin.

Part eleven: the deadline timeline

Here’s where we are. The trade deadline is Tuesday, November 10th, at 4 p.m. Eastern. That’s four days after this video.

Les Snead has made four deadline-day trades over the years, so if anything happens, history says it’s more likely Tuesday than today. The Colts say they’re waiting until after Sunday, so a Downs move would most likely come Monday or Tuesday. And the Patriots’ coach is publicly saying “business as normal,” which doesn’t sound like a team in a hurry to move Barmore.

So here’s what I’ll be watching this weekend:

How Garrett looks on Friday’s injury report.

Whether McDuffie and Curl can play, because if they can’t, a cornerback move jumps up the list.

How the passing game looks against Washington. Another game with one explosive pass would make a receiver trade more likely.

Whether the Kyren restructure happens. If the Rams suddenly create cap space, that’s the clearest sign they’re getting ready to make a move.

And here’s my reminder for the next four days. When you see a title that says “massive trade,” check three things. Does a named insider say it’s done?
