Let’s start with the good news, because it’s big.

According to the Rams’ official website, McVay said on Monday that Myles Garrett will practice this week. That starts his 21-day practice window. Once that window opens, the Rams have 21 days to decide whether to activate him from injured reserve.

McVay was careful not to promise anything. According to ESPN’s Sarah Barshop, he said: “I want to be careful to even set any sort of expectations.” He added: “I think just taking it a day at a time is going to be the best way that we can approach it.” And he said: “Think that’s the right thing for him, and really just to see how he responds.” But he also said: “I think it’s a real positive that we’re starting his clock.”

Here’s the background, according to ESPN. Garrett has been on injured reserve since having arthroscopic knee surgery in mid-September. He played in the season opener, but said afterward he wasn’t 100 percent healthy. And he sat out most of training camp with what McVay called knee irritation. So this has been a long road. Back in September, according to Pro Football Talk, NFL Network’s Ian Rapoport reported that the Week 9 game against Washington was Garrett’s most likely return date. McVay didn’t put a timeline on it, and ESPN didn’t say whether he could play this Sunday. But for the first time in weeks, there’s real movement.

Let me quickly explain what happens next, because the timing matters. Once Garrett practices, the 21-day clock starts. The Rams can activate him at any point in that window. If they think he’s ready by Sunday, he could play against Washington. If they want more practice time, the next chance would be the Cardinals game the following week. And after that comes the bye, which would give him even more time to build up. If the 21 days run out without him being activated, he’d have to stay on injured reserve for the rest of the season — which is why teams don’t open the window until they’re confident. That’s my own explanation of the rule. So the fact that the Rams are starting the clock tells you they believe he’s close.

And think about the timing relative to the trade deadline. The deadline is Tuesday, November 10th. By then, the Rams will have seen Garrett practice for about a week. That’s exactly the kind of information Les Snead needs before deciding whether to add anyone on defense.

And remember, Garrett was the 2025 AP Defensive Player of the Year with a record 23 sacks, according to Pro Football Talk. According to the Rams’ official website, the Rams came into Week 8 with just 10 sacks, near the bottom of the league. Getting Garrett back would be the biggest “addition” the Rams could make this season — and it wouldn’t cost a single draft pick.

Connors is a bigger loss than people might think. According to Rams On SI’s Blaine Grisak, Connors had the highest Pro Football Focus grade on the Rams’ special teams against the Chargers, at 84.0. On a team that’s been fighting special teams problems all year, that matters.

Now let’s talk about the standings, because Monday night changed them.

According to The Everett Herald, the Bears beat the Seahawks 34-17 on Monday Night Football at Lumen Field. Caleb Williams threw for 247 yards and a touchdown, and D’Andre Swift scored three touchdowns. Seattle’s running game managed just 52 yards on 16 carries. And this was the game where Quenton Nelson — the All-Pro guard Seattle traded for last week — played left guard for the Seahawks. They still lost by 17.

So here’s the NFC West now. The 49ers are 6-1 after their bye, according to Yahoo Sports. The Seahawks are 4-4, according to The Everett Herald. The Rams are 4-4. And the Cardinals are 3-5 after beating Dallas in overtime, according to CBS Texas.

Two weeks ago, the Rams were 2-4 and stuck near the bottom of the division. Now they’re tied for second. That’s what back-to-back wins do.

And here’s something to think about. Last week, a lot of people — including the video I’m responding to — worried that Seattle and San Francisco were getting stronger while the Rams stood still. Seattle traded for Nelson and then lost by 17. The Rams didn’t make a trade and won by 15. Trades don’t win games by themselves. Execution does.

Jaxon Smith-Njigba had 118 receiving yards and a 43-yard touchdown. So Seattle still has weapons. But the Bears controlled the game. The Bears are now 6-2, according to The Everett Herald. And remember, Chicago is the team that traded two first-round picks for Jeffery Simmons last week, according to Pro Football Rumors. So the Bears are clearly all-in. They could be a team the Rams have to deal with in the NFC playoff race.

Now let’s get to the main point of the original video, because it’s a fair one: the offense.

The Rams won 24-9, but the offense was a mess for most of the first half. According to ESPN, the Rams had no first downs on their first five drives — the first time that’s happened in McVay’s career. According to The Sporting Tribune, their first 15 plays gained just 24 yards. The original video says the first five drives produced only 14 yards. I couldn’t confirm that exact number, so I’ll stick with the 24 yards on 15 plays from The Sporting Tribune.

And Stafford’s numbers back up the concern. According to Pro Football Talk, he went 17-of-25 for 157 yards, one touchdown and one interception. According to Grisak, his QBR was 35.7, he averaged negative 0.23 expected points added per pass, and Pro Football Focus gave him a 63.8 grade. Grisak graded the quarterback position a C-minus.

Let me quickly explain those numbers, because they get thrown around a lot. QBR is ESPN’s quarterback rating, on a scale of 0 to 100, where 50 is about average. A 35.7 is below average. And expected points added, or EPA, measures how much each play helps or hurts a team’s chances to score. A negative number means the average pass actually hurt the Rams. That’s my own explanation, but it tells you the passing game didn’t help much against the Chargers, even though the Rams won.

And this isn’t just one game. According to the Rams’ official website, before the Chargers game, Stafford had averaged under five air yards per attempt in his previous two games — something he hadn’t done in his prior 23 outings. And after the Raiders game, offensive coordinator Nate Scheelhaase said: “There are some games, like the last one, where your biggest gain is 17 yards.” So the Rams have been winning with short, efficient passes and a strong run game, not with big plays down the field. Against the Chargers, the only big passing play was Atwell’s 40-yard catch.

Now, to be fair to Stafford, he’s still the 2025 MVP, and the week before, according to the Rams’ website, he completed 79 percent of his passes against the Raiders — his best mark as a Ram. This isn’t about Stafford suddenly being a bad quarterback. It’s about an offense that’s missing Davante Adams and Terrance Ferguson and is still figuring out how to create explosive plays without them.

But here’s what’s interesting — and this is where I agree with the original video’s point. It doesn’t look like protection was the problem. According to Grisak, the offensive line allowed “just one pressure,” and he gave the line an A-plus. Steve Avila had an 86.1 PFF grade, and Kevin Dotson had a 79.0. Coleman Shelton, who was questionable with a hip injury, played all 54 offensive snaps, according to Athlon Sports.

So if the line was blocking well, why was the passing game so quiet? It comes down to who was getting open.

According to Grisak, Puka Nacua caught 11 of his 12 targets for 80 yards. The rest of the Rams’ receivers combined for two catches on six targets. The original video said seven targets — a small difference, but I’ll go with Grisak’s number. And according to Grisak, Tutu Atwell was the only other receiver with a catch. Stafford’s interception was thrown to Konata Mumpfield.

So the original video asks the right question: who becomes Stafford’s answer if a defense takes Puka away? Right now, there’s no clear answer at receiver.

And there’s another detail that tells you how McVay is solving the problem. According to Athlon Sports, citing Pro Football Focus, the Rams used three tight ends heavily against the Chargers. Tyler Higbee played 45 snaps, Davis Allen played 40, and Max Klare played 25 in his return. Meanwhile, the receivers behind Puka barely played: Mumpfield played 27 snaps, Atwell 21 and Jordan Whittington just four.

So McVay’s answer, for now, isn’t a third receiver. It’s more tight ends. That’s important for the trade debate, and I’ll come back to it.

Let me explain why a coach would do that. When you put three tight ends on the field, the defense usually has to bring in bigger players to stop the run. That makes it easier to run the ball, and it gives the quarterback more help in pass protection. And if the defense stays in a lighter package, the offense can run right at them. The downside is that tight ends usually don’t create as much separation as receivers, which can make the passing game more predictable. That’s my own explanation of the trade-off. And that’s exactly what we saw against the Chargers: a great running game in the second half, a strong offensive line performance and very little from the receivers behind Puka.

Now, the turnaround. The original video gives McVay credit for leaning on the run game, and that’s fair.

According to Pro Football Talk, the Rams had just 10 rushing yards at halftime and 129 in the second half. Blake Corum finished with 73 yards on 11 carries, including a 23-yard touchdown run to open the second half. Kyren Williams ran 16 times for 56 yards and a touchdown. Grisak gave the running backs an A-minus.

And McVay has been talking about exactly this. Before the Chargers game, he said he’s “not necessarily as interested in like balance as much as how do we consistently move the ball and score points.” But he also said the offense is at its best when it has “the threat of both” — run and pass — because “you kind of keep people off balance.”

After the game, according to The Sporting Tribune, McVay said: “It’s really hard, you know, to even establish any sort of momentum or rhythm.” And he summed up the day: “It’s not really how you start, it’s how you finish.”

The original video asks: did McVay rediscover an offensive identity? I think the answer is: he’s still building one. Against the Raiders, the Rams ran 38 times and threw 29 passes, according to the AP. Against the Chargers, the run game took over in the second half. Two weeks in a row, the Rams won by leaning on Kyren and Corum. That’s starting to look like a pattern, not an accident.

But the original video is also right about the risk. Eventually, the Rams will face a defense that can stop the run. And when that happens, the passing game will need more than Puka.

The good news is that help is coming on offense, too — just not right away. According to Heavy, McVay said last week that Davante Adams’ surgery “went really well,” and that his recovery is “probably closer to the shorter term” of the six-to-10-week timeline. And Ferguson could start practicing this week. So by the time the Rams come out of their bye, they might have Adams and Ferguson back alongside Puka, Higbee, Allen and the running backs. That would be a very different offense than the one that went five drives without a first down.

Now let’s talk about the part of the team that’s carrying everything: the defense.

The original video says the defense created 10 points off turnovers, and that checks out. According to the Rams’ official website, Quentin Lake’s interception set up a Brandon McManus field goal, and Trent McDuffie’s interception set up Puka’s touchdown. That’s three plus seven — 10 points.

Grisak gave the secondary an A-plus. McDuffie had an 86.6 PFF grade and led the Rams with nine tackles, according to Grisak. Lake had a 77.9, and Emmanuel Forbes had a 75.9. Linebacker Omar Speights had an 85.6, and Grisak noted he had four impact plays and no missed tackles. Defensive lineman Tyler Davis had an 83.3. And edge rusher Wesley Bailey had a sack and a forced fumble on Justin Herbert.

According to The Sporting Tribune, Herbert was under pressure on 17 of his 37 dropbacks. According to ESPN, the Chargers haven’t scored a touchdown in their last nine quarters. And according to the Rams’ official website, the Rams’ defense has now gone two straight home games without giving up a touchdown.

The original video asked whether this defense can become the foundation of a playoff push. I think it already is. And now think about what happens if Garrett comes back. A defense that’s already playing at this level, adding the reigning Defensive Player of the Year — that’s a scary thought for the rest of the NFC.

Now, let’s get to the trade debate, because the original video lays out both sides, and I want to do the same — with what we know now.

Side one: stay patient. The Rams have won two straight. They’re tied for second. Garrett is coming back to practice. Ferguson might be next. Davante Adams’ recovery is trending toward the shorter end of his six-to-10-week timeline, according to McVay. And last week, McVay said, according to the New York Post: “Hopefully, some of those trades will be guys getting back from injury that haven’t played for us.” This week, that’s starting to actually happen.

Side two: add one piece. The offense needs a receiver who can take pressure off Puka. Two catches on six targets from the rest of the receiver room isn’t good enough. And with Watson’s shoulder flaring up again, cornerback depth could become a concern too.

Here’s where I land, and remember — this is my opinion, not a report.

Garrett’s return changes the math on defense. If he’s back soon, I don’t think the Rams need to trade for a pass rusher or a defensive tackle. The defense is already playing well, and it’s about to get its best player.

But the offense is a different story. Garrett can’t catch passes. If the Rams make one move, I think it should be a receiver. But here’s the twist: McVay’s heavy use of three tight ends suggests he might not see a big need for a third receiver. The Athletic’s Nate Atkins pointed out last week, according to Heavy, that Josh Downs “is a slot-only player, which makes him a harder fit with the three-tight end sets the Rams love.” And the snap counts from Sunday back that up.

So if the Rams add a receiver, it might need to be someone who can play outside and block — someone who fits a run-first, tight-end-heavy offense.

Let’s run through the names quickly.

Josh Downs of the Colts. According to ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler, he’s requested a trade. According to Grisak, he’d cost about 2.4 million dollars for the rest of the season. But he’s a slot receiver, and ESPN listed the Chiefs and Steelers as his fits.

Jauan Jennings of the Vikings. ESPN listed the Rams as a fit, with a 50 percent chance he’s traded. He’s a physical outside receiver who blocks. But according to Athlon Sports, he has an 8-million-dollar base salary, and the Vikings are unbeaten.

And a new name: Dontayvion Wicks of the Eagles. According to Heavy, FanSided’s John Sbisa floated Wicks as a Rams target on Monday. According to Heavy, Wicks has 19 catches for 284 yards and a touchdown in six games this season. But Heavy also noted that Wicks signed a one-year, 12.5-million-dollar extension that runs through 2027, and that “no formal interest has been confirmed.” That contract makes him a tough fit for a team with almost no cap space.

And that’s the biggest issue with all of them. According to Athlon Sports, citing Over The Cap, the Rams have 206,406 dollars in cap space. Any trade would need a contract restructure first. According to Grisak, restructuring Kyren Williams’ deal could free about 3.5 million dollars.

And here’s a timing note. According to Grisak, Monday was the most realistic day for a Rams trade this week, based on Les Snead’s history. Monday came and went with no trade. So if the Rams do something, history says it’s most likely to happen on deadline day itself — Tuesday, November 10th, at 4 p.m. Eastern. According to Grisak, Snead has made four trades on deadline day during his time as general manager.

Now let’s look ahead to Sunday, because the Rams have a big road game.

According to RotoWire, the Commanders beat the Eagles 27-10 last week to improve to 2-4-1. Jayden Daniels threw for 217 yards and a touchdown to Stefon Diggs. According to ESPN’s game page, the Rams play at Washington on Sunday at 1 p.m. Eastern on FOX.

Here are my three early keys.

Key number one: start fast. The Rams can’t afford another five drives without a first down on the road. If McVay goes to tempo early, like the two-minute drive that sparked the comeback against the Chargers, the offense could get into rhythm sooner.

Key number two: contain Daniels. A dual-threat quarterback can punish a defense that loses its rush lanes. If Garrett is ready, great. If not, the Rams need the same discipline they showed against Herbert.

Key number three: protect the cornerback room. With Watson’s shoulder and McDuffie’s back both day-to-day, the Rams might be thin at corner against a receiver like Diggs. Forbes played well against the Chargers, with a 75.9 PFF grade according to Grisak. He might need to do it again.

My early prediction: Rams 24, Commanders 20. Washington is coming off its best win of the season and it’s at home, so I expect a close game. But the Rams’ defense is playing too well right now, and if Garrett is anywhere near ready, I like the Rams to win their third straight and stay tied for second — or better — in the NFC West heading into the deadline.

So here’s my bottom line.

No, the Rams didn’t make a blockbuster trade. The video behind that headline admits it. What it does get right is the concern about the offense: five drives without a first down, a C-minus grade for the quarterback position, and only two catches from receivers not named Puka. That’s a real issue, and it’s the main reason the trade deadline still matters.

But the bigger news this week is good news. Myles Garrett is returning to practice. Ferguson might follow. McDuffie feels better than expected. The defense is playing at a high level. And after Seattle’s loss on Monday night, the Rams are tied for second in the NFC West at 4-4.

If the Rams make a move before November 10th, I think it should be on offense. But even if they don’t, the “blockbuster addition” this week might be the one McVay talked about all along — the Defensive Player of the Year finally getting back on the field.
