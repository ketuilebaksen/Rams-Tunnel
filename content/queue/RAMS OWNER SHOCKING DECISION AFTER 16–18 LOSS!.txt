Now let’s get to the part that actually matters for Rams fans: would Jerry Jeudy make sense for this team right now?

Let’s start with the contract, because this is where the story surprised me. The claim that Jeudy’s base salary is only 1.5 million dollars this season? That part is actually true. According to Pro Football Rumors, Jeudy signed a three-year, 52.5 million dollar extension with Cleveland, but only the guaranteed portion covers this season. His 2026 base salary is 1.5 million dollars. And there’s a non-guaranteed 21.49 million dollar option bonus due in 2027. According to Heavy, that means an acquiring team would owe him only his 1.5 million dollar base salary this year and could release him after the season if things don’t work out.

Why does that matter so much? Because the Rams have roughly one million dollars in cap space. Remember what we talked about with Courtland Sutton — a receiver making 23 million dollars a year simply doesn’t fit. But when you trade for a player midseason, you only take on the remaining portion of his base salary. By the November 10th deadline, a good chunk of Jeudy’s 1.5 million would already be paid by Cleveland. That makes him one of the very few established receivers the Rams could realistically fit under their cap without sending big salary back. That’s a real point in his favor.

Now let’s talk about the player. Jeudy was a first-round pick by the Denver Broncos in 2020 out of Alabama. Denver traded him to Cleveland in March of 2024 for a fifth-round pick and a sixth-round pick. And in his first season in Cleveland, he broke out. In 2024, he caught 90 passes for 1,229 yards and four touchdowns and made the Pro Bowl. According to Heavy, he has 356 career catches for 4,946 yards and 17 touchdowns. He’s 27 years old.

And his trade value has been a roller coaster. According to Pro Football Rumors, at the 2023 trade deadline, Denver turned down an offer of a third-round pick and a fifth-round pick for Jeudy. A few months later, in March of 2024, the Broncos traded him to Cleveland for just a fifth and a sixth. Then he made the Pro Bowl. Now, after a quiet 2025 and a slow start to 2026, his value is back down to the Day 3 range. That history tells you two things. First, teams have always believed in his talent — even when his production didn’t match it. And second, the price for Jeudy has never stayed high for long. If the Rams are patient, the cost might come down even further as the deadline gets closer.

And remember where he came from. At Alabama, Jeudy won the Biletnikoff Award as the nation’s best receiver. He’s a smooth route runner who can separate at all three levels. That’s the kind of skill set that, in theory, fits perfectly next to a receiver like Puka Nacua, who does so much damage after the catch and in contested situations.

But here’s what the viral story got wrong: it claims Jeudy is coming off a 1,000-yard season “last season.” He isn’t. In 2025, according to Pro Football Rumors, Jeudy had 602 receiving yards in 17 games. His only 1,000-yard season was 2024.

And this season has been even quieter. According to NFL Trade Rumors, through five games, Jeudy has eight catches on 15 targets for 81 yards and no touchdowns. The Browns have been leaning on their young receivers, rookies KC Concepcion and Denzel Boston. So Jeudy has gone from Pro Bowler to a reduced role in less than two years.

So now let’s ask the big question: why would the Rams even consider it?

The answer is Davante Adams. Late in the fourth quarter against Arizona, Adams came up limping after cutting on a route, grabbing at his left leg. It was a non-contact injury, and he didn’t return. As of now, there’s no official diagnosis, and I’m not going to speculate on how serious it is. But if Adams misses significant time, the Rams lose a receiver who has 30 catches for 480 yards and four touchdowns this season — the player who carried this passing game when Puka Nacua was out earlier this year. Adams is 33 years old, in his 13th NFL season, and a six-time Pro Bowler. He’s been the veteran voice in that receiver room. Losing him for any real stretch would hurt on and off the field.

And think about what it would mean for Puka. Against Buffalo, Puka was targeted 24 times and caught 17 passes. Against Arizona, he caught six for 80 yards, with 59 of those yards on the final drive. When asked about the drop in targets, Stafford said the volume against Buffalo “was indicative of how the game was going. And it just wasn’t this week.” But without Adams, defenses can put two defenders on Puka on every key down. The Rams would need someone else to make them pay for it. That’s the strongest argument for adding a receiver like Jeudy.

Without Adams, the Rams’ receiver room would include Puka, Tutu Atwell, Konata Mumpfield, Jordan Whittington, Alex Bachman and rookie CJ Daniels. That’s a group with some real ability, but it’s thin on proven production behind Puka. And this offense was already struggling on Sunday. According to the AP, the Rams were held to 286 total yards against a Cardinals defense that came in ranked last in the league in points allowed. They had fewer than 100 yards at halftime, according to Pro Football Talk.

So if Adams’ injury turns out to be serious, a cheap, experienced receiver who could step in as a number two or number three option would make sense. CBS Sports’ Carter Bahns said Jeudy could do exactly that for a team right away.

And let’s compare Jeudy to the other options, because there’s a reason his name keeps coming up for the Rams.

Option one is Courtland Sutton. We covered that rumor in a recent video. Sutton signed a four-year, 92 million dollar extension in Denver, the Broncos are 4-2, and the money simply doesn’t work for a Rams team with about one million in cap space.

Option two is staying in-house. Tutu Atwell is back with the Rams after the August trade that sent running back Jarquez Hunter to Miami. Atwell had 105 catches for 1,535 yards and five touchdowns in his first five seasons with the Rams, and he knows McVay’s system inside and out. McVay said when he came back: “There’s a lot of love for Tutu Atwell in this locker room and a lot of familiarity.” But Atwell was inactive on Sunday, and last season he caught just six passes for 192 yards as the Rams leaned heavily on three-tight-end sets. Konata Mumpfield, Jordan Whittington, Alex Bachman and CJ Daniels give the Rams depth, but not much proven production.

Option three is Jeudy. A Pro Bowler two years ago, on a 1.5 million dollar base salary this season, available for what might be a Day 3 pick. Of all the outside options, he’s the one that fits the Rams’ cap. That’s why analysts keep connecting the dots.

Now let me give you the case against it, because I promised you both sides.

First, the production. A receiver with 81 yards through five games isn’t playing at a Pro Bowl level right now. You’d be betting that a change of scenery and a better quarterback bring back the 2024 version. That’s possible — a better quarterback and a bigger role can change a receiver’s season quickly — but it’s still a bet.

Second, the fit. Even the analyst who suggested the Rams, Blaine Grisak, had reservations. He wrote: “Once upon a time, Jeudy may have been a perfect fit in Sean McVay’s offense.” And he added: “Playing for a contender may change his attitude on the field.” That’s a polite way of saying there are questions about how he’d fit in. The Rams’ locker room has a strong culture, and McVay doesn’t add players without thinking about how they’d affect it.

Third, the price. If the Browns really want a third- or fourth-round pick, as Jordan Schultz reported, that’s more than a team should pay for a receiver with 81 yards this season. The Rams already traded their 2027 first-round pick, their 2027 third-rounder, their 2028 second-rounder and their 2029 third-rounder in the Garrett and McDuffie deals. Every remaining pick matters.

There’s also the question of how to read the conflicting reports about Cleveland. On one side, Mary Kay Cabot says the Browns aren’t interested in moving Jeudy. On the other, Jordan Schultz says teams know he’s available and the Browns want a third- or fourth-round pick. Those two things can both be true. A team can say it isn’t trying to trade a player and still listen if the right offer comes in. That’s how most trade deadline talks work. It just means the Rams shouldn’t expect a bargain-bin price unless Cleveland’s season starts to slip.

And fourth, the bigger problems. On Sunday, the Rams didn’t lose because they lacked receivers. They lost because of mistakes. McVay said it himself: “We won’t win a game unless we figure out how not to beat ourselves.” A kickoff return, a kicker injured on coverage, a fumble deep in Arizona territory, a false start on fourth down, and a 15-yard penalty on the final drive. A new receiver doesn’t fix any of that.

So here’s my verdict. If Davante Adams’ injury is serious, and if the Browns’ price drops to a fifth-round pick or lower, then Jerry Jeudy becomes a smart, low-risk move for the Rams. The contract fits. The need would be real. And if it doesn’t work, the Rams could move on after the season. But if Adams is only going to miss a game or two, or if Cleveland keeps asking for a third- or fourth-rounder, I’d pass. This is a “wait for the medical report” decision, not a “make the trade today” decision.

And let me explain how a deal like this would actually be structured, because the details matter. A conditional pick usually means the compensation changes based on what happens — for example, a fifth-round pick that becomes a fourth if the player reaches a certain number of snaps or catches. That protects the Rams if Jeudy doesn’t fit, and it rewards the Browns if he does. The Rams would also need a roster spot. They’re already going to need one for a new kicker after Grupe’s injury. So adding Jeudy would mean making another tough cut somewhere on the 53-man roster. None of this is impossible. But it’s why front offices take their time with these decisions.

And honestly, that’s exactly how the Rams’ front office has operated. They don’t make panic moves on a Monday after a loss. They gather information, they wait for the medical results, and then they act. Remember, the Rams and Browns already have a relationship — they made the Myles Garrett trade this offseason. If the Rams want Jeudy, Les Snead knows exactly who to call. And that Garrett deal had a very interesting clause. The Rams sent Jared Verse, a 2027 first-round pick, a 2028 second-round pick and a 2029 third-round pick to Cleveland — and that 2029 third becomes a first if the Rams ever trade Garrett to an AFC North team. The Browns protected themselves from seeing Garrett come back to haunt them twice a year. That tells you these two front offices know how to negotiate creative deals with each other.

Now, while we’re talking about real decisions, let’s go through the ones the Rams actually have to make this week — because these are much more urgent than any trade.

Decision number one: the kicker. Blake Grupe hurt his knee making a tackle on the kickoff after his 44-yard field goal — his very first kick as a Ram. According to Yahoo Sports, he was on crutches after the game. When McVay was asked for an update, he said: “I don’t have an update on him. I just know he got his knee.” According to The Athletic’s Nate Atkins, the Rams will need to sign a new kicker. That means the Rams will likely have their third kicker in a little more than a week, and they’ll need to get the snap and hold timing right before Sunday’s game in Las Vegas. According to the AP, Grupe was already the Rams’ fifth kicker over their past 57 games. The next one will be number six. Whoever it is, I’d want a veteran who’s made big kicks in real games — not another project. The Rams can’t afford to learn on the job again. And they need a kicker who stays out of the coverage play — Grupe got hurt because the coverage unit let Devin Duvernay break free on the kickoff, forcing the kicker to make the tackle.

And that decision mattered on Sunday more than any receiver. Without a kicker, McVay ran out the clock at the end of the first half. He explained: “We’re playing for a touchdown. We didn’t have a field goal operation.” And then: “If we had a kicker healthy, then that would have changed the approach.” The Rams had to go for two after their first touchdown and failed. They went for it on fourth-and-1 at the Cardinals’ 27 instead of trying a field goal, and were stopped. In a two-point loss, that’s the ballgame.

Decision number two: Davante Adams. The Rams need a diagnosis, and they need it before they can make any decision about adding a receiver. Everything about the Jeudy conversation depends on this.

Decision number three: Myles Garrett. Last Friday, McVay said he’d have an answer on Monday about whether Garrett’s 21-day practice window opens this week. He said Garrett “has a good look in his eye” and is “making good progress.” If that window opens, it’s the best news this team could get.

And decision number four: the identity of this team. After the game, McVay was asked what this team’s identity is right now. He said: “We’re figuring out. I can’t answer that right now.” He also said: “I don’t have those answers right now, otherwise it would have gotten fixed.” And he took the responsibility on himself: “I have to do a better job.”

That’s the real story of this week. Not an owner’s shocking decision. Not a confirmed trade. It’s a coach and a team trying to figure out who they are at 2-4.

Against Arizona, the Rams’ defense allowed zero touchdowns on five red zone trips, according to the Rams’ official website. Stafford threw two touchdown passes and no interceptions. He also led an 86-yard touchdown drive in the final two minutes, and Puka finished with six catches for 80 yards — 59 of them on that final drive.

So this is not a team that’s out of talent. It’s a team that keeps losing close games because of mistakes. Stafford said it plainly: “We are what our record is.” And he added that football “is a game of execution.” The Rams aren’t executing right now. Adding a receiver might help a little. Cleaning up the mistakes would help a lot more. Puka said it after the game, too: “I think there’s just a lack of execution, obviously on our offensive side of the ball.” But he also said: “We’re still a connected group.” That matters. A connected locker room can fix execution problems. A divided one can’t.

Let’s look at the schedule, too, because it shapes everything. Next up is Las Vegas on the road. Then the Chargers at home. Then Washington on the road on November 8th, two days before the trade deadline. Those three games will decide whether the Rams are buyers at the deadline. If they go 3-0, they’re 5-4 and right back in the playoff race, and a move like Jeudy could be the final piece. If they go 1-2 or worse, the front office might be thinking about the future instead of the present.
