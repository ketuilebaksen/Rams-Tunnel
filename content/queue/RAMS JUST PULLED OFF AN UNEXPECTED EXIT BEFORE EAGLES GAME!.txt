So let's start with the exit itself, because the Tanner Ingle story is genuinely surprising when you lay out the timeline. Rewind two weeks. Kam Kinchens goes down with his hamstring, and the Rams elevate Ingle to the 53-man roster — we covered it on this channel at the time, and we said the move told you everything about where the Kinchens situation was heading. Ingle had been grinding between the practice squad and the active roster for FOUR years. A classic journeyman story: undersized, fearless, flying down on kickoffs like his nickname — the Human Missile — demanded. Two weeks ago, he finally got his shot at sticking. And now? Waived. Released into the void days before Philadelphia. That is the NFL at its most brutal: from promotion to unemployment in fourteen days.

So why? Here's where the story connects to everything we've been covering this month — because this isn't random. Think about what McVay has been saying about special teams since the Giants game. After the win, he flagged it: "a lot of areas for improvement," the field-position mistakes, the penalty that tacked fifteen yards onto a return. Then Denver happened — and the special teams problems showed up AGAIN. The re-punt sequence where a penalty for not re-establishing wiped out a good deep kick and bled crucial field position in a four-point loss. Two straight weeks of the head coach publicly calling out the third phase. And in the NFL, when a unit keeps failing, the guys whose entire roster spot depends on that unit are the ones who pay. Ingle was a core special teams player on a special teams group that kept costing this team hidden yards. The Human Missile got caught in the blast radius of his own unit's film. Is it harsh? Absolutely. Is it surprising they moved this fast? A little. But is it consistent with everything McVay told us Monday — "honest assessments," and then "the next thing on my plate will be personnel oriented"? It's exactly that. He told us a personnel move was coming. Forty-eight hours later, here it is.

Now the other side of the shake-up, because for every exit there's an entrance — and this one solves a little mystery from last week's press conference. Remember when McVay casually mentioned "we'll elevate Dean" before the Denver game, and nobody was quite sure of the full story? Meet Dean Connors — and as of Tuesday, he's not an elevation anymore. He's been signed to the 53-man active roster, full time. Here's his arc: impressed everybody in preseason, got respect throughout the building, but couldn't crack a roster that already had Kyren Williams and Blake Corum locked into every carry. So he waited on the practice squad. Then Ronnie Rivers — the third back — landed on injured reserve, Connors got the Week 3 elevation in Denver, and he made his snaps count: a 23-yard kickoff return right out of the gate, plus work on the punt return units. That's the audition that got him the permanent job. And let's be honest about what this is, because McVay was honest about it: Connors is unlikely to see carries or offensive snaps as long as Kyren and Blake are healthy. This is a special teams signing, full stop. The Rams didn't just cut a special teams problem — they signed what they hope is a special teams answer in the same week. One out, one in, same phase of the game. That's not coincidence. That's a message to the entire roster: the third phase is being held to the same standard as the other two.

The Rams aren't rebuilding their special teams. They're patching the two or three specific holes that keep bleeding — coverage discipline and return value. Which is exactly what this week's moves target.

And a word on why Dean Connors specifically matters more than a "third running back" headline suggests: the return game. That 23-yard kickoff return he ripped off in Denver was the kind of field-position win this team has been giving away, not creating. In a league where the kickoff rules have made every return a genuine opportunity, having a decisive, downhill returner who hits the crease without hesitation is a real weapon — and it's been missing. If Connors gives the Rams even five extra yards of average starting position per drive, that's the quiet difference between punting from midfield and kicking field goals — the exact margins that decided Denver. Watch where the Rams start their drives on Sunday. That's Connors' box score.
