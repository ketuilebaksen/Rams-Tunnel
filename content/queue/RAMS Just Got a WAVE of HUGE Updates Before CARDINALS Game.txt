Update number one: the special teams overhaul — and the numbers that show just how bad things have been.

Let’s start with the hard truth, because I’m not going to sugarcoat it. According to NFL.com, the Rams rank dead last in the NFL in special teams DVOA through five games, at minus 15.3 percent. That’s the 11th-worst start through five games since 1977. And at this pace, the unit would finish as the worst special teams in the history of that metric. The Rams have also allowed an explosive kick or punt return in three straight games — including Ray Davis’s 70-yard kickoff return on the opening play against Buffalo.

And it’s not just this season. According to Yahoo Sports, since Greg Zuerlein left after the 2019 season, the Rams have used 11 different kickers in seven seasons. Eleven. Joshua Karty made 10 of 15 field goals last year before the Rams replaced him in Week 9. Harrison Mevis made five of nine this year before the Rams released him. As one analyst put it, both kickers “started hot and finished ice cold.”

So what are the Rams doing about it? More than most fans realize.

Special teams coordinator Bubba Ventrone has a simple philosophy, and he borrowed it from Bill Belichick. He said: “Let’s eliminate the bad football. Let’s stop from losing.” And he added: “We just have to get back to no self-inflicted wounds to a certain degree.”

The Rams have made real changes to back that up. Special teams work has moved to the start of practice, when players are fresh. Offensive and defensive starters are now playing on those units. The Rams signed long snapper Joe Cardona and coverage player Grant Stuard this offseason — both of them played for Ventrone before. Alex Bachman has taken over punt returns, Jordan Whittington is handling kickoffs, and of course, Blake Grupe replaced Mevis this week. And this past week, the Rams also added cornerback Damarion Williams to the practice squad — a player who’s known for his special teams work. That’s not a coincidence. Every move this front office is making right now has the third phase in mind.

And you understand why when you look at how special teams have hurt this team. Last season, Rashid Shaheed returned a punt 58 yards for a touchdown against the Rams in Seattle in a one-point overtime loss. In the NFC Championship Game, a muffed punt by Xavier Smith was one of the biggest swings in a 31-27 loss. The Rams released Smith on October 2nd, and he’s now on the 49ers’ practice squad. According to NFL.com, special teams cost the Rams in nearly all six of their losses last season. That’s the history Ventrone was hired to change.

And this is where the criticism of McVay himself comes in. The Ringer’s Sheil Kapadia pointed out before the Bills game that the Rams’ special teams had ranked 26th or worse in DVOA in four of the last five seasons. His line was that special teams are “Maybe the one thing McVay can’t figure out, and it continues to cost them games.” That’s tough to hear, but the numbers back it up. McVay has built one of the best offenses of his generation and turned around this defense with Garrett and McDuffie. But the third phase has been a weak spot for years. That’s why he said on Friday that he wants Ventrone to know “I’m right there to help.” McVay is putting his own name on this fix.

McVay has been honest about why this has been so hard. He said: “You have a lot of moving parts because when you get injured on defense and offense, it has a trickle-down effect.” Think about it: when starters get hurt, the backups move up — and those backups are usually your special teams players. Then the special teams units have to use players who are even further down the depth chart. And McVay said something encouraging about this week: “I think you lean into the process, and I have seen the intentionality from the group that I’m looking for.”

Now let’s talk about Blake Grupe, because today is his Rams debut.

Here’s what I like about Grupe’s attitude. He’s not pretending this is easy. He said: “It’s all about getting comfortable.” And he added: “There’s a lot of factors in switching cities, switching teams, switching organizations.” He’s also leaned on what happened to him last season. After the Saints released him, the Colts picked him up for the second half of the year, and he didn’t miss a field goal. He said: “Those six weeks in Indy were big for me, big for my career.”

He went 11 for 11 on field goals with the Colts, including four for four from 50-plus. But I want to be honest with you about one number. According to NFL.com, Grupe has been over 90 percent from inside 40 yards in his career — but only about 73 percent from beyond 40. That’s the range where Mevis struggled, too. So if the Rams need a 45-yarder today, that’s a real test. But don’t forget that Grupe has been a reliable starter before. As a rookie with the Saints in 2023, he made 30 of 37 field goals. In 2024, he made 27 of 31. It was only in 2025 that things went wrong in New Orleans, before he turned it around in Indianapolis. The Rams are betting that the real Blake Grupe is the one who made 68 of 79 field goals across those three good stretches — not the one who went 18 of 26.

Here’s the operation he’ll be working with today. Ethan Evans is the holder, and Joe Cardona is the long snapper. According to Yahoo Sports, timing between snapper, holder and kicker usually develops through repetition — and Grupe has had only two days of reps with this group. That’s why McVay said earlier this week that the ideal outcome is for Grupe to kick a lot more extra points than field goals. Score touchdowns, and the new kicker’s debut gets a lot easier.

And let’s not forget the man holding the ball. Ethan Evans is the Rams’ punter and holder, and his punting has come under fire too. According to Ramblin’ Fan, his 47.4-yard gross average ranks 20th in the league, but his 38.2-yard net average ranks 30th. That gap tells you the problem isn’t only his leg — it’s the coverage behind him. And to be fair to Evans, he made one of the most important tackles of the Buffalo game. On the opening kickoff, when Ray Davis broke loose for 70 yards, it was Evans who brought him down and saved a touchdown. Today, his most important job might be the quietest one: a clean hold for a kicker he’s only worked with for two days.

And here’s one more angle on the kicking conversation that a lot of fans have been talking about this week: Sean McVay’s own decision at the end of the Bills game. Some fans believe Mevis shouldn’t take all the blame for the blocked 48-yarder, and that McVay could have been more aggressive to get the offense closer before the kick. And honestly, McVay agreed. Right after the game, he said: “I wish I would have had a better play call and gone for it, got it a lot tighter.” That’s a head coach owning his part of the loss. And today, if the Rams are in that same situation, I’d expect McVay to push for a few more yards before calling on his new kicker.

Update number two: David Quessenberry’s big moment.

With Alaric Jackson suspended for punching Bills linebacker Bradley Chubb after Monday’s game — a suspension that was reduced from two games to one on appeal — David Quessenberry will start at left tackle today. And this one is special.

According to the Rams’ official website, this will be Quessenberry’s first start as a Ram and his first start anywhere since Week 18 of the 2023 season, when he was with the Minnesota Vikings. He’s in his second season with the Rams. Last year, he played in 13 games — all on special teams. And over his career, he’s played in 102 regular-season games with 30 starts for the Titans, Bills and Vikings. In 2021, with Tennessee, he started every single game.

And if you know his story, you know why this matters. Years ago, early in his career, Quessenberry was diagnosed with cancer, fought through treatment, and made it back to the NFL. Every start he makes is a reminder of what he’s overcome. And now, at a moment when the Rams really need him, he’s ready.

Listen to how he described it: “This is kind of why I’m here, for when things like this happen.” He said he’s “played this position my whole life.” And he said something that tells you a lot about his mindset. As a swing tackle, he usually has to prepare for multiple spots. This week, he knew he was starting at left tackle. He said: “I actually enjoy it more.”

His teammates and coaches clearly love him. McVay said: “He has great awareness and he has a lot of wisdom through experience he’s able to share.” He also said: “Guys love him, coaches love him.” And he added that he was “really grateful that he came back this year with us.” Stafford said: “Obviously, a lot of trust in DQ.” And: “He’s proven himself in this league.”

The Rams also got some depth back. Keagen Trost practiced fully on Thursday and Friday after coming off injured reserve with a hamstring injury, and McVay said he could be available today.

And here’s the matchup Quessenberry will be dealing with. Arizona’s veteran edge rusher Josh Sweat had a rest day on Wednesday but practiced fully on Thursday and Friday, so he’ll be ready. Baron Browning forced two fumbles last week against Detroit. The Cardinals will try to test the new left tackle early, especially on third down. That’s why I expect McVay to give Quessenberry help with tight ends and running backs, and to call plenty of quick passes in the first quarter until he settles in.

Update number three: the secondary.

Trent McDuffie is questionable with a groin injury that popped up at the end of Thursday’s practice. McVay said: “Trent felt a little something in his groin so we’re going to be smart with him.” Safety Kam Curl is also questionable with an ankle injury. According to Rams Wire on Yahoo Sports, they’re the only two Rams with game designations today.

McDuffie has been elite this season. According to Yahoo Sports, he’s allowed the lowest passer rating in coverage of any cornerback in the NFL through five weeks: 21.1. If he can’t go, Josh Wallace and Emmanuel Forbes Jr. are the most likely candidates to step up opposite Jaylen Watson. We’ll know for sure when the inactives come out 90 minutes before kickoff.

And there’s plenty of good news on the rest of the report. Davis Allen was cleared to play after being limited Friday with a foot injury. Colby Parkinson, Nate Landman, Omar Speights, Josh Wallace, Alex Bachman and Byron Young all came off the final report without a designation. Tyler Higbee and Byron Young had veteran rest days on Friday — not injuries.

So how will the Rams decide on McDuffie? Here’s what I’d expect. He’ll test the groin in pregame warmups, and the medical staff will watch how he moves when he opens his hips and drives on the ball — the exact movements a cornerback needs on every snap. If there’s any hesitation, I’d expect the Rams to sit him. Remember, this is a team that held Puka out for two weeks with groin soreness earlier this season, and Puka came back and caught 26 passes in his next two games. Patience has paid off for the Rams before. With a 1-4 opponent, a depleted Cardinals receiving corps and the Raiders up next, there’s no reason to gamble with a cornerback the Rams just signed to a four-year, 124 million dollar contract.

And Curl is a different story. McVay described his ankle as soreness from the Bills game, and he said: “We feel good about him.” My read is that Curl plays, McDuffie is a true game-time decision, and the Rams lean toward caution.

Update number four: the Cardinals got hit hard.

Arizona ruled out rookie running back Jeremiyah Love with an ankle injury. Love was the fourth overall pick in this year’s draft, and according to the AP, he leads all rookies with 245 rushing yards and ranks second among rookies with 281 yards from scrimmage. Tyler Allgeier will take over, but as Rams Wire put it, he’s not as dynamic a runner or receiver.

Then there’s Budda Baker. The Cardinals’ veteran safety has a groin injury, missed Friday’s practice and is questionable. According to the AP, Baker is the only Cardinal with more than one sack this season. Linebacker Zaven Collins is questionable with a hip injury. And Arizona has already ruled out safeties Andrew Wingard and Wydett Williams. So the Cardinals could be very thin in the back end against an offense led by Matthew Stafford, Puka Nacua and Davante Adams.

And look at how Arizona’s defense has played even when healthy. According to the AP, the Cardinals have allowed at least 31 points in every game of their four-game losing streak. They rank last in the NFL in scoring defense, allowing 32.4 points per game. Meanwhile, the Rams are averaging 424.4 yards per game on offense. That’s a massive mismatch.

Let me put the season numbers side by side, because they tell the story of this game. According to Fox Sports, the Rams are averaging 307.6 passing yards per game. The Cardinals are averaging just 209.6. The Rams average 116.8 rushing yards per game, compared to 91.6 for Arizona — and that’s with Love, who won’t play today. The Rams score 23.8 points per game and allow 24.0. The Cardinals score 21.6 and allow 32.4. Stafford leads the NFL with 1,572 passing yards. Brissett has 1,110. On paper, this is the kind of game where the Rams’ offense should be able to name its score.

And think about how the Rams’ passing game matches up with Arizona’s banged-up secondary. Puka Nacua just caught 17 passes against Buffalo, the most ever in a Monday Night Football game. Davante Adams caught two touchdowns. Kyren Williams has caught at least six passes in three straight games. And Stafford just played his first interception-free game of the season. If Budda Baker can’t play — or even if he plays at less than 100 percent — the Cardinals will be trying to cover all of those weapons with backup safeties. That’s a nightmare for any defensive coordinator, and it’s why the betting total has climbed into the low 50s.

And Arizona’s best remaining weapon is tight end Trey McBride, who has 42 catches this season. With Marvin Harrison Jr. traded to Houston and Love out, McBride is going to see a ton of targets. Quentin Lake, who’s been one of the best defensive backs in football at breaking up passes, will likely see a lot of him. If the Rams take McBride away, Brissett is going to have to beat them with receivers who haven’t been reliable all season.

And don’t forget Aaron Donald. He has 16 career sacks against the Cardinals — his most against any opponent. Arizona’s offensive line has already given up 12 sacks this season, and it’s playing without left tackle Paris Johnson Jr., who’s on injured reserve. Donald nearly had a sack against Buffalo that was wiped out by a penalty. Today could be the day he gets his first official sack since coming out of retirement.

Update number five: the LaFleur reunion.

This is the first time Sean McVay will face Mike LaFleur since LaFleur left to become the Cardinals’ head coach. LaFleur spent the last three seasons as McVay’s offensive coordinator, including Stafford’s MVP season last year.

And both coaches kept it light this week. LaFleur said: “As much as I love Sean, I haven’t really thought of him too much.” And then he added with a smile: “I’m sure he’s kind of feeling the same way about me right now.” McVay said: “I love Mike, and it’s about the Rams versus the Cardinals.” And he warned against overthinking LaFleur’s inside knowledge, saying you want to be careful “to chase a ghost because it is still a player’s game.”

Stafford gave maybe the most honest answer of all. He admitted the familiarity is real: “Obviously, with only one year removed, there’s things you sit there and you could stay up all night” thinking about. But he also said: “We still got to go out there and execute it.”

And LaFleur is just the latest branch of one of the most impressive coaching trees in football. His older brother, Matt LaFleur, was McVay’s first offensive coordinator with the Rams before becoming the Packers’ head coach. Zac Taylor, Kevin O’Connell and Liam Coen all worked on McVay’s offensive staff before getting head coaching jobs of their own. And former Rams defensive coordinators Brandon Staley and Raheem Morris also became head coaches. Now Mike LaFleur joins that list — and today, he gets his first shot at the man who helped him get there. And LaFleur’s own path shows how deep that tree runs. Before he joined the Rams, he was the New York Jets’ offensive coordinator in 2021 and 2022, and before that he spent years working under Kyle Shanahan in Cleveland, Atlanta and San Francisco. The Cardinals gave him a five-year contract to rebuild the franchise.

Update number six: history is firmly on the Rams’ side.

According to the AP, the Rams lead the all-time series 55-41-2. They’ve won five straight home games against Arizona, and they’ve won nine of 10 meetings at the Coliseum or SoFi Stadium since McVay became head coach. The last time these teams met, the Rams won 37-20 on January 4th in Inglewood. And the Cardinals have lost eight straight games against NFC West opponents, dating back to 2024.

There’s one more interesting stat about Arizona’s quarterback, Jacoby Brissett. According to the AP, he’s thrown a touchdown pass in 17 straight starts — the second-longest active streak in the NFL, behind only Jared Goff’s 26. But Arizona is just 2-15 in those starts. Brissett is going to move the ball at times. The question is whether he can keep up.

And there’s one more update that isn’t about today’s game, but it’s coming soon: Myles Garrett. McVay said on Friday that he’ll talk to the medical staff and have an answer on Monday about whether Garrett’s 21-day practice window opens next week. He said Garrett has “a good look in his eye” and is “making good progress.” He also joked that he sounds like “Woody from Toy Story” because he keeps repeating the same message — return to performance, not just return to play. And he made it clear: “I’d love to have him back out there, but not at the expense of rushing him back.” So after today’s game, Monday becomes the next big date on the calendar.

But here’s a warning sign for the Rams, too. According to the AP, the Rams have allowed 30 or more points in seven of their last 14 games, and they’re 2-5 in those games. The defense gave up 37 to Buffalo on Monday. Without Love, Arizona’s offense is much less dangerous — but the Rams’ defense still has to prove it can close games.

So what do the Rams need to do today? Here are my four keys.

Key number one: start fast on offense. The Cardinals’ defense has given up 31 or more points in four straight games. If the Rams score touchdowns on their first two drives, this game could be over early.

Key number two: protect Stafford on the left side. Quessenberry is a pro, but Arizona will test him. Expect quick throws, chips from the tight ends, and plenty of check-downs to Kyren Williams early.

Key number three: a clean special teams game. No explosive returns allowed. No blocked kicks. No penalties on returns. If Ventrone’s unit just plays “no self-inflicted wounds” football, that’s a win.

And key number four: finish. The Rams have lost two one-score games already. When they get the lead, they need to put Arizona away — not let a 1-4 team hang around into the fourth quarter.

Now let me talk about the stakes, because they’re bigger than a lot of people realize. The 49ers are 5-0 and have already beaten every other team in the NFC West. The Seahawks, the defending Super Bowl champions, are 3-2. The Rams are 2-3 and 0-1 in the division after losing to San Francisco in Melbourne. A win today gets them to 3-3, with a winnable stretch coming up: at Las Vegas, the Chargers at SoFi, and then at Washington. A loss today, at home, to a 1-4 team missing its best young player? That would put the Rams at 2-4 and raise real questions about whether this team can catch up in the division at all.

So yes, the Rams are heavy favorites. But this is exactly the kind of game that good teams win easily and struggling teams make difficult. Today, we find out which one the 2026 Rams are. And whatever happens, I’ll be right here after the final whistle with the full breakdown.
