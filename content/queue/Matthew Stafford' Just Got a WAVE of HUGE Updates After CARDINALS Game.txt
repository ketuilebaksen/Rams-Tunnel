Part one: the context before the first throw

Before we get to the plays, let’s set the scene, because the broadcast crew made a great point about it.

They said this was the most injuries McVay has dealt with in his tenure with the Rams. And this is McVay’s tenth season as head coach. So in ten years, he’s never had a stretch like this.

Think about who was out. Puka Nacua, on IR. Davante Adams, on IR. Terrance Ferguson, on IR. Steve Avila, out. And during the game, Coleman Shelton and Dylan McMahon both got hurt on the offensive line.

And the game didn’t start well. Arizona led 6-3. Stafford threw an interception. For a while, it looked like the Rams might have a hard time scoring at all.

Then the Rams scored 31 straight points. And Stafford was at the center of it.

The slow start, and why it didn’t matter

Let’s be honest about the beginning of this game, because the highlights skip it.

The Rams offense was stuck early. Arizona kicked two 49-yard field goals from Chad Ryland. The Rams only had a short Brandon McManus field goal. And Stafford threw an interception to Cody Simon.

For a lot of quarterbacks, that’s when things go wrong. You start forcing throws. You try to make up for the mistake with one big play. And it gets worse.

Stafford didn’t do that. He stayed patient. He kept taking what the defense gave him. And when the opportunities came, he was ready.

That’s the difference between a young quarterback and a veteran. On the other side of the field, rookie Carson Beck was making his first NFL start, and he finished with 108 passing yards and two interceptions. Stafford, in his 18th NFL season, shook off a bad start and threw three touchdowns.

Part two: the new look, 20 personnel

The broadcast crew noticed something right away. The Rams were using a formation they don’t normally use.

The call was: “Back to 20 personnel. Two backs, three wide receivers. No tight ends on the field.” And they said: “It’s new to Matt Stafford, but they are doing it this week.”

So what is 20 personnel? It means two running backs and zero tight ends. For the Rams, that meant Kyren Williams and Blake Corum on the field at the same time, with three receivers.

On one of the first plays in this package, Stafford hit Kyren with a quick pass on first down.

And later, the crew called it again: “Two backs again, Williams and Corum.”

Why does this matter? McVay explained it after the game. He said it was “a way to be able to get two of our best players on the field at the same time.” He also said the tight ends were “still so banged up,” so the two-back look helped give them a rest. And he wanted “something different that we hadn’t put on tape yet.”

Stafford said after the game that players were “doing things that maybe they don’t have a ton of reps at or really accustomed to in the two-back stuff.” And he said it was great to see everybody “buying into that and making it come to life.”

So this wasn’t just a random wrinkle. It was a whole new package, built for this game, and Stafford ran it like he’d been doing it for years.

Part three: fourth-and-6, and the throw that changed the game

This was the play that woke up the offense.

The Rams were facing fourth-and-6. McVay decided to go for it. And the broadcast crew said McVay was “trying to jump-start it.”

Stafford dropped back, looked deep down the field, and found Tutu Atwell. The call: “He’s got a man. It’s a first down. What a throw by Stafford, as Atwell picks up the first. Gain of 31.”

Thirty-one yards on fourth-and-6. That’s not a safe throw. That’s a quarterback trusting his receiver to win down the field on the biggest play of the drive.

And the broadcast crew explained why Atwell was the guy. They said he has “the most experience” with Stafford among the healthy receivers. When Puka and Adams are out, that history matters. Stafford knows exactly where Atwell is going to be.

That’s the kind of play that changes the mood of a game. Before it, the offense was stuck. After it, the Rams started rolling.

Part four: CJ Daniels’ first NFL catch

Then came a moment I loved.

Out of the two-back look again, Stafford looked for an open man and found him. The call: “That is C.J. Daniels with his first NFL catch.”

And here’s a detail from the broadcast that tells you how far Daniels has come. The crew said he was inactive for the first six games of the season. He wasn’t even dressing for games. And now he’s catching passes from Matthew Stafford in a division game.

The broadcast crew also said something important: “Production will come from the scheme if he keeps trusting the process.”

That fits perfectly with what McVay said about Daniels on Friday. McVay praised his “maturity” and said he “plays big at the catch point.” He also said Daniels has been learning from Robert Woods, being “in Robert Woods’ back pocket.”

So the young receiver who couldn’t get on the field for six weeks is now part of the plan. That’s a big moment for him.

Part five: the Higbee touchdown drive

Now let’s go through the drive that gave the Rams their first lead.

It was a long one. The broadcast said Higbee’s touchdown came on the tenth play of the drive. Let’s go through the key plays.

Higbee catches one. Stafford threw to Tyler Higbee for a completion early in the drive.

Corum on third-and-7. This was a smart play. Stafford pump-faked, came down underneath, and hit Blake Corum, who went for a first down. The pump fake made the defenders freeze, and Corum had room to work.

Xavier Smith over the middle. Stafford found Smith over the middle, and he almost broke it for a big gain. The broadcast said Kalen King tripped him up just in time.

Higbee again. On second-and-10, Stafford went back to Higbee, who held on through contact.

Mumpfield for 27. Then Konata Mumpfield caught a pass along the sideline and spun forward to the 20. A gain of 27 yards.

And then, in the red zone, the question was: who does Stafford trust most when the field gets small?

The answer: Tyler Higbee. “Stafford to the end zone. Touchdown, Tyler Higbee! His second touchdown of the season.”

The broadcast crew pointed out something smart. On an earlier play, Stafford had missed Daniels. But in a similar situation, he came back to Higbee. And one analyst said: “He might be old and banged up,” but he’s still the guy Stafford trusts in the red zone.

That’s veteran chemistry. Higbee has been catching passes from Stafford since 2021. When it’s tight in the end zone, you go to the guy you know.

Rams 10, Cardinals 6.

Konata Mumpfield’s big day

Let’s give Konata Mumpfield his own moment, because he had a quietly great game.

He caught that 27-yard pass along the sideline on the Higbee drive, spinning forward to the 20. And later in the game, he had a 44-yard catch. Two big plays down the field.

Those are the kinds of plays Puka usually makes. A young receiver, getting open against one-on-one coverage, and giving Stafford a target he can trust.

McVay mentioned him after the game too, saying Mumpfield and Tutu Atwell were part of “a lot of guys” who “ended up making big timely plays for us.”

If Mumpfield keeps making plays like this, defenses won’t be able to focus only on the run game and the tight ends. That opens everything up.

Part six: the two-minute magic

Here’s a stat from the broadcast that explains a lot about this Rams team.

The crew said the Rams were fourth in the NFL in points scored in the final two minutes of each half.

Think about that. The end of the first half and the end of the second half are where games are often won or lost. And the Rams have been one of the best teams in football at scoring in those moments.

And against Arizona, it showed up again. The Higbee touchdown came with about a minute left in the first half. Then, after Kamren Kinchens’ interception, the Rams scored again before halftime.

Stafford talked about this after the game. He said the Rams “finished the half really strong, offense and defense, and then came out in the second half and put another touchdown on the board.” And he said, “We’ve done that at a pretty consistent clip, and it’s really helped us.”

That’s what a veteran quarterback gives you. In the two-minute drill, Stafford doesn’t panic. He knows exactly what to do.

Part seven: the Xavier Smith screen

Now the play everyone’s talking about.

Right after the Kinchens interception, the Rams had the ball with time running out in the half. Stafford threw over the middle on a wide receiver screen to Xavier Smith. The call: “Touchdown! Xavier Smith. Nobody tackled him. And Smith has his first NFL touchdown.”

The broadcast crew loved it: “Xavier Smith, what a way to make a name for himself. Great play call.”

And here’s the backstory. McVay said after the game that the screen wasn’t his idea. He gave the credit to assistant coach Nate Scheelhaase: “That was his play call suggestion.” McVay said Smith “did a great job breaking the initial tackle.”

So it was a great idea from an assistant, a quick, accurate throw from Stafford, and a great run from a player who was on the 49ers practice squad just a couple of weeks earlier.

Rams 17, Cardinals 6, at halftime. And Arizona never recovered.

The defense handed Stafford short fields

I have to give the defense credit here, because they made Stafford’s job a lot easier.

The biggest play was Kamren Kinchens’ interception. He picked off Beck and returned it 33 yards. Four plays later, Stafford hit Xavier Smith for the touchdown. That’s a short field, and Stafford took advantage of it.

Then Wesley Bailey added the first interception of his career. Shaun Dolac forced a fumble with a “peanut punch” that set up a field goal. And the Rams had five sacks, including the first of the season for Aaron Donald and Tyler Davis.

In total, Arizona only managed 123 yards of offense.

Here’s why that matters for Stafford. When the defense keeps getting the ball back, the offense doesn’t have to be perfect. It gets more chances. And when it gets the ball near midfield or closer, it’s a lot easier to score.

The Rams offense and defense fed off each other in this game. That’s how you go from 6-3 to 34-6.

Part eight: the deep shot and the penalty

The broadcast also showed a play that didn’t end in a catch but still helped a lot.

Stafford threw deep, and a receiver got wide open and turned upfield before being caught from behind by Denzel Burke. Then there was a penalty call too.

The analyst explained what happened. He said the receiver “shakes Denzel Burke,” and Burke “has been too grabby.” So the receiver drew a penalty.

And then the analyst said something I thought was really interesting: “This is what happens in the NFL. You don’t know who you are until you’re playing behind Davante Adams. Now you get an opportunity with one-on-one coverage.”

That’s the hidden story of this game. Young receivers who usually play behind Puka and Adams are now getting one-on-one matchups. And they’re winning some of them.

The broadcast also called it “a big play” for Stafford. And the analyst said that receiver “can get open in a phone booth,” which is a compliment for a receiver who can get separation in a tiny space.

Part nine: the Colby Parkinson grab

Then came one of the best catches of the day.

The call: “He froze on the run, and it is caught! What a catch by Parkinson, and they’re going to say he’s down at the 1.”

So Stafford used play-action. The defense “froze on the run,” expecting a handoff. And Stafford found Colby Parkinson for a big gain down to the 1-yard line.

The broadcast called it “what a grab by the seventh-year tight end.”

This is exactly what McVay was talking about. He said the tight ends were “still so banged up,” but they all played and made plays. And this catch set up a touchdown.

And it shows why the run game matters so much. When Corum and Kyren are running well, linebackers have to step up to stop the run. That’s when play-action works. That’s when a tight end like Parkinson gets open.

The broadcast said it perfectly: “They’re one after another.” The Rams were making play after play.

Part ten: the Davis Allen touchdown

And then Stafford found another wide-open man.

The call: “Throws, and he’s got a wide-open man. Davis Allen is in for the touchdown! Third touchdown of the year for Davis Allen from 19 yards away, and the Rams are pouring it on in the desert.”

Third touchdown of the season for Davis Allen. That’s a big number for a tight end who isn’t always the first option.

Remember, Allen played through a back injury against Washington. McVay called him “tough as hell.” And now he’s scoring touchdowns.

And the broadcast analyst summed it up: “This is when it gets ugly for a defense. They test your eye discipline.”

What does “eye discipline” mean? It means defenders have to keep their eyes on their own job. If a linebacker looks at the quarterback or the running back instead of the man he’s covering, he gets beat. And the Rams’ play-action and two-back looks were making Arizona’s defenders look in the wrong place all day.

That’s McVay’s offense at its best. And Stafford executed it perfectly.

The backs as receivers

One more thing the broadcast showed: how much Stafford used his running backs in the passing game.

He hit Kyren Williams on a quick first-down pass out of the two-back set. He hit Blake Corum on that pump-fake on third-and-7. Corum finished with three catches for 19 yards, on top of his 100 rushing yards.

Stafford said after the game: “Both our backs played great. We leaned on them heavily, both in the run and the pass game.”

This is important, because it’s how you replace Puka without a receiver who plays like Puka. One of the ESPN analysts said the Rams use Puka so much on short throws that “it’s almost like he’s a running back.” So without him, McVay just used actual running backs in some of those same spots.

Short throws to Kyren and Corum keep the offense on schedule. They keep Stafford from getting hit. And they make the defense worry about one more thing.

Part eleven: the numbers behind it all

Let’s put it all together.

Stafford finished 18 of 26 for 249 yards, three touchdowns, and one interception.

He completed passes to nine different players. Let’s name them: Tutu Atwell, Konata Mumpfield, CJ Daniels, Xavier Smith, Tyler Higbee, Colby Parkinson, Davis Allen, Blake Corum, and Kyren Williams.

Three touchdown passes, to three different players: Higbee, Smith, and Allen.

And the Rams scored 31 unanswered points after falling behind 6-3.

Here’s what impresses me most. None of those nine pass catchers is Puka or Adams. Stafford didn’t have his two best weapons, and he still spread the ball around and made it work.

After the game, Stafford said: “Just trying to spread the ball around. Obviously wasn’t perfect, but proud of those guys. Each guy kind of got their opportunity and made the most of it, which I was happy for.”

And he gave credit to McVay: “Sean obviously did an unbelievable job of figuring out how to attack this defense, which is really multiple and difficult to kind of solve.”

Part twelve: what this tells us about Stafford

Let me step back and talk about Stafford himself.

He’s 38 years old. He’s in his sixth season with the Rams. He’s already won a Super Bowl. And in this game, he did something that maybe only a veteran like him can do.

He trusted young players. CJ Daniels was inactive for six games. Xavier Smith was on a practice squad. And Stafford threw to both of them in big moments.

He trusted his veterans. When it got tight in the red zone, he went to Higbee. On fourth-and-6, he went to Atwell, the receiver he has the most history with.

He ran a brand-new package. The broadcast said 20 personnel was “new to Matt Stafford.” And he ran it like he’d been doing it for years.

He stayed calm after a bad start. He threw an interception early. The offense was stuck. And instead of forcing things, he stayed patient until the offense found its rhythm.

McVay said before the game that Stafford’s “steadiness is the key thing.” He said Stafford keeps “everybody nice and calm” in the huddle and that “there’s just a confidence that he breathes.” This game was proof.

Part thirteen: the offensive line made it possible

I have to mention the offensive line, because none of these throws happen without protection.

The Rams lost Coleman Shelton to a hamstring injury and Dylan McMahon to a knee injury. Beaux Limmer slid from guard to center. Bill Murray came in at guard. McVay said, “We needed all eight of our offensive linemen.”

Stafford said after the game: “We’re down to the third center. Guards are moving and shaking. It was a lot of moving parts there for a couple drives.” And he said he was “really proud of those guys,” because they could “not get a lot of reps in practice and go in there and execute in the game.”

So when you watch Stafford’s highlights from this game, remember the guys in front of him. A patched-together line gave him enough time to hit Parkinson down to the 1 and find Allen wide open in the end zone.

The big question now is how serious the Shelton and McMahon injuries are. We don’t have official timelines yet. That’s the thing I’ll be watching most closely during the bye.

Part fourteen: the real “update” on Stafford

So what’s the real “update” on Matthew Stafford after this game?

Here it is. He’s healthy. He’s playing at a high level. And he’s proving he can win without his two best receivers.

That matters a lot for what comes next. The Rams play the Packers at home on the Wednesday before Thanksgiving. Puka will still be out. Davante Adams’ realistic timeline, according to McVay, is “after Green Bay.” So Stafford will probably have the same group of receivers against the Packers.

And after this game, I feel a lot better about that.

If Stafford can spread the ball to nine guys, run new packages, and lean on his tight ends and backs, the Rams have a real chance against anybody. Then, when Adams comes back, and eventually Puka, this offense could be scary.

This is not a report, it’s my opinion: this was one of Stafford’s most impressive games of the season. Not because of the stats, but because of who he did it with.

What the Packers will see on tape

Here’s something to think about going into the Packers game.

McVay said the two-back look was “something different that we hadn’t put on tape yet.” Well, now it’s on tape. The Packers’ coaches will study every snap of it this week.

So what happens next? My guess is McVay won’t just run the same plays again. He’ll use the two-back look as a starting point and build new plays off it. Maybe the backs line up the same way, but the plays are different. That’s how McVay usually works. He shows you something, and then he uses it to set up something else.

McVay said the bye is time to “creatively come up with new ideas based on who’s available and who’s not.” So expect some new wrinkles.

And remember, Stafford knows Green Bay well. He played them twice a year for 12 years when he was in Detroit. He’s seen just about everything the Packers can do.

This is not a report, it’s my prediction: the Rams will use the two-back look again against Green Bay, but with at least one new play we haven’t seen yet.
