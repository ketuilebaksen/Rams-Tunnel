Let’s start with the man under center, because there’s been a lot of noise about Matthew Stafford over the last month.

Here’s where things stood after four games. Stafford had thrown at least one interception in every game. He had six interceptions in total. National panels called it “yolo ball.” And there were people — not many, but some — who started asking whether the Rams should think about the future. Stafford is 38 years old. He’s in his 18th NFL season. And the Rams had just spent a first-round pick on a quarterback. So you can see how some people connected the dots.

But then came Monday night. Forty completions on 54 attempts. 383 yards. Three touchdowns. Zero interceptions. His first interception-free game of the season — against a Buffalo defense led by Greg Rousseau, one of the best pass rushers in the league. That’s now four straight games with 300-plus passing yards. And according to Fox Sports, Stafford leads the entire NFL with 1,572 passing yards through five games.

So let me say this as clearly as I can there is no quarterback controversy in Los Angeles. The reigning MVP is playing at a high level, and on Monday night he looked like the guy who won that award last season.

And remember how carefully the Rams have handled him. In training camp, Stafford missed the first day while the team managed his workload. That’s been the approach all along protect the 38-year-old in practice so he’s at his best on Sundays. During the week before the Bills game, when the criticism was at its loudest, Stafford made it clear he wasn’t going to change who he is. He said “crawling in a hole” was “not an option,” and that he trusts his preparation and lives with the results. Then he went out and played his cleanest game of the year. That’s not a quarterback on the decline. That’s a veteran who knows exactly how to handle pressure.

Now, here’s the first decision I want to talk about. After everything — the interceptions, the 2-3 start, the criticism — McVay didn’t overhaul anything. He didn’t take the ball out of Stafford’s hands. He didn’t tell him to play it safe. And Stafford didn’t change his approach either. Listen to what he said this week about the Cardinals game “Just go play the game. Trust the guys to go play the game.”

And when he was asked about why the offense was better in the second half against Buffalo, Stafford didn’t point to a new scheme or a new plan. He said “We just executed a little bit better in the second half.” And then “It just boils down to execution.”

That’s the mentality of an MVP quarterback who knows what he’s doing. He even pointed to his own mistake from Monday — an early third-down throw to Puka Nacua that he believes he could have completed, which might have kept the opening drive alive and turned it into a touchdown. He’s not hiding from his errors. He’s owning them and moving on.

And his teammates see it. Davante Adams — a player who’s caught passes from Aaron Rodgers and Derek Carr — called Stafford “the coolest and most collected guy” he’s been around in late-game situations. Think about that. Two weeks in a row, the Rams needed a touchdown drive in the final two minutes. In Philadelphia, Stafford led the Rams down the field for the game-winner with 110 left. Against Buffalo, he took them 62 yards to tie the game with five seconds left. When the pressure is highest, Stafford gets calmer.

So decision number one is simple McVay is riding with his quarterback, his system and his approach. And McVay’s message to the whole team this week fits that perfectly. He said “There are no moral victories.” And about the blocked kick that ended Monday’s game, he said “We missed the chance to win it on that play, but that’s not why we didn’t win it.” In other words, McVay isn’t blaming one moment, and he isn’t panicking. He’s asking everyone — including his quarterback — to execute better in the moments that decide close games. And after Monday night, why would he change anything

Now let’s get to decision number two — and this is the one that I think a lot of Rams fans haven’t fully noticed.

Ty Simpson has not played a single snap this season. He’s been inactive for every game the opener against San Francisco in Melbourne, the Giants, Denver, Philadelphia and Buffalo. Each time, he’s been designated as the emergency third quarterback. And it’s very likely to be the same story on Sunday — though, to be clear, the Rams won’t officially announce their inactives until 90 minutes before kickoff.

So who is Ty Simpson, and why did the Rams draft him with the 13th overall pick

Simpson played his college football at Alabama, and his path there was anything but easy. He redshirted in 2022 and spent years waiting behind Bryce Young and Jalen Milroe. He backed up Milroe for six games in 2023 and six more in 2024. Most quarterbacks with that kind of talent would have transferred. Simpson stayed. And in 2025, he finally got his chance. He started all 15 games, was a team captain, completed 305 of 473 passes for 3,567 yards, with 28 touchdowns and only five interceptions. He was named second-team All-SEC and was a finalist for the Manning Award, which goes to the nation’s top quarterback.

One season as a starter. That’s it. And the Rams still took him in the first round. He signed a four-year rookie contract worth about 25.4 million dollars.

So here’s the key question. If the Rams drafted Simpson that high, why isn’t he the backup

The answer goes back to training camp. McVay made it clear from the beginning that Simpson would have to earn it. Right after the draft, he said “We’ll see, he’s going to compete with Stetson.” And for most of the summer, that competition was real. In the final preseason game against the Chargers, Simpson completed 18 of 23 passes for 119 yards and a touchdown, with no interceptions, in a 20-18 Rams win. That’s a solid performance for a rookie.

But in late August, McVay told reporters the Rams would carry three quarterbacks on the initial 53-man roster. He said “We feel fortunate that you got two guys that we believe in, feel really good about.” And he made one more important promise “One of those guys will be the backup in Australia.”

That guy turned out to be Stetson Bennett. And Bennett has held the job ever since.

Now, let’s talk about Bennett for a second, because he’s got a story of his own. He won back-to-back national championships at Georgia. The Rams drafted him in the fourth round in 2023. He’s now in his fourth NFL season. And here’s the surprising part he still hasn’t taken a single regular-season snap. Every bit of his game action has come in the preseason. But he knows McVay’s system. He’s been in that quarterback room for years, learning from Stafford. And when you’re picking a backup quarterback for a contender, experience in the system matters more than draft position. And remember, Bennett has been an underdog his entire career. He started at Georgia as a walk-on and ended up winning two national titles as the starter. Being overlooked is nothing new for him. The fact that he held off a top-15 pick for the backup job tells you how much the coaching staff trusts his command of this offense — and how seriously McVay meant it when he said the job would be earned, not handed out.

Here’s the bottom line on the quarterback room right now, based on the Rams’ depth chart. Stafford is the starter. Bennett is QB2. Simpson is QB3. And Matthew Caldwell, the fourth quarterback, is on injured reserve.

So why does this matter for Sunday Because of how the emergency third quarterback rule works. As long as Simpson is designated as the emergency QB3, he can only enter the game if both Stafford and Bennett are out because of injury or disqualification. So the Rams keep a third quarterback available without using one of their game-day active roster spots. That’s a roster decision as much as a quarterback decision. It lets McVay dress an extra player at another position — and with injuries at tight end and linebacker this week, every active spot matters.

And there’s another reason the Rams aren’t rushing Simpson. According to Rams On SI, the team would consider it a win if Simpson never had to start a game during his rookie contract. Think about what that means. The Rams drafted Simpson as the long-term plan — not as a short-term fix. They want him to learn from Stafford for as long as possible. Simpson himself said it when he was drafted. He said he was looking forward to learning from McVay and Stafford, calling Stafford one of the greatest ever.

And if you think about it, this approach has worked before around the league. When the Green Bay Packers drafted Jordan Love in the first round in 2020, they still had Aaron Rodgers, who went on to win back-to-back MVP awards the next two seasons. Love sat for three seasons, learned, and then took over in 2023. That’s the kind of patience the Rams appear to be showing with Simpson. And McVay has already lived through both sides of quarterback team-building in Los Angeles — drafting Jared Goff first overall in 2016, then trading for Stafford in 2021 and winning a Super Bowl in his first season. He knows how valuable the right quarterback is, and he knows you don’t rush the next one.

That’s the plan. Not a benching. Not a controversy. A future quarterback quietly learning behind a future Hall of Famer. And think about the urgency on the other side of that plan. Stafford is 38. The Super Bowl will be played at SoFi Stadium this February. The Rams went all-in this offseason with Myles Garrett and Trent McDuffie. Every decision this front office makes in 2026 is about maximizing the window with Stafford — and keeping Simpson on the sideline, learning, is part of that same plan.

Now, could anything change that plan this season Only one thing an injury to Stafford. And that’s why the Bennett-versus-Simpson question matters more than people think. If Stafford were to miss time, McVay would have to decide between the four-year veteran who knows the system and the first-round rookie with the higher ceiling. Right now, the depth chart says Bennett. But if the Rams ever had to play without Stafford for more than a game or two, I’d expect that conversation to get very loud, very fast.

So should Simpson be the backup right now Let me give you both sides. The case for Simpson he’s the long-term investment, he showed real poise in that preseason finale, and live game reps — even a few garbage-time snaps — would speed up his development. The case for Bennett he’s been in McVay’s system for four seasons, he knows the protections and checks, and if Stafford got hurt in the middle of a tight game, the Rams would want the quarterback who can operate the offense without a learning curve. For a team with Super Bowl expectations, I understand why McVay is choosing Bennett for now. But if Simpson keeps progressing in practice, I wouldn’t be surprised to see that order flip later in the season.

That’s why it’s so important that the Rams protect Stafford. And that brings us to the other big change this week.

Left tackle Alaric Jackson won’t play on Sunday. After his postgame altercation with Bills linebacker Bradley Chubb, he was suspended two games — and on Wednesday, according to the Rams’ official website, that suspension was reduced to one game on appeal. So David Quessenberry will start at left tackle against the Cardinals.

That’s a big deal for Stafford, because the left tackle protects his blind side. And here’s the context Quessenberry hasn’t started a game since 2023. McVay called him “a consummate pro.” And Stafford said “I have a lot of trust in DQ.”

There’s also some help on the way along the line. Keagen Trost, who’s been on injured reserve since the preseason with a hamstring injury, started his return-to-practice window and practiced fully on Thursday. McVay said he could be available on Sunday. Justin Dedich is progressing in his rehab, but his practice window hasn’t opened yet.

So watch Stafford’s protection on Sunday. If Arizona gets pressure off the left side early, McVay might adjust with quicker throws, more help from the tight ends and more screens to Kyren Williams. And remember, Stafford is smart enough to adjust on his own. He’s been doing this for 18 years.

And there’s another factor to watch on Sunday Stafford’s supporting cast isn’t fully healthy. On Thursday, linebacker Nate Landman, tight end Colby Parkinson and defensive back Josh Wallace were all limited in practice. Tight end Davis Allen didn’t practice because of a foot issue, which McVay described as a precaution. Davis Allen made one of the biggest catches of the night in overtime against Buffalo, and Parkinson’s blocking matters in protection. If either tight end is limited on Sunday, Stafford may have fewer options in the middle of the field — and the running backs become even more important as safety valves.

Now let’s talk about the most interesting storyline of the week the Cardinals’ head coach knows Stafford’s offense from the inside.

Mike LaFleur spent the last three seasons as the Rams’ offensive coordinator. He was in every quarterback meeting. He knows Stafford’s checks, his preferences, his tendencies, the language of this offense. And in February, he became Arizona’s head coach on a five-year contract. So on Sunday, he gets to face the quarterback he helped coach.

So what did the Rams say about that this week McVay said they won’t “chase a ghost because it still is a player’s game,” and that decisions are made “in real time.” He also said “I love Mike and it’s about the Rams versus the Cardinals.” And he made it clear there’s no bad blood. “Mike and I talk a lot,” he said. And he added “There’s a lot of good tape. He’s done a great job there, and we miss him a lot here.”

Stafford had a similar message. He said that familiarity with the Rams’ system “only goes so far.” And then “We still have to go out there and execute it.” He also gave credit to McVay for building such a strong coaching tree “Sean has done a heck of a job of curating coaching candidates around here for a long time.”

Here’s my take. LaFleur’s knowledge is real. He knows what the Rams like to do on third down, in the red zone and in two-minute drills. But knowing what’s coming and stopping it are two different things — especially when you’re facing Puka Nacua, Davante Adams and Kyren Williams with a defense that’s been struggling. And Arizona’s pass defense ranks dead last in the NFL in dropback EPA, according to WalterFootball’s matchup hub. Knowledge can only help so much when your secondary has been the worst in the league.

And Davante Adams is already focused on the one Cardinal who can change the game safety Budda Baker. Davante said “We obviously know what he does and respect the way that he plays and plays fast, but you gotta react.” Baker had 11 tackles and a sack against Detroit last week. He’s the player Stafford will have to know where he is on every snap.

And here’s one more layer to the LaFleur storyline. He wasn’t just the offensive coordinator during some average seasons. He was in that role last year, when Stafford won the MVP award. He saw Stafford’s best season up close, every single week. So he knows exactly what Stafford looks like when he’s in rhythm — and he’ll know what he wants to take away. But that cuts both ways. Stafford spent three years in meetings with LaFleur, too. He knows how LaFleur thinks about attacking a defense, and that can help him predict what LaFleur’s staff will try to do against him.

Let’s also talk about the quarterback on the other sideline, because Arizona went through a quarterback change of its own this year. Kyler Murray is gone. Jacoby Brissett is the starter. And last week against Detroit, Brissett completed 29 of 47 passes for 292 yards, three touchdowns and one interception in a 45-21 loss. He’s a veteran who won’t panic, but this Cardinals offense also just traded away Marvin Harrison Jr. to Houston. That’s one fewer weapon for Brissett — and one more reason the Rams’ defense should be able to get after him.

And that brings me to Aaron Donald, because the Rams’ pass rush could decide this game. Donald has 16 career sacks against the Cardinals — the most he’s ever had against any opponent. He returned from back soreness against Buffalo and nearly had his first sack since coming out of retirement before a penalty wiped it out. He said this week “It just felt good just watching the film, looking more like myself.” If Donald gets to Brissett early, this game could get out of hand quickly.

And history is on the Rams’ side. The last two times these teams met, the Rams won 45-17 at SoFi in December of last season and 37-20 in Week 18. Arizona is 1-4 now, and the Rams are 12.5-point favorites, according to FanDuel’s line as reported by Sports Illustrated.

Now let me tell you what I want to see from Stafford on Sunday.

Number one another interception-free game. One clean game is a step. Two in a row is a trend. If Stafford protects the football again, the talk about “yolo ball” ends for good.

Number two a fast start. Stafford said himself that the offense executed better in the second half against Buffalo. Against a 1-4 team, the Rams need to set the tone in the first quarter. A touchdown on the opening drive would send a message — especially after the opening drive against Buffalo ended with a missed field goal.

Number three efficiency with the new left tackle. Quick throws, smart protections and no unnecessary hits. The Rams need Stafford healthy in December and January far more than they need 400 passing yards on Sunday.

Number four Puka and Davante both involved. Puka said this week that the offense needs to get “in rhythm and when we’re in flow” and do it “more consistently.” He also talked about the deep ball he just missed near the end of the first half on Monday — one that grazed his fingertips. If those two connect on a deep shot early, Arizona’s secondary is in trouble. And Puka made a point this week of crediting the coaching staff for “holding everybody accountable” after the loss. Even after a 17-catch game, he’s talking about the plays he didn’t make. That’s the culture Stafford has helped build in that receiver room.

Number five finish drives with touchdowns instead of field goals. Blake Grupe took part in his first Rams practice on Thursday, and McVay said the goal is ideally for Grupe to kick more extra points than anything else. The best way to make a new kicker comfortable is to give him a lot of easy kicks after touchdowns.

And number six if the game gets out of hand in the fourth quarter, get Stafford off the field. Let Bennett take some snaps. A 38-year-old quarterback doesn’t need to be taking hits with a three-touchdown lead. And if that happens, it might also be a small window into how McVay views the backup job right now.
